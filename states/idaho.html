Bannock County

  • Type: County
  • Recorded status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-07-30 (Board of County Commissioners signed the emergency moratorium ordinance)
  • Normalized date: 2026-07-30 (exact)
  • Duration: 180 days from adoption (2026-07-30 to 2027-01-26)
  • Current end date: 2027-01-26
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: id-bannock-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Emergency moratorium ordinance, Instrument # 22609310, adopted under Idaho Code Section 67-6523 by the Bannock County Board of Commissioners (Jeff Hough, Chairman; Ernie Moser and Ken Bullock, Commissioners); signed 2026-07-30.
  • What prompted it: Board found an imminent peril to public health, safety, and welfare from continued unregulated siting of data centers, citing water quality, environmental impact, viewsheds, decommissioning bonds, electrical grid impact, and aquifer impacts; adopted alongside an April 13, 2026 statewide drought emergency declaration by the Idaho Department of Water Resources; gives county time to draft a standalone data center ordinance (separate from an ongoing, years-long Land Use and Development Ordinance rewrite that did not originally address data centers).
  • Affected projects: No specific project named as pending in unincorporated county jurisdiction at adoption; the moratorium does not affect the separately-pending, city-jurisdiction Lex Developments/Hoku-site data center proposal in Pocatello.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23. Moratorium on the receipt, processing, and approval of data center permit applications in unincorporated Bannock County, effective 2026-07-30 through 2027-01-26. Does not apply to any permit already issued or requested before adoption, and does not apply within city limits (including Pocatello, where a separate, contested $2.6 billion Lex Developments data center proposal for the former Hoku polysilicon plant site is working through the city's own permitting/appeal process). Commissioner Jeff Hough is separately convening a regional work group with Power, Oneida, Franklin, and Caribou counties to draft model data-center ordinances for neighboring jurisdictions, expected to begin public hearings around January 2027.
  • Outcome: Board of Commissioners adopted a 180-day emergency moratorium on data center permits in unincorporated Bannock County while staff drafts a standalone data center ordinance.

Kootenai County

  • Type: County
  • Recorded status: ⚪ Replaced
  • Date enacted: 2025-03-11, retroactive to 2025-02-27
  • Normalized date: 2025-03-11 (exact)
  • Duration: 182 days from 2025-03-11 adoption (nominal expiration 2025-09-09), retroactive to 2025-02-27. Resolution 2025-23 itself states the term as '182 days following adoption,' which places the adoption date (March 11) as day zero and the moratorium's last day as day 182 after that (September 9, 2025) -- consistent with the already-recorded computed_expiry. Replaced by Ordinance 620, noticed 2025-10-07.
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: id-kootenai-county-2025
  • Legal basis: Resolution 2025-23, "Emergency Moratorium - Data Centers"
  • What prompted it: Developer interest in unincorporated county parcels over the Rathdrum Prairie Aquifer; county findings cited water quantity and quality, electricity demand, air pollution, building size, noise, parking, and traffic circulation.
  • Affected projects: No named project or MW total identified in captured county records. County records and press coverage state only that developers had made inquiries.
  • Detailed status: Expired and replaced
  • Outcome: Ordinance 620, noticed 2025-10-07, amended county code to prohibit data centers in the Commercial zone and allow them in Light Industrial and Industrial zones only with a conditional use permit. The replacement ordinance also added data-center standards, application requirements, and a county code definition. Spokane Public Radio reported that commissioners also directed staff toward a possible outright ban and that the adopted rules barred data centers over the aquifer.

  • Sponsor / party: House Ways and Means Committee; Committee bill
  • Recorded status: Passed House 65-3-2 on 2026-03-20 and Senate 34-1-0 on 2026-03-30. The Legislature's 2026 Enacted Legislation table separately lists H911 as chapter 335, governor signature 2026-04-10 and effective 2026-07-01, corroborating the signature date stated in the earlier bill history despite its inconsistent 04/02 row label. The signed session-law or governor action record has not been inspected; the introduced bill declares an emergency and specifies July 1.
  • Provisions: Idaho Code 61-335 establishes a new large-load utility framework: a service entrance must have an increase in cumulative power requirement of at least 50 MW in any consecutive 60-month period and a public-utility contract entered on or after July 1, 2026. The increase is maximum contracted demand relative to the entrance’s highest contracted demand in the previous 60 months. The law requires commission-approved contracts and a no-harm test, full cost assignment, anti-evasion measures, financial security and rate-case review; data centers qualify only when the statutory criteria are met.
