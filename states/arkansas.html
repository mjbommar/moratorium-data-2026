Harrison

  • Type: City
  • Status: 🔴 Rescinded
  • Date enacted: 2023-05-30
  • Normalized date: 2023-05-30 (exact)
  • Duration: One year or until specific land-use standards were developed, whichever occurred first
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, cryptocurrency mining
  • Moratorium ID: ar-harrison-2023
  • Legal basis: Ordinance 1515, "An Ordinance Imposing a Temporary Moratorium on Data Centers and Crypto Mining Facilities in the City of Harrison, Arkansas"
  • What prompted it: Green Digital conditional-use application; Arkansas Data Centers Act of 2023; zoning code did not list data centers or crypto mining facilities; concerns over electricity use, greenhouse gas emissions, pollution, e-waste, noise, and local impacts
  • Affected projects: Green Digital / Metahash crypto-mining data center; Chamber timeline reports a 10 MW Entergy contract and estimated $2.5 million capital investment
  • Detailed status: Repealed; Harrison City Council voted 6-2 to repeal Ordinance 1515 at a specially called meeting on Tuesday, 2023-11-28, on a motion by Council Member Mitch Magness, seconded by Council Member Joel Williams (councilors Jeremy Ragland and Wayne Cone voted no). City Attorney Nancy Cartwright recommended repeal as a litigation precaution tied to a federal court matter involving Green Digital and the state's data-mining protections; the council also amended its noise ordinance the same night (Ordinance 1516). No separate numbered repeal ordinance for Ordinance 1515 itself was identified in the Harrison Daily Times' contemporaneous account of the meeting, and the city's own ordinance/clerk portal (harrisonar.gov) is behind a bot-protection challenge page that could not be bypassed to check the clerk's ordinance index directly.
  • Outcome: Moratorium was removed after officials concluded it increased legal exposure under the state "right to mine" framework; later state Acts 173 and 174 of 2024 restored more local and state regulatory authority over crypto mining

Independence County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-08-10 (Independence County Quorum Court unanimous adoption of Ordinance No. 2026-21 at its regular August meeting, reported the next day as a Monday-night vote)
  • Normalized date: 2026-08-10 (exact)
  • Duration: Five-year moratorium on construction of commercial data centers, effective immediately under an emergency clause; runs through Aug. 31, 2031
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: ar-independence-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Ordinance No. 2026-21, adopted with an emergency clause
  • What prompted it: Preemptive pause adopted with no data center project currently proposed in the county; Justices of the Peace cited the heavy water and electricity consumption of commercial data centers, lack of transparency in land acquisition, and a recent wind-turbine development dispute (a company holding turbine contracts predating the county's 2025 wind moratorium) as reasons to act before any project arrives.
  • Affected projects: None identified; officials said no data center had been proposed in Independence County at the time of adoption.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23. Adopted 2026-08-10 by unanimous vote under an emergency clause; runs through 2031-08-31, or until the Quorum Court determines a path forward and adopts applicable rules and regulations. No specific data center project had been proposed in the county at adoption.
  • Outcome: Preemptive five-year moratorium adopted while the Quorum Court studies commercial data centers and drafts future rules and regulations; the longest data-center moratorium term reported in Arkansas as of this pass.

Union County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-06-30 (Union County Quorum Court unanimous adoption of Ordinance 1795 at an emergency meeting); reported to run 2026-07-10 through 2027-07-09
  • Normalized date: 2026-06-30 (exact)
  • Duration: One-year moratorium, reported to run from 2026-07-10 through 2027-07-09
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: ar-union-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Ordinance 1795, adopted with an emergency clause
  • What prompted it: Pause on new data center development in Union County following resident concerns about electricity, water, transparency, and use of public resources
  • Affected projects: A proposed data center development called the 'Union County Campus' in El Dorado, tied to Paradox Data LLC. On June 25, 2026, Fort Lauderdale-based Z Squared Inc. announced a binding letter of intent to acquire a majority ownership interest in Paradox Data LLC; per SEC filings, the transaction had not closed as of the reporting reviewed. The site has an existing 8-MW utility connection, a roughly 10-acre parcel under a binding purchase contract, and company documents describing a potential campus of up to 170 acres and a long-term target of as much as 150 MW combining utility power and on-site natural-gas generation. The June 30, 2026 emergency Quorum Court meeting that adopted the moratorium was held amid heavy public interest in this proposal.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23; set to run through 2027-07-09 absent further action. Confirmed by South Arkansas Now's report of its own livestream coverage of the June 30, 2026 emergency Quorum Court meeting (Ordinance No. 1795, unanimous vote, emergency clause, term July 10, 2026 - July 9, 2027) and independently corroborated by AI GridWatch's specialty tracker, which cites the same ordinance number and end date.
