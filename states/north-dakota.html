Barnes County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-05-19, Barnes County Commission (Valley City, ND), motion by Pete Paulson, seconded by Mike Schwehr, passed 5-0.
  • Normalized date: 2026-05-19 (exact)
  • Duration: Six months from adoption (to approximately 2026-11-19).
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: nd-barnes-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Barnes County Commission motion adopting a six-month moratorium on new data center building permits/applications countywide, per official commission minutes; no separately numbered resolution/ordinance was cited in the minutes.
  • What prompted it: Preemptive: no data center project was formally pending, but the board acted on rumor of a possible project in southern Barnes County to allow time to draft zoning/ordinance regulations before any application could be filed. Several citizens asked the board to extend the pause to a full year, but the motion that passed and was recorded was for six months.
  • Affected projects: None formally pending; rumored project in southern Barnes County cited as the impetus.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-07-31 (expires approximately 2026-11-19). No extension vote found in records reviewed.
  • Outcome: Commission unanimously approved a six-month moratorium on new data center building permits/applications countywide effective 2026-05-19.

Harmony Township

  • Type: Township
  • Status: 🟡 Pending
  • Date enacted: —
  • Normalized date: — (unverified)
  • Duration: Proposed two-year zoning moratorium on all industrial-scale development, including solar, wind, data centers, battery storage systems, electric transmission lines and gas-fired electrical generation facilities, to allow the township to update its zoning ordinances; not yet adopted
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, battery storage, solar, wind, general
  • Moratorium ID: nd-harmony-township-undated
  • Legal basis: Not yet enacted. On 2026-09-22 the Township Board unanimously passed a motion (moved by Board Chair Dustin Burchill) to hold a formal public hearing on whether to adopt a two-year moratorium; the specific ordinance language has not been finalized
  • What prompted it: Geronimo Power (through affiliated LLCs including CCDC LLC) has purchase agreements for roughly 2,072 acres in Harmony Township and 934 acres in neighboring Durbin Township, and is pursuing a data center and a 200-MW battery energy storage system in the township in addition to an already-under-construction 200-MW solar facility (Harmony Solar); residents raised concerns about tonal noise, property values, water use, road traffic, organic-farming impacts, and inadequate study of impacts, and about a conflict of interest (a former township board chair who did not recuse himself from 2025-2026 zoning-ordinance votes had a pending land-purchase agreement with a Geronimo Power affiliate)
  • Affected projects: A proposed Geronimo Power data center and a proposed 200-MW Harmony Storage LLC battery energy storage system in Harmony Township (Cass County); Harmony Solar, a 200-MW solar facility by Geronimo Power, began construction 2026-08-24 and is not itself blocked by the proposed moratorium's timing.
  • Detailed status: Pending as of 2026-09-23. The Township Board voted unanimously on 2026-09-22 to hold a public hearing on whether a two-year moratorium on industrial-scale development (data centers, battery storage, solar, wind, transmission lines, gas generation) is appropriate; no hearing date had been set. The board separately voted to seek a legal review of 2025-2026 zoning-ordinance amendments that eased approval conditions for the proposed data center, including whether a conflict of interest by a then-board-chair voids those amendments, and whether the pending conditional use permit applications for the data center and battery storage facility remain legally eligible for consideration pending that review.
  • Outcome: Board voted to schedule a public hearing on a proposed two-year moratorium and to seek a legal review of recent zoning amendments and pending conditional use permits before further action; no moratorium has been adopted yet.

McIntosh County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Extended
  • Date enacted: First enacted in July 2025 (exact date not confirmed in sources reviewed); extended by County Commissioner vote on 2026-06-01
  • Normalized date: 2025-07 (month_only)
  • Duration: A moratorium on the use of county-maintained roads for the transport and delivery of equipment and materials associated with wind turbine, solar panel, and related infrastructure construction; originally a 12-month term from its July 2025 enactment, extended by commissioner vote on 2026-06-01 (the length of the extension was not specified in the source reviewed)
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: wind, solar
  • Moratorium ID: nd-mcintosh-county-2025
  • Legal basis: McIntosh County Board of County Commissioners motion/action, first enacted July 2025 and extended by vote on 2026-06-01 (instrument/resolution number not located)
  • What prompted it: Commissioners raised concerns about the impact of large-scale wind and solar construction traffic on county-maintained roads and bridges (heavy loads, vehicles exceeding weight restrictions) after TransAlta announced and then dropped plans for a wind farm in the county; the county wanted road-use agreements and oversight in place before any wind or solar developer (including Orsted, whose Badger Wind Farm spans McIntosh and Logan counties) could use county roads for construction
  • Affected projects: Badger Wind Farm (Orsted, 250 MW, spanning Logan and McIntosh counties) came online in January 2026 and is not affected (construction predates/was completed around the moratorium's operative window); the moratorium is framed as preventing any future wind or solar developer from using county roads without a road-use agreement.
  • Detailed status: Extended as of 2026-06-01 per the McIntosh Democrat; long-form reporting by Canary Media (2026-09) and Mother Jones (2026-09) independently describes McIntosh County as having 'adopted a one-year moratorium on new wind projects' in April 2026 -- a description that may refer to this same road-use moratorium (simplified) or to a distinct action not otherwise located in this pass. As of 2026-09-23 the road-use restriction on wind/solar construction traffic remains the confirmed, sourced instrument. [VERIFY instrument number; primary source (news-only evidence) not confirmed at confidence 0.55 in the 2026-09 refresh]
  • Outcome: A road-use moratorium restricting wind- and solar-construction traffic on county roads, in effect since July 2025 and extended by the County Commission on 2026-06-01.

Morton County

  • Type: County
  • Status: ⚪ Replaced
  • Date enacted: 2024-09-12
  • Normalized date: 2024-09-12 (exact)
  • Duration: Ran until siting and performance standards for data centers and cryptocurrency mining facilities were developed and adopted. The County Commission adopted a Data Center Standards section of the Morton County Land Use Code on 2026-09-10, satisfying that condition, per the county's own website.
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, cryptocurrency mining
  • Moratorium ID: nd-morton-county-2024
  • Legal basis: Resolution 30-2024-03 (Sept. 12, 2024 moratorium). Superseded in substance by a County Commission resolution adopted 2026-09-10 (4-1 vote, Commissioner Tokach dissenting over a tonal-noise feasibility concern) that added a Data Center Standards section to the Morton County Land Use Code, following Planning & Zoning Commission review at its Feb. 25, March 25, April 22, May 27, and July 22, 2026 meetings, a subcommittee process (June-August 2026), and a P&Z recommendation of approval on Aug. 26, 2026.
  • What prompted it: County findings cited Williams County impacts, lack of local standards, and need to protect health, safety, and welfare while drafting data center and cryptocurrency mining standards.
  • Affected projects: No data center application is pending within Morton County's own jurisdiction. A proposed Stark Power data-center campus and natural-gas generation facility in north Mandan (which drew substantial public comment at county and city meetings) falls within the City of Mandan's extraterritorial area, so Morton County has no jurisdiction over that specific project.
  • Detailed status: Replaced as of 2026-09-23. The County Commission adopted a Data Center Standards section for the Morton County Land Use Code on 2026-09-10 (4-1), and the county's website states this final draft 'has been incorporated into the Morton County Land Use Code.' The standards set noise limits (noise-producing equipment generally at least three-quarters of a mile from an occupied home), require closed-loop cooling, and set setback, water/power-use, road, emergency-planning, and reclamation-bond requirements (with a waiver path if a former data center site is repurposed). Per the Bismarck Tribune's Sept. 11, 2026 report, the Planning & Zoning Director stated a separate notice period was required before the Commission could formally vote to lift the underlying moratorium resolution itself (since it was adopted by resolution), and she expected that vote at the Commission's next meeting after Sept. 10; no report of that specific repeal vote was found as of 2026-09-23. No data center application is currently pending in the county's own (non-extraterritorial) jurisdiction; a separate proposed Stark Power data-center/gas-generation project in north Mandan falls within the City of Mandan's extraterritorial area, not Morton County's jurisdiction.
  • Outcome: Following P&Z review at five 2026 meetings (Feb. 25, March 25, April 22, May 27, July 22), a subcommittee process (June-August 2026), and a P&Z recommendation of approval (Aug. 26, 2026), the County Commission adopted the Data Center Standards section of the Morton County Land Use Code on 2026-09-10 by a 4-1 vote. The standards apply countywide and are not tied to approval of any specific project.
