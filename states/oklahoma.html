  • Normalized date: 2026-03-07 (exact)
  • Duration: Indefinite by design, not merely undisclosed. Per KGOU reporting (June 25, 2026), the resolution's author, Rev. Chebon Kernell, originally drafted a ban on data-center discussions/inquiries/development, but 'that proposal turned into an indefinite moratorium at the suggestion of another council member.' The resolution's own phrase 'the moratorium period' (as quoted by Native News Online) therefore refers to an open-ended condition rather than a fixed numeric term that simply has not been published. The full resolution text remains unposted on sno-nsn.gov.
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: ok-seminole-nation-of-oklahoma-2026
  • Legal basis: General Council of the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma, Tribal Resolution TR 2026-38, 'A Verbal Resolution... Implementing a Moratorium on the Advancement of Generative Artificial Intelligence Technology and Hyperscale Data Center Development within the Jurisdiction of the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma,' passed and approved at Mekusukey Mission on March 7, 2026 by a vote of 24 Yes, 0 No, 0 Abstaining (quorum of 15 present).
  • What prompted it: Startup approach seeking an NDA and letter of intent to develop a data center on Seminole lands; environmental, water, sovereignty, and community-health concerns
  • Affected projects: Any inquiries, discussions, or developments concerning data centers of any size within the Seminole Nation during the moratorium period
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23. TR 2026-38 bars 'any inquiries, discussions, and/or developments concerning any entity seeking to develop a data center of any size within the Seminole Nation... during the moratorium period,' with no stated end date (open-ended by design, per KGOU/KOSU reporting on the resolution's drafting history). Multiple independent sources through Aug. 20, 2026 (KOSU, Turtle Talk blog, Current Tribal Law) continue to describe the moratorium as in force, with no rescission, amendment, or replacement framework identified as of 2026-09-23. No Tribal Council minutes confirming continued force were located on the Seminole Nation's own website in this pass either.
  • Outcome: Moratorium adopted before project approval; permanent replacement framework not identified

  • Normalized date: 2026-08-17 (exact)
  • Duration: From adoption through December 31, 2027
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: ok-shawnee-2026
  • Legal basis: Shawnee City Commission resolution (specific resolution/instrument number not located in captured coverage), sponsored by Commissioner Chris Odneal, freezing new data center applications through Dec. 31, 2027
  • What prompted it: Preemptive pause to allow city staff to study how large-scale data centers might strain local power and water systems, and to update city regulations, before accepting new proposals; city staff had initially estimated a 90-day study period would suffice, but Commissioners opted for a longer runway through the end of 2027. Discussions ahead of the vote also touched on land-use questions involving the Citizen Potawatomi Nation, whose tribal headquarters are in Shawnee.
  • Affected projects: None named; no current applications were pending at adoption. Applies only to new applications going forward, not to any existing operating or under-construction projects.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23. Moratorium adopted Aug. 17, 2026 on a unanimous 7-0 vote, effective immediately, freezing new data-center applications through Dec. 31, 2027; does not affect any data center projects already operating or under construction in Shawnee. Staff committed to reporting findings within 90 days of adoption.
  • Outcome: Shawnee City Commission adopted a moratorium freezing new data center applications through the end of 2027, directing staff to study power and water impacts and update city regulations before the pause lifts.

Tulsa

  • Type: City
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-03-25 (unanimous Council vote; reported by Oklahoma Energy Today and Tulsa Flyer; ordinance text included emergency clause requiring immediate effectiveness, so adoption and effective dates coincide)
  • Normalized date: 2026-03-25 (exact)
  • Duration: Through 2026-12-31, or earlier if data center zoning amendments are adopted
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: ok-tulsa-2026
  • Legal basis: Tulsa City Council File/Ordinance No. 26-196 (final substitute version "26-196-3"), reviewed by the Urban Development committee and Council on 2026-03-11 and adopted by the Budget/Special Projects committee and Council on 2026-03-25. A redlined draft of the ordinance (marked 'JCB 3/25/26') shows the bill's caption and Section 4 term were amended from a flat '365-DAY' moratorium to a fixed expiration of 'December 31, 2026, or on the effective date of an ordinance amending data center regulations..., whichever is earlier'; the amendment also renumbered sections to add a Section 4 '60-DAY REPORTS' requirement for the Planning Office. This resolves the tension between the city's caption text and Tulsa Flyer's '9 months' reporting: the final adopted term is the Dec. 31, 2026 calendar date, not a literal 365-day count from adoption.
  • What prompted it: Re-evaluation of Tulsa's 2024 classification of data centers as low-impact manufacturing and industry; concern that large-scale data centers may have off-site impacts
  • Affected projects: New building permits for data center use in Tulsa; Project Anthem Phase 1 and one pending Phase 2 zoning case are exempted
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23; runs through Dec. 31, 2026 (the term as actually adopted, notwithstanding the ordinance's '365-day' caption -- see legal_basis) or the effective date of new data-center zoning regulations, whichever is earlier. An Aug. 24, 2026 Tulsa Flyer report confirms the moratorium 'went into effect March 25 and expires Dec. 31,' that the Planning Office's first 60-day report has been produced and recommends reclassifying data centers by square footage and widening setbacks from 250 to 1,500 feet, and that final zoning recommendations are due to Council in November 2026, after which Council could adopt them or extend the moratorium. No extension or repeal had occurred as of 2026-09-23.
  • Outcome: Planning Office must report to Council approximately every 60 days on zoning-code review and recommendations
