City of Ephrata

  • Type: City
  • Status: ⚪ Replaced
  • Date enacted: 2018-10-17 (Spokesman-Review reported the "City Council voted Wednesday" of 2018-10-17 to block new cryptocurrency operations for 12 months; vote 6-1 with Mayor Reim absent)
  • Normalized date: 2018-10-17 (exact)
  • Duration: 12 months (per Spokesman-Review: "yearlong ban on new cryptocurrency mining")
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: cryptocurrency mining
  • Moratorium ID: wa-ephrata-2018
  • Legal basis: Interim moratorium adopted by City Council vote on 2018-10-17 (per Spokesman-Review); later codified and superseded within Ephrata Municipal Code (EMC) Chapter 19.30 'High Intensity Computer Operations,' whose legislative-history citation reads '(Ord 18-13, 2018; Ord 18-28, 2018).' Note: EMC 19.02's definition of 'high intensity computer operations' expressly excludes the exchange of cryptocurrency/virtual currency and peer-to-peer distributed-ledger maintenance, so the permanent code is framed as a broader high-density-computing-load prohibition rather than a cryptocurrency-specific one.
  • What prompted it: Grant County PUD power-capacity uncertainty (Grant PUD received 125 service requests totaling >2,000 MW with ~75% from cryptocurrency firms), noise complaints from residents on Nob Hill Drive ("the noise was like an ocean"), and local concern over whether cryptocurrency mining fit the city utility and land-use framework
  • Affected projects: Four existing cryptocurrency operations were reported (two at the Port of Ephrata, one in a city-zoned industrial area, and one in a residential area being relocated); the moratorium applied to new operations only
  • Detailed status: Replaced. The 2018 temporary cryptocurrency-mining moratorium was superseded the same year by a permanent citywide prohibition on 'High Intensity Computer Operations' (EMC 19.30.010: 'All high intensity computer operations shall be prohibited within the City of Ephrata, including those conducted as a home occupation'), citing Ordinance 18-13 (2018) and Ordinance 18-28 (2018), confirmed via the City's own published Municipal Code as of 2026-09-23.
  • Outcome: The temporary 2018 cryptocurrency-mining moratorium was followed, within the same year, by a permanent zoning prohibition on high-intensity computer operations (EMC Chapter 19.30), citing Ordinance 18-13 and Ordinance 18-28, confirmed via the current Ephrata Municipal Code (Title 19, Zoning Code).

City of Seattle

  • Type: City
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-06-09 (Seattle City Council unanimous final passage of Council Bill 121214 and companion Resolution 32204, under emergency designation); signed by Mayor Katie Wilson 2026-06-11; Ordinance No. 127447
  • Normalized date: 2026-06-09 (exact)
  • Duration: 365 days from the effective date (immediate, under emergency designation), automatically expiring thereafter unless renewed for one or more additional six-month periods in accordance with RCW 36.70A.390; a public hearing is required within 60 days of adoption.
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: wa-seattle-2026
  • Legal basis: Seattle Ordinance No. 127447 (Council Bill 121214, amended), with companion Resolution 32204 (amended); adopted under the RCW 36.70A.390 interim-zoning-control procedure. Amends SMC 23.84A.008 to define "data center" as a facility used primarily for storing and processing digital data, with power capacity over 20 Megavolt-Amperes and requiring uninterruptible power, and imposes a moratorium on the filing, acceptance, processing, or approval of applications to establish or expand such uses.
  • What prompted it: Five proposed large-scale data centers reportedly seeking Seattle City Light service, with combined maximum demand of 369 MW (about a third of Seattle's average daily power consumption); public concern over ratepayer, environmental justice, and power-supply impacts
  • Affected projects: Five proposed Seattle data centers, 369 MW combined maximum demand, per Seattle Times report (2026-04-10) and GeekWire follow-up (2026-04-19)
  • Detailed status: Active. Adopted by City Council 2026-06-09 (unanimous), signed by Mayor Wilson 2026-06-11. Companion Resolution 32204 directs a study/policy process (grid capacity, water use, utility rates, land use, jobs, and public health impacts) with reports and proposed permanent legislation due by January 2027. Two data center projects that were already vested under prior rules are not covered by the moratorium; new applications from companies including Prologis, Equinix, and Sabey are paused pending the study process. No source dated after early-to-mid summer 2026 was located to further update this status as of 2026-07-31.
  • Outcome: Moratorium enacted 2026-06-09; the city is using the pause and the companion Resolution 32204 working-group process to study grid, water, ratepayer, and environmental-justice impacts and to develop permanent siting/zoning rules, with reports and proposed permanent legislation due by January 2027.
