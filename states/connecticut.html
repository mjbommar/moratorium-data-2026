  • Moratorium ID: ct-groton-2022
  • Legal basis: Planning and Zoning Commission application REGA22-01, Data Center Moratorium Text Amendment, town-wide
  • What prompted it: Local proposals for large data centers, uncertainty about how data centers differed from telecommunications facilities, and resident concerns about noise, stormwater, generator use, energy use, and environmental impacts
  • Affected projects: Data center applications; public reporting states the pause applied to developments larger than 5,000 square feet and followed proposals for two 250,000-square-foot facilities near Interstate 95
  • Detailed status: Replaced
  • Outcome: Permanent regulations adopted in 2023 limit principal data centers to 12,500 square feet and impose siting and operating limits; public reporting describes this as blocking hyperscale projects

  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: ct-trumbull-2026
  • Legal basis: Resolution/Ordinance TC31-65, adopted unanimously by Trumbull Town Council on September 9, 2026 and unanimously passed as emergency legislation. Attached text sections A-D imposes a two-year moratorium on establishment/development of new data centers, expressly excludes existing data centers, defines the covered use and sunsets twenty-four months from effectiveness.
  • What prompted it: specific_project concerns: 365 Data Centers (Norwalk) and Aphorio Carter (a data center division of private equity firm Carter Funds) announced in May 2026 plans for a 200 MW pipeline of AI-ready data centers, naming Trumbull's decades-old former Nasdaq data center at 80 Merritt Blvd. as a likely site for modernization into a 'high density, AI-ready' facility; a resident petition and advocacy group (Trumbull AI Data Center Watch) pushed for a moratorium and had sought (unsuccessfully) to have it cover the 80 Merritt Blvd. project itself.
  • Affected projects: The existing 80 Merritt Boulevard facility was discussed as exempt under TC31-65 section B, which states that existing data centers are unaffected. Counsel described written communications and operator commitments concerning the modernization but said there was no formal agreement or agreed enforcement provision at the meeting. This is not a finding that negotiated operating limits were executed or enforceable.
  • Detailed status: Recorded active. September 9, 2026 council minutes confirm unanimous adoption of TC31-65 and a separate unanimous emergency-legislation vote. The attached ordinance imposes a two-year pause on new data-center establishment/development and expressly exempts existing data centers. Counsel explained that publication, not the adoption vote alone, would trigger effectiveness. Actual publication/effective date, any executed final copy and later amendment/repeal remain unverified. The existing 80 Merritt Boulevard facility was discussed as outside this new-facility moratorium.
  • Outcome: Official minutes confirm unanimous adoption of TC31-65 and emergency treatment, with the complete attached ordinance preserving an existing-facility exemption and a twenty-four-month sunset from effectiveness. The prior secondary effective-immediately-on-adoption account is superseded by the recorded publication explanation. Actual publication, operative interval and subsequent legal force remain unresolved.
