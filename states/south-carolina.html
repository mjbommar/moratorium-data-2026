Clarendon County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: September 14, 2026 (County Council third and final reading and public hearing, per WIS; second reading was at an Aug. 24, 2026 special called meeting, first reading Aug. 10, 2026)
  • Normalized date: 2026-09-14 (exact)
  • Duration: Six months
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, battery storage
  • Moratorium ID: sc-clarendon-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Ordinance 2026-18 (Clarendon County Council)
  • What prompted it: County's Unified Development Code does not specifically define data centers or battery energy storage systems; council sought to close a loophole after learning of a proposed "Project 59" data center in the county industrial park, and to give time to set standards for setbacks, buffers, noise, lighting, and emergency access.
  • Affected projects: Proposed "Project 59" data center in the county industrial park (Santee Electric Cooperative / developer identified as "Five Nines"); this project is not paused by the moratorium because it is sited in an Industrial I district.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23; adopted unanimously on third and final reading Sept. 14, 2026, following a public hearing. Pauses land-use and development approvals for data centers and battery storage facilities outside areas zoned Industrial I or Industrial II; does not affect the proposed Project 59 data center, which is sited in an Industrial I district and received a conditional-use determination from county planning staff.
  • Outcome: Adopted six-month moratorium on data center and battery energy storage system approvals outside industrially zoned areas.

  • Duration: Six months
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, battery storage
  • Moratorium ID: sc-colleton-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Ordinance 26-O-04 (Colleton County Council), partially repealed as of 2026-09-14 for areas west of Interstate 95 and land already zoned Light/Industrial/Heavy Industrial, by a new ordinance establishing a "Digital Infrastructure Overlay District" (which also repeals and replaces the county's separate data-center zoning ordinance, No. 25-O-10) and a companion zoning-map amendment, both adopted on third reading 2026-09-14.
  • What prompted it: Pauses consideration of special exceptions, conditional uses, and other land-use approvals for data centers while county staff and the Planning Commission study appropriate locations/conditions and prepare comprehensive-plan and code amendments; also holds received-but-unconsidered applications in abeyance.
  • Affected projects: Unspecified data center project(s) subject to related Southern Environmental Law Center litigation, paused during the moratorium.
  • Detailed status: Partially superseded as of 2026-09-14. Colleton County Council unanimously adopted (third reading) a Digital Infrastructure Overlay District ordinance permitting data centers and battery energy storage systems, subject to conditions (300+ acre minimum, 65 dB noise cap, 200-ft buffers, closed-loop cooling), west of Interstate 95 and on land already zoned Light/Industrial/Industrial/Heavy Industrial; the moratorium (Ordinance 26-O-04) is repealed with respect to those areas but continues to apply elsewhere in the county (east of I-95, non-industrial-zoned land) through its original ~180-day term (adopted 2026-07-06, running to approximately 2027-01-02) absent further council action.
  • Outcome: Adopted six-month moratorium in July 2026 (Ordinance 26-O-04); on 2026-09-14 the county partially repealed that moratorium and replaced it, for areas west of I-95 and already-industrial-zoned land, with a Digital Infrastructure Overlay District permitting data centers and battery storage under conditions (minimum 300 acres, 65 dB noise cap, 200-ft buffers, closed-loop cooling); the moratorium remains in force elsewhere in the county pending its original term.

Spartanburg County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: September 21, 2026 (County Council third and final reading, 5-1 vote, per Post and Courier and Fox Carolina; first reading occurred in late June 2026)
  • Normalized date: 2026-09-21 (exact)
  • Duration: One year (12 months)
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: sc-spartanburg-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Spartanburg County Council data center moratorium ordinance (ordinance number not located in reviewed sources); amended before third reading to exempt data centers of 65 IT megawatts or less that are sited in an established industrial park or former industrial site and meet specified conditions
  • What prompted it: Public outcry over power grid strain, water resources, and air quality; moratorium was first proposed in response to the Lightstone Group's proposed data center on Jones Road (a 60-megawatt facility on a former industrial site).
  • Affected projects: Lightstone Group's proposed Jones Road data center (the original trigger); does not apply to the already-approved Valara data center under construction on South Pine Street.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23; adopted on third reading Sept. 21, 2026 by a 5-1 vote (Coker dissenting). The moratorium applies to data centers exceeding a combined 65 IT-megawatt electrical load; data centers at or below that threshold sited in a named industrial park or former industrial site, that do not self-generate power (except emergency backup), may still be approved under specified conditions. Does not apply to the already-approved Valara data center or to cryptocurrency mining.
  • Outcome: Adopted one-year moratorium on new data center development, with amendments exempting facilities of 65 megawatts or less on qualifying industrial sites.
