  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, battery storage
  • Moratorium ID: or-city-of-hillsboro-2026
  • Legal basis: Resolution No. 2932, adopted by the Hillsboro City Council at a special meeting on July 27, 2026
  • What prompted it: City sought time to study data center and battery storage impacts and develop code amendments; contemporaneous coverage described Hillsboro as the first Oregon city to adopt a data center land-use moratorium

  • Outcome: Enacted moratorium on new data center and battery storage land-use applications while the city studies impacts and develops code amendments.

Salem

  • Type: City
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-09-21 (City Council unanimous vote at a special session)
  • Normalized date: 2026-09-21 (exact)
  • Duration: 120 days from adoption (2026-09-21 to 2027-01-19); Oregon law allows extensions of up to 300 additional days with additional findings.
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: or-salem-2026
  • Legal basis: Moratorium ordinance adopted by the Salem City Council on 2026-09-21, following an August 3, 2026 council vote to move forward with the moratorium and a resolution to begin updating zoning/land-use code; required 45-day notice to the Oregon Department of Land Conservation and Development preceded formal adoption.
  • What prompted it: specific_project
  • Affected projects: Verrus Group filed a land use application in late July 2026 for a roughly 600,000-square-foot data center in Salem; the application proceeds under pre-moratorium review rules, but the moratorium bars construction while it is in effect.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23. Moratorium halts construction of all data centers (as a primary use) and accessory data centers with facility capacity greater than 2 megawatts, effective 2026-09-21 through 2027-01-19. City Attorney Dan Atchison based the moratorium on a compelling need to prevent public harm, citing the city's water supply risk from the impending deep drawdown of the Detroit Reservoir (serving roughly 220,000 water customers). The moratorium does not block Verrus's already-filed land use application for a 600,000-square-foot data center (filed late July 2026, before the moratorium), since Oregon law generally requires applications to be reviewed under rules in effect when filed, but the city attorney confirmed construction itself can be prohibited during the moratorium.
  • Outcome: City Council unanimously adopted a 120-day moratorium halting data center construction while the city develops permanent zoning and land-use regulations.

Woodburn

  • Type: City
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-09-14 (City Council unanimous 6-0 vote)
  • Normalized date: 2026-09-14 (exact)
  • Duration: 120 days from adoption (2026-09-14 to 2027-01-12); the City Manager stated the council could extend it to up to six months total.
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: or-woodburn-2026
  • Legal basis: Moratorium ordinance adopted by the Woodburn City Council on 2026-09-14; the city filed the required 45-day notice of intent to consider a moratorium with the Oregon Department of Land Conservation and Development on 2026-07-30 before the council could act.
  • What prompted it: specific_project
  • Affected projects: No formal application received; city staff learned that data center developer Verrus was exploring a site 'in or near Woodburn,' including the 237-acre Southwest Industrial Reserve Area.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23. Moratorium prevents city planning staff from considering any new data center land-use application, effective 2026-09-14 through 2027-01-12. Adopted after the city learned that data center developer Verrus (which had separately applied to build in Salem, where its application drew significant public resistance) was exploring development of a data center on a 237-acre privately owned Southwest Industrial Reserve Area parcel brought into Woodburn's urban growth boundary in 2025; Woodburn had not received a formal application as of adoption. The city separately adopted an ordinance in June 2026 designating data centers as a conditional use in that industrial reserve area, and has hired a consultant (Thomas Consulting Group) to help review and potentially revise its development code for data centers.
  • Outcome: City Council unanimously adopted a 120-day moratorium on considering new data center applications while the city reviews and potentially revises its development code.
