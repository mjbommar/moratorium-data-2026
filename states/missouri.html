Benton County

  • Status: 🟢 Active

  • Normalized date: 2026-08-10 (exact)

  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center
  • Moratorium ID: mo-benton-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Benton County Commission order enacting a six-month moratorium on data centers, adopted 2026-08-10; the county has no planning and zoning code, and the county attorney has argued a due-diligence/public-health rationale for the pause.
  • What prompted it: Commissioners responded to citizen concerns about a proposed data center after a developer (Trent Overhue / NSI6 LLC) began seeking to acquire land in the county specifically because it lacked planning and zoning restrictions.
  • Affected projects: A roughly 12-acre data center project (two ~20,000-sq-ft buildings) on Old Highway 65 pursued by developer Trent Overhue through NSI6 LLC.

Developer Trent Overhue/NSI6 LLC closed on a roughly 12-acre parcel Sept. 9, 2026 and began clearing land; a Sept. 8, 2026 temporary restraining order against county enforcement of the moratorium as to that project was extended by the court on Sept. 21, 2026 pending a ruling on a preliminary injunction. The county disputes that NSI6 LLC (which it says did not legally exist until Aug. 13, 2026, three days after the moratorium) is entitled to rely on the pre-moratorium status quo. No final ruling had been reported as of 2026-09-23.
  • Outcome: Benton County commissioners issued a six-month moratorium on data centers on 2026-08-10; the developer whose project the moratorium affects sued and obtained a temporary restraining order (granted 2026-09-08, extended 2026-09-21) while a preliminary-injunction ruling is pending.

Cedar County

  • Type: County
  • Status: 🟢 Active
  • Date enacted: 2026-09-08 (Cedar County Commissioners)
  • Normalized date: 2026-09-08 (exact)
  • Duration: 12 months; commissioners may extend once for up to six additional months
  • Current end date: —
  • Sectors: data center, solar, wind, battery storage
  • Moratorium ID: mo-cedar-county-2026
  • Legal basis: Cedar County ordinance imposing a 12-month moratorium, adopted by commissioners 2026-09-08, citing (among other authority) RSMo §49.650 and county public-health authority; legal commentators note §49.650 by its terms applies to first-, second-, and fourth-class noncharter counties while Cedar County is third class, raising an open legal question about the ordinance's validity.
  • What prompted it: Commissioners cited possible effects on water, power, noise, drainage, and public health from large new solar, wind, battery-storage, and data-center developments outside city limits, describing the pause as 'due diligence'; no specific project was pending at the time of adoption.
  • Affected projects: None named; commissioners acknowledged no specific project was then proposed.
  • Detailed status: Active as of 2026-09-23; set to expire 2027-09-08 absent a single permitted extension of up to six additional months. Residents and at least one local business owner (who called the measure 'backdoor zoning') have publicly questioned whether the county had legal authority to adopt it without voter-approved planning and zoning; commissioners have declined to comment publicly and the county's own attorney has not returned press inquiries.
  • Outcome: Cedar County commissioners imposed a 12-month moratorium on new large solar, wind, battery-storage, and data-center developments outside city limits (small on-site systems under a 25-kilowatt exemption and existing projects are exempt), while promising future public forums.
