About this update

The local inventory contains 1,329 records from 47 states. It includes adopted moratoria, proposals, and actions that have ended.

The status of each record reflects the sources we found. Some dates and legal details remain unconfirmed. A record listed as active may have changed since its source was published.

The November 13 paper is now available below. The latest record correction identifies Pima County Resolution 2026-56 and the county’s reported 120-day term. Its precise operative and expiration dates remain unresolved.

Local actions, state policy, and ballot measures

We count these separately. State bills can change local authority or state permit rules without creating a local moratorium. Ballot measures can ask for a temporary pause, a permanent restriction, or a zoning change. A vote in favor does not always put a restriction into effect.

What the search covers

We searched all 50 states, but the inventory is not complete. The main November search ended November 7, 2026. Later checks have added sources and corrected records. Each record keeps its source dates.

The November 13 second edition updates the paper for the current data, adds ballot measures, and revises the legal reference. It withdraws the earlier clause-prevalence percentages. See the known gaps for the limits of the evidence.

What is a moratorium?

A moratorium is a temporary pause on specified development applications or approvals. Local governments often use the pause to study a proposed use or write land-use rules. The exact restrictions and exceptions depend on the adopted text. Read the FAQ.

Ballot measures are listed separately because they can address temporary pauses, permanent restrictions, or other zoning changes. See ballot results.

How we collect the data

We search government records and news reports, then compare the findings with ordinances, resolutions, and meeting minutes where available. We mark details we cannot confirm.

The earlier model-based clause percentages are withdrawn. The extraction cohorts included unrelated documents, summaries, and duplicates. The revised paper uses six reviewed texts as drafting examples, without estimating national clause prevalence.
