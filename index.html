Release v2026.07 · 31 July 2026

What changed since April

The inventory grew from 222 instruments to 416, across 35 states instead of 30. Method explains most of that jump. This is the first release to sweep states month by month, so read 416 as a floor.

New activity

  • 170 moratoria were adopted between May 1 and July 31. June accounted for 80 of them, the busiest month on record.
  • Big cities joined in. Seattle passed an emergency ordinance on June 9. Cleveland followed on July 15 by a 14-1 vote, after committee cut the term from twelve months to three. Minneapolis, Denver, Charlotte, and Montgomery County, Maryland all acted. Durham city and Durham County each passed their own.
  • Five states appear here for the first time: Florida, Nevada, New Mexico, South Carolina, and Texas.

What happened to the older ones

  • Extension is the usual outcome. DeKalb County, Georgia voted its permanent ordinance down on June 23, then pushed the pause out to March 2027. Prince George's County, Maryland renewed for two more years.
  • Nine became permanent rules - among them Griffin and Polk County in Georgia, Montour County in Pennsylvania, Brevard in North Carolina, and St. Charles Parish in Louisiana.
  • Six simply lapsed. La Grange, Kentucky and Waterville, Ohio let theirs run out with nothing ready to replace them.
  • A few went the other way. Lowell Township, Michigan voted a moratorium down 2-5. Hill County, Texas repealed one after a developer sued for $100 million. Oliver County, North Dakota repealed its third pause seven weeks in.

State legislation

All 438 tracked bills now carry a final disposition, and 84 are law. Legislatures went after cost allocation more than siting. New Jersey signed its large-load tariff act on July 7. Oklahoma passed a Data Center Customer Protection Act, Maryland the Utility RELIEF Act, and Idaho now requires a "no harm" test for any new load above 50 megawatts. Florida moved the opposite way and protected local land-use authority.

How to read the coverage

18 states got the month-by-month sweep. The other 32 did not, so their May-July counts are floors. Florida shows why: it held one row before its sweep and 14 after. The state did not change. Our looking did. Five swept states recorded no local adoptions at all - a finding worth as much as a count. Known gaps has the full accounting.

What is a moratorium?

A moratorium is a temporary pause. A city, county, or township stops accepting or approving a certain kind of development while it writes rules for it. Most run six to twelve months. The trigger is almost always the same: a project arrives that the zoning code has no word for. Read the FAQ →

Methodology

We collect the primary documents first - ordinances, resolutions, board minutes - then code them. The corpus runs to roughly 4,600 files gathered across all 50 states. 348 moratorium texts have been read line by line against a 44-clause taxonomy; that sample predates 28 April 2026 and covers 211 jurisdictions, so it describes what moratoria contain rather than all 416 of them. Coordinates were checked three ways across 89 verifications, with no confirmed errors. Where we could not confirm something, the row says so.
