How to read the tracker

One buildout, two layers of restriction

A local moratorium stops or delays approvals in one jurisdiction. State policy can instead bind an agency, utility process, incentive, or every municipality. The datasets stay separate so unlike legal effects do not collapse into one count.

Data refresh · 19 August 2026

The August picture

Local governments are still using temporary pauses, but the fastest-moving policy layer is now state government. The local inventory contains 533 instruments across 42 states; 429 remain in force.

What changed in this refresh

  • The status review reduced the recorded in-force total by six. The current mix is 381 active and 48 extended, with 27 expired, 37 replaced, and 6 rescinded. That movement reflects elapsed terms and documented outcomes, not a six-place wave of repeals.
  • 1 local adoption is confirmed for August so far: Lakeland, Florida adopted Ordinance 26-018 on August 3. Parma Township, Michigan was also added after its primary public notice was located.
  • Extensions now carry a current end date. The tracker keeps the original term for provenance but uses current_end_date_iso for the operative extension, preventing the original deadline from being mistaken for a present-day lapse.

State restrictions are a separate policy layer

The state tracker now distinguishes 438 bills from 2 binding non-bill actions, with 84 enacted bills. New York Executive Order 62 holds specified state-agency permits in abeyance while a statewide environmental review proceeds. Texas's August 3 directive pauses ERCOT interconnection progress pending an audit. Pennsylvania SB 1345 would authorize municipal pauses, while SB 1359 proposes a statewide moratorium; both remain proposals. The typed policy fields preserve those legal differences.

How to read the coverage

All 50 states received a month-by-month sweep for May through July, so that window is the most comparable part of the series. August is current only through the 19th and is not a completed monthly sweep. Pre-May totals remain lower bounds because they came from document search and opportunistic discovery. Known gaps records the coverage boundaries.

What is a moratorium?

A moratorium is a temporary pause. A city, county, or township stops accepting or approving a certain kind of development while it writes rules for it. Most run six to twelve months. The trigger is almost always the same: a project arrives that the zoning code has no word for. Read the FAQ →

Methodology

We collect the primary documents first - ordinances, resolutions, board minutes - then code them. The corpus runs to roughly 4,600 files gathered across all 50 states. 348 moratorium texts have been read line by line against a 44-clause taxonomy; that sample predates 28 April 2026 and covers 211 jurisdictions, so it describes what moratoria contain rather than all 533 of them. Coordinates were checked three ways across 89 verifications, with no confirmed errors. Where we could not confirm something, the row says so.
