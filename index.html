How to read the tracker

One buildout, two layers of restriction

A local moratorium stops or delays approvals in one jurisdiction. State policy can instead bind an agency, utility process, incentive, or every municipality. The datasets stay separate so unlike legal effects do not collapse into one count.

The November dataset update

The inventory tracks 1328 local inventory records across 47 states. Recorded classifications include 888 active and 198 extended records, 64 pending, 84 replaced, 76 expired, 12 rescinded, 2 rejected proposals and 4 unknown. Source dates vary by record; these totals do not establish freshly verified current legal force.

Ballots and state policy have separate denominators

Reviewed ballot results distinguish vote observations, certification and legal effect. Proposed initiatives and intended election dates do not establish ballot appearance; recorded litigation is distinct from electoral defeat. Advisory questions, permanent prohibitions and site-specific rezoning votes are not added to the local moratorium total merely because they concern data centers. Known gaps retains unresolved candidates and instrument histories.

The state-policy tracker contains 449 records spanning several mechanisms. It is not a count of enacted state moratoria or a deduplicated count of independent bills. Reviewed precursor memos and legislative resolutions have separate legal-form types; memo links to numbered bills do not merge their identities. Compound rows and unresolved identity grains remain. Read the typed mechanism, scope, legal-effect fields and documented identity gaps.

What is a moratorium?

A moratorium is a temporary pause. A city, county, or township stops accepting or approving a certain kind of development while it writes rules for it. Most run six to twelve months. The trigger is almost always the same: a project arrives that the zoning code has no word for. Read the FAQ →

Methodology

We collect the primary documents first - ordinances, resolutions, board minutes - then code them. Sources span all 50 states; a nationwide search is not a complete census. 348 moratorium texts have been read line by line against a 44-clause taxonomy; that sample predates 28 April 2026 and covers 211 jurisdictions, so it describes what moratoria contain rather than all 1328 of them. An earlier coordinate-check sample covered 89 verifications with no confirmed errors; that sample is not a fresh check of every mapped record. Where we could not confirm something, the row says so.
