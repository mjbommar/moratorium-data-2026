                                     RESOLUTION #260601

                       TYRONE TOWNSHIP, LIVINGSTON COUNTY
                         CIVIL INFRACTION ORDINANCE #39c

Due to the document length, a summary of the Civil Infractions Ordinance No. 39C is provided
below. See the original resolution or ordinance for the complete language.

This ordinance establishes the procedures for enforcing Tyrone Township Ordinances from the
investigation of alleged ordinance violations, designating the officials with authority to prepare a
civil infraction citation, setting forth the required contents of a civil infraction citation, to the
issuance and service of a civil infraction citation, and the penalty for a violation.

Section 1. Title. Identifies the ordinance as Tyrone Township Civil Infractions Ordinance 39C.

Section 2. Ordinance Repeal. The ordinance repeals Tyrone Township Ordinance 39A, 39B and
22.

Section 3. Definitions. Provides definitions of terms used in the ordinance.


                             TYRONE TOWNSHIP
                          REGULAR BOARD MEETING
                    APPROVED MINUTES – JUNE 16, 2026 – PAGE 3

Section 4. Ordinance Enforcement Officer. Authorizes the Board of Trustees to appoint or
remove an Ordinance Enforcement Officer and sets forth the Ordinance Enforcement Officer’s
duties.

Section 5. Municipal Civil Infraction Action: Commencement. Directs when the Supervisor or
Ordinance Enforcement Officer may issue a municipal civil infraction citation.

Section 6. Municipal Civil Infraction Citations: Contents. Sets forth the required information to
be included in a citation.

Section 7. Service of Municipal Civil Infraction Citations. Provides the method for serving a
municipal civil infraction citation on the alleged ordinance violator.

Section 8. Fines And Sanctions for Violations and Infractions. Authorizes fines from $100.00 to
$500.00 for ordinance violations.

Section 9. Availability of Other Enforcement Options.       Permits Tyrone Township to seek
enforcement of its ordinances in any other lawful manner.

Section 10. Records and Accounting. Requires maintenance of records and accounting of fines
received by the Township from the Court.

Section 11. Severability. Provides that any portion of the ordinance found to be unlawful or
unconstitutional may be removed rather than invalidating the entire ordinance.

43.53 Payment for construction and maintenance of fence; compensation for use of fence
by adjoining property owner; constructing fence in lieu of compensation.

Sec: 3.

(1) The owner of real property who constructs a fence shall pay for the construction and
maintenance of that fence.

(2) If an adjoining property owner or a tenant using the property of the adjoining property
owner uses or begins to use the fence for purposes of restraining or containing animals the
adjoining property owner or tenant shall compensate the owner of the real property who
constructed the fence for the adjoining property owner's proportionate share of the current
value of the fence as determined by the parties and based upon the adjoining property owner's
use of the fence. In the alternative, the adjoining property owner may construct his own fence.

                                CORRECTING THE RECORD



There has been much disinformation published in our community about Tyrone Township
board and commission members having nondisclosure agreements, commonly referred to
as NDA, with data center ﬁrms or prospects that may seek to establish a presence in Tyrone
Township.



It is simply untrue. No Tyrone Township board or commission member has such an NDA. A
recent Freedom of Information Act request asked Tyrone Township to produce any such
NDA’s and the response to that request was that none exist. If such public documents
existed, the NDA would not prevent the disclosure of that NDA. What you are hearing and
reading about these NDA’s and board or commission members is just fake news.



Recently, a video of a short portion of the June 16, 2026 Tyrone Township Board of Trustees
meeting has been circulated. That video reveals a member of the public speaking at the call
to the public. Tyrone Township has clearly indicated in the past what the call to the public is
and what it is not. The call to the public is a time set aside during the board meeting for
anyone to voice their opinions. The call to the public is not a time for debate between a
member of the public and any member of the board. The call to the public is not a time for a
question and answer session. From a review of that posted video one can see a member of
the public directing questions to each board member. The member of the public complained
when several of the board members did not respond to the question. That same member of
the public routinely complains if a board member says anything during that persons
opportunity to speak at the call to the public.
