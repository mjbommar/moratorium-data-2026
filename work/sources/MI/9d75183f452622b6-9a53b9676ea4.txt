RESOLUTION URGING THE MAYOR TO IMPLEMENT

A TWO YEAR MORATORIUMON ON DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT

BY COUNCIL MEMBER SCOTT BENSON

WHEREAS, It is incumbent upon those elected to serve the citizens of the City of Detroit to expend

maximum efforts to protect the public peace, health and safety of all its constituents; and

WHEREAS, Recent events surrounding the development of data centers in Michigan and across the

United States has produced legitimate concerns regarding their unique resource-intensive

utilization of energy and natural resources combined with the exasperation of

environmental risk; and

WHEREAS, Quest Technology Management1 defines the data centers as:

[a] physical facility that houses computing infrastructure, including servers,

storage devices, networking equipment, and other systems necessary for

managing and storing data. It is the central hub for processing, transmitting,

and safeguarding critical information for businesses, governments, and

other organizations. Data centers are at the heart of digital operations,

helping companies enable cloud services, run e-commerce platforms, or

support financial systems.

WHEREAS, Data centers come in a variety of types (Enterprise Data Centers, Co-Location Data

Centers, Cloud Data Centers, Edge Data Centers, and Hyperscale Data Centers) and in

addition to different types, data centers also have different classifications based on their

design, reliability, and redundancy capabilities. The Tier Classification System, developed

by the Uptime Institute2, is the most widely used framework for assessing a data center’s

capabilities and performance. The classifications are Tier I- Basic Capacity; Tier II

Redundant Capacity Components; Tier III- Concurrently Maintainable; and Tier IV-V-Fault

Tolerant; and

WHEREAS, According to a story in US Today3, “The majority of the concerns have been due to

environmental issues or surging utility costs. Artificial intelligence data centers require

more resources and power than a typical data center… In 2025, 25 data center projects

were canceled in the United States, compared to eight in 2024 and 2023 combined. The

advocacy against data centers has seen 14 states issue moratoriums (a temporary ban)

within respective local communities, according to Tech Policy Press.”; and

WHEREAS, The Detroit News reports “There are 2,717 operational data centers in the U.S. and 35 in

Michigan, according to an analysis by João Ferreira, a regional economist for the

University of Virginia's Welden Cooper Center for Public Service… Great Lakes states are

home to approximately 20% of the nation's data centers. Michigan has the least number of

data centers in the region, Ferreira said. Illinois and Ohio have the most data centers in the

region and are attracting the most data center development”4; and
