e. DAFD Interlocal Proposed Changes

Supervisor Scott presented proposed revisions to the Dexter Area Fire Department (DAFD) Interlocal

Agreement, noting this was a first presentation to the Township Board ahead of upcoming discussion with

partner municipalities. Several administrative language updates were noted throughout the document,

including updating "Village of Dexter" to "City of Dexter" and changing "firemen" to "firefighters."

Two substantive governance changes were proposed. First, the at-large board member term would be

reduced from six years to three years, and the position would be restricted from being held by any elected

trustee of the three member municipalities, to better ensure the neutrality that the at-large role is intended

to provide.

The most significant proposed change involves the cost allocation methodology. Currently, 100% of the

annual budget is allocated based on each municipality's share of total fire runs. Under the current formula

for the FY26 budget of $4.3 million, the City of Dexter pays 36.67% ($1,589,000), Dexter Township pays

34.5% ($1,495,000), and Webster Township pays 28.82% ($1,249,000).

Supervisor Scott argued that run-based allocation is a poor policy metric for several reasons: over 80% of

department activity in the past year constituted readiness time—firefighters on station awaiting calls—

which is shared equally across all municipalities. Run counts are also volatile year-to-year, creating large

and unpredictable budget swings. Additionally, a run-based system disadvantages municipalities that

generate repeat calls through group homes or high-traffic commercial areas despite having no control over

those call volumes. The system also creates inequity relative to population and property values: Webster

Township has the highest population and SEV of the three municipalities yet pays the least.

The proposal would allocate costs as follows: 75% based on readiness (distributed equally), 12.5% based

on population share, and 12.5% based on run share. Under this formula applied to the FY26 budget, the

City of Dexter would pay 32.74% (a reduction of $170,000), Dexter Township would pay 34.02% (a

reduction of $20,000), and Webster Township would pay 33.24% (an increase of $191,000).

Trustee Sikkenga added important context, noting that the City of Dexter has indicated the current model

is financially unsustainable and has discussed the possibility of withdrawing from DAFD—an outcome

that would increase costs substantially for the remaining municipalities and conflict with the DAFD

strategic plan's goal of expanding membership. She also noted the inequity to City of Dexter residents,

whose smaller population bears a disproportionate cost burden, and that repeat calls from group homes—

which the Township actively welcomes—further distort the run-based metric.

The Board also discussed building lease arrangements: currently, the three municipalities charge the

Department different rental amounts and have differing arrangements for maintenance expenses. The

proposal would standardize leases across all three at a nominal one-dollar amount to create consistency.

Supervisor Scott acknowledged that this proposal has not yet been presented to the other member

municipalities and emphasized that it is not final—h—he remains open to alternative models—b—but stressed

the urgency of moving away from the current methodology. The proposed changes will be brought to the

next DAFD Interlocal meeting.

DEXTER TOWNSHIP ENVIRONMENTAL STEWARDSHIP COMMITTEE CHARGE

WHEREAS, the Dexter Township Board of Trustees adopted the 2024 Master Plan, which

establishes the "Protection of our Natural Resources" and "Creating a Sustainable Community"

as core cornerstones for the township’s future;

WHEREAS, the 2025-2028 Dexter Township Strategic Plan identifies "Environmental

Stewardship" as a primary pillar, with a mandate to actively preserve and protect natural

resources, waterways, and open spaces through responsible policies and community education;

WHEREAS, the Township Board seeks to move from high-level strategic goals to a

"multifaceted, feasible environmental stewardship action plan" that provides a clear

implementation schedule for these objectives;

1. Committee Purpose and Charge:

The Committee is established as an advisory body to the Township Board. Its primary charge is

to:

a. Develop a Roadmap and Schedule: Create a comprehensive implementation roadmap

for the "Environmental Stewardship" actions identified in the 2025-2028 Strategic Plan.

b. Master Plan Alignment:  Ensure all proposed actions and schedules directly support the

long-range goals and ecological priorities outlined in the 2024 Master Plan.

c. Policy Recommendations:  Research and draft recommendations for zoning and

ordinance options related to the protection of waterways, shorelines, and forests,

including potential restrictions on fertilizers and wake boats.

d. Education and Outreach:  Design a public education strategy to increase resident

engagement with environmental stewardship practices.

e. Committee Coordination:  Provide recommendations on the structure and timing for

establishing a permanent Waterways Committee or other specialized subcommittees as

needed.

2. Membership and Terms:

a. Composition:  The committee shall consist of up to seven (7) residents of the Township

including one (1) liaison appointed from the Township Board, and one (1) ex officio staff

member

.

16 of 162

b. Selection:  All members shall be appointed by a majority vote of the Township Board.

c. Term of Office: Members shall serve a term of two years, with initial appointments

staggered to ensure continuity.

d. Officers: The committee shall elect a Chair, Vice Chair, and Secretary from its members.

3. Attendance, Removals, and Vacancies:

a. Attendance:  Members are expected to attend all regularly scheduled meetings. If any

member misses three (3) consecutive meetings without informing the Chair in advance,

the Chair shall notify the Township Supervisor and the Board of Trustees.

b. Removal:  Any member of the Committee may be removed from office by a majority

vote of the Board of Trustees at any time, with or without cause.

c. Vacancies:  In the event of a vacancy resulting from resignation, removal, or change in

residency, the Board of Trustees shall appoint a successor to serve the remainder of the

unexpired term.

d. Resignations:  Members wishing to resign shall submit a written notice to the Township

Supervisor and the Committee Chair.

4. Deliverables and Timeline:

The Committee shall provide the following to the Board of Trustees:

1. Phase 1 Roadmap:  A draft schedule of tasks, responsible parties, and measurable goals

for the 2026–2028 period, to be delivered within six months of the committee’s first

meeting.

2. Annual Work Plan:  A defining document of tasks, timelines, and resource requirements

submitted annually for Board approval.

3. Status Reports:  Quarterly updates on progress toward Strategic Plan environmental

milestones.

5. Operational Rules:

a. Open Meetings Act: The Committee shall operate in full compliance with the State of

Michigan Open Meetings Act (OMA). All meetings must be posted and open to the

public.

b. Authority:  The Committee is advisory only and does not have the authority to bind the

Township to contracts or expend funds without prior Board approval.

c. Meetings: The Committee shall meet at least quarterly at the Township Hall.

6. Subcommittee Structure:

17 of 162

In order to  carry out its charge, the Board of Trustees may form subcommittees as

necessary. Current subcommittees include the Decarbonization and Resilience

Subcommittee, Waste and Recycling Subcommittee, and Land Use and Natural

Resources Subcommittee. Subcommittee  members shall be appointed by the Supervisor

and approved by a majority vote of the Board of Trustees. Subcommittees are advisory

only and their meetings are not intended to be subject to the OMA, unless otherwise

required by state law.

Township Board of Trustees Rules

Adopted September 19, 2024

1. AUTHORITY

1.1. Authority. These rules are adopted by the board of a general law w township pursuant to the provisions

of the Revised Statutes of 1846  (Chapter 41 of the Michigan Compiled Laws), as amended.

2. MEETINGS

2.1. Regular schedule. The Township Board shall meet on the third Tuesday of each month in regular

session unless otherwise specified by the Board. Annually, the Township Board shall adopt by resolution

a schedule of the regular meetings of the Township Board in accordance with the foregoing.

2.2. Special meetings. The Township Board shall meet in special session at the call of the Clerk upon the

written request of the Supervisor or two members of the Township Board. Notice of special meetings shall

be given to the public in accordance with Rule 3.3 and to each Township Board member at least 24 hours

in advance of the special meeting. Such notice shall be served personally and/or emailed with phone

notification by the Township Clerk or their designee. The notice shall also contain the time, place, and

purpose of the meeting.

2.3. Place of meeting. Regularly scheduled meetings shall be held in the Township Hall. Whenever the

regular meeting place of the Township Board appears to be inadequate to accommodate all of the public

expected to attend, the Supervisor and Clerk may change the meeting to a larger facility located in the

Township. A notice of such change shall be posted on the Township website, and prominently posted on

the door or bulletin board of the regular meeting place. The Clerk shall also give notice of such change in

the place of meeting in a newspaper if time permits.

2.4. Time of meetings. Regularly scheduled meetings shall begin at 6:00pm unless the Board shall by

majority vote of members present at a meeting at which a quorum is present set a different starting time.

The Township Board shall not begin considering any matter on the agenda not yet under consideration by

the hour of 9:00 P.M. except by majority consent of the members present. Matters on the agenda and not

yet acted upon at the time of adjournment will be placed on the agenda of the next regular meeting or special

meeting if one is called.

2.5. Change in schedule. Changes in the regular schedule shall not be made except upon the approval of a

majority of the Board members present in a meeting at which quorum is present. In the event the Board

meets, and a quorum is not present, the Board, upon the action of a majority of those present, may adjourn

a regular or special meeting to another day provided that proper notice to members and public is given as

provided in Rules 3.2 and 3.3.

2.6. Virtual meetings. Meetings may be made available for public attendance via videoconference. If the

meeting is made available for virtual public attendance, then the public may participate remotely during a

public comment period or public hearing.

3. NOTICE OF MEETINGS

3.1. Public notice of meetings. The Township Clerk shall be responsible for providing the proper notice

for all meetings of the Township Board. Such notification shall include but not necessarily be limited to the

following:

3.2. R Regular meetings. The Clerk shall post or publish a notice within 10 days after the first meeting of the

Township Board in each calendar year, indicating the dates, times, and places of the Board’s regular

meeting schedule. The Clerk shall also post the schedule and schedule changes on the Township webpage.

3.3. Schedule change. Whenever the Board shall change its regular schedule of meetings, the Clerk shall

post or publish a notice of the change within 3 days following the meeting in which the change was made

but not less than eighteen (18) hours before the rescheduled meeting time.

3.4. Notice of special/changed meetings, emergency meetings. If the Board reschedules a meeting under the

provisions of Rule 2.5 or calls a special meeting under Rule 2.2, the Clerk shall post a notice of such change

immediately and no meeting, except emergency meetings as provided herein, shall be held until the notice has

been posted for at least 18 hours. An emergency meeting shall be held only upon the consent of 2/3 of the

Board members. Nothing herein shall bar an emergency meeting if a delay would threaten severe and imminent

danger to the health, safety, and w welfare of the public.

3.5. Recessed meetings. A meeting that is recessed for more than 36 hours shall be reconvened only after

public notice as provided in Rule 3.2.

4. QUORUM; ATTENDANCE

4.1. Quorum. F Four members shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business at all meetings of the

Township Board.

4.2. Attendance. The Township Board, by a vote of at least 2 members, may compel the attendance of

Board members and other officers of the Township at a regular or special meeting and enforce orderly

conduct in a meeting.

5. MEETING AGENDA

5.1. Regular meeting agenda. The Township Supervisor shall prepare the agenda of business for all

regularly scheduled Township Board meetings. Any other Board member or representative of Township

committees, boards or commissions desiring to place a matter on the agenda shall notify the Supervisor of

such item ten days prior to the meeting.  Documents for inclusion in the packet shall be provided eight

days prior to the meeting. Only in unusual situations, such as where a deadline must be met, health, safety

or welfare are at risk, or risks of financial adverse consequences, may matters be added on with the

support of at least two (2) Board Members less than ten days before the meeting.

5.2. Special meeting agenda. Whenever the Board shall be called into a special meeting, the matters to be

considered shall be stated in the notice of the meeting. However, if all of the members of the Board are

present at a special meeting, then business that might lawfully come before a regular meeting of the Board

may be transacted at the special meeting.

5.3. Distribution of agenda and materials. Upon completion of the agenda the Supervisor shall

immediately distribute to Board members copies of the agenda together with copies of reports, explanations,

etc., that relate to the business matters coming to the Board. The Supervisor may distribute such materials

by email, mail or by personal delivery.

6. CONDUCT OF MEETINGS

6.1. Chairperson. T The Township Supervisor shall moderate and chair all meetings of the Township Board.

In the absence of the Supervisor the Board shall appoint one of its members temporary chair by voice vote,

who shall assume the duties of the chair for that meeting.

6.2. Discussion. B Board members wishing to speak shall first obtain the approval of the chair and each person

who speaks shall address the chair. Other persons at the meeting shall not speak unless recognized by the

chair.

6.3. Disorderly conduct at meetings. The Supervisor may call to order any person who is being disorderly in

the following ways: speaking or otherwise disrupting the proceedings, failing to be germane, speaking longer

than the allotted time, or speaking vulgarities. The person shall then be seated until the Supervisor determines

whether the person is in order. If the person shall then be called out of order, he or she shall not be permitted to

continue to speak at the same meeting except on special permission by a majority of the Township Board. If

the person shall continue to be disorderly and disrupt the meeting, the Supervisor may request a sheriff’s

deputy to remove the person from the meeting. No person shall be removed from a public meeting except

for an actual breach of the peace committed at the meeting from which such person is removed.

7. RECORD OF MEETINGS

7.1. Clerk responsibility. The Township Clerk shall be responsible for maintaining the official record and

minutes of each meeting of the Board. In the absence of the Clerk, the Board shall appoint one of its

members as temporary Clerk for that meeting. The minutes of each meeting shall show the date, time, place,

members present, members absent, any decisions made at a meeting held open to the public and the

Election-Related Expenditure Exception for Legal Services

Purpose

To ensure the timely protection of Dexter Township election workers and Township staff during the

administration of elections, the Township Board authorizes a limited exception to its General Expenditure

Policy.

Policy

Notwithstanding the Township's General Expenditure Policy, which authorizes the Executive Committee to

approve expenditures of up to $5,000, the Executive Committee is authorized to approve expenditures of up to

$50,000 for election-related legal services and associated legal expenses when necessary to protect Township

election workers, election officials, and Township staff in connection with the conduct or administration of an

election.

This authority:

• Applies only to legal services and legal expenses directly related to the administration of elections or

legal actions arising from the administration of elections.

• May be exercised only when prompt action is necessary to protect Township personnel or ensure the

lawful administration of elections.

• Constitutes a limited exception to the Township's General Expenditure Policy and does not modify that

policy for any other purpose or circumstance.

• Applies only during the conduct of elections, including reasonable pre-election preparation, Election

Day activities, post-election canvassing, recounts, and election-related litigation.

Any expenditures authorized under this exception shall be reported to the Township Board at its next regular

meeting and documented in accordance with the Township's financial policies.

This exception starts upon passage by the board and shall remain in effect unless amended or rescinded by

action of the Township Board.

Request for Bid – Rain Garden and Prairie Projoject

Introduction:

Dexter Township is soliciting proposals from qualified contractors to furnish all labor, materials,

equipment, supervision, and services necessary to install and establish a native rain garden and

prairie project at Dexter Township Hall in accordance with the requirements outlined in this

Request for Proposal (RFP).

This project supports Dexter Township’s Strategic Plan goal of Environmental Stewardship, which

seeks to actively preserve and protect the Township’s natural resources through responsible

policies, sustainable practices, and community education. The Township seeks to enhance native

habitat, support pollinator populations, increase biodiversity, and demonstrate sustainable land

management practices through the installation and establishment of native landscaping features

at  Dexter Township Hall. The project is located at the Township Hall (6880 Dexter-Pinckney Rd.,

Dexter, MI 48130) and is intended to serve as a visible demonstration of Dexter Township’s

commitment to environmental stewardship while contributing to the long-term ecological health

and resiliency of Township-owned lands.

Dexter Township is not obligated by issuing this Request for Proposal to undertake this project.

In accordance with Dexter Township Resolution No. 25-694, the Township may apply preference

criteria during proposal evaluation to support local businesses, fair labor standards,

and environmentally sustainable practices. Preference criteria will be sent via email.

Project Description:

The Project site  is located at: 6880  Dexter-Pinckney Rd, Dexter MI 48130

The project consists of:

• A 600-square-foot native rain garden located south of the Township Hall entrance.

• Approximately 5,000 square feet of native prairie plantings located south of the Township Hall

building.

The project is intended to enhance native habitat, support pollinator populations, increase

biodiversity, and demonstrate sustainable land management practices consistent with Dexter

Township’s Environmental Stewardship goals.

SECTION 02 — HOW DISPATCH FUNDING WORKS

Michigan’s E911 Legal Framework

State Law Framework

Michigan’s Emergency 9-1-1 Service Enabling Act governs emergency communication

districts, PSAP operations, and surcharge collection.

Service suppliers remit the surcharge directly to the county quarterly.

A county can collect up to $0.42/month without bringing it forward to voters. Washtenaw’s

$0.43 rate is grandfathered per MPSC U-15489.

Voters may approve a surcharge increase up to the state cap of $3.00/month. This is a per

line monthly fee — not a millage — and the Board of Commissioners would then set the final

rate within the state-approved range if approved. The recommended estimated rates could

follow a staggered model; you can only raise the rate based on the projected budget, as the

local 911 surcharge can only be used for E911 dispatch services.

KEY FACTS SUMMARY

E911 Surcharge:

Key Facts for the Board and Community.

The Surcharge Is Not a Millage

The E911 surcharge is a per-line monthly fee on phone, wireless, and VoIP connections —

not a property tax. It does not appear on your tax bill. Michigan law (MCL 484.1401 et

seq.) authorizes counties to collect up to $3.00/month per line.

Current Operations & Costs

Metro Dispatch and neighboring PSAP's handled 241,966 emergency calls in 2025. The

Metro Dispatch operating budget was $6,717,489. The current surcharge ($0.43/mo)

generates ~$1.6M/year. The balance is funded through the county general fund and

municipal contracts.

an example. The Board sets the actual rate at or below that ceiling.

If Approved or Not Approved

If voters approve, the BOC sets a final rate, and additional revenue supports only dispatch

operations as described. If voters do not approve: the current rate of $0.43/month

remains, and Metro Dispatch and other PSAPs continue at current funding levels.

FROM: Megan Masson-Minock AICPPrincipal and Brady Heath, Community Planner

DATE:  July 7, 2026

RE:  Proposed Bed and Breakfast Zoning Ordinance Amendment

Beginning in December 2025, the Planning Commission has been working with the Director of Planning

and Zoning and Carlisle/Wortman Associates to update the bed and breakfast standards in Section 17.07

of the Zoning Ordinance. Following the Township's discussion of short-term rentals, the standards were

reviewed and revised to clarify the regulations and strengthen the operational requirements for bed and

breakfast establishments. The following summarizes the proposed amendments.

• Bed and Breakfast Definition: Revises the definition to clarify that a bed and breakfast is an

owner-occupied single-family residence that serves as the owner's principal residence and

allows guest stays of up to 30 consecutive days.

• Short-Term Rental Definition: Adds a definition for Short-Term Rental and distinguishes short

term rentals from bed and breakfasts and other lodging or care facilities.

• Owner Occupancy Standard: Clarifies that the property owner must remain on-site overnight

when guests are present and may only be absent for limited periods during the day.

• Events: Clarifies that receptions, parties, and similar events are limited to registered overnight

guests and are not intended to operate as event venues.

• Guest Occupancy: Revises the maximum guest stay to allow rentals for up to 30 consecutive

days, consistent with the updated definition.

• Application Requirements: Expands the special land use application requirements by identifying

the materials necessary to demonstrate compliance with the ordinance.

• Inspection Requirements: Requires inspections by the Chelsea Area Construction Agency and

the Dexter Area Fire Department prior to Planning Commission review and every three (3) years

thereafter.

• Sanitary Sewer and Septic Systems: Requires documentation demonstrating that the sanitary

sewer or septic system has adequate capacity to accommodate the proposed bed and breakfast.

A resolution to amend the definition of Bed and Breakfast; define Short-Term Rentals in Article 2;

amend Section 17.04(T) to clarify and modernize rules for bed and breakfasts.

IT IS HEREBY ORDAINED BY THE PEOPLE OF THE TOWNSHIP OF DEXTER:

SECTION 1. MODIFY SECTION 2.02 TO DEFINE SHORT-TERM RENTALS.

Bed and breakfast: A residential facility that is owner-occupied in which overnight

accommodations are provided or offered for compensation, including the provision of bathing

and lavatory facilities and a breakfast meal.

Bed and Breakfast: A single-family residential facility with a rental period of up to 30 consecutive

days which is the owner(s) principal place of residence and occupied by said owner(s) during

the rental period.

Short-Term Rental: A residential facility with a rental period of up to 30 consecutive days. The

lawful rental of the following shall not be considered Short-Term Rentals: bed and breakfast

establishments, motels, campgrounds, group homes such as nursing homes and adult-foster

care homes, substance-abuse rehabilitation clinics, and other similar healthcare related facilities.

SECTION 2. MODIFY REGULATIONS IN SECTION 17.04(K)

(K) Bed and Breakfasts: Bed and breakfasts shall meet the additional standards outlined below.

1) Parking: Parking shall be arranged so as not to pose negative impacts on adjacent

properties. The parking lot shall be screened in accordance with Section 20.04. There

shall be 1 space per guest room; plus 1 space per non-resident employee in addition to

the required parking for single-family dwelling. Parking layout and design standards shall

meet the requirements in Section 18.04.

2) Appearance: The exterior appearance of the structure and lot shall not be altered from its

single-family character, except for a permitted sign, as outlined in Article 19 Signs.

3) Owner-Occupied: The bed and breakfast facility shall be a single-family dwelling which is

operated and occupied by the owner of the dwelling. The property owners must be on

site when guests are present overnight from 10 pm to 7am but can be off-site for a period

of less than four (4) hours during the day when guests are present.

4) Meals: Meals may be served to overnight guests only. Meals shall not be served to the

public at large.

5) Retail: Retail sales are not permitted beyond those activities serving overnight patrons.

Data Explanation

Reporting Period: June

CCW REPORT:

This report lists all incidents verified as carrying a concealed weapon violation that occurred within

the area during the reporting period.

OUT OF AREA REPORT:

This report details the amount of time (in minutes) that deputies assigned to the contract area

(patrol area) spend outside of that area, along with the reasons for being out of area. If the contract

area is part of a collaboration, it is not possible to separate the data for each individual area within

the collaboration in this report.

INTO AREA REPORT:

This report details the amount of time (in minutes) that deputies not assigned to the contract area

spend in that area, along with the reasons for their presence. If the contract area is part of a

collaboration, this report separates the data by each area within the collaboration.

VIOLATIONS:

This report lists all violations that occurred during the reporting period. A single citation may include

multiple violations. The “sum” value represents the number of citations, not the total number of

violations.

TRAFFIC STOPS:

This report is generated from deputies’ activity logs, which document each traffic stop and the

associated reason. The numbers in this report may differ from those on the “Police Service Data

Report” page, as that report reflects only verified offenses.
