Caledonia Township Utility Committee Meeting                                        January 12, 2026


Present: Bryan Harrison, Alison Nugent, Tim Bradshaw, Dick Robertson
Other Attendees: Todd Boerman, Ryan Moseley, Kathy VanKalker
Absent: None
Guests: Jordan Baker, Mary-Catherine Baker, Daughter Baker


Bryan Harrison called the meeting to order at 8:00am

1.) Smoking Joe Sewer Phase II 84 – Todd Boerman notified the committee that the adjusted bid amounts
    have been collected. Those amounts came in higher than expected. Todd then reviewed the “Tabulation
    of Costs” with the committee, noting the recent bids. Tim Bradshaw inquired concerning how many
    parties need to agree for the voluntary assessment to proceed, and Todd replied that two of the four
    owners need to agree. He went on to add that Mr. Mars and Mr. Arnold will be offered a deferment
    based on their ages which will be due upon development or the sale of the parcel.

Todd left the meeting at 8:10am to retrieve the Baker family.

2.) Minutes from December 8, 2025; Bryan – Motion to Approve, Tim – Support. Approved.

    Jordan Baker and his family entered the room and were introduced to each of the Utility Committee
    members and attendees. Todd provided a brief history of the project which originated with Joe Arnold
    (Country Turf/Smoking Joe), and the intention that the parcel would be served by sanitary sewer and
    financed by Joe. A Performance Bond is in place to make sure he follows through. Todd also informed
    everyone that Phase I of the project brings the sewer down the service road next to Wendy’s in the spring
    of 2026, with Phase II of the project to be constructed with the reconstruction of M37 in 2027. Todd
    reminded everyone that Joe’s previous business, Country Turf was built in 1996, and the sewer was to
    be built within five years, at the cost of the owner’s – Joe and Sue Arnold. In 2018, the use of the building
    was modified, and the terms of agreement were yet to be met so the agreement was modified to note
    that the sewer would be built within 3 years. Jordan Baker inquired about the culvert under the road that
    it now had to be switched to route to the east side of M37, greatly increasing the cost of the project?
    Todd stated that due to the depth, construction and placement of the culvert, that was no longer feasible.
    In 2019, a Performance Bond was established by Joe Arnold. The Township can call the bond and require
    the insurance agency to pay in full, which would then seek the funds from Joe Arnold. At this time, Jordan
    took a moment to share his story, including his desire to be a respectable business owner and community
    member as well as a successful businessman and family man. Bryan reiterated the original scope of the
    project and commitments as well as the impact of calling the bond, noting that the burden and the
    benefits lie therein. Bryan then asked Jordan to clarify his request for consideration by the committee.
    Jordan responded that he has a perfectly operational septic system that he would prefer to continue to
    utilize, he would also prefer that the cost of the project is divided equally between all four parcels as well
    as to defer the portion of the cost for 8285 Broadmoor Ave SE. The committee discussed the future of
    Jordan’s parcels as well as his vision for the properties and the potential need to go before the Planning
    Commission.


The Baker family left the meeting at 8:57am.

    The committee discussed the differences between voluntary and involuntary assessments, the current
    septic system on the Comodo Motors parcel, Jordan’s desire that the Township NOT call the bond, as
    well as his preference to keep it as simple and easy as possible by not bringing Joe Arnold into the
    equation at all.
    Bryan made a motion to recommend to the Board of Trustees to allocate the cost of the extension as
    ”Requested” on the “Tabulation of Costs” sheet. No support. Motion Failed.
    Tim made a motion to recommend to the Board of Trustees to allocate the cost of the extension as
    “Proposed” on the “Tabulation of Costs”, also to call the bond within 30 days of the next board meeting
    if Jordan is unable to source the funding on his own, and the deferral offered to the remaining parcels as
    presented, with a length of term as determined by the Township. Dick – Support. Approved.

    ACTION ITEM – Todd will contact Mark Nettleton for further clarification concerning potential legal
    implications and whether the Township’s insurance would cover any bonding issues.

3.) Water Transition Update – Todd noted that it was a nice day for the transition! The weather is the primary
    facet to the delay in the water source transition at this point. Just waiting for a couple warm days (nearly
    40 degrees), to complete the transition.

4.) Patterson Place Update – Todd briefly informed the committee that a design is already in place as a
    condition of Phase I. A further discussion with Mark Pung concerning refreshing the cost will be
    necessary.

Bryan left the meeting at 9:27am.

Alison shared with the committee the good news that the federal grant for the Kraft Trunkline has been
approved by the House Committee.

Todd also briefly touched on the right-of-way for the J&H Oil project is incorrect and they have yet to
secure a Civil Engineer. He added that there is the possibility of reorienting the driveway and the sewer.
The committee quickly discussed the potential of the parcel, concluding that it could also include a sit-down
restaurant.




Meeting adjourned at 9:30am.

Recording Secretary: Kathy VanKalker

Zoning Administrator Concerns:
   • Our Zoning Ordinance discusses agri-tourism and agri-business.
          o According to our Zoning Ordinance (ZO), agri-tourism allows for a farm market -
             to educate the public and to generate additional farm income, through the sale of
             products, as well as offering additional activities such as hayrides, corn maze,
             etc. This does not require a Special Land Use Permit.
          o An agri-business is viewed as a bigger endeavor and requires a Special Land Use
             (SLU) Permit. The ZO agri-business definition notes that the farm enterprise has
             a retail character, as displayed by a variety of characteristics. (See ZO Section
             2.2.) One requirement for an agri-business that sells alcohol (via cider mills,
             distilleries, breweries, or wineries selling product "in a tasting room") is to
             observe the 50 percent rule. Also, there is a maximum seating capacity of 100 for
             a restaurant or café (in this case a tavern) within the agri-business. The addition
             of a tavern (selling alcohol) puts Deep Roots into the agri-business category,
             which requires a SLU Permit.
   • In the files for the pole barn in 2023, the Building Official had noted "Occupant load is
      67 people as presented."
          o If Deep Roots required some interior remodeling, or an addition, in order to
             incorporate a "tavern", they’d need a building permit, and the occupancy might
             change.
          o Agri-business allows a MAXIMUM seating capacity of 100. Their liquor license
             application sounds like the occupancy may be more than 100, with the outdoor
             area described as "expansive". My concern with this scenario is that their
             occupancy might actually be larger-than-allowed for an agri-business. They are
             not zoned commercial. They would have to have limit their seating to stay within
             the Agri-business limits, as well as address all the concerns that the Building
             Official mentions.

                                                            MEMORANDUM
                                                                   Date: 02/02/2026




TO:         Caledonia Township Board of Trustees
FROM:       Alison Nugent, Township Manager
RE:         Amendments to Sewer and Water Ordinances


Background:

The Caledonia Township Utility Committee has requested amendments to the
Township Ordinances related to utilities administration and committee
composition.

First, the Committee recommends revising the ordinances to designate the
Township Manager, or another person designated by the Township, as the “Utility
Administrator.” This change is intended to provide greater administrative
continuity and operational efficiency.

Second, the Committee recommends modifying the composition of the Utility
Committee. Under the current ordinance, the Committee membership is designated
as the Township Clerk and Township Treasurer. Due to timing considerations and
the specialized nature of utility oversight, the Utility Committee determined it
would be more effective for committee members to be selected based on relevant
expertise, regardless of their statutory position. As a result, the proposed
amendment would establish the Utility Committee as consisting of the Township
Supervisor and two members of the Township Board.

Attached are draft Word versions of the proposed ordinance amendments for the
Board’s consideration. The Board must approve and sign ordinances for both
sewer and water use and rate regulations. If approved, the ordinances will take
effect upon publication or publication of a summary.


                                                           MEMORANDUM
                                                                 Date: 02/02/2026

Recommended Motion:

Approve amendments to the Sewer and Water Ordinances to:

  •   Designate the Township Manager or another designated individual as the
      “Utility Administrator”; and
  •   Change Utility Committee membership from statutory officials to the
      Township Supervisor and two members of the Township Board.
