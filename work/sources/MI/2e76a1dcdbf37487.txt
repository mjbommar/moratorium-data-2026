ONEIDA CHARTER TOWNSHIP
EATON COUNTY, MICHIGAN
RESOLUTION NO. 2026-07-14

RESOLUTION EXTENDING DATA CENTER MORATORIUM
At a regular meeting of the Township Board of Oneida Charter Township, Eaton County,
Michigan, held at 11041 Oneida Road, Grand Ledge, Michigan 48837 on the 14"" day of July 2026,

at 7 p.m.,

PRESENT: Supervisor Rick Jones, Treasurer Melissa Goschka, Clerk Michaela Mosher, Trustee
Thomas Campbell, Trustee Jeri Haddix. Trustee Madelyne Lawry. Trustee Janet Schultz

ABSENT: None

The following Ordinance was offered by Township Board Member Melissa Goschka and
supported by Township Board Member Rick Jones.

WHEREAS, the Township enacted a moratorium on Data Centers that became effective on
January 24, 2026, for a period of six (6) months (the “Ordinance”) which, in summary, enacted a
temporary moratorium on the permitting, consideration, approval, location, construction, and/or
installation of any Data Center facilities in the Township. Such a temporary moratorium was to
allow the Township time to draft appropriate ordinances and regulations governing Data Center
facilities; and

WHEREAS, during that time the Township Planning Commission has been diligently working
on crafting a Data Center ordinance by studying the proper and appropriate regulations for a Data
Center within the Township; and

WHEREAS, the Township Board finds that data center facilities can present significant and site-
specific impacts requiring careful study and regulation, including without limitation: (a)
substantial electrical demand and the associated need for new or expanded transmission
infrastructure and substations; (b) significant water consumption for cooling and the corresponding
effect on local groundwater and municipal water supplies; (c) continuous noise generation from
cooling systems, transformers, and emergency backup generators operating on a twenty-four (24)
hour basis; (d) air emissions and fuel storage associated with backup diesel generators; (e) heat
dissipation and microclimate effects; (f) compatibility with the Township’s existing land uses,
master plan, and rural and agricultural character; (g) appropriate setbacks, screening, height limits,
lighting standards, and buffering for adjacent residential and agricultural properties; (h)
construction-period impacts including traffic, dust, and hours of operation; (i) site security, fire
suppression, and emergency response capacity, including risks associated with on-site battery
energy storage systems; and (j) decommissioning, financial assurance, and end-of-use site
restoration; and


WHEREAS, since enactment of the Ordinance, the Township Planning Commission has
commenced its review of these issues, including reviewing data center ordinances and best
practices adopted by other Michigan and out-of-state municipalities, and additional time is
required for the Planning Commission to complete its review, conduct public input, consult with
subject-matter experts and legal counsel, evaluate consistency with the Township’s master plan,
and prepare draft amendments to the Township Zoning Ordinance for the Board’s consideration;
and

WHEREAS, the Township Board remains desirous of assessing its regulations to implement an
appropriate regulatory approach for addressing the potential impacts of Data Centers while
permitting the productive and healthy development of Data Centers within the Township, which
may include addressing the proper design, size, location, and operation of a Data Center in the
Township; and

WHEREAS, studies regarding the impacts of Data Centers continue to evolve and present
complex regulatory issues which require additional time to complete the proper and appropriate
regulations of Data Centers within the Township; and

WHEREAS, the Township Board requires additional time to study and review the potential
regulations for Data Centers within the Township to ensure that Data Centers do not interfere with
other land uses within the Township or negatively impact the surrounding environment; and

WHEREAS Michigan case law supports the extension of a temporary, short-term moratorium,
that delays the consideration of relevant applications, but provides an opportunity for waiver, until
after a review of the master plan and subsequent zoning scheme is complete as such action does
not interfere with an applicant’s constitutional rights, Dan & Jan Clark, LLC v Charter Twp of
Orion, decided June 25, 2009 (Docket No. 284238) (unpublished); and

WHEREAS, recognizing that the moratorium is temporary in nature and is set to expire on July
24, 2026, the Township Board desires to extend the moratorium as specified in the Ordinance for
an additional six (6) months from July 24, 2026, that being until January 24, 2027 in order to allow
the Township to promulgate new ordinances and regulations, consult legal counsel, as well as
develop, consider and make necessary changes and modifications to the existing Township
Zoning Ordinance regarding Data Center projects; and

WHEREAS, Section 3 of the Ordinance expressly authorizes the Township the opportunity to
extend the moratorium by resolution.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Township Board of Oneida Charter
Township, Eaton County, Michigan as follows:

THE CHARTER TOWNSHIP OF ONEIDA, EATON COUNTY, MICHIGAN ORDAINS:
a. Extension of Moratorium: The temporary moratorium imposed by the Ordinance is

hereby extended for an additional six (6) months, through and including January 24, 2027,
unless terminated earlier as provided below. The moratorium shall terminate prior to


January 24, 2027, upon the Township’s adoption of new regulations governing Data Center
facilities and rescission of the moratorium.

. Continuing Effect of Ordinance: All terms, conditions, findings, and waiver provisions
of the Ordinance, including any provision authorizing the Township Board to grant a
waiver from the moratorium upon a showing of hardship or other appropriate grounds,
shall remain in full force and effect during the extension period.

Repealer: All resolutions or parts of resolutions in conflict with this resolution are hereby
repealed only to the extent necessary to give this resolution full force and effect.

Severability: If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or provision of this Resolution
is for any reason held to be invalid or unenforceable by a court of competent jurisdiction,
the remaining sections, subsections, sentences, clauses, and provisions of this Resolution
shall remain in full force and effect. At a minimum, the Resolution shall be considered as
an internal policy of the Township related to the receipt, processing and approval of
applications for data centers.

Effective Date: This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon its adoption by the
Township Board.
