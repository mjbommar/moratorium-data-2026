                                       Public Comment Policy
PUBLIC COMMENT
   As required by PA 276 of 1976, The Open Meetings Act, there shall be a minimum of one (1) Public
   Comment period at any Open Meeting of Norvell Township government. Any member of the public
   wishing to address the public body will limit their comments to one (1) three (3) minute period during each
   period of Public Comment so that all members of the public wishing to address that body may have an
   opportunity to be heard. In cases where more than 30 members of the public are present at a meeting and
   wish to provide public comment, the Township Board limits the length of public comment made by an
   individual to two (2) minutes in duration during each period of Public Comment. The Chair, at their
   discretion, may extend that period. A member of the public not using the full three (3) minutes allotted,
   may not cede any unused time to another speaker. In the event that a group of more than three (3)
   persons supporting or opposing the same position desires to be heard, in the interest of time, a
   spokesperson may be designated to express the group’s concern and the spokesperson may be allotted up
   to ten (10) minutes to speak.
MANNER OF ADDRESSING THE BOARD/COMMISSION
   Upon recognition by the meeting Chair, each person addressing the Board/Commission is asked (but not
   required) to stand and give his/her name and address in an audible tone of voice for the record. All
   comments are to be addressed to the meeting Chair. No person other than members of the
   Board/Commission and the person recognized shall be permitted to enter into any discussion, either
   directly or through the members of the Board/Commission. No question shall be asked of the
   Board/Commission members except through the meeting Chair.
BREACHING THE PEACE
   Any person who breaches the peace may be requested to leave. Breaching the peace includes repeatedly
   speaking without being recognized, threatening harm to any person or property, or otherwise disrupting
   the orderly proceeding of the meeting. If an individual who is breaching the peace refuses to leave, law
   enforcement will be called to remove the subject.
RECORDING OF TOWNSHIP MEETINGS
   Any person shall have the right to record (either audio or video), broadcast or take pictures of the
   proceedings of an Open Meeting but shall not utilize the electric outlets of the Township without prior
   permission of the Township Clerk. Any recording device and picture taking device shall be kept a minimum
   of ten (10) feet from any Board member and may not be placed behind them. Said devices shall not be
   placed so as to impede the vision of other members of the public. All cords related to those activities are
   to be kept in a secure manner so as not to cause a trip/fall hazard.
Adopted 11/15/2023

                             Regular Meeting-February 17, 2026

Present: William Sutherland, Supervisor; Sharon Pero, Clerk; Andrew Haystead, Trustee; Paul
Francis, Trustee; Norvell Township, Jackson County
Excused: Treasurer, Sauers

The Regular Meeting of the Norvell Township Board was called to order by Supervisor Bill
Sutherland at 6:00 p.m. followed by the Pledge of Allegiance. Supervisor Sutherland read a
statement on the status of the review of the AAOM application for CUP for an aggregate mine.

APPROVAL OF AGENDA
Supervisor Sutherland made one change to the agenda, noting that Norvell Police Chief Truchan
had turned in his resignation. A motion was made by Supervisor Sutherland, seconded by Trustee
Francis to approve the agenda as presented for February 17, 2026, board meeting. Motion
carried.

PUBLIC COMMENT
Public comments were made by Sybil Kolon, Silas Bilecki, and Mary Goode about their concern
for Watkins Lake State Park and the aggregate mine and its impacts. Kelly Truchan and Jackie
Martin spoke about the resignation of Norvell Township Chief of Police Truchan.

A motion was made by Trustee Francis, seconded by Trustee Haystead to approve the minutes as
presented for January 20, 2026, regular meeting and the minutes for January 20, 2026, Joint
Meeting. Motion carried.

Agenda as presented. Motion carried.

meeting.

Francis stated that he would like the GL accounts on the accounts payable reports. A motion was
made by Supervisor Sutherland, seconded by Trustee Francis to approve payment of the
following bills: Paid Bills totaling $48,821.45, Unpaid/Open Bills totaling $8,260.65, and

Assistance for Clerk Training

Sutherland, Haystead, Pero. Nays - None. Excused - Sauers.

NEW BUSINESS
Norvell Township Chief of Police
Along with the preface letter from Attorney Stachurski, Mr. Truchan submitted his letter of
resignation as Chief of Police on February 17, 2026. Clerk Pero asked for clarification on Mr.
Truchan’s MCOLES license being inactive, not suspended. Supervisor Sutherland affirmed such.
A motion was made by Supervisor Sutherland and seconded by Trustee Haystead to accept the
resignation from Mr. Truchan. Motion carried on roll call vote as follows: Ayes - Sutherland,
Haystead, Francis (reluctantly), Pero (sadly). Nays - None. Excused - Sauers.

seconded by Trustee Haystead to approve $1,225 for clerk, deputy clerk, and treasurer 8- hour
training with BS&A. Motion carried on a roll call vote as follows: Ayes - Francis, Haystead,
Sutherland, Pero. Nays - None. Excused - Sauers.

Resolution 2026-03 – Moratorium on Data Centers
After consulting with the Township Attorney, a two-step approach was recommended. Amend
the Zoning Ordinance to allow the Township Board to adopt temporary moratoria on emerging
land uses (such as data centers) by resolution. This follows the standard process of a Planning
Commission public hearing followed by Board approval. Once the amendment is active, the
Board can formally pause data center applications via resolution while specific zoning
regulations are developed. This strategy addresses the current need while providing flexibility to
handle future unforeseen land-use issues. A motion was made by Supervisor Sutherland and
seconded by Clerk Pero to approved motion to instruct the Planning Commission to prepare an

emerging land issues, including data centers, by resolution. Motion carried on a roll call as

Resolution 2026-02 – Contract with Cambridge Township for Emergency Services
The proposed four-year Emergency Services contract renewal with Cambridge Township is due
for renewal. Questions arose that require clarification. Supervisor Sutherland will get some
clarifications on the questions asked by Trustee Francis and bring it back to the Board for adoption.

Pension Plan
Norvell Township is reviewing its pension plan provider, currently with The Principal, due to
administrative challenges and limited support, with options to retain the current provider, transition
to MERS or Equitable via the Decker Agency, or implement direct payments. Both Supervisor
Sutherland and Clerk Pero pointed out that both MERS and Equitable offered distinct advantages.
A special meeting will be scheduled for a full presentation by MERS, with potential legal counsel
attendance to review the transition process.

Norvell Personnel Policy
The Township Personnel Policy and Procedures Handbook has needed some updates. With the
help of our attorney, these updates have now been made. Trustee Francis requested that in the
future, proposed changes be highlighted for Board members in the Board packet. A motion was
made by Supervisor Sutherland and seconded by Clerk Pero to approve the updates to the
Personnel Policy as presented. Motion carried on a roll call as follows: Ayes – Sutherland, Pero,
Francis, Haystead. Nays – None. Excused - Sauers

Budget Amendments – 2nd Quarter
Budget amendments will continue as we implement the General Ledger adjustments recommended
in the fiscal year 2024-2025 Audit. Trustee Francis believes that the Auditor should book the
Pension payments that are being made for the last year to last year’s accounting, as this was the
largest driver on out of budget items. The Township retains $40,000 in Contingency Reserves and
plans to reduce certain expenditures to help offset the increases reflected in this amendment cycle.
A motion was made by Supervisor Sutherland and seconded by Trustee Haystead to approve the
Budget Amendments as presented. Motion carried on a roll call as follows: Ayes – Sutherland,
Haystead, Francis, Pero. Nays – None. Excused – Sauers.

Fox, Christine C., Joe Crisante, Andrea Hathaway, Cathy King, Debbie Owen, and Linda Knox
on behalf of Michael W. Eric Johnson made comment about Jay Truchan.

To: Norvell Township Board of Trustees
From: Supervisor Bill Sutherland
Subject: Renewal of Emergency Services Contract with Cambridge Township
Date: March 16, 2026

Overview
The current Emergency Services contract with Cambridge Township is due for renewal. Norvell
Township has a long history of receiving excellent service from the Cambridge Township Fire
Department (CFD). The proposed agreement includes a three-year initial term with automatic
one-year renewals thereafter.
Contract language has been clarified to specify monthly payments, and legal counsel has
confirmed that prepayment for these services is permissible. The accompanying Resolution
has been updated to reflect these changes.

Call Volume Trends
        Between 2015 and 2020, CFD responded to an average of 144 calls per year.
        From 2021 through 2026, the average increased to 191 calls per year (a 32.6%
        increase).
        Norvell Township’s share of calls remained consistent at 17.5%.

Cost Trends and Township Share
       CFD’s annual operating expenses increased from $443,072 to $641,585 (a 44.8%
       increase).
       Norvell Township’s proportional share rose from $77,537 to $112,277 (44.8%).
       Actual payments by Norvell increased from $62,736 to $80,926 (29%), which has not
       kept pace with CFD’s rising costs.
       This gap has contributed to a funding shortfall under the current cost-sharing
       arrangement.




                  Fire Emergency Services Contract Renewal CFD 3.16.2026.docx
                  Page | 1                                 William Sutherland


Proposed Contract Adjustments
To address the shortfall and align contributions with actual service costs, the proposed renewal
includes:
       8% increase in the first year of the agreement.
       3% annual increases in each subsequent year.
       Four-year initial term with automatic one-year renewals.
       Monthly payment structure, with prepayment authorized per legal review.

Drivers of Increased Costs
The cost adjustments reflect rising expenses associated with modern fire protection services,
including:
        Planned acquisition of a new fire truck (estimated $1.5 million).
        Increased staffing levels, with full-time firefighter coverage now averaging 20 hours per
        day.
        Significant improvement in response times to Norvell Township—from 14 minutes to 7
        minutes, a critical factor in fire and rescue outcomes.

Conclusion
Operational data demonstrates increased service demand and rising costs for CFD. The
proposed contract adjustments—including clarified payment terms and the updated
Resolution—are intended to ensure continued high-quality fire protection for Norvell Township
while maintaining a fair and sustainable cost-sharing structure.

             given the circumstances, but Cambridge makes no guarantee that the

                           CHIEF OF POLICE JOB DESCRIPTION

Position:               Chief of Police
Supervised By:          Norvell Township Board of Trustees
Supervises:             All Police Department Personnel



General Summary
Under the direction of the Norvell Township Board of trustees, the Chief of Police plans, develops
and directs a complete program of policing and law enforcement to protect the lives and property
of the public and to preserve peace in the Township. The Chief acts as a spokesperson for the
Township Board regarding policing, emergency response, and emergency preparedness. He or she
interacts with other agencies and departments to ensure a comprehensive and coordinated approach
to public safety throughout the community. This position occasionally requires evening, weekend,
and holiday hours and on-call responsibilities. This position is a working Chief position requiring
road patrol and related duties.

Essential Duties and Responsibilities
An employee in this position may be called upon to perform any or all of the following essential
functions. This list is illustrative of the nature of responsibilities for an employee in this position;
it may not include all the duties to be performed.

   1. Planning the activities, programs and goals of the Police Department including operations,
       patrol, and investigation to insure maximum service to the community. This includes
       performing patrol and investigative duties.
   2. Developing and implementing necessary and appropriate policies, procedures, practices,
       rules and regulations for the Police Department.
   3. Properly and efficiently enforcing all state and federal laws, as well as local ordinances
       which the Police Department is authorized to enforce.
   4. Preparing departmental schedule and assigning personnel to best meet the needs of the
       public.
   5. Providing supervision to police personnel, including assigning and reviewing work,
       evaluating performance, disciplining, and setting up training programs.
   6. Making reassignments and appointments within the department with the approval of the
       Township Board.
   7. Assisting with the coordination of efforts and communication between police agencies.
   8. Establishing and maintaining effective public relations between the department and the
       community.
   9. Meeting and conferring with members of the community, the media, and groups concerning
       complaints, problems, requests and suggestions.
   10. Studies and determines law enforcement needs of the Township and recommends solutions
       to the Township Board.

   11. Prepares and presents a budget for the Police Department to the Township Supervisor.
   12. Ensures that all records are kept by the department that are required to insure accurate
       reporting to the Township Board, as well as state and federal agencies.
   13. Conducts internal investigations on accusations or complaints against police officers,
       reports to the Township Board and prescribes disciplinary action where warranted.
   14. Must stay current with Michigan Commission on Law Enforcement certification.

Tools and Equipment Used: Emergency response motor vehicle, firearms, non-lethal weapons,
speed detection devices, mobile radio, phone, and a variety of office equipment.

Required Minimum Qualifications Education
and Experience:
   1. A bachelor’s degree or knowledge and experience equivalent to a bachelor’s degree in

   3. Knowledge of police communications and computer applications.
   4. Ability to organize and direct work of self and others and to manage or coordinate multiple

   5. Ability to exercise discretion in the use of force and law enforcement powers.
   6. Ability to communicate effectively verbally and in writing and the capacity to understand
       and carry out written and oral instructions. Skilled in public speaking.
   7. Ability to establish and maintain effective, positive working relationships with employees,
       supervisors, other departments, officials, and the public.
   8. Ability to maintain confidentiality of highly sensitive data and information.

Physical Demands and Work Environment

be met by an employee to successfully perform the essential functions of this job.

employee is frequently required to walk; talk or hear; sit; climb or balance; stoop, kneel, crouch,

                                Norvell Township
To: Norvell Township Board of Trustees
From: Bill Sutherland, Township Supervisor
Subject: Ahrens Park Baseball Field Usage Agreements – Trackside Baseball & Great Lakes
Renegades
Date: March 16, 2026

Background
In 2021, I was contacted by Trackside Baseball, represented by Keith and KJ Neibauer. Trackside
is an organization composed of little league baseball travel teams. At that time, they were
practicing at the baseball fields in Grass Lake Township but were experiencing competition for
field availability and were seeking an alternate site.
Trackside had previously spoken with former Supervisor Eric Johnson regarding the potential
use of Ahrens Park. However, due to the COVID-19 pandemic, all discussions were put on hold.
Trackside expressed willingness to rehabilitate the baseball diamonds at Ahrens Park in
exchange for the right of first usage. This arrangement could be supported by establishing a
calendar and reservation system on the Township website. Their proposal aligned with the
Township’s goal of restoring Ahrens Park to a functional recreational facility.
In 2022, the Township entered into a usage agreement with Trackside Baseball.

Developments in 2024
In early 2024, Trackside reduced the number of teams they were fielding, and the Great Lakes
Renegades absorbed those remaining teams.
On March 20, 2024, I met with Ryan Davis of the Great Lakes Renegades. His organization
expressed interest in using the Ahrens Park baseball fields on days when they were available,
along with the remaining Trackside teams.
Prior to this meeting, I had already met with Chad Brown of Trackside Baseball. Working
collaboratively with both organizations, a coordinated usage schedule was developed that
accommodates each group.
Because the original usage agreement did not anticipate multiple organizations sharing the
facility, I requested that Township legal counsel review the agreement to ensure it would
function appropriately under a multi-organization structure.

Request for Board Action
I am requesting that the Board approve authorizing the Township Supervisor to enter into
usage agreements with both the Great Lakes Renegades and Trackside Baseball, pending the
submittal of all required documentation specified by Township legal counsel.
As I have consistently communicated, the Township will maintain the park grounds, and the
organizations will maintain the ball fields.
Once finalized, the coordinated field-usage schedule will be posted on the Township website.
