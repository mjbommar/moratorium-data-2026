                                            Policy for Public Comment
                       Township Board Regular Meetings, Planning Commission & ZBA Meetings

All public comment shall be received during one of the following portions of the Agenda of an open meeting:

a. Citizen Comment on Non-Agenda Items or Public Comment – while this is not intended to be a forum for
   dialogue and/or debate, if a citizen inquiry can be answered succinctly and briefly, it will be addressed or it may
   be delegated to the appropriate Township Official or staff member to respond at a later date. More complicated
   questions can be answered during Township business hours through web contact, phone calls, email
   (oshtemo@oshtemo.org), walk-in visits, or by appointment.

b. After an agenda item is presented by staff and/or an applicant, public comment will be invited. At the close of
   public comment there will be Board discussion prior to call for a motion. While comments that include questions
   are important, depending on the nature of the question, whether it can be answered without further research,
   and the relevance to the agenda item at hand, the questions may not be discussed during the Board deliberation
   which follows.

    Anyone wishing to make a comment will be asked to come to the podium to facilitate the audio/visual
    capabilities of the meeting room. Speakers will be invited to provide their name, but it is not required.

    All public comment offered during public hearings shall be directed, and relevant, to the item of business on
    which the public hearing is being conducted. Comment during the Public Comment Non-Agenda Items may be
    directed to any issue.

    All public comment shall be limited to four (4) minutes in duration unless special permission has been granted in
    advance by the Supervisor or Chairperson of the meeting.

    Public comment shall not be repetitive, slanderous, abusive, threatening, boisterous, or contrary to the orderly
    conduct of business. The Supervisor or Chairperson of the meeting shall terminate any public comment which
    does not follow these guidelines.

 Memorandum
 Date:                  March 10, 2026
 To:                    Township Board
 From:             James Porter, Legal Counsel & Sierra Lucas,
 Paralegal Manager
 Subject:            First Reading re Text Amendments to Hawkers, Peddlers, and Solicitors – Section 122.001:
 Definitions & Section 122.003: License




Objective
 MOTION: I make a motion to approve the first reading of the Text Amendments to Hawkers, Peddlers, and Solicitors
 – Section 122.001: Definitions & Section 122.003: License, and set second reading for March 24, 2026.

Background

The proposed ordinance amendments would require any individual seeking to sell or solicit the sale of goods outside of
a commercial establishment to either (1) be a recognized charitable organization registered with the Township, or (2)
obtain a valid Hawkers and Peddlers license issued by the Township.

These amendments are intended to address ongoing concerns involving juveniles unlawfully obtaining candy and other
retail goods from local businesses and subsequently attempting to resell those items outside nearby commercial
establishments. By establishing clear registration and licensing requirements, the Township would provide the Police
Department with defined authority to approach individuals engaged in such sales activities and request verification of
nonprofit registration or proper licensure.

This framework would allow officers to better identify the individuals involved, discourage the resale of unlawfully
obtained merchandise, and more effectively address related criminal conduct, while preserving legitimate charitable and
licensed sales activities.

                               THE CHARTER TOWNSHIP OF OSHTEMO
                                 KALAMAZOO COUNTY, MICHIGAN
                                           ORDAINS:

                  HAWKERS, PEDDLERS, AND SOLICITORS’ ORDINANCE

SECTION I.                 AMENDMENT TO SECTION 122.002 - DEFINITIONS:

Hawking, peddling, vending, door to-door selling or soliciting or any variation of the same
shall be defined for the purpose of this Ordinance as any activity involving selling or soliciting
the sale of any goods, merchandise, services, or donations upon or from the public streets, parks
and places or upon or from private property, commercial businesses open to the public, or
premises not owned, rented, leased, managed or occupied under a valid claim of right or title by
the person, firm or corporation engaged in such selling or soliciting activity.

SECTION II.                AMENDMENT TO SECTION 122.003 - LICENSE:

A.       Licensing required. No person, firm or corporation shall hereafter operate or engage in, or aide
and abet, either directly or indirectly, any business of hawking, peddling, vending, door-to-door selling or
soliciting within the Charter Township of Oshtemo without first obtaining a license therefor from the
Township Clerk or Ordinance Enforcement Officer of said Township, which shall only be issued where
the applicant qualifies therefor under the terms of this ordinance. This requirement shall not apply to
charitable/religious organizations which are considered non-profit pursuant to IRC § 501(c)(3) or duly-
appointed representatives soliciting donations on behalf of same. However, such an organization shall
register with the Township Clerk annually and file with the Township Clerk or Ordinance Enforcement
Officer a list of the names of all persons soliciting on behalf of the organization.

SECTION III.               EFFECTIVE DATE:

This Ordinance will take effect upon Publication after Adoption in accordance with state law.
Ordinances, or parts Ordinances, in conflict herewith are hereby suspended until the Moratorium
provisions of this Ordinance are otherwise amended or repealed or until the Moratorium ends.

 Subject:                2026 Local Road Preventative Maintenance “PAR” Projects



Objective
 Provide the Board the list of recommended local road preventative maintenance projects, commonly referred to as
 PAR projects, with the Road Commission of Kalamazoo County (RCKC) and approve the respective budget
 amendment of $2,038.50.

  Proposed Motion: I move to approve the 2026 Local Road Contract with RCKC and respective budget amendment.

Background
 The Township utilizes its dedicated road millage to complete preventative maintenance and capital improvements to
 the local road network. Based on the total number of miles of local roads and a life cycle cost analysis, the goal is to
 complete approximately $750,000 in preventative maintenance annually to meet our targeted level of service.

  Every year, the RCKC allocates funds for the PAR program from the funding received through Act 51 from gas tax
  and vehicle registrations. Allocations are made between all the Townships based on total road mileage, population,
  and urban area. For 2026, Oshtemo has been allocated $323,101.00 which must be matched 1 to 1 for cost-sharing
  per statute. Oshtemo Township historically overmatches the PAR funds and elects to receive any additional funds
  that are not used by others.

  Project selection is done with an asset management approach, where the RCKC evaluates and rates the surface
  condition of the roads (PASER) and then recommends an appropriate treatment based on the category of the rating
  and funding available. Because of Oshtemo’s road millage, amount of traffic, and level of service, we can more
  proactively and appropriately select the fixes when a road gets to a PASER 4 or 5 then try to extend to a 3 or below.
  This year, proposed projects consist of hot mix asphalt (HMA) overlays, chip/fog seals, and a chip seal trial.

  For 2026, RCKC has revised portions of the standard PAR contract with the new Neighborhood Road Funds, which
  are anticipated to be available for use in 2027. Attached is a copy of the contract with staff notes and feedback for
  future discussions and considerations as implementation is navigated. We have participated in budget discussions
  and applicable board meeting items to provide this feedback during public comment to encourage open dialog with
  the RCKC Board, RCKC staff and the Township partners. The contract with notes is only for reference. (The only
  required change is to name the correct Township in item 8D.)

Proposed Motion
I move to approve the budget amendment for the KL Ave NM Path construction costs.

Background
The Michigan Department of Transportation (MDOT) agreed to include the Township’s construction items for the
segment of the KL Ave non-motorized path underneath the US-131 bridges in conjunction with their replacement
project and contract. MDOT, per their own Complete Streets Policy, paid to accommodate the facility in their design
and their respective construction scope (e.g. abutment/retaining wall) and the Township was only responsible for items
directly related to installing the path (e.g. gravel base, asphalt, etc.). The project is on schedule and expected to be
complete (and KL Ave back open!) at the end of June.

The agreement signed in 2024, and associated deposit paid, was based on Engineer’s Estimated construction costs of
$91,700 for the Township portion with additional construction engineering costs to be apportioned to the Township as
a ratio of total construction costs.

Actual bid prices were approximately $20,000 higher than the engineer’s estimate for the Township’s portion of the
project costs (and construction engineering costs increased proportionally to that overage). This budget amendment is
expected to cover the remaining Township project costs to be incurred in 2026 as the project wraps up and the path is
complete. Any reductions based on actual quantities of materials used in construction will be returned to the Township,
if applicable.
