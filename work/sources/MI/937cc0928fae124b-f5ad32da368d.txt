CONTRACT WITH LOYOLA UNIVERSITY OF CHICAGO FOR REFINING THE MANOOMIN HABITAT SUITABILITY MODEL


WHEREAS, the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians (“Tribe”) is a federally

recognized Indian Tribe organized under the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934, as

amended; and

WHEREAS, the mission of the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians is to

provide for the perpetuation of our way of life and the welfare and prosperity of our

people, to preserve our right to self-f-government, and protect our property and

resources as ordained by the establishment of our constitution and bylaws; and

WHEREAS, the mission of the Sault Tribe Natural Resources Division,

Gidayaangwaami’idimin Ezhi-inawendiyang, is to serve as leaders in collaborative

stewardship within the Great Lakes region to ensure ecological resilience and

sustainable relations, honoring those who came before us and providing for those

yet to come; and

WHEREAS, the Tribe has received grant funding from the the Bureau of Indian

Affairs (BIA) Great Lakes Restoration Initiative (GLRI) to support and expand

invasive species management, assess wildlife response to management strategies,

and identify key areas within the Great Lakes basin for manoomin restoration and

revitalize these critical habitats through seeding; and

WHEREAS, a central objective of the awarded BIA GLRI proposal is to refine a

habitat suitability model that will identify local sites with the highest probability of

successful manoomin reintroduction, informing targeted restoration efforts and

creating new opportunities for harvesting across the Great Lakes Basin; and

WHEREAS, t the Loyola University of Chicago possesses relevant n niche c capacity to

refine the manoomin habitat suitability model as a co-developer of the original

model; and

WHEREAS, the Tribe proposed to subaward Loyola University of Chicago to

support the refining of the manoomin habitat suitability model; and

WHEREAS, t the total value of the proposed subaward shall not exceed $61,008.00,

and the contract term shall run from March  of 2026 through May  of 2027; and

WHEREAS, The contract will be funded exclusively through the BIA GLRI  grant

awarded to the Tribe and will not obligate the Tribe beyond the approved grant

budget or term.

awarding a professional services contract to Loyola University of Chicago, for the

the manoomin habitat suitability model.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the NRD is authorized to administer the

BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED, that the Chairman (or designee) is authorized to

NRD APPLICATION TO 2026 NATIONAL COASTAL RESILIENCE FUND


WHEREAS, the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians is a federally

recognized Indian Tribe organized under the Indian Reorganization Act of 1934, as

amended; and

WHEREAS, the mission of the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians is to

provide for the perpetuation of our way of life and the welfare and prosperity of our

people, to preserve our right to self-f-government, and protect our property and

resources as ordained by the establishment of our constitution and bylaws; and

WHEREAS, the mission of the Sault Tribe Natural Resources Division,

Gidayaangwaami’idimin Ezhi-inawendiyang, i is to serve as leaders in collaborative

stewardship within the Great Lakes region to ensure ecological resilience and

sustainable relations, honoring those who came before us and providing for those

yet to come; and

WHEREAS, coastal wetlands play an important ecological and cultural role by

providing essential habitat for native plant communities, wildlife, and Tribal

subsistence use; and

WHEREAS, coastal wetland ecosystems are increasingly threatened by invasive

species, land-use changes, hydrology changes, and erosion; and

WHEREAS, the Sault Tribe previously received funding through the National

Coastal Resilience Fund to develop a comprehensive inter-agency resilience plan

that prioritizes coastal wetlands for protection and restoration in the St. Marys

River; and

WHEREAS, the Natural Resources Division seeks to apply for National Fish and

Wildlife Foundation (NFWF) National Coastal Resilience Fund funding to  support

coastal wetland stewardship and restoration on the St. Marys River based on

previous planning efforts; and

WHEREAS, this resolution supports the Tribe’s broader stewardship goals and

reaffirms its commitment to protecting culturally and ecologically significant

species within the 1836 Ceded Territory.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, the Sault Tribe Board of Directors

approves the Natural Resources Division to apply for up to $7,000,000 in NFWF

National Coastal Resilience Fund funding.

ESTABLISHING A MORATORIUM PROHIBITING THE DEVELOPMENT, APPROVAL, OR OPERATION OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (AI) DATA CENTERS ON TRIBAL LANDS


WHEREAS, the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of Chippewa Indians (“Tribe”) is a federally

recognized Indian Tribe with inherent sovereign authority to regulate land use,

environmental protection, economic development, and infrastructure within its

jurisdiction; and

WHEREAS, the Tribe has a sacred r responsibility to protect its lands, waters, natural

resources, and cultural heritage for present and future generations; and

WHEREAS, artificial intelligence (AI) data centers are large-scale industrial

facilities that require substantial and continuous electricity consumption, often

placing significant strain on regional power infrastructure; and

WHEREAS, such facilities may also require extensive water usage for cooling

systems, potentially impacting local water resources, ecosystems, and community

sustainability; and

WHEREAS, AI data centers may introduce long-term land use impacts, including

industrialization of Tribal lands, disruption of culturally significant areas, and

potential environmental degradation; and

WHEREAS, impacts from AI data centers may introduce long-term and

detrimental impacts within the 1836 ceded territory placing significant strain on

ecosystems and water resources that are vital to the long-term sustainability of the

Tribe’s treaty reserved rights in the 1836 ceded territory; and

WHEREAS, the long-term economic benefits of AI data centers remain uncertain

for Tribal communities, while the risks to treaty p protected resources, environmental

health, and infrastructure burden may be substantial; and

WHEREAS, the Tribe has not yet completed a comprehensive Tribal impact

assessment, environmental review, or community consultation process regarding

AI data center development; and

WHEREAS, it is the duty of the Board of Directors to act with c caution and foresight

when considering developments that may irreversibly impact Tribal lands and

sovereignty.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the Sault Ste. Marie Tribe of

Chippewa Indians hereby establishes a moratorium prohibiting the approval,

permitting, development, construction, leasing, or operation of artificial

intelligence (AI) data centers on all Tribal lands and trust lands under its

jurisdiction.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this moratorium shall apply to all Tribal

governmental departments and any external entities seeking to conduct such

activities within Tribal jurisdiction.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that no Tribal official, department, or entity shall

enter into agreements, negotiations, or commitments that would allow or facilitate

the development of AI data centers  during the duration of this moratorium.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this moratorium is enacted to protect Tribal

treaty reserved resources, environmental integrity, water resources, energy

sustainability, cultural preservation, and community well-being.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this moratorium shall remain in effect

indefinitely unless and until explicitly lifted by a subsequent resolution of the Board

of Directors following:

• A comprehensive Tribal impact assessment

• Environmental and cultural resource evaluations

• Infrastructure and energy capacity analysis

• Robust community consultation and input process

BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED, that the Board of Directors affirms that any future

consideration of such developments must be grounded in Tribal values, long-term

sustainability, and the protection of future generations.
