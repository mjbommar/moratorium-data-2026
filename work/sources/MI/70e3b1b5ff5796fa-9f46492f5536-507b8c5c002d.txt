7. Review of a proposed Peddlers and Solicitors Ordinance.

Township attorney Kali Henderson submitted a proposed ordinance regarding commercial

solicitation, noting it requires registration with the township, allows a no knock list, and restricts

soliciting hours to match the township’s noise ordinance or dusk. The board and the attorneys

discussed this issue at length.

Mr. Reiber asked who will maintain the no knock list. Ms. Henderson stated it would be kept and

maintained by the township clerk. The solicitor will need to ensure they have an updated copy of

the list. He asked if the list will be public information and Ms. Henderson stated it will only be

addresses, not names. If a resident has “No Trespassing" or “No Solicitating” signs on their

property and if a solicitor disregards this, a complaint can be made to the township’s code

enforcement officer and there is a fine. There was a discussion regarding fees for the solicitors,

providing window clings for residents on the no knock list, and what to do if residents sign up

other people on the list.

Ms. Henderson stated that this ordinance will only apply to commercial solicitors. It does not

apply to civic or philanthropic organizations, such  as Girl Scouts, church organizations, caroling,

political canvasing, etc.

Mr. Reiber requested to amend “it protects the people” to “it is intended to protect the people”.

Ms. Hunt agrees. There was a discussion regarding limiting the number of times that a solicitor

can go to a resident’s house. The township cannot do this.

There was further discussion regarding the time limits. Ms. Henderson advised that whatever

time is chosen, there needs to be a specific reason  why that time is chosen.

Mr. Seward and Ms. Henderson will review the feedback received this evening and present an

ordinance for the board’s review.

The project site is located on the west side of Chilson Road, between E. Grand River Avenue

and I-96, on Parcel No. 4711-06-400-003. The parcel contains approximately 33 acres and  is

The proposed development area is currently wooded and generally undeveloped. Existing

natural features on the site include mature wooded areas and undulating topography. Steep

the area within 10 feet of the property have been identified on the accompanying existing

conditions plan.

Areas immediately adjacent to the property consist primarily of Suburban and High Density

Residential across Chilson, existing public/utility-related facilities within the parcel, and

wooded or undeveloped land surrounding portions of the proposed development area. No

disturbance is proposed outside the project limits except as necessary for grading, utility

connections, access, and related site improvements shown on the plans.

D. Impact on storm water management: Description of measures to control soil erosion

and sedimentation during grading and construction operations and until a permanent

ground cover is established. Recommendations for such measures may be obtained

from the Livingston County Drain Commission at (517) 546-0040.

During grading and construction operations, soil erosion and sedimentation will be

controlled using temporary and permanent soil erosion control measures. Proposed

measures include silt fence, a temporary gravel construction access drive, temporary

seeding, permanent seeding, mulch blankets, and check dams where necessary. These

measures will be installed and maintained to reduce  sediment migration from disturbed

areas, protect downstream drainage facilities, and stabilize exposed soils until permanent

ground cover is established.

Silt fence will be installed along downgradient limits of disturbance and other appropriate

locations to intercept sediment-laden runoff. A gravel construction access drive will be

provided to reduce the tracking of soil and debris onto adjacent roadways. Check dams will

be installed in swales or concentrated flow areas where needed to reduce runoff velocity

and encourage sediment deposition. Disturbed areas w will be stabilized with temporary seed,

permanent seed, and mulch blankets as appropriate based on the stage of construction and

final site conditions.

During construction, there may be temporary dust, noise, vibration, and construction

related disturbance. These conditions will be temporary and will be controlled through

appropriate construction practices, including watering for dust control when necessary,

maintaining erosion control measures, and stabilizing disturbed areas in a timely manner.

The site plans show the proposed locations of site improvements and soil erosion and

sedimentation control measures. The project will be  reviewed for compliance with

applicable Livingston County Drain Commissioner requirements prior to issuance of a Soil

Erosion and Sedimentation Control permit. All erosion control measures will be installed,

inspected, and maintained as required until construction is complete and permanent

stabilization has been established.

the property and will serve as an accessory/supporting facility for the owner.

improvements, and final restoration. The proposed use does not represent a significant

Dear Mrs. Ruthig,

Thank you for your consideration of Trinity Health Livingston Hospital's request to construct

additional parking to support our growth, maintain safe operations, and ensure adequate access for

patients, visitors, and staff.

Since opening on April 19, 2026, the hospital has consistently experienced a shortage of onsite

parking to support both existing operations and ongoing growth. At the time of opening, the limited

availability of staff parking resulted in employees utilizing spaces intended for patients and visitors,

creating challenges for those accessing healthcare services. To mitigate this issue, Trinity Health has

partnered with neighboring properties, including Genoa Woods and 242 Community Church, to

secure temporary off-site parking accommodations for staff while a permanent solution is pursued.

As part of the original hospital development plans, banked parking was identified as a future option

should operational demand warrant expansion. At that time, projected parking demand did not

indicate that the additional capacity would be needed immediately upon opening, and therefore the

banked parking was not constructed.

Over the past several months, we have closely monitored  parking utilization and staffing growth. The

data and daily operational experience demonstrate that existing staff-designated parking areas are

no longer sufficient to meet current demand. Staff members are frequently required to park in

remote locations and utilize shuttle transportation, which impacts operational efficiency and presents

safety concerns during early morning, evening, overnight, and inclement weather conditions.

The need for additional parking will become even more critical as the hospital continues to expand

services to meet community healthcare needs. Trinity Health is scheduled to add 24 inpatient beds in

May 2027 and anticipates bringing an additional 16 beds online in the future, along with a future

procedure room. These expansions will require additional clinical, support, and operational staff.

Additionally, the hospital's growth reflects the increasing healthcare needs of the Livingston County

community. Adequate parking is a critical component of supporting that growth and ensuring that

patients, visitors, physicians, and staff can safely and efficiently access the facility.

The proposed parking expansion is necessary too:

• Preserve convenient and accessible parking for patients and visitors.

• Provide adequate parking capacity for existing and future staff.

• Improve employee safety by reducing reliance on off-site parking and shuttle transportation.

• Support the hospital's planned bed expansion and growing outpatient services.

• Ensure the hospital can continue meeting the healthcare needs of the Livingston County community

efficiently and responsibly.

The requested parking is not intended to accommodate speculative future development, but rather

to address a demonstrated operational need that exists today and will continue to grow as patient

volumes and healthcare services expand. Constructing the additional parking now will allow the

hospital to safely support current operations while preparing for anticipated growth needed to serve

our community.

In addition, Trinity Health has carefully evaluated alternatives for both parking and stormwater

management. Given the limited amount of remaining developable land on the hospital campus, an

underground stormwater detention system represents the most responsible long-term solution. This

approach allows the hospital to address current parking deficiencies while preserving valuable land

for future healthcare development. Rather than dedicating surface acreage to stormwater detention,

the proposed design maximizes the efficient use of the campus and maintains flexibility for future

patient care services, clinical expansion, and supporting healthcare infrastructure needed to serve

our growing community. The proposal reflects Trinity Health's commitment to thoughtful planning,

responsible land stewardship, and the long-term healthcare needs of Livingston County.

We respectfully request approval of the additional parking and associated underground stormwater

detention system as a necessary improvement to support the current and future operational needs of

Trinity Health Livingston Hospital. The proposed expansion will provide the infrastructure required to

accommodate continued patient growth, future service line expansion, and planned campus

development while maintaining a safe and accessible environment for patients, visitors, physicians,

and staff. This investment will ensure the hospital can  continue to effectively serve the evolving

healthcare needs of Livingston County and the surrounding region for years to come.

Thank you for your consideration and continued support.

b. Map(s) and written description/analysis of the project site

The Trinity Health of Michigan site (Parcel ID: 4711-13-200-009) is in Section 13 of Livingston County, north on

Grand River Road, west of South Hacker Road and east of the Genoa Business Park. It is a 77.59-acre site that

currently has a medical facility and a senior living facility residing on it. These establishments are supported by

surface parking as well as an access road that has shared connections shared with the Business Park. Please see

attached documentation PEA Group Existing Conditions Plan – Overall (sheet C-1.0) for easement information and

surrounding zoning.

c. Impact on natural features:

1. Slopes and Grade: The site presents steeper slopes in some areas between the proposed parking and the water

body to the northeast. It is intended that this plan will minimize the amount of grading that will need to be done on

site. This grading will be performed on 4 acres of the site. Sidewalks being replaced will be ADA accessible. Refer

to PEA Group Grading Plan (sheets C-4.0 and C-4.1).

2. Wetland and Soils: The site contains two creeks and two wetland/detention ponds north of the existing

medical building. The proposed development is staying out of wetlands as delineated previously by SmithGroup.

The proposed outlet will be over restricted from 0.15 cfs/acre to 0.10 cfs/acre in compliance with Livingston County

Drain Commissioner due to flooding concerns.

3. Vegetation: There is minimal vegetation on the  proposed site, as shown on the Existing Conditions Plan sheets.

Most of this vegetation tends to be undergrowth and brush. Some of this vegetation will need to be removed to

accommodate the proposed additional parking. It is the intent of this design to minimize vegetation removal, and the

improvements will not be removing more than 25% of existing vegetation on site. Refer to PEA Group Demolition

Plan (sheets C-2.0 and C-2.1) and PEA Group Landscape Plans.

d. Impact on stormwater management:

The existing stormwater drainage is contained within the site and drain to a regional pond/detention system.

An existing wet pond with forebay is currently functioning as the stormwater mitigation system for the

entirety of the site’s drainage area. Existing on-site underground storm sewer will be utilized to continue to

convey stormwater to the existing wet pond. Proposed underground storm sewer and an underground detention

system will be added to support the additional parking areas and impervious cover. The improvements to the

existing detention system are to enhance the overall quality of the stormwater system. Surface runoff during

construction will be controlled by silt fences, inlet filters, and seed and mulch.

e. Impact on surrounding land used:

The proposed land banked parking is planned to benefit the community by providing more parking spaces at the

medical center. Currently, there is not an adequate number of spaces and visitors are having to park across the street

at 242 Community Church. The land banked parking will increase the safety of the community since visitors will not

need to cross a busy road to get to the medical center. Site improvements include 318 parking spaces, relocation of

sidewalks and ramps associated with land banked parkrking entrances, relocation of signs and light poles, and curb

and gutter in newly paved areas. There will be no impact to the current noise level and air pollution at the site.

f. f. Impact on public facilities and services:

The proposed development will include demolition of sidewalk and curb, relocation of light poles and signs,

grading, paving, and installation of storm sewer. There will be no impact to public schools. Police and fire

protection will not be needed for the project.

g. Impact on public utilities:

Sanitary sewer and water utilities are reviewed by Marion, Howell, Oceola, and Genoa (MHOG) Sewer and

Water Utilities. The proposed development does not include any changes to the sanitary sewer or water utilities.

h. Storage and handling of any hazardous materials:

The proposed development will include demolition of sidewalk and curb, relocation of light poles and signs,

grading, paving, and installation of storm sewer. There will be no hazardous materials needing storage or handling

on the site as part of this project.

RE: Resolution Declining Public Ownership of Lake Chemung Outdoor

Resort, Inc. Water Supply System

Lake Chemung Outdoor Resort, Inc. is requesting Board approval of a resolution

formally declining public ownership and operational  responsibility for the private

community water supply system serving Lake Chemung Outdoor Resort. Lake

Chemung Outdoor Resort, Inc. operates an independent private community water

system utilizing localized well infrastructure to serve its campground facilities and RV

sites.

Under the Michigan Safe Drinking Water Act (1976 PA  399), the Michigan

Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE) must provide the local

unit of government with the opportunity to assume ownership  and operation of a

proposed community water supply system before issuing the necessary operating

approvals for a privately-operated system. The Township’s formal decision to decline

ownership and operation is therefore required for the resort to proceed with

obtaining its private water supply permits from EGLE.

From the Township’s perspective, approval of the resolution is appropriate because

the water system is private infrastructure serving a private development, and there

is no municipal need or benefit associated with the  Township assuming responsibility

for the system. By formally declining ownership and operation, the Township allows

the resort to maintain responsibility for its own water infrastructure while avoiding

the potential costs and administrative obligations associated with operating and

maintaining a private community water system. The action also preserves a clear

distinction between the Township’s municipal water service, including the MHOG

system, and the privately-owned water infrastructure serving the campground.

Approval of the resolution does not transfer an existing Township water system or

create a new municipal service. Rather, it confirms the Township’s decision not to

assume ownership or operational responsibility for the resort’s private system and

allows the resort to satisfy the state requirement necessary to obtain its EGLE

ARTICLE I —  — PURPOSE AND DEFINITIONS

Sec. 1. Purpose. This ordinance i is intended to p protect the privacy, peace, and safety of Township residents

and protect the public from fraud,  theft, and harm, while respecting the right to engage in expression.

Sec. 2. Definitions.

“Peddler” and “solicitor” mean any person who, for a commercial purpose, travels from house to house or

place to place to sell, take orders for, or offer goods, wares, merchandise, or services. The terms “peddler”

and “solicitor” do not include a person invited to a social gathering within a home by the occupant, or a

person selling the products of their own farm, orchard, or garden.

“Commercial solicitation” means solicitation that proposes a commercial transaction.

“Noncommercial solicitation” means a request for support, including a money donation, for a charitable,

religious, patriotic, civic, educational, political, or philanthropic purpose.

“Charitable or civic organization” means a nonprofit qualified under Section 501(c)(3) or 501(c)(4) or a

tax-exempt veterans’ organization.

A “minor” means any person under the age of 18.

ARTICLE II — LICENSING OF COMMERCIAL PEDDLERS AND SOLICITORS

Sec. 3. License required. No person shall act as a commercial peddler r or solicitor within the Township

without first obtaining a license from the Township Clerk. This Article applies only to commercial activity.

Sec. 4. Application. An applicant shall file with the Clerk a signed application stating:

• the applicant’s name,

• address,

• telephone number,

• the nature of the goods or services,

• name, address, and telephone number of the business they work for,

• whether they have ever been convicted of, are serving a sentence, including probation or parole,

or are currently being charged with any of the following:

o A violation related to their peddling or solicitation  activities;

o A fraud crime;

o A theft crime;

o A crime involving violence against a person;

o A crime of breaking and entering  any dwelling;

o A crime of manufacturing and distributing illegal materials.

• and provide a current photo identification.

RE: Request for Approval of Phase 2 Baseline Noise Study

I respectfully request that the Board of Trustees approve Phase 2 of the Memtech Acoustics

proposal, which consists of establishing baseline noise conditions within the Township.

At the June 15, 2026, Board of Trustees meeting, the Board previously approved Phase 1 and

Phase 3 of the proposal. Phase 1 addresses the development and review of the ordinance

framework and applicable regulations, while Phase 3  addresses the development of the ordinance

language and related process.

Phase 2, which is the subject of this request, involves establishing baseline noise measurements

within the Township. This baseline information will  provide the factual foundation necessary to

appropriately develop the standards included in the  proposed zoning ordinance for emerging

commercial and industrial uses, as well as amendments to the general noise ordinance.

My original intent was to be fiscally responsible and avoid additional Township expense by

utilizing noise information that applicants would be required to provide as part of the

development or site plan submittal process. Based on the  consultant’s subsequent guidance,

however, that approach would not accomplish the purpose of Phase 2. Proceeding without an

independent baseline could result in the Township developing standards without sufficient

information regarding existing conditions. Staff was also advised that relying solely on

measurements provided by individual applicants would not be an appropriate substitute for an

independent baseline study.

Applicant measurements are generally associated with a particular property, proposed use, or

project. They do not necessarily establish existing  sound conditions throughout the Township and

are not an independent representation of the Township’s ambient noise environment.

Obtaining the baseline information now will allow the Township to use the same data in

developing both the proposed zoning ordinance and the general noise ordinance amendments,

providing value beyond a single ordinance or development application.

Baseline information will help determine whether proposed noise limits are realistic for the Township.  Without

an understanding of existing ambient conditions, there is a risk of establishing standards that existing, lawful

activities may already exceed. This could create enforcement difficulties or require exceptions or variances for

existing activities that were lawful prior to adoption of the new standards.

Additionally, if a noise standard is challenged or an enforcement matter proceeds to a hearing or court, the

Township will be better positioned to demonstrate that the standard was based on objective, measurable

conditions within the community rather than an arbitrary number or a standard simply borrowed from another

municipality. This is particularly important when establishing regulations for certain uses, such as data centers.

Data centers can involve significant mechanical and  cooling equipment, and the Township should have reliable

information regarding existing ambient conditions before determining what noise standards are appropriate for

these facilities and other commercial and industrial uses.

Based on the consultant’s recommendation and the need for objective baseline information to support the

development of the proposed regulations, staff recommends the following motion:

Approve the additional services with Memtech Acoustics for ambient sound level data collection (Phase 2)

related to zoning and noise ordinance development at an additional cost not to exceed $10,000 to be paid from

Planning and Zoning Contractual Services budget line item 101-701-802-000.

ACOUSTIC CONSULTING ENGAGEMENT

Noise Ordinance Development & Review

Prepared for: Genoa Township Date: June 3, 2026

ENGAGEMENT OVERVIEW

This document outlines a four-phase plan structured around that request. The City may engage  Phase 1 as a

standalone deliverable or commit to a strategic incremental approach. Phases are sequential, with each one

building on the deliverables of the prior phase.

Most municipal noise ordinances were written before  continuous-operation industrial developments such as

data centers, AI compute facilities, and cryptocurrency mining became common. These facilities produce  low

frequency and tonal noise that often complies with basic numeric limits in current ordinances while still

generating sustained community complaints. Infrasound and steady-state hum are rarely addressed in

conventional ordinance language.

An ordinance that anticipates these characteristics  protects the City’s ability to permit responsibly, proactively

respond to resident concerns with defensible data, and avoid the kind of post-construction mitigation disputes

that have become common in other jurisdictions.

FOUR-PHASE SCOPE

PHASE 1 | Ordinance Review and Baseline Framework

This is the diagnostic step. We compare the City’s existing draft against the goals stated by the Planning

Department, evaluate it against the local environment (rural, suburban, mixed industrial, high-traffic corridors),

and return a marked-up framework that identifies gaps, ambiguities, and language that  may be difficult to

enforce. Skipping this step risks reinforcing weaknesses already present in the draft.

Scope of work:

• Review of the current draft ordinance and the City’s stated goals

• Assessment of the City’s land use mix, zoning patterns, and ambient noise environment

• Identification of regulatory gaps, including infrasound, tonality, and continuous-operation noise sources

• Marked-up baseline framework with recommended structural changes

• If necessary, simulated playbacks of various scenarios using the proposed ordinance can be arranged

Typical cost/duration: Approximately $1,800-$2,000 for deliverables outlined in the above scope.  Deliverable

within 5-10 Business days.

Page 1 of 3

PHASE 2 | Ambient Sound Level Data Collection

An ordinance that sets numeric limits without knowledge of current ambient sound levels can either be too

strict (creating problems for existing businesses who are already in violation) or too permissive (failing to

prevent the very issue it was intended to address).  Field measurements across representative districts  give the

ordinance a defensible, technical foundation and provide a baseline for future enforcement.

Scope of work:

• Sound level monitoring at selected locations (residential, commercial, industrial, downtown)

• Day/night and weekday/weekend sampling as appropriate to land use

• Statistical results delivered alongside the Phase 1  framework

• Documentation suitable for reference in adopted ordinance language

Typical cost/duration: Quoted per project based on site count and monitoring duration.

Single site, 24-hour environmental noise study, data analysis, and summary report ~ $4,950 + $1,600/additional

site, e, deliverable within 15-Business days of data acquisition.

PHASE 3 | Collaborative Ordinance Development

The baseline framework becomes a working ordinance through iteration with City staff, legal counsel, and

elected officials. This phase covers attendance at planning sessions, response to legal review feedback, and

translation of technical acoustic concepts into language the Planning Commission, Council, and  public can act

on. Ordinances most often fail at the adoption stage when this step is rushed.

Scope of work:

• Attendance at Planning Commission and Council meetings (in-person or virtual)

• Workshop sessions with City staff and legal counsel

• Iterative redlines, clarifications, and definitional refinements

• Educational support for staff and elected officials  on acoustic concepts (tonality, dBA vs. dBC, infrasound)

• Audio playback demonstrating the subjective effects  of various proposed criteria

Typical cost: Minimum 1 or 2-hour A Acoustical Consultant per s service. Full support and iterative redlining for

collaborative ordinance development approximately $2,500.

PHASE 4 | Site Study Requirements & Permitting Framework

An ordinance is only as effective as its enforcement mechanism. This phase provides guidance on

preconstruction noise considerations, and builds post-construction acoustic compliance requirements into the

City’s site plan review process. The result is that any future data center, industrial facility, or other significant

noise source comes to the City with a qualified noise impact. This phase ensures proactive action rather than

complaint driven post-remediation, and shifts the burden of proof onto the applicant, where it belongs.

Scope of work:

• Drafting of zoning and site plan ordinance language  requiring acoustic validation

• Definition of risk factors (use type, capacity, equipment thresholds)

• Standardized study scope language for applicants and City reviewers

• Optional review checklists and reviewer training materials

MEMORANDUM

TO: Board of Trustees

FROM: Administrative Committee

DATE:  August 11, 2026

RE: Recommendation for Renewal – Cooper’s Turf Management Snow

Removal Contract

Dear Board Members,

Cooper’s Turf Management has provided snow removal and salt/ice melt services to

Genoa Charter Township under the current contract covering the 2024–2026 winter

seasons. Cooper’s is a long-term, preferred Township vendor that has consistently

provided reliable, responsive, and high-quality service. The company is familiar with

Township facilities, service expectations, and the need for timely response during winter

weather events.

Cooper’s is requesting a new two-year agreement for the 2026–2028 winter seasons.

Other than an adjustment to the salt/ice melt application rate to reflect increased

commodity costs, the proposed agreement maintains the current pricing and provisions

of the existing contract. The salt/ice melt application rate will increase from $295 to

$335 per application, while the snow removal rates and all other contract provisions

remain at their current rates. The proposed renewal  therefore provides the Township

with two additional years of price stability across  the contract, with an adjustment

limited to the salt/ice melt application rate.

The cost of salt and other deicing materials has been subject to increased market

pressures over the past several years. Availability, transportation costs, weather

conditions, and demand can all affect the cost of these materials, with severe winters

creating additional pressure on supply. Cooper’s has  limited its proposed adjustment to

the portion of the contract directly affected by increased commodity costs rather than

seeking broader increases across the agreement. The continuation of current rates for

the remainder of the contract reflects Cooper’s commitment to maintaining a long-term

relationship with the Township and should not be viewed as an indication that prior

contract rates were excessive.

The Township’s Purchasing and Bid Policy provides for consideration of a vendor’s

established history and successful performance when  determining whether competitive

bidding is necessary. The policy specifically provides an exception where there is a “long-standing

successful relationship for services and the knowledge gained through this relationship adds

value integral to the services provided.” The policy also recognizes the value of recurring service

relationships and provides for review of vendor selection and pricing to maintain adequate and

reasonable competitiveness.

Cooper’s Turf Management meets these criteria based on its established relationship with the

Township and consistently satisfactory performance.  Continuing with an experienced contractor

provides operational continuity and the benefit of a vendor already familiar with Township

facilities, access requirements, and service expectations.

ADMINISTRATIVE COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATION

The Administrative Committee recommends approval of f the two-year renewal with Cooper’s Turf

Management. The proposed agreement provides the Township with continued service from a

proven vendor and two additional years of price stability, while limiting the pricing adjustment

to the salt/ice melt application rate in response to increased commodity costs. The Committee

believes the proposed renewal represents a favorable value for the Township and is consistent

with the Township’s Purchasing and Bid Policy.

RECOMMENDED MOTION:

Approve the renewal of the snow removal and salt/ice melt services contract with Cooper’s Turf

Management for the 2026–2028 winter seasons, at the proposed contract rates,  as

recommended by the Administrative Committee, and authorize the Township Manager to

execute the agreement on behalf of the Township.

Respectfully submitted  on behalf of the Administrative Committee,

Kelly VanMarter

RE: Request for Speed Study  – Bauer Road, South of Brighton Road

At the Livingston County Road Commission meeting on 8/13/2026, a Genoa

Township resident asked the Road Commission to perform a speed study on the

paved portion of Bauer Road, south of Brighton Road.

The unposted, statutory speed limit on Bauer Road is 55 mph, which is the

Livingston County standard for paved county roads. The speed limit drops to 25

mph during designated arrival and dismissal times near Maltby Intermediate

School and Hornung Elementary School, and is posted with a flashing light.

Per Livingston County Road Commission guidelines, absolute  or modified speed

limits lower than 55 mph are only posted if a joint traffic engineering study has

been conducted with the Michigan State Police.  Safety Engineer Mike Goryl

informed the resident he would perform the study if  Genoa Township made a

formal request. As I was present at the meetingI was asked to do so, and

,

informed the LCRC I would take the request to my board for consideration.

How The Process Works:

1. Data Collection: Traffic engineers measure the speeds of vehicles on a  specific

road during normal, free-flowing traffic conditions.

2. Speed Sorting: They sort the recorded speeds from lowest to highest.

3. Finding the Cutoff: They identify the speed at or below which 85% of the

vehicles travel.

4. Setting the Limit: The official speed limit is typically set within 5 mph of that 85th

percentile speed.

The Logic Behind It

• Targets the Reckless: A limit set at the 85th percentile safely accommodates

85% of drivers. It leaves enforcement officers to focus strictly on the top 15% of

truly dangerous speeders.

• Reduces Speed Variance: When a speed limit matches how people naturally

drive, it groups traffic together at a uniform speed. This reduces tailgating,

weaving, and overtaking, which causes fewer crashes.
