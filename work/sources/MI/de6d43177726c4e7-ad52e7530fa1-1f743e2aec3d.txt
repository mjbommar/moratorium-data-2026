Policy for Public Comment

Township Board Regular Meetings, Planning Commission & ZBA Meetings

All public comment shall be received during one of the following portions of the Agenda of an meeting:

open

a. Citizen Comment on Non-Agenda Items or Public Comment – while this is not intended to be a forum for

dialogue and/or debate, if a citizen inquiry can be answered succinctly and briefly, it will be addressed or it may

be delegated to the appropriate Township Official or staff member to respond at a later date. More complicated

questions can be answered during Township business hours through web contact, phone calls, email

(oshtemo@oshtemo.org), walk-in visits, or by appointment.

b. After an agenda item is presented by staff and/or an applicant, public comment will be invited. At the close of

public comment there will be Board discussion prior to call for a motion. While comments that include questions

are important, depending on the nature of the question, whether it can be answered without further research,

and the relevance to the agenda item at hand, the questions not be discussed during the Board deliberation

may

which follows.

Anyone wishing to make a comment will be asked to come to the podium to facilitate the audio/visual

capabilities of the meeting room. Speakers will be invited to provide their name, but it is not required.

All public comment offered during public hearings shall be directed, and relevant, to the item of business on

which the public hearing is being conducted. Comment during the Public Comment NonAgenda Items be

-may

directed to any issue.

All public comment shall be limited to four (4) minutes in duration unless special permission has been granted in

advance by the Supervisor or Chairperson of the meeting.

Public comment shall not be repetitive, slanderous, abusive, threatening, boisterous, or contrary to the orderly

conduct of business. The Supervisor or Chairperson of the meeting shall terminate any public comment which

does not follow these guidelines.

Subject: First  Reading re Text Amendments to Hawkers, Peddlers, and Solicitors –  – Section 122.001:


Definitions & Section 122.003: License

Objective


MOTION: I make a motion to approve the first reading of the Text Amendments to Hawkers, Peddlers, and Solicitors

– Section 122.001: Definitions & Section 122.003: License, and set second reading for March 24, 2026.

Background


The proposed ordinance amendments would require any individual seeking to sell or solicit the sale of goods outside of

a commercial establishment to either (1) be a recognized charitable organization registered with the Township, or (2)

obtain a valid Hawkers and Peddlers license issued by the Township.

These amendments are intended to address ongoing concerns involving juveniles unlawfully obtaining candy and other

retail goods from local businesses and subsequently attempting to resell those items outside nearby commercial

establishments. By establishing clear registration and licensing requirements, the Township would provide the Police

Department with defined authority to approach individuals engaged in such sales activities and request verification of

nonprofit registration or proper licensure.

This framework would allow officers to better identify the individuals involved, discourage the resale of unlawfully

obtained merchandise, and more effectively address related criminal conduct, while preserving legitimate charitable and

licensed sales activities.

Background


The Township utilizes its dedicated road millage to complete preventative maintenance and capital improvements to

the local road network. Based on the total number of miles of local roads and a life cycle cost analysis, the goal is to

complete approximately $750,000 in preventative maintenance annually to meet our targeted level of service.

Every year, the RCKC allocates funds for the PAR program from the funding received through Act 51 from gas tax

and vehicle registrations. Allocations are made between all the Townships b based on total road mileage, population,

and urban area. For 2026, Oshtemo has been allocated $323,101.00 which must  be matched 1 to 1 for cost-sharing

per statuteOshtemo Township historically overmatches the PAR funds  and elects to receive any additional funds

.

that are not used by others.

Project selection is done with an asset m management approach, where the RCKC evaluates and r rates the surface

condition of the roads (PASER) and then recommends an appropriate treatment based on the category of the rating

and funding available. Because of Oshtemo’s road millage, amount of traffic, and level of service, we can more

proactively and appropriately select the fixes when a road gets to a PASER 4 or 5 then try  to extend to a 3 or below.

This year, proposed projects consist of hot mix asphalt (HMA) overlays, chip/fog seals, and a chip seal trial.

are anticipated to be available for use in 2027. Attached is a copy of the contract with staff notes and feedback for

future discussions and considerations as implementation is navigated. We have participated in budget discussions
