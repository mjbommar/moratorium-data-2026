       2. Request of Kevin Ross to appeal his FOIA denial.

Mr. Ross submitted a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request for an audio recording
allegedly made by a board trustee. The request followed a resident's social media post claiming
to have overheard the conversation. The FOIA coordinator initially denied the request because
the municipality could not obtain the recording, as the trustee maintained he did not possess one.
Mr. Ross appealed the denial to the board. During the appeal, Trustee Ropeta reaffirmed that, to
his knowledge, no such recording existed. Consequently, Clerk Moughler moved to uphold the
original denial, which was seconded by Treasurer Eden. The motion carried; 6 ayes, 1 nay
(Ferguson). A denied FOIA applicant has the right to appeal the final decision in circuit court.

       3. Medical Marijuana Facilities moratorium.

                               RESOLUTION #260501
                       TYRONE TOWNSHIP, LIVINGSTON COUNTY

                      TO ESTABLISH A 6-MONTH MORATORIUM
                    ON THE DEVELOPMENT, PERMITTING AND/OR
                    OPENING OF MEDICAL MARIJUANA FACILITIES

        WHEREAS, the State of Michigan has in place the Michigan Medical Marijuana Act,
Initiated Law 1 of 2008, (the “Act”).

        WHEREAS, inquires have been made of Tyrone Township (the “Township”) concerning
the regulation of Medical Marijuana in the Township.

                     APPROVED MINUTES – MAY 19, 2026 – PAGE 3


WHEREAS, the Tyrone Township Board (the “Board”) believes it is in the best interest of the
Township to re-examine the Act and any rules, regulations, case law, attorney general opinions
and/or other information concerning the Act, and to amend the Tyrone Township Zoning
Ordinance #36 with respect to the Act and to receive public opinion with respect to the effect and
impact of same.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, by the Tyrone Township Board as follows:

1. The Board hereby places a 6-month moratorium on any and all development, permitting
   and/or opening of medical marijuana facilities in the Township.

2. The Board hereby requests the Tyrone Township Planning Commission to receive public
   input and examine the Act and any rules, regulations, amendments, case law, attorney
   general opinions and/or other information concerning the Act it deems prudent in order to
   make a recommendation to the Board as to what regulations/ordinances, if any, it believes
   should be amended by the Township with respect to the Act.

       4. Data Centers moratorium.

                               RESOLUTION #260502
                       TYRONE TOWNSHIP, LIVINGSTON COUNTY

                            MORATORIUM ON DATA CENTERS

        WHEREAS, the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act, 2006 P.A. being MCL 125.3101 et seq.,
authorizes Tyrone Township (the "Township") to adopt reasonable regulations to control the
establishment and use of Data Centers in accordance with the Township's Master Plan; and

        WHEREAS, the Township has become aware of an increase of Data Centers in the
region, which is an emerging land use not sufficiently contemplated in the current Master Plan
nor by the current zoning for anywhere in the Township; and

        WHEREAS, integration of Data Center land use within the Township's existing land uses
requires suitable regulations and controls to ensure compliance with the Township's Master Plan,
among other things, by preserving agricultural property, and for the protection of general health,
safety and welfare of all of the Township's residents;

        WHEREAS, this Board believes it is prudent and wise in light of the emergence of Data
Center land uses in the region, to assure proper locating and regulation of these land uses in the
Township and must consider this Resolution for Moratorium on Data Centers so as to better
protect the public health, safety, and welfare of Township residents; and

        WHEREAS, imposing a moratorium, on a limited temporary basis, is reasonable and
necessary in order to allow the Township to fully explore, analyze, research and develop
proposed amendments to the Master Plan and the Township Zoning Ordinance, and to make
recommendations to the Township Board regarding such amendments applicable to Data Center
uses in the Township; and

        NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, a moratorium is hereby imposed upon the
issuance of any and all permits, licenses, or approvals for any property subject to or under the
jurisdiction of the Township's Zoning Ordinance for the establishment or use of any new Data
Center uses in the Township. The moratorium imposed by this Resolution shall remain in effect
for nine (9) months following the effective date of this Resolution, or until Data Centers
regulatory amendments to the Township's Zoning Ordinance become effective, whichever occurs
first. Before this moratorium expires, the Township may by resolution extend the moratorium for
up to six (6) months to allow sufficient time to complete amendments to its Ordinances, if
necessary.

          Tyrone Township Contract Expansion Analysis:
               Citizen-Generated Calls for Service
Author: Livingston County Sheriff's Office, Crime Analyst Mark Klein | Date: May 26, 2026



                                                Summary

This report evaluates citizen-generated calls for service (CFS) demand in Tyrone Township to inform
contract expansion decisions. Citizen-generated calls are defined as calls received via telephone
(PHONE) or 911 -- representing direct requests for service from Township residents and visitors.
Self-initiated deputy activity (traffic stops, patrols, building checks) is excluded from this analysis to focus
solely on community demand.

The current contract provides coverage from 1500-2300 hours, 7 days per week (56 hours/week).
Analysis of 4,066 citizen-generated calls over 5.4 years (January 2021 - May 2026) reveals that only
46.8% of citizen-generated demand falls within current contract hours. More than half (53.2%) of all
citizen requests for service occur when no dedicated Tyrone Township deputy is on duty.

Of particular concern: 337 Priority 1 (emergency) citizen calls occurred outside contract hours during the
study period -- representing 52% of all citizen-generated emergencies. These include welfare checks,
domestic situations, personal injury accidents, and threats/intimidation calls where response time is
critical.

#1: White Lake Rd Corridor -- 31 Crashes
The White Lake Rd corridor is the highest-volume crash location with 31 crashes spanning multiple
intersections including Old US 23 Hwy, Denton Hill Rd, Runyan Lake Rd, and Hartland Rd. This corridor
accounts for 16.5% of all secondary road crashes in the township.

- 12 intersection-involved crashes at multiple cross-streets
- 19 mid-block crashes (single-vehicle lane departures)
- 12 stop sign controlled intersections
- 71% occur in daylight on dry roads
- 12 single-vehicle lane departure crashes (38.7%)
- 6 crashes involve snow/ice road conditions (19.4%)

Key Pattern: This corridor has two distinct crash types -- intersection angle/turning crashes at
cross-streets (stop sign compliance) and mid-block lane departures on the corridor itself. The mix of
high-speed corridor traffic with multiple stop-controlled side streets creates conflict points.

#2: Center Rd @ Old US 23 Hwy -- 13 Crashes
This intersection complex (including nearby Townsquare Blvd and White Lake Rd approaches to Old US
23) has 13 crashes with a 15.4% injury rate.

- 8 intersection-involved crashes (61.5%)
- Stop sign controlled (9 crashes) with 2 at a stop with flashing beacon
- 77% occur in daylight
- 54% on dry roads; ice/snow conditions in 23% of crashes
- No drug involvement
- No single-vehicle lane departures -- these are multi-vehicle intersection crashes

Key Pattern: This is primarily an intersection compliance problem. Drivers are failing to yield at the stop
sign, resulting in angle/turning crashes. The high percentage of daylight, dry-road crashes indicates
visibility and road conditions are NOT the issue -- driver behavior is.

#3: White Lake Rd @ Runyan Lake Rd -- 10 Crashes (Highest Injury
Rate)
This intersection has the highest injury rate at 40% (4 of 10 crashes result in injury). It is almost
exclusively an intersection-crash location with 90% of crashes being intersection-involved.

- 9 of 10 crashes are intersection-involved (90%)
- 40% injury rate -- highest of all top 5 locations
- Stop sign controlled (8 crashes), 1 with flashing beacon
- 70% occur in daylight


- Only 1 single-vehicle lane departure
- Predominantly multi-vehicle angle/turning crashes

Key Pattern: The extremely high intersection-involvement rate and injury rate suggest this is a
high-energy intersection where vehicles are entering at speed. The stop sign compliance issue combined
with the approach speed on White Lake Rd creates severe-outcome collisions.

#4: Linden Rd @ Hogan Rd -- 9 Crashes (1 Fatal)
This location is the only top-5 site with a fatal crash. It has 9 crashes with a combined injury/fatal rate of
33.3%.

- 5 intersection-involved crashes (55.6%)
- 1 fatal crash (drug-involved)
- Stop sign controlled (5 crashes with documented traffic control)
- 67% occur in daylight on dry roads
- 33% in dark/unlighted conditions
- Drug involvement in 1 crash (11.1%)

Fatal Crash Detail: The fatal crash occurred in dark/unlighted conditions on a dry road in clear weather.
Drug involvement was confirmed. This single crash significantly elevates the severity profile of this
intersection.

#5: Old US 23 Hwy @ Faussett Rd -- 9 Crashes
This corridor location on Old US 23 near Faussett Rd has a distinct crash profile dominated by darkness
and lane departure -- markedly different from the other top locations.

- Only 3 intersection-involved crashes (33.3%) -- primarily a corridor problem
- 67% occur in dark/unlighted conditions -- highest darkness rate of all top 5
- 5 single-vehicle lane departures (55.6%) -- highest lane departure rate
- 33.3% injury rate
- Drug involvement in 1 crash (11.1%)
- Speed limit: 55 mph

Key Pattern: This is fundamentally different from locations #1-4. Rather than an intersection compliance
issue, this is a speed + darkness + curves problem. Vehicles are departing the roadway at night on the
high-speed Old US 23 corridor. This location warrants engineering countermeasures (delineation, rumble
strips) rather than enforcement.

                                           Key Findings
1. White Lake Rd is the dominant crash corridor -- locations #1 and #3 combined account for 41
   crashes (21.8% of all secondary road crashes)
2. Stop sign compliance is the #1 factor -- 4 of 5 top locations are stop-sign controlled intersections
   with multi-vehicle angle crashes in daylight/dry conditions
3. Most crashes occur in ideal conditions -- daylight, dry roads, clear weather. This indicates DRIVER
   BEHAVIOR, not environmental factors, as the primary cause
4. Old US 23 Hwy @ Faussett Rd is the exception -- dominated by nighttime single-vehicle lane
   departures requiring engineering solutions


5. The fatal crash (Linden Rd @ Hogan Rd) involved drugs and darkness
6. Overall injury rate of 20.2% across all 188 secondary road crashes

                                     Recommendations
Enforcement Priorities
1. Targeted stop sign enforcement at White Lake Rd @ Runyan Lake Rd (40% injury rate) and Center
    Rd @ Old US 23 Hwy (highest volume intersection crashes)
2. Speed enforcement on White Lake Rd corridor approaches to intersections
3. Impaired driving patrols on Old US 23 and Linden/Hogan area during evening/night hours

Engineering Referrals (Livingston County Road Commission)
1. White Lake Rd @ Runyan Lake Rd -- Signal warrant study recommended given 40% injury rate
   and 10 crashes in 2.4 years
2. White Lake Rd corridor -- Intersection advance warning signs and rumble strips on approaches
3. Old US 23 Hwy @ Faussett Rd -- Enhanced delineation, reflective curve signs, edge line rumble
   strips for nighttime lane departure prevention
4. Center Rd @ Old US 23 Hwy -- Intersection sight-line evaluation; consider enhanced stop sign
   visibility (larger sign, reflective border)

                       CIVIL INFRACTIONS ORDINANCE NO. 39C


An Ordinance adopted pursuant to Public Act 246 of 1945 and Act 236 of 1961 as amended to
establish the office of Ordinance Enforcement Officer, establish procedures for the enforcement of
general ordinances and to repeal or amend all ordinances or parts of ordinances of the Township
which conflict with the provisions hereof.

SECTION 1: TITLE

This ordinance shall be known and cited as the Tyrone Township Civil Infractions Ordinance.

SECTION 2: ORDINANCE REPEAL

Tyrone Township Ordinance No. 39 as amended by Tyrone Township Ordinance No. 39A and 39B
is hereby repealed in its entirety and declared to be of no further force and effect; provided,
however, the repeal does not affect or negate any act done, offense committed or liability, penalty
or fine incurred prior to the time of the repeal.

Tyrone Township Ordinance 22 is also repealed.

SECTION 3: DEFINITIONS

       A.      Act: Refers to Michigan Public Act 236 of 1961 amended by Public Act 12 of 1994.

       B.      Municipal Civil Infraction: A civil infraction involving a violation of an
               ordinance.

       C.      Municipal Civil Infraction Action: A civil action in which the defendant is alleged
               to be responsible for a municipal civil infraction.

       D.      Municipal Civil infraction citation: A written complaint prepared by a person
               authorized by Tyrone Township to do so or a member of law enforcement
               authorized to enforce laws and ordinances in Tyrone Township, Livingston County.

       E.      Municipal civil infraction determination: One of the following:

               1.     An admission of responsibility by the accused;


         2.     An admission of responsibility by the accused with an explanation;

         3.     A determination of responsibility based upon a preponderance of the
                evidence at an informal or formal hearing;

         4.     A default judgment issued against the accused for failure to appear;

         5.     A finding of not responsible;

SECTION 4: ORDINANCE ENFORCEMENT OFFICER

         and three (3) copies of a citation.

SECTION 6: MUNICIPAL CIVIL INFRACTION CITATIONS: CONTENTS

     A.   A municipal ordinance citation shall contain:

          1.      The name and address of the alleged violator,

          2.      The municipal civil infraction alleged,

          3.      The place for appearance shall be the 53rd District Court, Judicial Center,
                  204 S. Highlander Way, Howell, MI 48843.

          4.      The telephone number of the court,

          5.      A statement that the alleged violator should contact the court within 10 days
                  for a court date.

     B.   The citation shall inform the alleged violator that he or she may:

          1.      Admit responsibility for the municipal civil infraction by mail, in person, or
                  by representation, at or by the time specified for appearance.

          2.      Admit responsibility for the municipal civil infraction "with explanation"
                  by mail by the time specified for appearance or, in person, or by
                  representation.

          3.      Deny responsibility for the municipal civil infraction by:

                i.       Appearing in person for an informal hearing before a judge or
                         district court magistrate, without the opportunity of being
                         represented by an attorney, unless a formal hearing before a judge is
                         requested by the Township.

                    ii.       Appearing in court for a formal hearing before a judge, with the
                              opportunity of being represented by an attorney.

       C.      The citation shall also inform the alleged violator that:

               1.     A hearing shall be an informal hearing unless a formal hearing is requested
                      by the alleged violator or the Township.

               2.     At an informal hearing the alleged violator must appear in person before a
                      judge or district court magistrate, without the opportunity of being
                      represented by an attorney.

               3.     At a formal hearing the alleged violator must appear in person before a
                      judge with the opportunity of being represented by an attorney.

               4.     Failure to appear within the time specified in the citation or at the time
                      scheduled for a hearing or appearance is a misdemeanor and will result in
                      entry of a default judgment against the alleged violator on the municipal
                      civil infraction.

       D.      Each citation shall be numbered consecutively and shall be in a form approved by

MUNICIPAL ORDINANCE VIOLATIONS BUREAU.

The Tyrone Township Municipal Ordinance Violations Bureau, the Bureau, is established
according to the provisions of Tyrone Township Civil Infractions Ordinance Number 39A as
amended pursuant to 1994 Public Act No.12. The purpose of this Bureau is to collect the funds
from persons given a Civil Infraction Violation for non-compliance with a Tyrone Township
Ordinance enacted. by the Township to regulate activities within the Township.

The Bureau is limited to collecting the money and cannot act in any way to reduce or waive any
portion of the fine. In addition to the fine the Bureau is required to include an administrative fee
of $ to cover the cost of the accounting required to maintain a record of money received

by the Township.

The individual cited and paying the fine admits responsibility for the citation and acknowledges
the condition causing the non-compliance has been corrected. It is further acknowledged that the
Township’s Ordinance Enforcement Off icier (OEO) will re-inspect the property to verify the
non-compliant issue has been corrected. If, in the opinion of the OEO, the infraction has not
been corrected the responsible party may be subject to additional violation and or citation tickets
as prescribed in Ordinance 39A.

not intended to be an exact copy of the articles of the ordinance but provides a general flow of

June 3, 2026

To:      Tyrone Township Board

Re:      Lake Shannon HOA Fireworks

Based upon the review of the application named above and inspection of the proposed site for the
fireworks display for June 27, 2026, the Fire Department recommends approval of the request
contingent upon the following:

      1) Ace Pyro, LLC is responsible for ensuring that NFPA 1123 is followed.
      2) Based upon vegetation / weather conditions, the fire department may have a brush truck at the
         launching site with sufficient personnel to handle any fire emergencies that may arise.
      3) No one will be allowed within 100 yards of the launch site during the display.
      4) Smoking and open flame will not be permitted within 50 feet of the launch site. Exception:
         Items required for the ignition of the displays by trained and certified professionals.
      5) The display will not begin until all persons are off the lake. The display will be stopped in the
         event anyone ventures onto the lake, and the display will not be resumed until the lake is
         cleared.
      6) It is understood that the fire department will not allow the display to commence or continue if
         these conditions are not met, or in the event of conditions arising, such as adverse weather or
         faulty firing of the displays, or any condition which this department deems unsafe for the
         display to continue.
      7) Insurance as agreed upon by the Tyrone Township Board.
      8) Final approval and permitting by the Tyrone Township Board.

The Lake Shannon HOA fireworks display continues to be a spectacular addition to events while
keeping the safety and welfare of all spectators, workers, and buildings first on their agenda. Should
you have any other questions, or we can be of further assistance, please call.




Fire Marshal
