74    2026 04-06          RESOLUTION OPPOSING HOUSE BILLS 5529, 5530, 5531,
5532, 5581, 5582, 5583, 5584, AND 5585
Moved by Glisman. Seconded by McCarthy.
Adopt the Resolution opposing House Bills 5529, 5530, 5531, 5532, 5581, 5582, 5583, 5584,
and 5585.

                   RESOLUTION OPPOSING HOUSE BILLS 5529, 5530,
                     5531, 5532, 5581, 5582, 5583, 5584, AND 5585

WHEREAS, the City of Lapeer is a municipal corporation organized under the laws of the State
of Michigan and operates under the principles of home rule authority as provided by the
Michigan Constitution and applicable statutes; and

WHEREAS, home rule is a foundational principle in Michigan that grants local governments
the authority to adopt and enforce zoning ordinances, land use regulations, and development
standards tailored to the unique needs of their communities; and

WHEREAS, the Michigan Legislature has introduced House Bills 5529, 5530, 5531, 5532,
5581, 5582, 5583, 5584, and 5585 (collectively, the “Bills”); and

WHEREAS, these Bills would impose statewide mandates on local zoning and land use
regulations, including but not limited to lot sizes, dwelling unit types, setbacks, parking
requirements, accessory dwelling units, and procedural requirements for development review;
and

WHEREAS, these Bills represent a significant preemption of local zoning authority, as they
would override locally adopted ordinances and eliminate the ability of municipalities to regulate
land use based on local conditions, infrastructure capacity, and community character; and

WHEREAS, Michigan law has historically recognized that zoning and land use decisions are
best determined at the local level, where elected officials are accountable to residents and
possess direct knowledge of community needs; and

WHEREAS, statewide zoning mandates that preempt local authority undermine the ability of
municipalities to implement comprehensive plans, manage growth responsibly, and protect the
health, safety, and welfare of their residents; and
                                Lapeer City Commission Regular Minutes
                                              April 6, 2026
                                                Page 2


WHEREAS, according to municipal organizations across Michigan, including the Michigan
Municipal League and the Michigan Townships Association, these Bills would “strip locally
elected officials of their decision-making authority” and constitute a “clear override from Lansing
on local voices and voter-backed decisions”.

WHEREAS, the proposed legislation would impose one-size-fits-all standards that fail to
account for the diversity of Michigan communities, including differences in infrastructure,
geography, density, and long-term planning goals.

WHEREAS, the City of Lapeer is concerned that these Bills would undermine locally developed
zoning standards, reduce public input, and diminish the effectiveness of community-driven
planning processes; and

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the City Commission of the City of Lapeer hereby
strongly opposes House Bills 5529, 5530, 5531, 5532, 5581, 5582, 5583, 5584, and 5585 as
an unlawful and inappropriate preemption of local home rule authority.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City of Lapeer urges the Michigan House of
Representatives and Senate to reject these Bills and to preserve the constitutional and
statutory authority of local governments to regulate land use and zoning within their
jurisdictions; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City of Lapeer respectfully requests that any future
housing policy be developed in collaboration with local governments, respecting home rule
authority and allowing flexibility for communities to address housing needs in a manner
consistent with local conditions; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City Clerk is directed to transmit a copy of this
Resolution to the Michigan House of Representatives, the bill sponsors, the Michigan Senate,
the Governor of Michigan, and the City’s state legislative delegation; and

BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED that the City of Lapeer reaffirms its commitment to responsible,
locally driven planning and opposes any legislation that undermines the principles of home rule
and local control.

Dated this 6th day of April 2026.

ROLL CALL VOTE:
Ayes:     Glisman, McCarthy, Petrie, Atwood, Brady.
Nays:     None.
MOTION CARRIED. RESOLUTION DECLARED ADOPTED.

   RESOLUTION URGING THE MICHIGAN SENATE AND GOVERNOR TO APPROVE
                   HOUSE BILL 5537 TO BAN KRATOM

WHEREAS, the City of Lapeer is committed to protecting the public health, safety, and welfare
of its residents; and

WHEREAS, kratom (Mitragyna speciosa) is a psychoactive substance with opioid-like effects
that has been associated with addiction, dependency, adverse health effects, and, in some
cases, death; and

WHEREAS, kratom products are frequently sold without standardized dosing, manufacturing
oversight, or age restrictions, creating significant risks to consumers, including minors; and

WHEREAS, local units of government have limited authority to effectively regulate emerging
and potentially harmful substances without clear and consistent direction from the State of
Michigan; and

WHEREAS, Michigan House Bill 5537 (HB 5537) has been passed by the Michigan House of
Representatives and is now under consideration by the Michigan Senate; and

WHEREAS, HB 5537 would establish a uniform statewide prohibition on kratom, promoting
consistency in enforcement and supporting broader public health and substance abuse
prevention efforts;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that the City Commission of the City of Lapeer hereby
urges the Michigan Senate to adopt House Bill 5537; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the City Commission urges the Governor of the State of
Michigan to sign HB 5537 into law upon its passage by the Legislature; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the City Clerk is directed to transmit copies of this resolution
to the Governor, members of the Michigan Senate and House of Representatives representing
the City of Lapeer, and relevant statewide organizations including the Michigan Municipal
League; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the City of Lapeer encourages other municipalities to take
similar action in support of protecting public health and safety.

Dated this 6th day of April 2026.

ROLL CALL VOTE:
Ayes:     McCarthy, Petrie, Atwood, Brady, Glisman.
Nays:     None.

Lapeer Cruise on Nepessing Street
Presented by the Lapeer Main Street DDA

Event Description:

The Lapeer Cruise on Nepessing St. is a weekly car show that has become an engaging
community event. The event is designed to bring more traffic to Historic Downtown Lapeer,
showcase our downtown as a whole and create increased sales for dining and retail
establishments. and showcase our downtown as a whole. Monday's were chosen as they
are generally a slower business traffic day.

Event Analysis:

At this time, visitor data for 2025 has not been received. However we are anticipating
receiving that information soon. Once we have updated numbers we will updated the traffic
counts and visitor information below.

Using visitor Data provided by Main Street America, using Placer.Ai software, In 2023 and
2024, Mondays were the second slowest day of the week in Historic Downtown Lapeer. In
2023, Historic Downtown Lapeer had 147,643 visitors on Mondays. In 2024, Historic Downtown
Lapeer had 155,157. There was an increase of 7514 visitors to Historic Downtown Lapeer in 2024.

Mondays with Car Cruise had 60,592 visitors over a 17 week period in 2023 and 60767 over a16
week period in 2024. There is an increase of 175 visitors in 2024 with one less week.

The average daily visitor count on Mondays in 2023 was 2839 people, and the average daily visitor
count in 2024 was 2927. An increase of 88 people.

In 2023, Mondays with the Car Cruise had an average daily visitor count of 1077 more visits than
Mondays without the Car Cruise.

In 2024, Mondays with the Car Cruise had an average daily visitor count of 1247 more visits than
Mondays without the Car Cruise.

This is the largest average daily visitor count increase of any weekly event that takes place in
Historic Downtown Lapeer.

                    In Recognition of Sergeant Jason Miner
WHEREAS, Officer Jason Miner officially began his career with the City of Lapeer Police Department on
April 9, 2001, as a Patrolman; he rose through the ranks, serving as Patrolman I in 2006, Patrolman II in
2011, Patrolman III in 2016, Sergeant II in 2020, and Sergeant III in 2021; and

WHEREAS, Sergeant Miner, while performing his duties in service to the citizens of the City of Lapeer,
received the Lapeer City Police Award for Bravery in 2025 for putting himself in peril while removing
occupants from an overturned vehicle underwater. He also received two Meritorious Service awards, 2002
for stopping a fire from spreading in an apartment complex, saving lives and property, and the second in
2006 for apprehending subjects involved in a crime ring that was stealing snowmobiles. Additionally, he
earned two Officer of the Year Awards, three Departmental Commendations, a Life Saving Citation Award,
and five Mothers Against Drunk Driving Awards for his efforts to remove drunk drivers from the roadway.
These recognitions include formal supervisory commendations, all while safeguarding lives, and protecting
the innocent in the City of Lapeer.

WHEREAS, Sergeant Miner has devoted his time and effort to training and education, which includes
graduating from Northwestern University School of Police Staff and Command in 2022. He was trained in
Barricaded Gunman/Active Shooter Training, Emergency Management, Forensic Science Training,
Advanced Narcotics Training, Terrorism & Bomb Recognition, and numerous other in-service training
sessions; and

WHEREAS, Sergeant Miner has a broad range of skills, including his detective assignment to the Thumb
Narcotic Unit from 2006 to 2009 and again from 2013 to 2015. He earned his TNU nickname of “Recon.”
Sergeant Miner brought expertise in police administration, along with a wealth of experience from proudly
serving this community for over 25 years, and

WHEREAS, Sergeant Miner will retire on April 28, 2026, after giving conscientious, responsible, and
professional service to the citizens of the City of Lapeer, leaving those in the City proud to have known him
and served alongside him.

THEREFORE, LET IT BE KNOWN, that I, Mayor Jeramy Hing, along with the Lapeer City Commission,
wish to honor and express deep appreciation to Sergeant Jason Miner for his dedication and devotion to

                       ARBOR DAY PROCLAMATION

WHEREAS, in 1872 J. Sterling Morton proposed to the Nebraska Board of Agriculture
that a special day be set aside for the planting of trees; and

WHEREAS, this holiday, called Arbor Day, was first observed with the planting of more
than one million trees; and

WHEREAS, Arbor Day is now observed throughout the nation and the world; and

WHEREAS, trees can reduce the erosion of our precious topsoil by wind and water, cut
heating and cooling costs, moderate the temperature, clean the air, produce oxygen,
and provide habitat for wildlife; and

WHEREAS, trees are a renewable resource giving us paper, wood for our homes, fuel
for our fires, and countless other wood products; and

WHEREAS, trees in our city increase property values, enhance the economic vitality of
business areas, and beautify our community; and

WHEREAS, trees, wherever they are planted, are a source of joy and spiritual renewal,

THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that I, Jeramy Hing, Mayor of the City of Lapeer, and
the Lapeer City Commission do hereby declare Thursday, April 23, 2026, as Arbor Day
in the City of Lapeer and urge all citizens to celebrate Arbor Day and to support efforts
to protect our trees and woodlands and urge all citizens to plant trees to gladden the
heart and promote the well-being of this and future generations.

to fulfill the direction of the Commission, but we also do not want to apply for a grant for work
that the Commission might ultimately reject. As I understand it, grant giving authorities don’t
like to have their grants rejected by the requesting party, may take a dim view of such actions
and could impact our ability to get other grants for several years.


In addition to the issues regarding the grant application, in order for the City to pursue the
installation of even a temporary safety island would require preparatory work, including an ADA
accessible ramp on the south side of the road and permanent signage which would have to be
installed ahead of time, at City expense.


Staff have been pursuing this pedestrian safety island in order to make the crossing from
Cramton Park into the downtown area safer, numerous studies have shown road diets and

directed to pursue road diet and pedestrian crossing design work (August 18th, 2025) as part

   • The City of Lapeer has obtained a grant for about $363,000 from Small Urban Funding,

As a part of the above referenced traffic study, ROWE prepared a concept plan (attached)
depicting the option to construct a new pedestrian island and marked crosswalk to the west of the
Calhoun Street/Bentley Street intersection. The HSIP Streamlined Application provides an
opportunity to request funding for a relatively low-cost improvement such as this, wherein 90
percent of the construction cost would be potentially paid for through the grant. Knowing that the
use of federal funds brings with it additional standards, requirements, and paperwork that can
drive up costs, often obtaining low value grants of this sort can become a questionable strategy.
However, in this case, since the city already plans to rehabilitate Genesee Street using federal
funding, being successful at obtaining additional funding for this pedestrian improvement would
represent a good value (roughly estimated at $40,000).

You may recall that this pedestrian island has been presented to the City Commission previously.
Some Commissioners expressed concern that installing an island as proposed could cause an
issue during those times when the left turn lane traffic queue is long, essentially filling all available
space in the left turn lane from M-24 to the proposed island location. While this condition has been
known to occur, it is rare and is difficult to predict. (The space that would remain for storage is 860
feet, enough to store over 40 passenger sized left turning vehicles.) ROWE was asked to study this
phenomenon in more detail. In order to do so, traffic counting equipment would be set up to collect
data to better determine how traffic functions under this condition. However, this condition happens
so rarely, and its occurrence is difficult to predict, making further study not practical.

                                    City of Lapeer
                               Lapeer County, Michigan

                           DATA CENTER MORATORIUM

      A Resolution Imposing a Temporary Moratorium for a period of twelve (12)
      months on the establishing, permitting, consideration, approval, location,
      construction, and/or installation of any data centers in the City of Lapeer

        WHEREAS, “data centers” are large facilities that house IT infrastructure—such as
thousands of computer servers, data storage systems, and networking equipment—to
store, process, and distribute massive amounts of data and are known to use significant
water and electricity utility resources and have the potential for creating noise, light and
other types of environmental pollution; and

      WHEREAS, the City currently has not established, approved, and implemented
zoning standards specific for data centers; and

       WHEREAS, it has recently come to the attention of the City Commission that the
City should study and review whether data centers require additional local regulation
within the City of Lapeer, and whether absent such additional local regulation, data
centers may interfere with other land uses, and may have substantial impacts on the
environment, public health, safety, and welfare; and

       WHEREAS, the City has a legitimate public purpose in assessing the regulation
of the establishment and use of data centers within the City; and

       WHEREAS, the City Commission is desirous of assessing its regulations to
implement an appropriate regulatory approach for addressing potential impacts of data
centers while ensuring the productive and healthy development of data centers within
the City, which may include designated areas where data centers may be located within
the City; and

       WHEREAS, the City Commission wishes to consider and assess amendments to
its current regulations regarding the use of data centers that are consistent with all
applicable state and federal laws, including applicable judicial precedent; and

        WHEREAS, the time to consider and assess regulation of data centers will take a
significant amount of time and the work of at least two City boards; and


                                            2


       WHEREAS, the City is legitimately invested in ensuring that data centers do not
interfere with other land uses, or have substantial negative impacts on the environment,
public health, safety, and welfare; and

     WHEREAS, it is a routine and an appropriate method to enact a reasonable and
temporary moratorium to consider enactment of any amendment; and

       WHEREAS, the City Commission finds that it is necessary and reasonable to
establish a temporary moratorium on the establishment and use of data centers within
the City for twelve (12) months or until the City has enacted a regulatory ordinance
setting forth the regulations for the establishment and use of data centers within the
City.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, the City Commission states as follows:

       Section 1: Moratorium. The City hereby temporarily enacts a blanket prohibition
on the establishment and use of data centers, including without limitation, approval of
such use within the City, pending further study and enactment of City regulation
addressing the same.

        Section 2: Term; Renewal. This Moratorium shall terminate and be of no further
effect twelve (12) months from the effective date of this Moratorium or upon further
Township Board action.

       Section 3: Waiver. A waiver of the moratorium may be granted upon the request
of an applicant if the City Commission finds that a waiver would not impact the health,
safety, and welfare of the City and is deemed reasonable by the City Commission in
exercising its legislative authority over land use regulation to remain consistent with the
City Zoning Ordinance.

        Section 4: Direction to Boards and Staff. The City Commission directs the
Planning Commission and City staff to begin the work of identifying and
recommending appropriate zoning regulations which would permit data center type
land uses within the City of Lapeer while protecting the environment, public health,
safety, and welfare of the citizens of Lapeer.

       Section 5: Effective Date. This Moratorium shall take effect immediately or as
required under the City’s Charter

                            CITY OF LAPEER POLICE DEPARTMENT



TO:       Mike Womack, City Manager

FROM:     Jeremy Howe, Chief of Police

DATE:     04/06/26

RE:       Monthly Report; March 1 – March 31, 2026

In the month of March 2026, our Police Department Officers responded to 1104 calls for service. Of these
1104 calls for service, 207 or approximately 14% were criminal activity reports, of which 52 required
submissions to the Michigan Incident Crime Reporting System (MICRS). Our officers made 22 arrests in
March, 4 felony crimes and 18 arrests were misdemeanor crimes.

This month our officers made 251 traffics stops, issued 26 citations, and gave 225 verbal warnings. Officers
also handled 34 vehicle crashes.

There were no overdose cases reported in the month of March.

Department members attended 116 hours of advanced police training which included the following: Women
in Law Enforcement Conference, ICS 400, Accreditation Management Training, Community Intervention
training, Crime Prevention for Business and Reading People.

Code Enforcement for the month of March totaled 81 contacts. The majority of the violations were signs,
blight, sidewalk snow and miscellaneous.

The month of March also had 163 hours of overtime. Most of the overtime was contributed to holiday pay,
investigations, active shooter training, court and meetings.

The City of Lapeer Police Department investigated a homicide that led to the arrest of Brandon Edwards.
Officers on scene did an incredible job locating the Edwards a short time later and took him into custody
without incident. Our Detective Bureau investigated the homicide and was able to secure homicide – felony
murder charges from the Prosecutor’s Office. I could not be prouder of the work that was demonstrated by
our officers that night!
