No. We are establishing clear standards and safeguards to help ensure that any future data center development is done responsibly, with careful consideration of its potential impacts on our community. We’re focused on protecting Rockford’s interests, addressing community concerns and making informed decisions about our city’s future.

A non-home-rule community - like Rockford - cannot simply impose an outright, permanent ban on all data centers without risking significant legal challenges. That’s why we have drafted regulations that address environmental impacts, neighboring properties, infrastructure, natural resources and public safety.

What are the next steps for the draft regulations?

The Codes and Regulations Committee will vote to approve advancing the public process through the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA). Once approved by committee, the full City Council will vote to approve advancing the public process through the ZBA.
The draft regulations advance to the ZBA for a public hearing, where community members are invited to share feedback.
After the public hearing, the draft regulations return to the Codes and Regulations Committee for further review and approval.
After approval at Committee, the draft regulations will go to the full City Council for review and final approval.

Did the moratorium halt the potential data center Monarch is exploring?

No. The Monarch property was annexed in 2008 – 2011 and is zoned I-2 (General Industrial), which allows a variety of industrial uses, including a high tech industry and data centers, under the property's existing zoning. Because those development rights were established through a legally approved annexation agreement in 2008-2011, the City is obligated to honor the terms of that agreement and cannot retroactively change them.

How do new regulations impact development of the site?

A number of regulations proposed are general regulations that impact all properties whether they have an annexation agreement or not. Additionally, annexation agreements are valid for a period of 20 years, after that time frame, development rights that are inconsistent with existing City regulations lapse.

How does the existing zoning ordinance treat properties under an annexation agreement?

The Rockford Zoning Ordinance states:

10-007-D CONFLICT WITH ANNEXATION AGREEMENTS OR COURT ORDERS

This zoning ordinance is not intended to interfere with, abrogate, or annul the terms and conditions contained in an annexation agreement between the City of Rockford and a property owner. Where there is a conflict between the annexation agreement and the terms of the zoning ordinance, the annexation agreement will govern so long as such annexation agreement is in full force and effect. Unless otherwise expressly provided, where this ordinance is amended to establish a higher standard for all new development including without limitation residential character standards, landscaping, screening, parking requirements such standards will apply to properties that are the subject of annexation agreements. The final order of a court that is in full force and effect will govern over the terms of the zoning ordinance.

10-007-E. CONFLICTS BETWEEN ANNEXATION AGREEMENTS AND REVIEW PROCEDURES

This zoning ordinance is not intended to interfere with, abrogate, or annul permitted uses within zoning districts approved within an annexation agreement at the time or the execution of said agreement. If the terms of Section 30-004 are more restrictive than the permitted uses of the zoning districts approved within an annexation agreement, the mandatory Planned Unit Development requirements of Section 30-004 of this ordinance shall not apply throughout the term of said agreement.

Was the moratorium resolution a smokescreen?

No. We've been clear from the beginning that the Monarch property was annexed and zoned in 2008-2011, and those legal rights must be honored. The proposed moratorium was intended to address future development opportunities by giving the City time to review best practices and consider whether additional regulations are appropriate. It's about using the authority the City has today, while recognizing the legal commitments that already exist, and planning for the future.
