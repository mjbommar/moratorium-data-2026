                          City of Troy -Planning Commission Public Hearing (1)
                            (Solar Special Use 1515 Formosa - PC Rec 2026-23)
                                             August 13, 2026
A. Call to Order with the Pledge of Allegiance: The meeting was held in Council Chambers of City Hall, 116 E. Market
   Street, Troy, Illinois, 62294. Chairman Compton called the meeting to order at 6:00 p.m., followed by the Pledge of
   Allegiance.
B. Roll Call in Alphabetical Order: Present: Danielle Bogue, Liz Compton, Rich Curtis, Rachel Lybarger, Shane McBride,
   Eric Patton and Matt Reiter. Absent: Mike Niermann. Other officials present: Building & Zoning Director Ann Byrd,
   City Engineer Tom Cissell, City Administrator Jay Keeven, Exec. Clerk Heather Klueter, and Building & Zoning Clerk
   Andrea Lambert.
C. Chairman's Presentation and Discussion of petition from Kent Higgins, on behalf of H3 Farm Enterprise, LLC, for a Special Use Permit to
   allow the construction and operation of a 5 -megawatt Community Solar Energy Facility on property located at 1515 Formosa Road, currently
   zoned I-1 Light Industry. Solar and wind systems require a Special Use to evaluate the entirety of the circumstances affecting the particular
   property in the context of the existing and intended future use of the property (PC Rec 2026-23).
D. Comments from the City Engineer and/or City Staff
   *If this special use is recommended, it will be forwarded to the City Council for its first reading on August 17 th or 31st, 2026.
   City Engineer Tom Cissell reviewed the proposed 5-megawatt community solar facility, which would be located in the
   I-1 Light Industrial District west of Formosa Road and south of an existing tree line. He explained that the project
   requires a Special Use Permit and two public hearings, and reviewed the property’s zoning, relationship to the City’s
   Future Land Use Map, and anticipated visual impact. Cissell also reviewed applicable State law, including provisions
   effective June 1, 2026, noting that while municipalities have limited authority to prohibit solar energy facilities, the
   City retains the ability to evaluate individual Special Use Permit requests and impose reasonable conditions. He
   recommended that approval be contingent upon final site plan approval by City staff.
   Cissell suggested consideration of a conservation easement to preserve required screening. The applicant was
   receptive to screening recommendations and noted that a neighboring property owner preferred vegetative screening
   rather than a solid fence. Cissell also expressed concerns regarding the limited time available to review the submitted
   materials and difficulties accessing meeting documents through the City’s online portal. Mr. Reiter confirmed that the
   underlying I-1 zoning would remain unchanged. The City’s existing community solar agreement and anticipated utility
   savings were discussed.
E. Comments of Petitioner and/or Representative (or Attorney, if Present)
   (Before the meeting, Cultivate Power/Ironbark provided the commissioners with informational packets.)
 Sean Fuda, Attorney for the Applicant, explained that the State’s “Solar Bill of Rights” limits a municipality’s ability to
   effectively prohibit solar development while allowing reasonable conditions to be imposed. He stated that the project
   has coordinated with the Illinois Department of Natural Resources (IDNR), State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO),
   and Illinois Department of Agriculture, including execution of an Agricultural Impact Mitigation Agreement (AIMA).
   The project also has an interconnection agreement with Ameren.
 Tess Buchanan, Applicant Representative, explained that community solar allows local Ameren customers to subscribe
   to the project and receive credits on their electric bills, with estimated savings of approximately 10%, and that
   enrollment and cancellation are free.
   Representatives of Ironbark/Cultivate presented the proposed approximately 5-megawatt community solar facility,
   including site design, screening, setbacks, drainage, emergency access, decommissioning, utility interconnection, and
   anticipated economic and tax benefits. The overall project area would encompass approximately 16 acres, with
   approximately 13.12 acres fenced, and the facility is expected to generate approximately 9 million kWh annually. The
   fixed-tilt solar panels would be located south of the existing tree line and would not be placed along the frontage road.
   The project would include a seven-foot security fence with vegetative screening, and proposed setbacks would exceed
   City requirements. The facility would not include any battery storage or require municipal water, sewer, or gas service,
   and emergency access and decommissioning plans would be provided. It was added that the developer would be a
   “significant partner in upgrading a section of Formosa Road”.

H. Comments from the Public
Laura Wise - Expressed safety and aesthetic concerns, noting that vehicles travel very fast on Formosa Road and
   questioning the visibility and appearance of the proposed project.
Kevin McKee - Stated that he saves approximately 20% on his energy bills through community solar and noted that his

Derrick Ramert - Questioned whether community solar is a permitted use within the I-1 zoning district.
I. Additional Comments/Rebuttals of Petitioner and/or Representative (or Attorney, if Present)
   The applicant advised that an interconnection application was filed with Ameren in February 2025 and an
   interconnection agreement was signed in August 2025.
J. Discussion by the Planning Commission
   The Commission discussed several issues related to the proposed community solar project, including the discovery
   that the City’s pre-designated solar areas are located within an incompatible utility territory. City Engineer Tom Cissell
   strongly recommended that the property be legally subdivided or properly platted to accommodate the proposed
   solar facility, existing residence, and potential future storage facility. The applicant indicated that subdivision could be
   considered, subject to the landowner’s approval. The Commission also discussed landscaping, preservation of existing
   trees, screening from Formosa Road and neighboring properties, the Osborn development to the south, and
   applicable residential setbacks.
   Several Commission members requested that the matter be tabled to allow additional time for research and review,
   while the applicant requested a definitive decision due to the project’s construction timeline.
 K. Motion to Approve or Deny Applicant’s Request
 Motion: To Approve the Special Use Permit including a 5 MW Community Solar Facility at 1515 Formosa Road, Zoned
 I-1 Light Industrial, with the Following Stipulations: adequate landscaping, that the parcel be platted properly (to
 address separate uses) and include vegetative screening, barriers between the solar facility and adjoining uses,
 Include 150 ft setbacks from residential structures, including the applicable area to the south, any additional
 applicable requirements established during final site plan review and site plan approval.
 The Motion made by Bogue died for lack of a second.
   Staff advised that, although State law limits the City’s ability to deny the project, reasonable conditions may be
   imposed, and recommended that any Special Use Permit approval be contingent upon final approval of the detailed
   site plan by City staff. The Commission discussed the adequacy of the information presented, the need for additional
   research, potential conflicts of interest and abstentions, and its role as a recommending body to the City Council.
 Steven Hartmann - cautioned the Commission about the potential for litigation and emphasized the importance of
   having a strong legal basis for its decision.
   The Commission discussed its role to determine whether the proposed location was appropriate for the Special Use
   Permit and emphasized the importance of adequate screening to prevent the facility from being visible from the road.
   Staff indicated that the Commission should proceed toward a decision rather than indefinitely delay the matter.
 Motion: To Approve the Special Use Permit including a 5 MW Community Solar Facility at 1515 Formosa Road, Zoned
 I-1 Light Industrial, with the Following Stipulations: adequate landscaping, that the parcel be platted properly (to
 address separate uses) and include vegetative screening, barriers between the solar facility and adjoining uses,
 Include 150 ft setbacks from residential structures, including the applicable area to the south, any additional
 applicable requirements established during final site plan review and site plan approval.
 Moved by: Bogue; Seconded by: Curtis. Vote: Motion failed. (Summary: Aye =2, Nay=3, Abstain=2.) (Aye: Compton and
     Curtis. Nay: Bogue, McBride, and Patton. Abstain: Lybarger and Reiter. Absent: Niermann.)
 L. Complete Recommendation
    Chairman Compton completed the recommendation on behalf of the Commission.
 M. State Chairman’s Final Comment: The Planning Commission is a recommending body. The City Council will hear this
   recommendation at the next scheduled meeting to make a final determination. [The City Council meets on the 1st and
