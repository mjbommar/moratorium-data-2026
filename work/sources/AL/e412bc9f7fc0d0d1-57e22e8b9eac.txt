The Birmingham City Council approved a comprehensive zoning ordinance establishing a robust set of 20 protective conditions for hyperscale data centers, positioning Birmingham as a national leader in responsible data center regulation. The ordinance follows months of research, public engagement, and amendments to ensure that all forms of data center development remain compatible with surrounding neighborhoods and aligned with community priorities.

The action comes amid rapid national expansion of large-scale data centers, which have grown in intensity and impact beyond existing ordinances. Birmingham’s strong electrical grid, industrial infrastructure, and land affordability make it an attractive market for data center developers, reinforcing the need for clear, protective local standards.

The ordinance provides some of the strongest safeguards in the state, emphasizing that no single protection stands alone—each condition works in tandem to regulate environmental impacts, energy use, noise, land compatibility, and community notification.

Key Features of the Newly Approved Ordinance include:

Strict location and separation standards, including a 500-foot setback from residential or urban neighborhood districts and a 1,000-foot separation from high-capacity transit facilities.

A 5-acre minimum lot size for eligible sites.

Closed-loop cooling systems to reduce water consumption, ensuring usage no greater than that of a similarly sized office building.

Comprehensive water and stormwater oversight, including identification of water sources, conservation plans, in addition to existing state monitoring.

Prohibitions on onsite power generation using gas turbines or 24/7 diesel generators, while allowing solar or fuel cells for on-site generation and permitting battery storage under emergency-response review.

Noise mitigation requirements, including acoustical walls and pre- and post-construction noise studies.

Detailed site development planning, including screening, landscaping, utility connections, and setback requirements.

Enhanced electrical demand transparency, including peak demand projections, development phases, and utility provider identification.

The ordinance also requires mandatory written notification via certified mail to all property owners within 500 feet for any new or expanded hyperscale data center.
