What Residents Need to Know

What is a data center?

A data center is a facility that houses computer systems and digital infrastructure used to store, process, and deliver data, as well

They power everyday services like cloud storage, streaming, financial transactions, business operations, and artificial intelligence.

Why are they being proposed in Birmingham?

Birmingham's infrastructure, available land, and regional connectivity make it a strong location for digital infrastructure investment and growing demand for these services.

What is the City doing to regulate data centers and protect residents?

The City adopted updated zoning rules to ensure:

Infrastructure readiness
Environmental responsibility
Compatibility with surrounding areas
Clear review and approval procedures
Greater transparency for residents

The zoning rules define the different types of data centers and provide 20 regulatory protections for hyperscale data centers, covering location, environmental safeguards, infrastructure, and community impacts.

No single condition is sufficient alone. No single condition stands alone. Each works together to provide community protection.

It is the strongest ordinance regulating data centers in the Southeast and among the strongest in the nation designed to protect residents and the community.

What the 20 protections do?

Defines location and separation: A hyper scale data center must be 500 feet from residential or urban neighborhood districts; includes all buildings and equipment. Not allowed within 1,000 ft of major transit stations. The minimum lot size is 5 acres.
Electrical Demand: Applicants must disclose peak megawatt demand, development phases, and the total build-out electrical load.
Water Use and Cooling: Requires water-use estimates, cooling system description, identification of water source, efficiency plan, and proof of adequate water/wastewater capacity.
Stormwater: No illicit connections or discharges permitted; violations are subject to enforcement.
Backup Generators: Applicants must disclose generator type, fuel, capacity, schedule, and noise mitigation.
Utility Infrastructure: Must identify all utility providers and required onsite/offsite infrastructure with confirmation of capacity.
Site Development Plan: Must include building layout, equipment placement, setbacks, buffers, circulation, and utilities.
Noise Studies: Pre and post‑construction noise studies required.
Backup Power Operations: Generators are restricted to outages, emergencies, and daytime testing. Generators cannot support grid operations.
On‑Site Power: Only solar or fuel cells may provide routine power; fossil‑fuel systems allowed only for emergencies or testing.
Equipment Screening: All rooftop and ground equipment must be screened from public view.
Battery Storage Safety: Emergency response coordination plans subject to review with Fire Department.
Material Changes: Any increase in facility scale triggers new compliance review.
Phasing: Applicants must disclose ultimate buildout capacity for full review.
Closed‑Loop Cooling: Designed to recirculate water and minimize potable water use for cooling purposes.
Documentation of Closed Loop Cooling System: detailed description of system must be submitted; including system type, parameters, anticipated make up water requirements, and measures to minimize water loss through evaporation, blowdown, or discharge.
Maintenance of Closed Loop Cooling System: Maintained to prevent accumulation of contaminants, scale, biological growth, and heavy metals; any cleaning, flushing, or blowdown activities must be conducted in a way that prevents uncontrolled discharge to stormwater systems, surface waters, or adjacent properties.
Regulations for Cooling Water Discharge: Must be pre-treated to remove heavy metals, chemical additives, and other contaminants associated with cooling operations; cooled to a temperature in compliance with applicable state and local discharge standards.
Lighting Standards: Strict limits on uplight, trespass, and color temperature (max 3000K).
Neighbor Notification: Certified mail notice required for all property owners within 500 feet.
