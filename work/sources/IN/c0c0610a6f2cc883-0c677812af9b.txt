PROPOSED TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON DATA CENTER IMPROVEMENT LOCATION PERMITS


Brian Campbell, Wabash County Planning Commission Director, appeared before the board and reported

that at the Planning Commission meeting on August 6th, the Commission voted to request that the

County Commissioners place a temporary moratorium on the issuance of improvement location permits

for data centers. Mr. Campbell stated that the current Wabash County zoning ordinance contains no

regulations related to data center projects, which constitutes a defect in the ordinance that should be

corrected before permits are issued in any zoning district. Concerns cited include rapidly changing

technology, unresolved environmental impacts, water use, noise, and the need for additional research

and due diligence to protect county residents. Mr. Campbell noted that the process would follow the

same procedure used for solar and wind/carbon capture ordinances, involving formation of a committee,

drafting of an ordinance, and presentation. He stated that the moratorium has an 18-month statutory

limit and that the goal is to have an ordinance in place within one year. He further stated that a draft is

already in progress.

Public comment was invited. No members of the public came forward on this item.

Ross made a motion to approve the moratorium on data centers and proceed with getting the

regulations added to the zoning ordinance. Dawes seconded the motion; motion carried 3-0

ROAD TRANSFER MEMORANDUM OF AGREEMENT WITH THE CITY OF WABASH


The presiding officer introduced the road transfer memorandum of agreement with the City of Wabash,

described as a memorandum of agreement to clarify jurisdiction and maintenance responsibility for

portions of County Road 50 North and County Road 100 West. Under the agreement, the city would

receive approximately 525 feet of 50 North, and the county would take over approximately 2,625 feet

from the city limits north to the intersection of 50 North and 100 West.

Mayor Long stated that the Wabash City Board of Works approved the agreement the previous Monday.

He noted that utility work on 50 North is planned, including a new force main and gravity sewer in the

south lane and a water main with fire hydrants, after which that road will be fully reconstructed. He

stated that all semi-traffic from Ford Meter Box will use Ringle Avenue and will not exit on 100 West, and

that the anticipated employee traffic will be minimal due to staggered shifts.

Brian Ridgeway, expressed concerns regarding trafifc conditions at the intersection of Road 50 and Road

13, describing access onto Road 13 from Road 50 as "nearly impossible," particularly during peak

commute hours. The speaker noted that the volume of traffic at that location would create a significant

hazard if additional access points were introduced during shift-change periods.

Dave Terflinger, County Council Member, who asked whether the agreement had been posted publicly,

what the financial benefit to the county would be, and requested that the item be tabled and a copy of

the agreement provided to the County Council for review before a vote, noting that the council must

Cheryl Ridgeway, who raised questions regarding whether a traffic study had been conducted, the

Ryan and Scott Richardson, the city's representative both noted that they had been coordinating on an

informal basis to maintain the roads and that they were still trying to determine who placed the new

Following discussion, Ross made a motion to table the road transfer memorandum pending further

review. Dawes seconded the motion; motion carried 3-0.

COUNTY HIGHWAY


Ryan Smith, Superintendent reported that North Manchester (Market Street) Bridge: Grouting and poly

sealing are planned. The bridge is anticipated to be open by Labor Day weekend. The full acceptance of

the road will occur after further work through the winter months.

Community Crossings Projects: One small structure is complete, awaiting asphalt. The remaining two

projects, America Road and the county line road structure with Huntington County, are expected to be

completed within 45 days, at which point all Community Crossings projects will be finished.

Discussion was held regarding county responsibility for bridge inspections. The Highway Superintendent

confirmed that the county is responsible for bridge inspections throughout the county, including

structures located within city limits, and that inspection costs are included in the Highway Department's

annual budget. The county has about 157 bridges. The cost of superstructure bridges was noted to be

approximately $4 million, with North Manchester Bridge cited as an example of a project that began as a

$2.2 million funded project and is now running approximately $3.6-$3.7 million.

WABASH COUNTY SHERIFF'S DEPARTMENT


Ryan Baker, Wabash County Sheriff, appeared before the board to request approval of a five-year

agreement with Axon for tasers, body cameras, and car cameras for all sheriff's vehicles. The annual

amount of $106,494.42 has been included in the 2027 budget as advertised. The contract start date is

February 1, 2027. Installation in sheriff's vehicles is planned for January 2027 so that equipment is

operational by the contract start date. The contract includes a provision addressing what occurs if

funding is not approved. Mark Frantz, County Attorney, confirmed he had reviewed the contract. Ross

made a motion to approve the contract with Axon for $106,494.42. Dawes seconded the motion; motion

carried 3-0.

4. GSM / Graves Sheet Metal, DOAS Ductwork Modification Quote; a quote from GSM /

Graves Sheet Metal in the amount of $12,045.13 for modification of the ductwork

serving the DOAS (Dedicated Outdoor Air System) units. The coordinator explained that

in February 2026, the board had approved $29,147.76 for hoods for the DOAS systems;

however, that project was not completed. The proposed modification involves expanding

the ductwork transition to draw air from the full louver opening (79 by 79 inches) rather

than through a concentrated area (18 by 42 inches), which is pulling in excess of 1,000

CFMs and causing rain and snow infiltration. The modification is intended to reduce

CFMs at that point and eliminate the moisture infiltration problem. The quote of

$12,045.13 includes installation and covers both DOAS units. It replaces the previously

approved but unexecuted $29,147.76 project. Greg Harnish confirmed that DOAS

manufacturer does not produce a compatible pre-manufactured product for this

application.

Dave Terflinger, County Council Member, asked for clarification that the $12,045.13

replaces rather than supplements the $29,147.76 previously approved. The coordinator

confirmed that the previously approved amount was never spent and the project was

not completed; and that this new quote takes its place.

Dawes made a motion to approve the GSM / Graves Sheet Metal quote of $12,045.13

for the DOAS ductwork modification. Ross seconded the motion; motion carried 3-0.
