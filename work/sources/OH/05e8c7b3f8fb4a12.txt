Amend Codified Ordinance Section 1066.04 (Park Rules) to include Columbia Woods
fishing rules — Mr. Lukens explained that Administration has recommended opening up the
Columbia Woods pond for fishing and has suggested these rules. These are mostly standard
park rules: Hours are 6 a.m. to dusk; open for fishing from May through October (no ice
fishing); fishing is at your own risk, etc. It also includes age limits for those fishing: fifteen
years or younger because a Fishing License is not required for that age group and children
under twelve years old must be accompanied by an adult. Mr. Lukens said this is excellent
and he is in full support of residents being able to enjoy the outdoors. Mr. DeHarpart said
that when he first came on Council, we were paying to maintain the ponds but there were no
fish at that time. A few years later, he hosted the fishing derby and it was great to see the
kids enjoying the ponds — it’s catch and release so it gives everyone an opportunity to enjoy
this. Mr. DeHarpart said he suggested to Administration that the pond should be open for
fishing so families could come out and enjoy the park. He expressed his appreciation to the
Mayor and Administration for making this happen. Mr. Harbert said this will get more
people enjoying the green space and utilizing the walking path, which fits with the City’s
Comprehensive Plan. Mr. Harbert announced the kickoff for this new park program/asset
will be a fishing night on August 23, 2026. The pond will be open in mid-August after the
City has posted signs outlining the rules. Mr. DeHarpart suggested that Administration may
want to consider getting more trash cans placed around the pond. Mr. Harbert said it’s
always good to leave an area better than how you found it and he encourages his family to
keep parks litter free and nice. Mr. Lukens moved to add Ord. No. 66-2026 to tonight’s
Agenda for a first reading; seconded by Mr. Harbert.

Roll Call: Yes: Lukens, Harbert
No: None
Motion passed unanimously.

election, while others may be addressed in future elections. Mr. Markey reported that Ms.
Macomber reviewed the document for typographical errors, grammar, pronouns, etc. which
are classified as scrivener changes. The Law Director can make those changes because they
are not material changes. Mr. Tousley requested that the scrivener changes be in a different
color such as blue so they can see the difference. Mr. Lukens asked if there are enough
meetings to pass this before the September deadline. Mr. Markey said that if they have the
list of which items will go forward at the July 20, 2026 meeting, legislation can be prepared
for the August meeting, which will allow them to make the September deadline.

Mr. Tousley said Section 5.11 establishes the Zoning Administrator and questioned if this
position should be added to Section 5.09, which is Administrative Department Removals and
includes the Engineer, Community Development, Public Service. Mr. Markey said that he
doesn’t believe the Zoning Administrator gets the Administrative Department removal
protection that is given to the Law Director, Administrative Officer, etc., but will go back to
verify that the position is classified. Mr. Tousley said the Administrative Officer appoints
this position and he questioned if it should be a Mayoral appointment. Mr. Markey said that
either one would be okay.

Mr. Tousley said under Section 3.20 Televised Meetings that “YouTube” was added for
additional viewing and he suggested keeping it generic as “streaming” so we are not tied to
YouTube or a specific service provider. Mr. DeHarpart concurred and said that we may be -
able to host our own. Mr. Markey suggested that when there was a lawsuit, there was
specific language (such as broadcast) and we should keep that language to ensure we are
complying with the court. Mr. DeHarpart suggested digital broadcasting.

Mr. Tousley addressed compensation for the Mayor and the Council, Section 2.09 and
Section 3.08, respectively. He said that the increases for both are probably necessary, but the
statement, “a potential annual increase of up to 2.5% for both the Mayor and Council is
subject to annual review by Council” is a conflict of interest. Mr. Markey agreed and said
there is case law that does not allow Council to ethically vote on their own pay raises.
Council can vote on the pay raise, but it will not go into effect until the next Council’s term.

Mr. Tousley thanked the Charter Review Commission. Mr. Harbert thanked Ms, Mullins for
leading the Commission and appreciates the hard work and time Ms. Burke and the
Commission put into this exercise.

Culvert Repair on SR 21 ~ Mr. Tousley explained the heavy rains are causing these culverts
to fail and this is another emergency culvert repair. It is a 15” crossover on SR 21 from
Kenmore Construction in the amount of $72,450. This is less expensive because Kenmore’s
equipment was already there. Mr. Rorar said that these are galvanized metal culverts that are
rotting from the bottom. When the water goes through, it causes a vortex and the dirt is
moving up and around the culvert, collapsing from within and going up through the asphalt.
Mr. Tousley noted Council became aware of this problem a couple of years ago and have
started to put money aside to address it. Mr. Tousley moved to add Ord. No. 69-2026 to
tonight’s Agenda for a first reading with intent to waive the second and third readings;

ODOT Consent Legislation (Median Cable Barrier SR 21) — Mr. Tousley explained that
ODOT would like to add a Median Cable Barrier to SR 21 from St Rt 585 to the northern
boundary at no expense to the City. Mr. Rorar said that they have done a study that
identified many head-on collisions in that area. He said that this is paid by ODOT and it is a
safety measure, but any future repairs to the cable will fall to the City. Mr. Tousley asked if
these are cheaper to repair than the regular guardrails and Mr. Rorar said he is not sure. Mr.
Lukens said he is concerned about the cost because the City is financially burdened with this
stretch of road and was hoping that there were grants available to assist with this road. Mr.
Harbert said we may know more after the meeting with ODOT and we need to establish
priorities because the water is causing more damage. Mr. DeHarpart understands that safety
is amain concern but asked what about the underlying conditions of SR 21 with the culverts.
He estimates it at over $3.6 million and if we put that money towards the culvert repair
instead of cable, we may be better off. They are both safety issues and he is hoping ODOT
can come up with solutions. Mr. Lukens reiterated that the initial installation will be covered
by ODOT and it is the repairs that may be needed in the future. Mr. Harbert answered
affirmatively and said that they will be working with insurance companies to get reimbursed.
Mr. Harbert said there is also maintenance costs associated with cable versus guardrail. Mr.
Tousley asked if this should be held back and Mr. Rorar encouraged Council to move
forward because of the safety issues. Mr. Tousley moved to add Ord. No. 72-2026 to
tonight’s Agenda for a first reading; seconded by Mr. Eader.

Roll Call: Yes: Tousley, Eader
No: DeHarpart
Motion passed: 2 - 1.
