ORDINANCE NO. 2026-065 A AN ORDINANCE APPROVING A CONTRIBUTION OF $60,000.00

TO MAIN STREET RAVENNA FOR DOWNTOWN REVITALIZATION EFFORTS FOR 2026, AND

DECLARING AN EMERGENCY.

Ms. West proposed an amendment reducing the contribution term from two years to one year by

removing references to 2027 throughout the ordinance.

Mr. Calfee noted additional wording changes were needed in the title, final “whereas” clause,

and Section One.

Ms. West said Council discussions indicated support for a one-year commitment and emphasized

the importance of increased communication and accountability from Main Street Ravenna.

Mr. Marovich said the one-year term avoided overcommitting the city.

Mr. Kairis stated he planned to attend Main Street board meetings as a liaison and report back to

council, while Ms. West added that Main Street representatives agreed to provide quarterly

updates to Council and submit future funding requests earlier in the year.

Ms. Michael said she continued to support Main Street at its previous funding level but opposed

the increase due to current financial concerns.

Ms. Laudato expressed optimism about new leadership within Main Street Ravenna, specifically

mentioning the owner of Books A Go Go and her programming abilities.

Mr. Moskun said he believed the funding amount was too high at this time.

Council unanimously approved an amendment to reduce the funding term to one year.

ORDINANCE NO. 2026-067 AN ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING A TEMPORARY

MORATORIUM ON THE APPROVAL OF NEW DATA CENTERS WITHIN THE CITY OF

RAVENNA FOR A PERIOD OF ONE (1) YEAR TO ALLOW FOR STUDY AND

DEVELOPMENT OF APPROPRIATE REGULATIONSAND DECLARING AN

,

EMERGENCY.

Ms. Laudato said the ordinance reflected lessons learned from other communities and praised

residents who attended previous meetings and helped inform council about the issue. She

referenced national attention Ravenna received for proactively addressing data center concerns

and criticized cryptocurrency mining and data center operations as burdens on community

resources and utilities. Ms. Laudato expressed disappointment after learning a Bitcoin mining

operation had already opened locally without Council review, stating the moratorium would help

prevent similar situations in the future.

Ms. Michael clarified that Council had no authority over the previously established Bitcoin

mining operation because it did not require city utility infrastructure or Planning Commission

approval due to its size. She said reports of the facility already needing additional electrical

service were concerning and questioned whether such operations could increase utility costs for

residents.

Ms. Laudato responded that the moratorium would allow the City to revise zoning and legislative

procedures to ensure future operations of that nature would require council or planning

commission review.

There being no further comments or questions.
