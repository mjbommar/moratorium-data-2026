OPEN TO THE FLOOR: Mr. Tom Murphy, the new Executive Director of the Ada Area
Chamber of Commerce, introduced himself to Council. He relocated from Cherry Hill, New
Jersey, though he has had ties to the area for roughly 30 years through Ohio Northern University;
he and his wife chose Ada as their retirement home. Mr. Murphy also owns a higher-education
consulting business. He invited Council to that Saturday's Made in Ada Festival, running from 5
to 11 pm, and said he looks forward to working with the Village on business and economic
development matters.

Mr. Wagner addressed Council regarding the Viva Marie fire incident report, raising a series of
concerns surrounding the incident and the subsequent investigation. He also referenced a statement
made by a former village administrator in earlier council minutes regarding the status of the
investigation, which he said was inaccurate at the time it was made. Mr. Wagner’s research into
the investigation and accompanying media is available upon request.

STREETS: Mr. Rowe: Following up on the prior committee meeting regarding the new
stop sign/crosswalk area, Mr. Rowe confirmed only one parking spot would be eliminated,
as verified by Joe Hefner; materials have been obtained and work is expected to move
forward once Joe returns from vacation, without need for a separate Council vote. On
audible crosswalks, the Chief reported that these units are offered by only three specialized
companies, at a rough estimate of $3,000 each before a bulk-purchase quote; the Village
would likely need about six units to cover the three signalized intersections at Highland,
Lincoln, and College (two per intersection). The Chief will continue researching grant
funding, including ADA (Americans with Disabilities Act) grants, before a committee
meeting is called. Council also discussed that installing new crosswalks would require
costly curb work for ADA compliance, but that painted stop bars could be installed in the
interim at the new intersection and potentially at the Montford intersection and agreed to
publicize the new traffic pattern through the schools and social media rather than adding
further signage. Mr. Rowe reported his committee's prior meeting ran from 8:13 pm to 8:22
pm and that he would e-mail the official minutes.

are being closed out this week, with reporting expected to be finalized shortly. Ms. Fountaine also
outlined the upcoming budget process, noting plans for a public Finance Committee meeting to be
held to gather community input on the budget; the first budget hearing before Council is targeted
for November 3, 2026.

POLICE CHIEF’S REPORT: Chief Cooper reported 121 calls for service, 13 incident/offence
reports, 0 accidents, one male arrested for kidnapping and strangulation assault, and two other
males cited for disorderly conduct stemming from a bar fight. The Chief reported the door key-fob
access project is expected to be completed the following day, with a fuller update to follow. The
Hardin County Municipal Court has agreed to fund equipment for a citation project (approximately
$2,000 for four printers, included on a recent claims register), consistent with support it has
provided to other municipalities in the county; the Court will also cover installation. Chief Cooper
thanked Council and Village officers for the National Night Out event, reporting approximately
100 people signed in, a Facebook post reach of about 179, and an estimated total attendance of
roughly 1,000 when accounting for additional attendees not formally counted, based on food and
beverages distributed. The Chief noted this year's volunteer/auxiliary turnout was the largest the
department has had.

VILLAGE ADMINISTRATOR’S REPORT: Ms. Smith reported that new office furniture is
scheduled for delivery and installation on Thursday, August 27, 2026. She reported that the
Village's road closure request to ODOT for the upcoming Harvest event has been approved, with
one-way traffic direction in effect from 6:00 am to 6:00 pm along a route from North Street to
College, continuing along Johnson Street. Ms. Smith gave an update on replacing the Village's
utility payment drop box, which is currently located at the Depot Park and is difficult for residents
to access by vehicle; a replacement has already been ordered and delivered and will be installed at
the west end of the Village's parking lot, which will also make daily retrieval easier for staff. Ms.
Smith also discussed the proposed Tyler Time and Attendance payroll module, noting the current
manual time clock process has been a persistent source of payroll errors. The module would replace
manual time-clock data entry across the Village's ESS and Tyler systems, with clock-in/clock-out
available at workstations, tablets, or via a geofenced app. The upfront cost is estimated at $15,000,
which includes a standard 50 hours of implementation; actual hours — and cost — are expected
to be substantially lower since the Village already has the required Tyler version. The ongoing
annual license cost is estimated at approximately $1,500, replacing the Village's roughly $3,000
annual MyCivic app subscription, for a net annual savings even after the new module's cost.

                                      RESOLUTION NO. 2026-109

        A RESOLUTION EXTENDING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE
        ESTABLISHMENT, CONVERSION OF OTHER STRUCTURES OR
        BUILDINGS INTO A DATA CENTER OR CRYPTO CURRENCY MINING
        CENTERS, INCLUDING THE DEVELOPMENT AND CONSTRUCTION OF
        ANY BUILDING, STRUCTURE, USE OR CHANGE OF USE THAT WOULD
        ALLOW DATA CENTERS OR CRYPTO CURRENCY MINING CENTERS
        FOR A PERIOD OF AN ADDITIONAL TWELVE (12) MONTHS FROM THE
        EFFECTIVE DATE OF THIS RESOLUTION IN ORDER TO ALLOW THE
        VILLAGE ADMINISTRATION AND COUNCIL TIME TO REVIEW
        APPLICABLE STATUTES, CODES AND REGULATIONS ALONG WITH THE
        VILLAGE’S CODIFIED ORDINANCES RELATIVE TO SUCH ACTIVITY,
        AND DECLARING AN EMERGENCY.

          WHEREAS, pursuant to the Constitution of the State of Ohio and the Ohio Revised Code
provide that municipalities have the power to enact laws that are for the health, safety, and welfare of
their citizens which may include restricting areas used for certain businesses and trades; and

         WHEREAS, pursuant to the Constitution of the State of Ohio and the Ohio Revised Code,
municipalities have the power to enact planning and zoning laws that are for the health, safety, welfare,
peace, and comfort of the citizens of the municipality, including restricting areas used for business and
trade; and

         WHEREAS, this Council continues to find it to be in the best interest of the health, safety, and
general welfare of the citizens of the Village of Ada to adopt a twelve (12) month moratorium on the
establishment, construction, conversion of other structures for data centers and Crypto currency mining
centers or commencement of construction of data centers and Crypto currency mining centers within the
Village, while it undertakes a review and/or study of the ordinances of the Village of Ada, including
zoning, building, and business regulations sections of the Village’s Codified Ordinances to assure that
such facilities are properly zoned, safe for the community, properly constructed or rehabilitated to
mitigate the potential harmful impact such facilities can have on residential and commercial areas; and

         WHEREAS, data centers and Crypto currency mining centers can be industrial-scale facilities
that could consume significant energy, water, and infrastructure capacity, creating water supply issues,
significant utility demands that the Village may be unable to support, potential adverse impacts on the
environment and Village residents, and potentially conflicting with the Village’s long-term planning
goals; and

        WHEREAS, Village Council wishes to continue to study the impact of data centers and Crypto
currency mining centers on the public health and safety of surrounding neighborhoods and consider and
study the benefits that a data center and Crypto currency mining centers may bring to the community; and

        WHEREAS, a continued moratorium will allow Council the time to enact necessary legislation
to preserve the Village’s character, protect environmental resources, and ensure sustainable land use
practices; and



      NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE VILLAGE OF
ADA, OHIO:
         SECTION 1. That the Council of the Village of Ada hereby extends the imposition of the
initial moratorium for an additional twelve (12) month period on the acceptance, consideration, approval
or authorization of any licenses, permits, permitted uses, special uses, accessory uses or variances that
would enable the granting of any permits, allow establishment, construction, conversion of other
structures into data centers and Crypto currency mining centers, or commencement of construction of data
centers and Crypto currency mining centers within the Village of Ada. Said moratorium includes the
submission, consideration or approval of all applications for licenses, permits, general development plans,
final development plans, special or accessory use permits, occupancy permits, or building permits as well
as approval of any annexation petition which would bring unincorporated property into the Village for
such uses. Data centers and Crypto currency mining centers are defined for this moratorium as AI data
centers, enterprise data centers, managed services data centers, colocation data centers, cloud data centers
or edge data centers and Crypto currency mining centers as well as physical facilities that organizations
use to store their computing machines and related hardware. This moratorium includes construction of
structures that house information technology (IT) systems infrastructure like servers, data storage drives,
and network equipment and construction of accompanying accessory uses such as utilities, external
mechanical equipment, water towers, and security facilities that may be located on the same site of a data
center.

         SECTION 2. “Data Center” and “Crypto Currency Mining Center” means a facility, used for the
storage, management, and dissemination of digital data, which houses computer, telecommunication,
and/or network equipment, systems, servers, appliances, and other associated components related to
digital data operations, and/or facility designed or modified to house networked computers and data and
transaction processing equipment and related infrastructure support equipment, including but not limited
to power and cooling equipment to provide data and transaction processing services, outsource
information technology services, and computer equipment colocation services, as well as server farms,
cloud computing hubs, and colocation centers, including Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs)
designed to validate transactions and secure the Crypto blockchain.

        SECTION 3. That this extension of the initial moratorium shall become effective on the date it is
passed by Council. The moratorium shall remain in effect for twelve (12) months following the date upon
which it became effective unless extended upon a majority vote of the Council, if the Council finds such
continuance is required to allow sufficient time for the administration to complete its study and
recommend what action, if any, the Village of Ada should take to safeguard the public health, safety, and
welfare through the provision of adequate planning, zoning, land use, or other regulatory controls that are
specifically applicable to activities covered under this resolution. Village Council may also rescind the
Moratorium prior to its expiration.

         SECTION 4. That this moratorium suspends and tolls the Village of Ada’s Codified Ordinances,
and any time periods prescribed by law, within which any official or official body of the Village of Ada,
is required to take action upon an application for permit, license, special use permit, building permit, site
plan approval or annexation petition or the like, for data centers and Crypto currency mining centers and
no such application shall be deemed to be approved by the failure of any other public official or official
body to approve or deny said application during the time when this moratorium shall be in effect.

         SECTION 5. That this moratorium suspends and tolls any time periods, prescribed by law, within
which the Zoning Inspector, Planning Commission, Board of Zoning Appeals or any other official or
official body of the Village of Ada, is required to take action, upon an application for a permit and/or in
the site plan review approval process for Data Centers and Crypto currency mining centers, and similarly
suspends any obligations or requirements to act for any such official or official body upon an application
for a permit and/or in the site plan review approval process for Data Centers and Crypto currency mining
centers, during the time when this moratorium shall be in effect.

        SECTION 6. That the Village Administration is again hereby directed to begin a study or review
to recommend what action, if any, the Village of Ada should take to preserve the public health, safety and
welfare through the provision of adequate planning, zoning, land-use, or other regulatory controls that are
specifically applicable to data centers and Crypto currency mining centers.

        SECTION 7. That this moratorium shall not be construed to apply to the following exceptions:

                a. Municipal or emergency service data infrastructure operated by the Village of Ada or
                its authorized agents.

                b. Small-scale server rooms incidental to existing permitted business operations, provided
                they do not exceed 500 square feet and are not primary use of the property.

                c. The manufacturing of components and/or hardware for data centers, including data
                center show rooms incidental to such businesses.

                d. Any other business when prohibited by law.

        SECTION 8. If any section, phrase, sentence, or portion of this resolution is for any reason held
invalid or unconstitutional by any Court of competent jurisdiction, such portion shall be deemed a
separate, distinct, and independent provision, and such holdings shall not affect the validity of the
remaining portions thereof.

          SECTION 9. That the Council of the Village of Ada hereby desires a moratorium in order to
allow Council, and any of its committees, Planning Commission, or any other official or official body of
the Village of Ada, to review applicable Ohio statutes and undertake a review and/or study of the
Ordinances of the Village of Ada, including the planning and zoning regulation sections of the Village’s
Ordinances, and perform further due diligence on Data Centers and Crypto currency mining centers to
determine how Data Centers and Crypto currency mining centers will impact the Village’s infrastructure,
utilities, environment, residents, and long-term planning goals as well as to protect the public health,
safety, and general welfare.

         SECTION 10. That the Village of Ada Council, as it has imposed by the terms herein a
moratorium on new permits for and Data Centers and Crypto currency mining centers for a period of
twelve (12) months from the effective date of this Resolution, again directs the relevant village officials
that no permits or approvals shall be issued for these facilities during any period the moratorium is in
effect.

        SECTION 11. That it is found and determined that all formal actions of the Council of the Village
of Ada concerning and relating to the adoption of this resolution were taken in an open meeting of the
Council and that all deliberations of this Village’s Council and any of its committees that resulted in those
formal actions were in meetings open to the public, in compliance with all legal requirements, including
without limitation, those set forth in 121.22 of the Ohio Revised Code.

        SECTION 12. That this Resolution is deemed to be an emergency measure necessary for the
immediate preservation of the public’s health, peace, safety, and welfare, the specific need being to
prohibit any permits or approvals for new permits for and Data Centers and Crypto currency mining
centers while the Village officials are undertaking review of village ordinances, including zoning and
planning regulations, to determine if addition legislation is necessary and the process for enacting such
legislation is completed. Wherefore, this Resolution shall take effect immediately upon its adoption.

AN ORDINANCE AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO ASSESS STREET LIGHTS
WITHIN THE VILLAGE OF ADA FOR YEAR 2027.


WHEREAS, the Village of Ada, County of Hardin, and State of Ohio, believes that it is in the best
interest of the Village to assess the costs of lighting the public streets, public roads, or public places
of the Village of Ada, as provided by the existing contract now in force and which will be in force
and operation for 2027, and

WHEREAS, it is deemed necessary for the safety and comfort of the citizens of this community
to continue said street lighting system, and

WHEREAS, Section 727.01, et seq., of the Ohio Revised Code provides a method of special
assessment by which such may be assessed against the specially benefitted property, and it is now
determined that each and every lot or parcel of land within the corporation limits of the Village of
Ada is specially benefitted, by reason of a depreciated value, in event of failure of the street lighting
system.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE COUNCIL TO THE VILLAGE OF ADA,
OHIO:

SECTION 1. That the assessment of the cost and expenses of the year 2027, except 1/50th part
thereof, of the lighting of streets, alleys and public places of the Village of Ada and now installed
be and hereby is levied upon all the lands and lots within said Village of Ada and which said
assessments shall be at the rate of seven and one half tenths (.75) mil of the taxation value of the
property assessed appearing on the Tax Duplicate in the County Auditor’s office and not in excess
of the special benefit to said property and are not in excess to any statutory limitations. Village
owned real property shall be exempted from this special light assessment.

SECTION 2. That the 1/50th part of the cost of lighting the streets, alleys and public places of the
Village of Ada shall be paid by said Village of Ada, from the general tax to be collected for the
year                   2027                   as                      other                   taxes.

SECTION 3. That the total assessment against each lot or parcel of land shall be payable in cash
within thirty (30) days from and after the passage of the Ordinance. All cash payments shall be
made to the Fiscal Officer of said Village. All assessments remaining unpaid at the expiration of
thirty (30) days shall be certified by the County Auditor, together with a certified copy of this
Ordinance, as provided by law, to be placed on the tax duplicate and collected as other taxes.

SECTION 4. That the assessments collected shall be placed in a separate and distinct fund and
used for no other purpose whatsoever.
SECTION 5. That this ordinance shall be in effect from and after the earliest period allowed by
law.
