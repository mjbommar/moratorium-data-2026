COMMUNICATIONS FROM THE PUBLIC

Tanya Vanderveen, 3004 Union Street, Norton — Ms. Vanderveen said she came to discuss
the proposed legislation for Data Centers and asked why the ordinance is necessary. She
recited the required setbacks identified in the legislation and requested clarification. She
noted that she came before Council previously to express concern when the Clinton
Aluminum company’s close proximity to her home affected her personal property. President
Harbert explained that the ordinance has been referred back to the Planning Commission for
recommendations on several areas and to address concerns brought up by residents and
Council members. Mr. Harbert said this ordinance is necessary because currently, there are
no regulations on data centers or its operations, including proper zoning locations. He
explained that tonight Council will vote on extending the moratorium for 90 days so that the
Planning Commission has time to research and finalize its recommendations. Mr. Tousley
noted that he would like the language on Required Setbacks clarified to include that the 1,000
feet is measured from any noise-producing apparatus on the property not just the building.
Mr. Harbert said these regulations will help to guide future Councils. He said it is important
to Council to get this right and to maintain transparency. All of the Planning Commission
meetings are available on YouTube and open to the public. He said that Council will review
the ordinance once the final recommendations are received from the Planning Commission
and explained that the 90-day extension on the moratorium is a different matter. Ms.
Vanderveen referenced her advocacy for the residents living next to the Clinton Aluminum
plant and said that the people of Norton have made it clear that they do not want a data center
in their backyard. Mr. Tousley recited the replacement language pertaining to Water Study:
“The applicant is responsible for providing all necessary water for the cooling system and
day-to-day operations. The use of existing wells and/or the digging of new wells shall not be
permitted.”

Finance — Capital Plan 2026-2030 - Mr. Harbert introduced the Capital Plan and thanked the
Administration for providing this information. He noted that with inflation this information
is important because prices keep rising. He noted that Chief McShane took time to meet with
Council to explain how implementing a ten-year plan could combat inflation. He opened the
floor for Council discussion. Ms. Keener explained that this is a planning tool and future
events may require changes to the Capital Plan. Mr. Lukens thanked the Administration for
providing this information. Mr. Tousley thanked Chief McShane for communicating his
plant to Council and said that the new body cameras will also capture conversations and
write reports. Mr. Tousley asked CPT Burnette how much time is spent writing reports on an
average day. CPT Burnette said it is hard to predict, but there could be five to ten a day. He
said this new body camera will save time by writing the report, but the officer will review
and edit the report. It will be a big help because the officers will have more time to patrol
neighborhoods or be in the schools. Mr. Tousley said there is a lot of value in protecting the
residents and he believes the 10-year plan will save money. Mr. Harbert noted that the 10-
year plan enables Norton to get all upgrades as technology improves over that time. He also
announced that the Police Department received a $25,000 grant. CPT Burnette explained
this will save the department 40% on each radio. He said the department is always pursuing
grant opportunities. Mr. Lukens said he also met with Chief McShane and he was really
impressed with the Al technology and believes it will save the officers’ time and will aid in
translation for non-English speaking individuals. Mr. Harbert said the Chief’s plan also
includes training and one additional drone, which is important because they currently share
one with the Fire Department. Ms. Lee also commended Chief McShane for the important
information he shared. She asked CPT Burnette how much time will be saved by using the
AI feature to write reports. He estimated 50 — 70% and believes the AI is accurate so the
editing time will be minimal. Mr. DeHarpart said that forecasting ten years out is good for
the residents and explained that this program will replace the drone at 2.5, 5 and 7.5 years.
CPT Burnette concurred and reported that the Police Department will keep the old drones.
Mr. Harbert asked Mr. Rorar about the SR 21 culvert and drainage expenses and asked if
these are related. Mr. Rorar answered in the affirmative. Ms. Keener requested a motion to
approve the Capital Plan. Mr. Harbert moved to accept the Capital Plan; seconded by Mr.
DeHarpart.
