Municipal Zoning and
Regulations for Mobile Food
Trucks and Retail Trailers

| can’t help but notice that the city of Bucyrus has no zoning
and regulations in place for mobile food trucks and retail trailers
like most cities around Ohio have, or you are simply not
enforcing them. Ohio law 3717 states that a food truck cannot
stay in one location more than 40 days or it is then considered
permanent and will have to comply with all restaurant
regulations and start the building process. (This doesn’t mean
you can drive it around the block every 39 days and put back in
the same spot) It also defines mobile units vs. restaurants. Then
it is up to local governments to have additional zoning and
regulations in place. Mobile units are meant for fairs, festivals,
special events, catering private parties, or when a local business
invites a truck in for an event like a bar with a band performing,
a car lot sales event, or a store trying to attract business for a
weekend. You can’t just park a food truck in one location, throw
a couple picnic tables out and call yourself a restaurant. There’s
a reason for having zoning and regulations on food trucks also
miles of red tape and regulations to build a restaurant otherwise
what’s stopping everybody in town from dragging their grill out
to the side of the road selling hamburgers and hotdogs for cash
money, no taxes, and no regulations? | would also like to point
out that there are many types of mobile units other than just
food trucks. If there are no zoning laws in place what’s stopping
someone from parking let’s say a mobile tattoo parlor, hair
salon, T-shirt shop, etc. in front of our existing businesses up
town? Are we going to become a tent city or what??

That being said if you’re operating a food truck and want to
sell food permanently in town you go to the bank, apply for a
commercial business loan and build a restaurant, otherwise you


are a mobile unit and need to conduct your business as one like
the State of Ohio has clearly defined in their laws.

A prime example of becoming a permanent food operation
is the new Scooter’s Coffee Shop project which I’m guessing is
going to be North of 400,000 when it’s completed. That’s how it
is Supposed to be done.

Also | would like to point out a few food trucks that operate
the way they are supposed to such as the Cousin’s Lobster
truck, who comes to town roughly 6 to 10 times a year and runs
out of the Ace Hardware parking lot, The Bourbon Chicken Grill
that usually sets up at Cooper’s a few times a year in the
summer months, or the young lady that runs the Gyro and
Italian sausage trailer at the Wren’s Nest parking lot when they
have a band or an event. These mobile operations “stay mobile”
and go to different cities and festivals everyday. That’s how you
run a mobile operation. Then there’s the Kibby’s trailer which
operates behind the Tap House Bar, this is a good example of
the one exception the state allows and the only way you can
become a stationary unit because you are part of the licensed
facility that you own which is totally acceptable.

| have taken the time to research the zoning and regulations
that other cities currently have in place such as Cincinnati,
Columbus, Akron, Delaware, Wooster, and everything in
between. Here is a list of regulations they all have in common.

1.) Mobile units cannot be parked near an established
restaurant. Usually 3 to 6 blocks any direction. Some restrict all
mobile units from their business districts.

2.) Consecutive rule. Mobile units cannot be parked in the same
location for more than 3 days in a row.

3.) Limited number of visits an individual unit can come to town
annually. Most cities limit visits to 24 times a year but the more
stricter cities are 12 or less.


4.) Most cities have designated areas and limit all mobile units
to park there or on a business lot. Basically no on street parking
or residential areas.

5.) Require a daily fee, permit, and health inspection for every
visit.

6.) Extra signage other than what’s on the side of the unit would
have to comply with local ordinance such as flags banners,
lights, etc. and an extra permit and fee.

| would like this list to be submitted to our zoning committee
or whomever makes these decisions. | would also like to point
out that our zoning and regulations should be even stricter than
any of the bigger cities due to our small population and the
amount of retail food service operations that already exist in the
area. Thank you

Chris Gasuras
Owner of Little Athens LTD
