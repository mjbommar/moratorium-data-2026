Audience Participation:
Laurie Fascina - 2950 Cannon Road
Ms. Fascina spoke regarding data centers and zoning code interpretation. She referenced prior Planning
Commission discussions and expressed concern that the proposed moratorium language may not
adequately address “similar use” or conditional use pathways. She advocated for stronger legislative
protection and raised concerns about transparency and prior statements regarding data center activity in
the city.

Will Hollingsworth – Ravenna, Ohio
Mr. Hollingsworth spoke on environmental and economic concerns related to data centers, including water
usage, sustainability, and broader impacts of large-scale technology infrastructure. He urged Council to
carefully evaluate long-term implications and consider a cautious approach.

No further audience participation this evening.

Pending Legislation:
Resolution 2026-036 - This resolution is to award the Glenwood Drive Resurfacing Project bid to Perrin
Asphalt. The total amount of the project is $773,354.45. This will be on its third and final reading tonight.

Resolution 2026-040 – This resolution is to donate 50 ballistic body armor plates that were used by the
fire department to the Metro SWAT team who will use them for training. These items are past their service
life and cannot be sold to the public or disposed of in the trash. This will be on its second reading this
evening. Council will be asked to suspend the three reading rule and place this on an emergency after the
third reading.

Resolution 2026-046 – This resolution is to allow the mayor to enter into a contract with Richard L.
Bowen and Associates in the amount of $48,800 to conduct a comprehensive Amphitheater needs
assessment and concept design. This will be on its second reading tonight.

Resolution 2026-059 – This resolution is to allow the mayor to purchase salt through the ODOT annual
salt bid program for the 2026-2027 season. This will be on its first reading this evening. Council will be
asked to suspend the three reading rule and place this on an emergency after the second reading to meet
ODOT’s May 1st deadline.

Resolution 2026-060 – This resolution is affirming the city of Macedonia’s acceptance of the bid of
Ronyak Paving for the Shepard Road Resurfacing Project. The city’s portion of the project is $190,506.33.
Council is being asked to suspend the three reading rule and place this on an emergency so the project can
begin as soon as possible.

Resolution 2026-061 – This resolution is affirming the city’s action to enter into an agreement with Axon
Enterprise, Inc. for the purchase of 40 taser bundles. Axon’s price was below the state bid price with a
total amount of $243,892 which will be paid over 5 years. Council is being asked to suspend the three
reading rule and place this on an emergency so the purchase can be paid for as soon as possible.

Ordinance 2026-062 – This ordinance is to enter a collective bargaining agreement with Teamsters #436.
Council is being asked to suspend the three reading rule and place this on an emergency so the contract
can be entered into as soon as possible.

Mr. Vazzana summarized the main points which includes: 3% annual wage increases (3-year contract),
Weekend shift differential, step increases for experienced hires, and increased certification incentives.

Ordinance 2026-063 – This legislation is to update the city’s Investment Policy. The policy provides
better details on the policy’s purpose, objectives and scope. This will be on its first reading this evening.

Mrs. Conway stated this will modernization of policy and improved guidance for future administrations.

Ordinance 2026-064 – This ordinance is to authorize a TOP agreement with Western Reserve Wire
Products. The agreement will provide a grant equivalent to 25% of the income tax collected for a total of
three years. The company has proposed to expand their employment with more than 30 new employees
and one million dollars in payroll. This will be on its first reading this evening.

Ordinance 2026-065 – This ordinance establishes a moratorium on data centers for 12 months. Council
is being asked to suspend the three reading rule and place this on an emergency so the moratorium can be
in effect as soon as possible.

Mr. Vazzana stated this applies to all zoning and occupancy applications, includes “similar use” pathways,
and prohibits expansion of existing uses into data centers.

Mr. Furey asked if this did not pass, can someone come in tomorrow and build a data center. Mr. Vazzana
stated no, the zoning code does not allow them.

Mr. Castillo stated he understands the moratorium is for twelve months but asked what the timeframe is
to get additional information. Mr. Vazzana estimated two to five months.

Mr. Castillo asked if there is anything the city can do to put regulations in place for the parcels in
Reminderville or the Township who use our wastewater treatment plant for service. Mr. Vazzana stated
not from the standpoint of the moratorium, but there would be an evaluation from the wastewater
department on whether their system would be able to handle the estimated flow. Mr. Zuege stated that
there is an extensive process to determine the estimated flow.

Ordinance 2026-066 – This ordinance amends Part Eleven of the codified ordinance and changes the
symbols used to identify permissions in certain tables within the zoning code. When this was sent to the
codification agent, they were unable to use the same symbols in the charts as what was passed by the
voters. The changes will replace the current symbols with alpha characters and will change the text to
reflect the same. A motion will be made at the regular meeting to send this ordinance to the planning
commission for their review and report.

There will be a public hearing regarding this legislation on May 12th at 6:45 pm. This will be on its first
reading this evening.

Nate Zuege – Director of Wastewater Treatment
• Provided an update on the Wastewater Treatment Plant Improvement Project, noting the project
   remains in the preconstruction phase and that additional updates will be presented at the Public Works
   Committee meeting this fall.
• To date, staff has held three monthly preconstruction follow-up meetings. A.P. O’Horo cleared the
   wooded area for the new Primary EQ tank site; the work was completed by the end of March to avoid
   impacts to the Ohio brown bat reproductive cycle. In coordination with B&N, a SharePoint site was
   established to manage project documentation (including shop drawings and related submittals).
• Provided an update on the 2026 Sewer Improvement projects managed by the Wastewater Department.
   Two projects are planned for 2026:
   • Serene and Belmeadow: The sewer main was found to have settled at a joint, creating an estimated
       40% blockage. Replacement of the 12-inch sewer main began last month and has been completed.
       Road repairs and grass restoration were completed this week.
   • Manhole projects: The Wastewater and Engineering Departments reimplemented the annual
       Manhole Rehabilitation Program last year. As the sewer maintenance crew cleans and televises
       the City’s sanitary infrastructure, a list of priority manholes is generated. The 2026 repairs are
       tentatively scheduled to begin in May, with notice to the Mayor and Council to follow once
       schedules are finalized (Serene Ct., Presidential Pk., Edison Blvd., and E. Aurora Rd.).
• Announced that staff will present this Thursday at the NESOWEA Innovation Seminar in Richfield,
   Ohio, alongside Pelton representatives, on the instantaneous BOD sensor leased and purchased last
   year. The presentation will address installation, system integration, preventive maintenance, and how
   the sensor has become an integral part of WWTP operations. Noted that only two wastewater treatment
   plants in Northeast Ohio currently utilize this type of biological sensor, including the Twinsburg
   Wastewater Treatment Plant.
