3:00 p.m. Workshop

1. Tax Increment Finance - Copper Recycle Center (Brandy Grace, Executive Director of the
   Utah Association of Counties)

The workshop was prompted by developer interest in establishing a proposed copper recycling plant at
Crescent Junction, as well as broader state trends in energy and industrial development. Grace presented
an overview of Tax Increment Financing (TIF) tools under Utah Code Title 17C, comparing locally controlled
Community Reinvestment Agencies (CRAs) with state-level authorities such as the Utah Inland Port
Authority (UIPA). Developers often seek tax incentives early in project planning. Grace advised the
Commission to take a proactive, cautious approach to protect county tax revenue, negotiate interlocal
agreements, and establish local conditions before entering financing commitments or inviting state
authorities into parcel-specific project areas.

Presentations

Land Use Code Audit Findings (Andrew Jackson, Planning and Zoning Director)

Planning & Zoning Director Andrew Jackson delivered a comprehensive overview of the structural and
procedural review of Grand County's current Land Use Code. Topics covered included: Code Modernization
& Clarity: with the audit identifying key areas where existing land-use regulations contain ambiguous
language, overlapping definitions, or outdated development standards, with the goal to streamline the code
for clearer interpretation by both the public and staff. Emerging Land Uses & Gaps: The review highlighted
missing or under-regulated development categories—such as high-density commercial digital infrastructure
(data centers), renewable energy projects, and specialized industrial uses—that require modern, standalone
regulatory standards. Alignment with General Plan & State Law: Staff emphasized updating land-use
provisions to ensure full compliance with recently enacted Utah statutory requirements (such as state-
mandated water preservation planning) and the goals of the Spanish Valley Future Land Use Plan. Next
Steps: The audit findings serve as the baseline framework for upcoming text amendments, public work
sessions, and code revisions that the Planning Commission and County Commission will draft over the
coming months.

2. Burr Oaks Rezone

Presentation
This proposal would rezone a vacant 5.02-acre parcel at 4500 South Zimmerman Lane, just south of the Blu
Vista Subdivision, from Rural Residential (RR) to Large Lot Residential (LLR). The change would allow about
eight half-acre single-family lots. The Spanish Valley

Future Land Use Plan maps this area as a Moderate Scale Neighborhood, which allows somewhat more
housing than is proposed. Staff recommends the Commission take public comment, review the request
against the Land Use Code criteria, and forward a recommendation to the County Commission.

The proposed Water Use and Preservation Element adds a water-planning chapter to the Grand County
General Plan, as required by state law. It is a planning and policy document, not a regulation. It examines

7. Allocation of the Secure Rural Schools Act Payment FY202 (Commission Administration)

Presentation
The State must transmit, for each county in which a national forest is situated, the county’s election to
receive a share of the Secure Rural Schools Act (SRS) State payment or a share of the State’s 25-percent
payment based on the 7-year rolling average annual receipts. The State may use the form to transmit county
elections to the Forest Service. If the State fails to transmit a county’s election by the deadline of August 1,
2026 (midnight, mountain time), the county will be considered to have elected to receive a share of the State
payment (SRS)

Motion by Commissioner McCurdy to approve a share of the State Payment (SRS) of approximately
$22,700, with 100% of the funding to be directed as 50% to public roads and 50% to schools under Title 1

Motion seconded by Commissioner Winfield

Discussion (none at this time)

Motion passes 7-0


8. Support for the eighth annual Moab ArTTrails (Facilities Supervisor Shawn Fugit)

Presentation
Since its inception on the river bridge in 2015 Moab ArTTrails Program has donated over $119,000 worth of
public art to the people of Grand County, Moab. Grand County has invested crucial matching support in this
year-round attractor for residents and visitors alike since 2017. The annual event brings well known and
rising artists, their support teams and their art to install the exhibit. Hundreds each year come for the event
itself, but over a million people see the art annually, spending more time and money downtown as a result.
Their works are for general sale for a year, and for the community's consideration for the permanent
collection. The cost of this transportation of works and artist lodging has increased dramatically. The request
remains the same, $10,000.00.

13. Resolution approving the final plat of Puesta del Sol, Phase xiii, lots 26 & 27 amended, a
   lot line adjustment between two commonly owned lots, Oyler properties LLC, pursuant to
   Utah code § 17-79-608 (Planning and Zoning Director Andrew Jackson)

Presentation
Beau Oyler of Oyler Properties LLC owns two neighboring lots in Puesta Del Sol. He wants to move the line
between them so the existing house on Lot 26 has the required 10-foot side yard. Lot 27 is vacant. No new
lot is created; both lots stay well above the minimum size, and the utility easement moves with the line. State
law allows the County to approve this without a public hearing because one owner holds both lots and no
street or common area is touched. Staff checked the survey math, the recorded plat, and the neighborhood
covenants, found no conflicts, and recommends approval with a short list of clean-up items before recording.
