Jon Regnier, Highway Superintendent, Marissa Pfile, Zoning Administrator and Brandy

Johnson, County Clerk, updated the Commissioners on activities of their departments.

Jalisa Gruhn, Public Transit Director, was present to discuss a potential purchase

contract from Meyer Automotive for the purchase of a fleet vehicle. Gruhn requested

direction from the Commissioners at the October 13th meeting and was advised at that

time to work with Zabrocki to find a potential vehicle and have legal counsel review a

purchase contract. Zabrocki and Gruhn found a vehicle at Meyer Automotive that is

similar to the one being replaced. Ahmic noted that the vehicle will be a Fleet/Public

Transit vehicle, and Public Transit will be able to utilize it when it is not in use by the

Bailiff.

Moved to Zabrocki and seconded by Curtis to accept the Purchase Agreement from

Meyer Automotive for the purchase of a 2015 Chevrolet Impala, to be used as a county

Fleet/Public Transit vehicle, in the amount of $11,995.00, to be paid from the

Miscellaneous line in the Miscellaneous General budget.

Affirmative Vote: Zabrocki, Curtis, Pekarek, Hain, Ahmic Motion Carried

Sara Stenger, Detention Center Director, and Andrew Truslow, Deputy Detention Center

Director, were present to discuss potentially amending the current Detention Center

staffing structure. The Detention Center currently operates on a “Panama Schedule”,

which consists of four teams, two day-teams and two night-teams, with three Sergeants

accommodating all four shifts. Stenger reported that they are seeing that it is necessary

to have more supervision on all shifts, especially due to the fact that nine of the

Correction Officers have only been employed for less than a year. Her goal is to have

four Sergeants, rather than three, as a way to reduce errors, have more supervision

and more guidance on the evening shifts. Stenger proposed hiring internally for a

Sergeant position. There is one vacant position that would not be filled but would

remain as a part-time position to fill in as needed to cover for staff when they have to

be gone. There was a brief discussion regarding budgeting moving forward, with Ahmic

noting that she would be in favor of proceeding with the proposed plan, if the vacant

position were to be changed to part time, at 29 hours per week, for the remainder of

the 2026-2027 fiscal year, as it would not change the Detention Center’s budget. The

vacant position will be re-evaluated during the next fiscal year budget time.

Moved by Pekarek and seconded by Curtis to promote one current Detention Center

Officer to Sergeant and move the current vacant Detention Center position to part-time,

at 29 hours per week.

Affirmative Vote: Pekarek, Curtis, Hain, Zabrocki, Ahmic Motion Carried
