# Dougherty County Approves 45-Day Moratorium on Data Center Apps

**ALBANY, Ga.** — The Dougherty County Board of Commissioners has approved a 45-day moratorium on accepting and approving zoning applications for data centers while county officials review whether new regulations are needed.

The temporary pause will allow the county to evaluate its zoning ordinance and determine whether data centers should be subject to specific development standards before any future proposals are considered.

Commissioner Gloria Gaines, chair of the Government Affairs Committee, said the review was prompted by questions from residents and is intended to ensure the county is prepared to address future data center development.

"We believe this review period gives us the opportunity to determine whether we need a specific zoning ordinance for data centers, establish appropriate development standards and evaluate the overall impact these facilities could have on our community," Gaines said.

The review will examine issues such as appropriate locations, facility size, compatibility with surrounding land uses and potential impacts on local infrastructure and water resources.

"We're sitting on some of the most pristine aquifers in the world, and we want to keep it that way," Gaines said. "While we welcome businesses that want to invest in our community, we also have a responsibility to protect our natural resources and ensure future development is in the best interest of our citizens."

County Attorney Alex Shalishali said Dougherty County's current zoning ordinance does not specifically address data centers, making the review an important first step.

"Our current zoning ordinance does not have any specific regulations as they apply to data centers," Shalishali said. "This resolution establishes a 45-day review period for the County Attorney, Planning Commission and appropriate county staff to develop proposed regulations for the Board's consideration."

Shalishali said legal research confirmed the county has the authority to temporarily suspend the acceptance of data center zoning applications while the review is underway. He also noted there are no pending data center zoning applications in Dougherty County.

During the moratorium, the County Attorney's Office will work with the Planning Commission and county staff to draft recommendations for the Board of Commissioners. If additional time is needed, the board may consider extending the moratorium in accordance with Georgia's public notice and hearing requirements. The proposed regulations will first be reviewed by the Government Affairs Committee before moving through the board's regular legislative process.

County officials said the goal is to develop clear, thoughtful regulations that protect community interests while providing guidance for future development.
