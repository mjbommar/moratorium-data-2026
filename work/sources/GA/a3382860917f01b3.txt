 I. PUBLIC HEARING – Madam Chair opened the Public Hearing. A sign-in sheet was provided for those
wishing to speak for or against the requests. No one present signed in to speak.

     A. Case# ZOA-2026-02 – Amend Use Table of Brooks County Zoning Ordinance to Permit by
Special Exception Carnival, Athletic Event, Community Fair or Other Event of Interest to the Public
Zoning Administrator, Jared Lanier, presented the proposed text amendment to regulate temporary public
events and gatherings, including concerts, carnivals, and other events open to the public. The proposal
would change these events from an automatic permitted use to a Special Exception, requiring review by the
Planning Commission and approval by the Board of Commissioners. Private, invitation-only events such as
weddings would generally not fall under this provision. Applicants may also need approvals or permits from
the health department, engineering/road authorities, and the County business licensing process. The
existing land-use classification titled “Conference Center and Retreat Center” would be revised to “Event,
Conference, Community, and Retreat Centers or Similar Gathering Venues. Staff have recommended
approval of the Zoning Ordinance amendment.

    B. Case# REZ-2026-01 – Rezone Request – Holly McAllister – From BR (Business Retail) to RR (Rural
Residential) – Zoning Administrator, Jared Lanier, presented the request from Holly McAllister to consider
a zone change from BR (Business Retail) to RR (Rural Residential) on land lot 215 located on Troupeville
Road. The property was originally developed and used for residential purposes. On May 7, 2012, the
property was rezoned to Business Retail to allow the existing long cabin-style structure, which had
previously been used as a residence, to be converted and operated as a small country store. The owner
requested rezoning back to Rural Residential so the property can be used as a single-family residence for
personal purposes. Staff recommended approval of the rezoning with no conditions; however, Staff would
like to make the applicant aware that a change of use in a structure may trigger building code requirements.
Madam Chair closed the public hearing to go into the special called and work session.

  3. Proposal – Volunteer Firefighter Compensation – Chief Weeks – Chief Weeks proposed changing
the volunteer compensation structure using funds already allocated in the current budget. Training pay
would increase from $25.00 to $40.00 per training session, supporting the required minimum of 24 training
hours annually. Volunteers responding to structure fires would receive $50.00 per incident; and volunteers
responding to brush fires or vehicle accidents involving entrapment would receive $25.00 per incident. This
proposal is intended as recognition of volunteer’s time, risk, and service rather than full compensation. The
proposal will be placed on the September 8th agenda for consideration.

  4. Road Closure – From Livingston Road to Ramsey Road – Commissioner Maxwell - Discussion Four
Oaks Land Company requested abandonment of approximately 4.07 miles of Livingston Road forming a
loop between Ramsey Road and the surrounding plantation property. The company owns most of the land
along both sides of the segment; small portions ae owned by other adjacent property owners. The request
is based on limited public utility and recurring trespassing concerns. The Board clarified that this must be
handled as a road abandonment, not an informal road closure. The county cannot simply place closure
signs without following the required process. The applicant will provide contact information and a written
request; county staff and the county attorney will explain the abandonment procedure, which includes
public notice/hearing and Board consideration.

   5. Position Request – Executive Assistant to Administrative Staff (Purchasing Office, Payroll Clerk,

consistent vendors and supplies, and allow bulk purchases that could reduce costs. The proposed salary
range is approximately $18.00 to $25.00 per hour, depending on qualifications and experience. Funding will
be transferred from the contingency line, with the expectation that purchasing savings and grant support
could substantially offset the cost.

IV. COMMISSIONERS BUSINESS PRESENTATIONS – (5 MINUTES)
    A. Legacy Behavioral Health – Update – Ashlie Prain - Ashlie Prain, Legacy Behavioral Health, presented
 an update to the Board. Mr. Prain reported that 183 individuals have been served in Brooks County during
 the most recent quarter, with services valued at nearly $1 million. The population served included 111
 males, 72 females, and 35 children or adolescents. Legacy is expanding its mobile clinic service. A second
 mobile unit will allow one unit to serve the northern area and another to serve the southern area, increasing
 the number of service days in Brooks County. They provide prevention programming that addresses
 vaping, alcohol, and marijuana use. The county schools received receptacles for safe disposal of
 confiscated vape devices containing lithium batteries. There are school based mental health services
 available through the APEX program at Brooks High School, Brooks Early Learning Center, and Delta
 Innovative School. Additional youth programs included life skills cooking classes, wellness classes, and
 summer programming.
