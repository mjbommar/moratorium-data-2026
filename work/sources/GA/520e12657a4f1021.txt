IGA with the Northeast Georgia Animal Shelter — Animal Control Ordinance
Discussion: Priya Patel explained that the Intergovernmental Agreement (IGA)
between Franklin County and the Northeast Georgia Animal Shelter is a standard
agreement similar to those adopted by neighboring counties. She explained that
the primary difference from the original agreement involves the payment structure.
Rather than operating solely on an invoice reimbursement basis, the shelter will
now submit a proposed annual budget before the County adopts its annual budget.
This allows the Board to review and consider the request during the budgeting
process. Once approved, the County would make quarterly payments based upon
the adopted budget and supporting invoices. Ms. Patel emphasized that the County
is not obligated to pay all expenses submitted by the shelter, as funding remains
subject to the Board's discretion and budgeted appropriations.

Commissioner Thompson asked whether the agreement was the same as Hart
County s agreement, she was told the IGA’s basically mirrored each other.

Ms. Patel confirmed that it was basically the same except for that one caveat. She
stated Hart County is on an invoice type relationship where the animal shelter
provides an invoice every quarter and they pay for it, whereas Frankin County's
agreement utilizes an annual budget process.

IGA with Banks County - Cross Jurisdictional Road Maintenance
Discussion: Priya Patel presented an IGA between Franklin County and Banks
County regarding maintenance of Nowhere Road also known as County Line Road.

Under the agreement, Franklin County will be responsible for routine
maintenance, including grading, bush hogging, ditch maintenance, and slope
mowing. Banks County will provide gravel for the roadway. Banks County has
already approved the agreement and is awaiting Franklin County's approval.

Commissioner Smith asked County Manager Terry Harris if the agreement was
necessary because of the distance from Franklin County's maintenance facilities.

Mr. Harris explained that the roadway is shared by both counties, with each
maintaining one side. The agreement allows Franklin County to perform the
maintenance work while Banks County supplies the gravel, resulting in a shared
cost for maintaining the roadway.

Chair Long noted that Banks County benefits from TSPLOST funding, allowing it
to dedicate more funding toward gravel.

Ms. Patel disclosed for the record that her law firm also represents Banks County
and that a conflict waiver had previously been executed before the agreement was
drafted.

Data Center Ordinance

Discussion: Commissioner Busby discussed the need to develop a strong
ordinance with multiple layers of protection regarding data centers and requested
that such regulations ultimately be incorporated into the Unified Development
Code (UDC). She also requested feedback from the Board.

Chair Long asked Ms. Patel whether other counties had adopted data center
ordinances.

Ms. Patel stated that many jurisdictions are currently evaluating data center
regulations and suggested adopting a temporary moratorium while permanent
language is developed for inclusion in the UDC.

Chair Long expressed support for a moratorium and referenced western
communities that have experienced significant water demands associated with data
centers.

Mr. Harris also supported implementing a temporary moratorium until permanent
regulations could be adopted.

Commissioner Busby asked Ms. Patel whether a proposed moratorium could be
prepared for consideration at the July 6 meeting.

Commissioner Smith stated that he believed the ordinance should simply prohibit
data centers in Franklin County rather than require an extensive document.

Ms. Patel stated she would review current case law regarding the issue and should
have a moratorium ready for approval on July 6".

Commissioner Thompson stated she also supported adopting a moratorium.

Water Meter Moratorium

Discussion: County Manager Terry Harris recommended lifting the water meter
moratorium and advised that he planned to request formal approval at the July 6
Regular Meeting.

Mr. Harris also reported that the County recently tested its ability to obtain water
from Madison County and estimated the available capacity at approximately 200
gallons per minute. Based on those results, he stated he was comfortable
recommending that the moratorium be lified.

Commissioner Busby asked for an update on the groundwater studies.
Mr. Harris reported that the County had received GPS coordinates for the Franklin
County School System to drill a well and noted that additional property owners are

also evaluating and testing potential well sites.

Commissioner Smith asked how many applicants remained on the water meter
waiting list.

Mr. Harris estimated that approximately 40 to 45 applicants remained on the
waiting list.

Item 7. TERRY HARRIS —- COUNTY MANAGER REPORT

1. The County has received LRA funding totaling $816,875.15, allowing the bid

process to begin. The LRA program requires no local matching funds.

A longstanding roof leak at the courthouse has been investigated using several
methods, including drone inspections. The County received a proposal from
United Contracting & Roofing to replace the TPO roofing system over the dome
for $35,198.27, complete copper roof repairs for $2,500.00, and repaint the
exterior of the clock tower for $10,700.00. Mr. Harris recommended proceeding
with the company.

Stone Bridge Road has sustained significant damage and numerous potholes.
Although repairs were completed the previous Friday, they did not hold. Mr.
Harris explained that dirt is currently being hauled from private property along
Stone Bridge Road for grading work at the future Tractor Supply development in
Lavonia. Approximately 350 loaded dump trucks, or roughly 700 total trips per
day, are traveling the roadway. He reported that the contractor has agreed to
assist with repairing some of the roadway damage caused by the increased traffic.
Commissioner Smith asked whether the contractor would pay for all necessary
repairs. Mr. Harris replied that was in discussion.

A major water main break occurred along Interstate 85 involving a 12-inch water
line. Mr. Harris stated the rupture nearly depleted the County's water system
before it was located. Repairs required most of the previous week and were
completed on Friday. He noted that some customers had reported air in the lines
and an unusual taste in the water, but expected those issues to be resolved this
week.
