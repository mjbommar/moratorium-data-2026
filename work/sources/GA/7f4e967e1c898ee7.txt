          Mr. Morton requested that the Board authorize the publication of the required Notice of Intent to submit
          local legislation.
          The Board discussed local homestead exemptions for seniors and disabled citizens.

              •   Following the decision to opt out of recent statewide legislation (HB 581), staff identified
                  existing local legislation governing homestead exemptions.
              •   Staff reviewed comparable jurisdictions and recommended increasing current exemptions as
                  follows:
                       o   Seniors (65+): Increase from $10,000.00 to $15,000.00 initially, with consideration
                           of phased increases up to $50,000.00 in $5,000.00 increments.
                       o   Disabled Citizens: Increase from $12,000.00 to $15,000.00 initially, with
                           consideration of phased increases up to $30,000.00 in increments.
          Mr. Morton emphasized that, given the General Assembly’s January start and statutory timing
          requirements, the Board must authorize publication of the notice of intent prior to the session to avoid
          a 60-day delay.
          Commissioners discussed the fiscal impact and the importance of transparent communication with the
          public.
              •   Estimates shared indicated:
                       o   Increasing the senior exemption from $10,000.00 to $15,000.00 reduces annual taxes
                           by approximately $44.00.
              •   Covering exemptions may require a millage increase of 0.06, resulting in approximately
                  $15.00 more annually for residents not qualifying as seniors.
          Commissioners expressed support for relief efforts but emphasized the need for transparent public
          outreach to avoid confusion. They also discussed the possibility of income caps to better target relief
          to residents on fixed incomes and those most in need.
          The County Manager noted complexities in state law regarding income definitions and highlighted the
          variety of models used in other jurisdictions. He suggested holding a workshop to refine options.
          CM Morton noted that the Boards decision to opt-out of the statewide floating homestead exemption
          (House Bill 581) will need to be reconsidered in 2027.
          The Board agreed to proceed with publication of the Notice of Intent to introduce local legislation, hold
          a workshop prior to finalizing exemptions and aim for final decisions at the first January meeting.
          Motion/second by Commissioners Pullin/Daniel to approve proceeding with the Notice of
          Intention to introduce Local Legislation and to authorize publication of said notice in the legal
          organ, motion carried 5-0.
