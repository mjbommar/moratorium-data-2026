Data Centers in Tulare County?

About This Effort

Tulare County is evaluating potential land use regulations for data centers in the unincorporated areas of the county, which are areas outside the limits of the county’s eight incorporated cities.

Residents, businesses, community organizations, property owners, and other interested parties are invited to learn about the process and share feedback.

This page will provide updates, documents, and opportunities for public input as the County considers potential regulations for data centers in unincorporated Tulare County.

What is a Data Center

A data center is a building, campus, or facility primarily used to house computer servers, data storage systems, networking equipment, cloud computing infrastructure, artificial intelligence computing equipment, cryptocurrency mining equipment, or similar electronic processing equipment.

Data centers may also include supporting infrastructure such as electrical substations, cooling systems, backup power systems, battery storage, and fuel storage.

Data Center Updates

Current Status

On the August 18, 2026 Board Meeting, the Tulare County Board of Supervisors adopted Uncodified Urgency Interim Ordinance No. 3669, establishing a temporary 45-day moratorium on new data center development in unincorporated Tulare County. At the September 22, 2026 Board Meeting, the Board extended the moratorium through August 6, 2027, following a Public Hearing at which public input was welcome.

The temporary pause allows the County to evaluate possible permanent zoning and development standards before considering new data center applications. The moratorium applies only to new data center development in unincorporated Tulare County. It does not apply within the incorporated limits of the county’s eight cities.

What the County Is Studying

The County is reviewing how data centers could affect land use, infrastructure, public services, the environment, and surrounding communities.

Utilities & Infrastructure

Water supply and cooling systems
Electricity demand and supporting utility infrastructure

Environmental & Public Safety Considerations

Noise, lighting, air quality, and backup power systems
Fire protection, emergency response, batteries, and fuel storage

Land Use & Community Compatibility

Facility location, building size, setbacks, buffers, landscaping, and screening
Compatibility with agriculture, rural communities, businesses, and planned growth

Transportation & Community Impacts

Transportation, telecommunications, public infrastructure, and decommissioning
Potential economic benefits, costs, and community impacts
