BACKGROUND:
Pursuant to the authority granted the City of Desert Hot Springs (“City”) by Article XI, Section 7 of the
California Constitution, the City has the police power to regulate the use of land and property within
the City in a manner designed to promote public convenience and general prosperity, as well as
public health, welfare and safety. Adoption and enforcement of comprehensive zoning regulations
and other land use regulations lies within the City’s police power.

Data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses may require extraordinary electrical demand,
substation or transmission improvements, substantial cooling systems, backup generators, water and
wastewater capacity, and 24-hour operations, and may thereby create or exacerbate land use
compatibility, noise, air quality, greenhouse gas, public health, public safety, increase
heat/temperature around the data center, emergency response, low human job/work generation, and
infrastructure capacity concerns. Such concerns may be especially significant when these uses are
proposed in proximity to residential neighborhoods, schools, parks, community facilities, and other
sensitive land uses.

Under the Desert Hot Springs Municipal Code in Section 17.04.050(D), “data centers” are defined as
an industrial building containing floor space that houses computer mainframes, servers, and IT
equipment primarily for the purpose of storing data and distributing data stored on site. Data centers
also contain all associated power distribution, cooling, cabling, fire suppression, and physical security
systems. The Desert Hot Springs Municipal Code currently does not define “high -intensity
computing uses”. The City’s existing General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning regulations, and other
applicable land use regulations currently do not comprehensively regulate data centers and similar
high-intensity computing uses, including with respect to their permissible locations, size and intensity,
utility demand, water consumption, cooling systems, backup generation, buffering, noise,
environmental performance, decommissioning, and community benefits.

The City Council intends within a reasonable time to study, consider, and potentially adopt
amendments to the City’s General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning ordinance, specific plans,
development standards, and other applicable regulations to address whether, where, and under what
conditions data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses should be allowed within the City

DISCUSSION:

The Proposed Ordinance

If approved, the proposed Ordinance would cause a temporary suspension of Data Centers and the
like in all areas of the City. Further, no use permit, variance, building permit, or any other entitlement
for use would be accepted, processed, approved or issued for the establishment or operation of data
centers or high-intensity computing uses. Adopting such proposed Ordinance would allow adequate
time for the City to consider, study, and enact regulations for same.

If passed, such proposed Ordinance would take effect immediately.           In order to adopt such an
ordinance, the following must be done:

    1) Adoption requires a four-fifths vote of the city council;

    2) The proposed Ordinance must contain findings, which are included with the proposed
    Ordinance, stating why the proposed Ordinance is needed to address a current and immediate
    threat to public health, safety, or welfare;

    3) 10 days prior to the expiration of the proposed Ordinance or any extension thereof, the Council
    shall issue a written report describing the measures taken to alleviate the condition which led to
    the adoption of the proposed Ordinance

    4) This extension would be for a maximum period of time up to 22 months and 15 days, for a total
    of 2 years.

None.

On June 16, 2026, the City Council of the City of Desert Hot Springs adopted Interim Urgency
Ordinance adopting a moratorium on the issuance of any license, permit, or entitlement for data
centers and high intensity computing uses (collectively “Data Centers”) in the City (“Underlying
Ordinance”). That Underlying Ordinance initiated a 45-day moratorium on Data Centers the City.

The City desires to study the location, operation, and potential impacts of Data Centers to
ensure that such uses are established, conducted, or possibly banned in a manner consistent
with the City's General Plan, Strategic Plan, and other plans with respect to permissible
locations, size and intensity, utility demand, water consumption, cooling systems, backup
generation, buffering, noise, environmental performance, decommissioning, and community
benefits. Accordingly, the City must evaluate appropriate zoning regulations, locational criteria,
development standards, and a possible ban applicable to Data Centers to ensure consistency
with the vision and policy objectives of the City Council.

The Underlying Ordinance was adopted to protect the public health, safety, and welfare while
the City evaluates the potential impacts of Data Centers and considers appropriate regulatory
measures. Pursuant to Government Code section 65858(d), the City has prepared this report
describing the measures undertaken to alleviate the conditions that led to the adoption of the
Underlying Ordinance.

RESEARCH
Since adoption of the Underlying Ordinance, City staff has been engaged in researching and
analyzing the potential impacts of Data Centers and evaluating development standards
necessary to ensure consistency with the Strategic Plan and other applicable City policies. Such
efforts include the review of land use compatibility, commercial impacts, zoning considerations,
development standards, possible bans, other planning-related issues associated with Data
Centers. As reflected in the Underlying Ordinance, the City Council expressed a desire to study
the appropriateness of Data Centers within the City and to evaluate whether additional
regulations, or even a ban, are necessary to further the City's planning and economic
development objectives. In connection with these efforts, City staff is evaluating the potential
land use, environmental impacts, economic impacts, community, and development impacts
associated with Data Centers, as well as potential zoning regulations and development
standards that may be appropriate.

Consistent with the Underlying Ordinance, any approvals/permit etc. for Data Centers subject to
the ordinance are suspended pending completion of the City's study and consideration of
appropriate regulatory measures. This temporary suspension allows the City Council adequate
opportunity to review relevant information, policy options, and consider long-term goals, policies,
and development standards applicable to such uses.
The Underlying Ordinance was adopted to provide staff with time to conduct necessary
research and analysis, including land use analyses, development standards, traffic and


circulation analyses, and other efforts necessary to evaluate the potential impacts of Data
Centers on the City.

The interim prohibition adopted by the City Council on June 16, 2026, will expire within forty-five
(45) days. Because it is not feasible to complete the necessary studies and analyses prior to the
expiration of the current ordinance, and the Underlying Ordinance should be extended for an
additional 22 months and 15 days. Based upon the foregoing, and consistent with Government
Code section 65858(c), the City Council finds and determines that there remains a current and
immediate threat to the public health, safety, and welfare, and that the approval of any permits,
entitlements, or land use approvals for Data Centers could result in uses that may be
inconsistent with the City's ongoing planning efforts and would therefore exacerbate such threat
to the public health, safety, and welfare.
