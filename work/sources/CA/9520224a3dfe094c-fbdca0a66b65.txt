STAFF REPORT


To:  City Council From: Jonathan Nicks, City Manager Prepared by: Brian Halvorson, Dir. Of Community Development


Subject: Extension of Urgency Ordinance 1838 for an interim moratorium on the establishment, expansion, or intensification of Data Centers and other similar high-intensity computing uses.


RECOMMENDATION:


Approve Ordinance 1838-Ext2 extending for a second and final time urgency ordinance 1838 for an

interim moratorium on the establishment, expansion, or intensification of Data Centers and other

similar high-intensity computing uses.

SUMMARY:


The City has taken several steps to address concerns regarding data centers following Council

approval of Urgency Ordinance 1838 on June 3, 2026, and July 15, 2026 (first extension), for the

establishment, expansion or intensification of data centers and other similar high-intensity

computing uses. Research on energy/water usage, tax revenue, air quality, noise,

decommissioning, General Plan and Zoning code review, planning conference attendance,

Sustainability Commission and Planning Commission review, public input, and a variety of other

topics have been considered for regulating, limiting, and prohibiting data centers in the City of Indio

during the moratorium period.

An extension to the existing moratorium on data centers would allow additional time for staff to draft

amendments to the General Plan and/or Unified Development Code that will be brought to the

Planning Commission with a recommendation to the City Council. The current Urgency Ordinance is

set to expire on October 16, 2026, and the extension to the Ordinance is proposed for 1 year and

would be the second and final moratorium ordinance.

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:


On May 6, 2026, the City Council directed staff to work with the Planning Commission to consider

new policies and provide options for prohibiting data centers and developments that include high

water, high energy or high land use, including warehouses or high-water use development and

provide a recommendation to the City Council.

day moratorium on Data Centers and directed staff to include options to extend the moratorium and

review alternatives for a permanent ban on Data Centers. On June 24, 2026, the Planning

Commission received a staff presentation summarizing various types of data centers, including

ORDINANCE NO. 1838-Ext2 AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF INDIO, CALIFORNIA, PURSUANT TO GOVERNMENT CODE SECTION 65858, EXTENDING FOR A SECOND AND FINAL TIME URGENCY ORDINANCE NO. 1838 AND THE INTERIM MORATORIUM ON THE ESTABLISHMENT, EXPANSION, OR INTENSIFICATION OF DATA CENTERS AND OTHER SIMILAR HIGH-INTENSITY COMPUTING USES WITHIN THE CITY OF INDIO


WHEREAS, the City of Indio (“ City”) is a municipal corporation organized and


operating under the laws of the State of California, with the authority to regulate land use within

its boundaries pursuant to its police powers and the Planning and Zoning Law;

WHEREAS, Government Code section 65858 authorizes the City Council to adopt and


extend an interim urgency ordinance prohibiting uses that may conflict with a contemplated

general plan, specific plan, zoning, or other land use regulatory proposal that the City is

considering, studying, or intends to study within a reasonable time, when necessary to protect the

public safety, health, and welfare;

WHEREAS, on June 3, 2026, the City Council adopted Urgency Ordinance No. 1838 by


four-fifths vote, establishing a forty-five (45) day interim moratorium on the approval of

applications, permits, or entitlements for the establishment, expansion, or intensification of data

centers and other similar high-intensity computing uses within the City;

WHEREAS, Ordinance No. 1838 was adopted to provide the City with reasonable time


to study, consider, and potentially adopt amendments to the City’s General Plan, Unified

Development Code, zoning regulations, specific plans, development standards, and other

applicable regulations to address whether, where, and under what conditions data centers and

similar high-intensity computing uses should be allowed within the City;

WHEREAS, during the initial moratorium period, City staff commenced the study and


review directed by Ordinance No. 1838, including review of land use compatibility,

infrastructure demand, electrical demand, water use, wastewater demand, cooling systems,

backup generation, noise, air quality, greenhouse gas emissions, emergency response, fire and

life safety, decommissioning, tax community benefits, and compatibility with

revenue,

surrounding land uses;

WHEREAS, on June 24, 2026, the Planning Commission received a staff presentation


regarding data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses, considered public input, and

recommended that the City Council move forward with a complete ban on data centers within

the City;

WHEREAS, following the Planning Commission’s review, the City also received input


from the Sustainability Commission supporting the Planning Commission’s recommendation and

identifying concerns regarding water consumption, electrical demand, greenhouse gas emissions,

heat generation, noise, air quality, and overall quality of life;

Page 19 of 23

WHEREAS, on July 15, 2026, following a duly noticed public hearing, the City Council


adopted an extension to Ordinance No. 1838 and the interim moratorium established thereby for

ninety (90) days, through October 16, 2026 (“First Extension”);

WHEREAS, in adopting the First Extension, the City Council considered the staff


report, the written report required by Government Code section 65858, the Planning Commission

recommendation, Sustainability Commission input, public comments, and all evidence in the

administrative record, and found that there remained a current and immediate threat to the public

health, safety, and welfare and that approval of applicable entitlements, permits, licenses, or

approvals for data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses would result in that

immediate threat;

WHEREAS, during the First Extension, City staff has continued the study and review


directed by Ordinance No. 1838 and the First Extension, including additional research,

coordination with city staff, and preliminary amendments to the Unified Development Code;

WHEREAS, the City has prepared and issued the additional written report required by


Government Code section 65858 describing the measures taken to alleviate the conditions that

led to adoption of Ordinance No. 1838 and its First Extension;

WHEREAS, despite the work completed to date, the City requires additional time to


complete the study of data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses and to prepare,

review, notice, consider, and, if appropriate, adopt permanent amendments to the General Plan,

Unified Development Code, zoning regulations, specific plans, development standards, or other

applicable regulations;

WHEREAS, the City’s existing General Plan, Unified Development Code, and other


applicable land use regulations continue not to define or comprehensively regulate data centers

and similar high-intensity computing uses, including with respect to their permissible locations,

size and intensity, utility demand, water consumption, cooling systems, backup generation,

buffering, noise, environmental performance, decommissioning, and community benefits;

WHEREASdata centers and similar high-intensity computing uses require


, may

extraordinary electrical demand, substation or transmission improvements, substantial

mechanical cooling systems, backup generators, water and wastewater capacity, and 24-hour

operations, and may create or exacerbate land use compatibility, noise, air quality, greenhouse

gas, public health, public safety, emergency response, and infrastructure capacity concerns;

WHEREAS, approval of new data centers or similar high-intensity computing uses, or


the expansion or intensification of existing such uses, during the period in which the City

continues studying and considering policy and regulatory changes could result in developments

that are inconsistent with or conflict with the land use policies, zoning standards, infrastructure

planning, or other regulations being contemplated, and could thereby frustrate or substantially

undermine the effectiveness of any future regulations;

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that there remains a current and immediate threat to


the public health, safety, and welfare because applications for data centers and similar high

intensity computing uses may be accepted, processed, and approved under existing regulations

that do not adequately address the potentially significant infrastructure, environmental, and land

use compatibility issues associated with such uses;

WHEREAS, the City Council further finds that approval of additional subdivisions, use


permits, variances, building permits, site development permits, grading permits, electrical

permits, mechanical permits, business licenses, or any other applicable entitlement, permit,

license, clearance, or approval for the establishment, expansion, or intensification of data centers

or similar high-intensity computing uses during the continued study period would result in the

threat to public health, safety, and welfare described in this Ordinance by allowing such uses to

proceed before the City can determine and adopt appropriate permanent regulations;

WHEREAS, Government Code section 65858(a) authorizes the City Council, after


notice pursuant to Government Code section 65090 and a public hearing, to extend an interim

urgency ordinance a second and final time for a period not exceeding one year, upon adoption by

a four-fifths (4/5) vote of the City Council;

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that this second and final extension of Ordinance


No. 1838 is narrowly tailored, in both scope and duration, because it temporarily maintains the

status quo while the City completes its study and considers appropriate permanent policy and

regulatory responses;

WHEREAS, on October 7, 2026, the City Council conducted a duly noticed public


hearing to consider this second and final extension of Ordinance No. 1838, received and

considered all public testimony, the staff report, the additional written report required by

Government Code section 65858, and all evidence in the administrative record;

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to extend Ordinance No. 1838 and the interim


moratorium established, as previously extended, for a second and final time authorized by

Government Code section 65858.

NOW, THEREFORE, THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF INDIO DOES ORDAIN AS FOLLOWS:


SECTION 1. FINDINGS.


The City Council finds and determines that the above recitals are true and correct and

incorporates them herein as substantive findings in support of this Ordinance. The City Council

further finds that there remains a current and immediate threat to the public health, safety, and

welfare, and that the approval of additional subdivisions, use permits, variances, building

permits, site development permits, grading permits, business licenses, or other applicable

entitlement or approval for the establishment, expansion, or intensification of data centers and

similar high-intensity computing uses would result in that threat.

Page 21 of 23

SECTION 2. SECOND AND FINAL EXTENSION OF INTERIM MORATORIUM.


Pursuant to Government Code section 65858, Urgency Ordinance No. 1838 and the interim

moratorium established thereby, as previously extended, are hereby extended for a second and

final time for one (1) year following expiration of the current extension.

The moratorium shall remain in full force and effect through October 16, 2027, unless earlier

terminated by the City Council or superseded by permanent regulations that become effective

before that date. This extension constitutes the second and final extension authorized by

Government Code section 65858.

SECTION 3. CONTINUED APPLICABILITY OF ORDINANCE NO. 1838.


During the extended moratorium period, all prohibitions, definitions, exemptions, procedures,

and requirements set forth in Ordinance No. 1838, as previously extended, shall remain in full

force and effect, except as expressly modified by this Ordinance.

SECTION 4. CONTINUED STUDY AND REVIEW.


During the extended moratorium period, the City Manager, or designee, is authorized and

directed to continue and complete the study and review directed by Ordinance No. 1838 and the

First Extension, including preparation and processing of potential amendments to the General

Plan, Unified Development Code, specific plans, development standards, and other applicable

regulations to address data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses. The City Manager,

or designee, is further authorized to return to the Planning Commission, Sustainability

Commission, City Council, or other appropriate bodies for additional review, recommendation,

or direction. Any permanent regulations shall be considered in accordance with applicable law

and following the required public review and hearing process.

SECTION 5. WRITTEN REPORT.


The City Council finds that the additional written report required by Government Code section

65858(d) has been prepared and issued, and that the staff report and supporting materials

describe the measures taken to alleviate the conditions that led to adoption and extension of

Ordinance No. 1838.

SECTION 6. CEQA.


The City Council finds that this Ordinance, which temporarily maintains the regulatory status

quo while the City continues to study potential permanent land use regulations, is not a “project”

subject to the California Environmental Quality Act (“CEQA”) because it does not have the

potential to result in either a direct physical change in the environment or a reasonably

foreseeable indirect physical change in the environment pursuant to CEQA Guidelines section

15060(c)(2).

Page 22 of 23

Alternatively, the City Council finds that this Ordinance is exempt from CEQA pursuant to

CEQA Guidelines section 15061(b)(3) because the extension of the existing temporary

moratorium will not result in a direct or reasonably foreseeable indirect physical change in the

environment and there is no possibility that the extension may have a significant effect on the

environment. To the extent applicable, the City Council further finds that the Ordinance is

exempt pursuant to CEQA Guidelines sections 15308 as an action taken by a regulatory agency

to assure the maintenance, restoration, enhancement, or protection of the environment.

SECTION 7. SEVERABILITY.


If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, or portion of this Ordinance is for any reason

held to be invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not

affect the validity of the remaining portions of this Ordinance. The City Council declares that it

would have adopted this Ordinance and each section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, and

portion thereof, irrespective of the fact that any one or more portions may be declared invalid or

unconstitutional.

SECTION 8. EFFECTIVE DATE; VOTE REQUIRED.


The City Council finds and declares, based upon the facts and findings stated in this Ordinance

and in Ordinance No. 1838, that this Ordinance is necessary for the immediate preservation of

the public peace, health, and safety.

This Ordinance is adopted pursuant to Government Code section 65858 and shall take effect

immediately upon its adoption by at least a four-fifths (4/5) vote of the City Council.

SECTION 9. PUBLICATION.


The City Clerk shall certify to the adoption of this Ordinance and shall cause the same to be

published or posted as required by law.
