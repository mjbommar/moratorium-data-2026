                                 ORDINANCE NO. ___________

  AN URGENCY ORDINANCE, PURSUANT TO GOVERNMENT CODE SECTION
   65858, OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF RICHMOND, CALIFORNIA
        ESTABLISHING A 45-DAY TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE
 ESTABLISHMENT OF NEW DATA CENTERS WITHIN THE CITY OF RICHMOND
   PENDING COMPLETION OF A COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW OF THE CITY’S
 GENERAL PLAN, ZONING ORDINANCE, RELATED LAND USE REGULATIONS
  AND A PUBLIC PLANNING PROCESS TO EVALUATE WHETHER AND UNDER
 WHAT REGULATIONS DATA CENTERS MAY BE PERMITTED, AND DECLARING
                        THE URGENCY THEREOF

         WHEREAS, the increasing demand for data centers associated with the expansion of
artificial intelligence and cloud computing has resulted in growing development interest
throughout the San Francisco Bay Area, warranting careful evaluation of the potential impacts on
local land use, electricity demand, water resources, infrastructure, and community priorities
before these facilities are approved; and

        WHEREAS, the City Council finds that the increasing interest in data center
development may have significant implications for electricity demand, water resources, public
infrastructure, land use compatibility, noise, air quality, aesthetics, and other factors affecting the
public health, safety, and welfare of Richmond residents that warrant further study before data
centers are established within the City; and

       WHEREAS, the City is evaluating whether its General Plan and Zoning Ordinance
adequately regulate data centers as a distinct land use; and

        WHEREAS, the City's Zoning Ordinance does not contain land use regulations
specifically addressing data centers or standards to evaluate and mitigate their potential impacts,
including but not limited to energy consumption, water demand, noise, air quality, visual
character, neighborhood compatibility, and other effects on surrounding properties and the
quality of life of Richmond residents; and

       WHEREAS, data centers differ from traditional industrial and office uses because they
may involve substantial electrical infrastructure, emergency backup generation, battery energy
storage systems, cooling infrastructure, utility demands, operational noise, and other
characteristics not specifically addressed by existing City regulations; and

         WHEREAS, a temporary moratorium will provide the City with time to undertake a
public planning process to determine whether data centers should be permitted within Richmond
and, if so, consider appropriate amendments to the General Plan, Zoning Ordinance, and other
applicable regulations to ensure that future data center development is consistent with the City's
planning objectives and community priorities; and

         NOW, THEREFORE, the City Council of Richmond does hereby ordain as follows:

SECTION 1: That the above recitals are true and correct and are adopted as the City Council’s
findings.

SECTION 2: Purpose and Authority.
In the interest of promoting the health, safety, and welfare of the residents in the City of
Richmond, it is the purpose and intent of this ordinance to place a temporary moratorium on the
issuance of any new permit, license, or other entitlement for any data center in the City of
Richmond. The City Council adopts this ordinance pursuant to Article XI, section 7 of the
California Constitution and Government Code sections 36937 and 65858 as an urgency measure
prohibiting any use that may conflict with a contemplated general plan, specific plan, or zoning
proposal that the city is considering or studying.

SECTION 3: Findings.
A.      Data centers present operational characteristics materially different from traditional
office, warehouse, research and development, and industrial uses, including but not limited to
significant electrical infrastructure demands, emergency backup generation, battery energy
storage systems, specialized cooling infrastructure, operational noise, utility demand, and other
land use characteristics requiring careful evaluation.

B.      The City Council finds that the City's current zoning regulations do not establish a
distinct land use classification or development standards governing the establishment and
operation of data centers.

C.     The City has initiated a planning effort to evaluate whether amendments to the General
Plan, Zoning Ordinance, development standards, infrastructure and utility policies,
environmental review procedures, and related regulations are appropriate to regulate data centers
within Richmond or, alternatively, whether such uses should be prohibited.

D.      The City Council finds that there is a current and immediate threat to the public health,
safety, or welfare because, absent a temporary moratorium, applications for the establishment,
construction, conversion, or expansion of data centers could be submitted, processed, approved,
and vested under regulations that do not specifically address the operational, environmental,
infrastructure, and public-safety characteristics of such uses. The City Council further finds that
approval of additional subdivisions, use permits, variances, building permits, or other applicable
entitlements for data center development during the City's study would result in or exacerbate
that threat.

E.       Data centers may impose large, concentrated, and continuous electrical loads and may
require upgrades to substations, transformers, transmission or distribution facilities, and backup
generation systems. The City has not yet completed the technical and policy review necessary to
determine whether and under what conditions such demands can be accommodated without
adversely affecting grid reliability, infrastructure capacity, emergency operations, or existing
utility customers.

F.      Data centers may also involve substantial potable or recycled water demand, cooling and
wastewater infrastructure, lithium-ion batteries, fuel storage, emergency generators, hazardous
materials, and continuous mechanical equipment. The City has not yet adopted data-center-
specific standards addressing water efficiency and drought response, fire and hazardous-
materials response, noise, air quality, heat, vibration, and compatibility with nearby residential
and other sensitive uses.

G.       The City Council further finds that completion of the public planning study described
herein is necessary before determining whether to permit data centers and, if allowed, to
establish permanent regulations governing the location and operation of data centers within the
City.

Considering these findings and all evidence in the record, including, but not limited to, the staff
report accompanying this Urgency Ordinance, the City Council finds that this moratorium is
necessary for the immediate preservation of the public health, safety, and welfare and satisfies
the requirements of Government Code sections 36937 and 65858.

Section 4: Environmental Review/California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA).
This ordinance is intended to preserve the status quo, and it will not cause a direct physical
change in the environment, nor a reasonably foreseeable indirect physical change in the
environment. The enactment of this ordinance, therefore, does not constitute the approval of a
project under the California Environmental Quality Act (“CEQA”), and it is exempt from CEQA.
(Pub. Res. Code §21065, CEQA Guidelines §§15060(c)(2)(3); 15061(b)(3); 15064(d); 1537(a);
15378(a))

Section 5: Definitions.
As used herein the following definition shall apply:

A. “Data center” means a facility, campus, building, structure, or portion thereof used primarily
or substantially for the housing, operation, or support of computer servers, server racks, data-
storage devices, networking equipment, switching equipment, or similar electronic equipment
used for commercial remote data storage, processing, or transmission; cloud computing;
colocation services; artificial-intelligence model training or inference; high-performance
computing; blockchain operations; digital-asset mining; cryptocurrency mining; or other similar
high-intensity computing activities. A data center may include associated cooling equipment,
generators, fuel storage, battery energy storage systems, fire-suppression systems, substations,
and other utility or security infrastructure supporting the primary use.

B. “Subordinate-use data room” or “server room” means an enclosed room, suite, or other
accessory space containing computer servers, data-storage equipment, network-switching
equipment, telecommunications equipment, and related supporting equipment that is used solely
in conjunction with and is incidental and subordinate to a lawfully established primary use on the
same lot or site. Such a room may not be used for independent commercial, wholesale, or third-
party data processing, storage, or transmission and is not a data center for purposes of this
ordinance.

Section 6: Application.
A. During the term of this ordinance, the establishment, construction, conversion, or expansion
of any data center shall be prohibited in all zoning districts and lands within the City of
Richmond. The City shall not accept, process, grant, approve, or issue any application for a use
permit, variance, building permit, license, certificate of occupancy, subdivision map, parcel map,
tentative map, general plan amendment, zone change, specific plan, development agreement, site
plan review, grading permit, or other permit or land use entitlement for any data center.

B. This moratorium does not apply to a subordinate-use data room or server room, as defined
herein.

C. Notwithstanding any other provision of this ordinance, any person who lawfully holds an
existing permit, license, and/or land use entitlement for an existing and lawfully operating data
center in the City of Richmond as of the effective date of this ordinance may continue to operate
under the standards governing such permit, license, and/or entitlement under existing law.
Nothing herein shall limit the authority of the City to revoke or suspend any existing license,
permit, and/or entitlement as authorized under existing law.

Section 8: No Mandatory Duty of Care.
This ordinance is not intended to and shall not be construed or given effect in a manner that
imposes upon the City or any officer or employee thereof a mandatory duty of care towards
persons and property within or without the City, so as to provide a basis of civil liability for
damages, except as otherwise imposed by law.

Section 9: Severability.
If any provision of this ordinance or the application thereof to any person or circumstances is
held invalid, such invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications of the ordinance
which can be given effect without the invalid provision or application, and to this end the
provisions of this ordinance are severable. This City Council hereby declares that it would have
adopted this ordinance irrespective of the invalidity of any particular portion thereof and intends
that the invalid portions should be severed and the balance of the ordinance be enforced.

Section 10: Effective Date and Publication.
This ordinance, passed by at least a four-fifths vote of the City Council, is declared to be an
urgency ordinance for the immediate preservation of the public peace, health, and safety and
shall take effect and be enforced immediately upon adoption pursuant to Government Code
sections 36937 and 65858. The City Clerk shall certify to the passage of this ordinance and shall
cause the ordinance, or a summary thereof, to be published or posted as required by Government
Code section 36933.
