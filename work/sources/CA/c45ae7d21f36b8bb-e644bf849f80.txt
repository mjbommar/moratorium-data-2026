URGENCY ORDINANCE NO. 14-26 N.S.

AN URGENCY ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF
RICHMOND, CALIFORNIA, PURSUANT TO GOVERNMENT CODE SECTION 65858,
AMENDING, RESTATING, AND SUPERSEDING URGENCY ORDINANCE NO. 13-26
NS. AND EXTENDING FOR 22 MONTHS AND 15 DAYS THE TEMPORARY
MORATORIUM ON THE ESTABLISHMENT OF NEW DATA CENTERS WITHIN THE
CITY OF RICHMOND, PENDING COMPLETION OF A COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW
OF THE CITY'S GENERAL PLAN, ZONING ORDINANCE, AND RELATED LAND
USE REGULATIONS AND A PUBLIC PLANNING PROCESS TO EVALUATE
WHETHER AND UNDER WHAT REGULATIONS DATA CENTERS MAY BE
PERMITTED, AND DECLARING THE URGENCY THEREOF

WHEREAS, the increasing demand for data centers associated with the expansion of
artificial intelligence and cloud computing has resulted in growing development interest
throughout the San Francisco Bay Area, warranting careful evaluation of the potential impacts on
local land use, electricity demand, water resources, infrastructure, and community priorities
before these facilities are approved; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that the increasing interest in data center
development may have significant implications for electricity demand, water resources, public
infrastructure, land use compatibility, noise, air quality, aesthetics, and other factors affecting the
public health, safety, and welfare of Richmond residents that warrant further study before data
centers are established within the City; and

WHEREAS, the City is evaluating whether its General Plan and Zoning Ordinance
adequately regulate data centers as a distinct land use; and

WHEREAS, the City's Zoning Ordinance does not contain land use regulations
specifically addressing data centers or standards to evaluate and mitigate their potential impacts,
including but not limited to energy consumption, water demand, noise, air quality, visual
character, neighborhood compatibility, and other effects on surrounding properties and the
quality of life of Richmond residents; and

WHEREAS, data centers differ from traditional industrial and office uses because they
may involve substantial electrical infrastructure, emergency backup generation, battery energy
storage systems, cooling infrastructure, utility demands, operational noise, and other
characteristics not specifically addressed by existing City regulations; and

WHEREAS, a temporary moratorium will provide the City with time to undertake a
public planning process to determine whether data centers should be permitted within Richmond
and, if so, consider appropriate amendments to the General Plan, Zoning Ordinance, and other
applicable regulations to ensure that future data center development is consistent with the City's
planning objectives and community priorities; and

WHEREAS, on September 15, 2026, following notice given under Government Code
section 65090 and a public hearing, and by a four-fifths vote, the City Council adopted Urgency
Ordinance No. 13-26 N.S. under Government Code section 65858(b), establishing a 45-day
moratorium on new data centers, which will expire on October 30, 2026; and

WHEREAS, at least 10 days before the expiration of Ordinance No. 13-26 N.S., the City
issued the written report required by Government Code section 65858(d), describing the
measures the City has taken to alleviate the conditions that led to the moratorium, and that report
is incorporated in the record; and

WHEREAS, the City Council now amends, restates, and supersedes Ordinance No. 13-26
N.S. through this single ordinance and, under Government Code section 65858(b), extends the
moratorium once for 22 months and 15 days—the longest single extension the statute allows for

Section 7: Term of Ordinance.

This ordinance amends, restates, and supersedes Urgency Ordinance No. 13-26 N.S. It extends
the moratorium, under Government Code section 65858(b), for a single period of 22 months and
15 days measured from the October 30, 2026 expiration of Ordinance No. 13-26 N.S. The
moratorium remains in effect through and including September 15, 2028, and expires at midnight
on that date unless the City Council terminates it earlier. This term is the longest single extension
Government Code section 65858 allows for an interim ordinance adopted after notice and a
public hearing, and the moratorium will not remain in effect for more than two years in total.

Section 8: No Mandatory Duty of Care.

This ordinance does not impose, and must not be construed to impose, on the City or any of its
officers or employees a mandatory duty of care toward persons or property inside or outside the
City that would create a basis for civil liability for damages, except as otherwise required by law.

Section 9: Severability.

If. court holds any provision of this ordinance, or its application to any person or circumstance,
invalid, that invalidity does not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect
without the invalid provision or application. To this end, the provisions of this ordinance are
severable. The City Council declares that it would have adopted this ordinance regardless of the
invalidity of any portion of it, and it intends that the invalid portions be severed and the balance
of the ordinance be enforced.

Section 10: Effective Date and Publication.

This ordinance, passed by at least a four-fifths vote of the City Council as required by Article III,
section 7 of the Richmond City Charter and Government Code sections 36937 and 65858, is an
urgency ordinance for the immediate preservation of the public peace, health, and safety. It takes
effect and must be enforced immediately upon adoption. The City Clerk must certify y the passage
of this ordinance and must cause the ordinance, or a summary of it, to be published or posted as
required by Government Code section 36933.
