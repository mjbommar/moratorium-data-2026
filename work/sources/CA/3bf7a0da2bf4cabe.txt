INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

The Delta Street Sewer Pump Station is a three (3) story wet well/dry well pump located at 440 West Main
Street (APN) 023-070-007. The well was constructed in 1977 and has been operating continuously for
the past 45 years. The Delta Street Sewer Pump Station handles the entire gravity wastewater collection
system flow for the City of Calipatria and transmits this flow to the Calipatria wastewater treatment
plant, located 3 miles northwest of the city at 106 Lindsey Road (APN) 022-160-004. Given the pump
station’s importance and continuous operation, the station requires significant maintenance and major
rehabilitation work every 10 years. The last recorded rehabilitation improvement work was completed
over 14 years ago in 2011. While the last improvements were completed in 2011, many of the essential
components of the pump station have received substantial operational damage and are reaching the
end of their useful life.

Components such as the electrical motor control center, load center, and control system panels have
been continuously exposed to wastewater gases for the past 34 years and are in need of replacement.
Other components such as the electrical system and flow metering system are more than 34 years old
and also require replacement. Furthermore, the highly concentrated and corrosive sanitary sewer gases
have significantly damaged the concrete wet well, steel stairs, landings, and electrical power and
control systems. Given the pump station’s importance, the pump station requires significant
rehabilitation to ensure the safe and continuous operations of the Delta Street Sewer Pump Station.


The City received funding from the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) and the North American
Development Bank (NADBank). Additionally, the City of Calipatria was awarded $1.5M from the Regional
Early Action Planning (REAP) 2.0 Program by the Regional Utilities to Support Housing (RUSH) under the
Southern California Association of Governments (SCAG) to repair the Delta Street Pump Station. The REAP
2.0 program is a competitive grant program designed to accelerate housing production through
investments in systemwide, sustainable utility infrastructure. The REAP 2.0 program prioritizes projects
that directly support housing development, including water, wastewater, stormwater management, and
electrical infrastructure. By promoting innovative utility planning and targeted infrastructure investments,
the program aims to reduce development costs and expand housing capacity in infill areas. The City is in
the process of repairing the Delta Street Pump Station to address current deterioration issues to ensure
housing sustainability and promote residential development within the Eastside Specific Plan area. While
the project is near the end of the construction phase, the City of Calipatria would like to host a workshop
to promote engagement with the community and provide an update on the status of the project.

Per the Section 3.2.2 of the REAP 2.0 — Programs to Accelerate Transformational Housing (PATH) Program
Guidelines, all award grantees are subject to outreach and engagement, especially with Disadvantaged
and Historically Underserved community members while the project is underway and through the funding
expenditure date. Outreach methods must consider language access and other potential barriers to
providing input. Thus, the purpose of this meeting is to hold a public workshop and comply with Section
3.2.2 of the program’s guidelines. In this workshop, the public will have the opportunity to comment on
any of the following topics related to the project: Project purpose and benefits; Construction schedule
and impacts; Traffic, access, noise, and safety considerations; Environmental considerations; Funding
sources and use of public funds; and Opportunities for public input. It is important to note these are only
topic suggestions and anyone participating in the meeting may comment on their desired topic related to
the Delta Street Pump Station.

ANALYSIS

Outreach and Engagement Plan

The City of Calipatria also prepared an Outreach and Engagement Plan (Attachment A) which details a
goal statement, goals and objectives, target audiences, methods and tools, outreach topics, and project
reporting. The purpose of this Outreach and Engagement Plan is to ensure meaningful, inclusive, and
transparent public participation throughout the planning, design, and implementation of the Delta Street
Pump Station. The City seeks to engage residents, stakeholders, and the community to inform project
decisions, identify community needs, and build public awareness and support. Outreach efforts will
prioritize accessibility and compliance with applicable state and federal public participation requirements.

One of the City’s goals during the outreach process is to inform the community in the project’s main
outcome to achieve, which is housing production in the Eastside Specific Plan area through the increase
in utility capacity. The project area boundary is delineated by Freeman Street on the north, Industrial
Avenue on the west turning east on Bonita Street, south on Brown Avenue, and east towards Church
Street until reaching and turning north on East Avenue to circle back to the northern boundary of Freeman
Street again. There are several vacant properties located within the subject site which are either in the
process of or have the potential to be developed into single-family and multi-family residences. The
project site has been identified as a priority focus area in terms of residential development and to assist
the current low-income and very-low income residents of the area. The project site has experienced a
significant decline over the past decades, but the City has made it a priority development area over the


past five years by establishing the Calipatria Eastside Specific Plan to identify key infrastructure
improvements needed to promote affordable housing in the project area.

Project Scope

The project scope of work includes the rehabilitation of the wet well by hydro blasting the walls, ceiling,
and floor at 36,000 psi to remove all existing coating and deteriorated concrete. Spalled concrete will be
replaced with a high strength mortar or cement. The interior of the concrete wall will be coated with 80
mils of a 100 percent high solids epoxy designed for wastewater immersion. All steel support members
and steel extrusion grating will be sandblasted and then coated. Also, the replacement of the existing
electrical and control system, and installation of a new single phase load center and transformer. A new
flow metering system will also be installed. The project also includes the installation of an Air Blower
System to remove corrosive gases from both the pump station wet well and dry well. Existing centrifugal
pumps, shafts, piping, valves, and motors will be upgraded and refurbished.

Project Status

The design of the project was completed on November 2024. In January 2025, the construction contract
was awarded to Pacific Hydrotech Corporation. The Construction Phase of the project is currently at
approximately 80% completion. The contractor has experienced equipment delays and is currently waiting
for equipment to arrive to continue the last items of construction. The equipment, being an odor
scrubbing system, is expected to arrive early September 2026 which places the project for an anticipated
end date of mid-October 2026. It is important to note that the project received a grant extension deadline
to December 2026.
