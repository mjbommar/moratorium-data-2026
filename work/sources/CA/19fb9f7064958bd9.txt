Re: SB 675 (Padilla) — Imperial County Air Pollution Control District — Request for Veto

Dear Governor Newsom:

On behalf of the City of [CITY], I respectfully request that you veto SB 675. The City supports what this
bill seeks to accomplish — a broader and more diverse air district governing board, and greater operational
transparency. Our objection is to how it would be paid for.

SB 675 adds subdivision (e) to Section 40701.5 of the Health and Safety Code. Any expense of the Imperial
County Air Pollution Control District not covered by grants, permit fees, and penalties would be met by an
annual per capita assessment on every city in the county and on the County itself. The City of [CITY] pays
no such assessment today. Under SB 675 we would pay one every year, in an amount set by the district’s
governing board — with no city council vote, no appeal, and no dollar limit anywhere in the statute.

The shortfall is unlikely to be small. SB 675 carries no appropriation, and the district cannot close the gap
through permit fees, which Section 41512.7 limits to a 15 percent increase in any year. An independent
analysis prepared for the County by Dr. Steven B. Frates, Senior Fellow at the Davenport Institute of
Pepperdine University’s School of Public Policy, puts ongoing costs at $140,000 to $650,000 a year before
facilities, and the County’s own estimate submitted to the Legislature is substantially higher. Divided by
population, a $1 million annual shortfall would cost a city of 20,000 residents roughly $110,000 every year,
indefinitely.

That is not an obligation this City can absorb at the margin. Our general fund is fully committed, our revenues
are not growing at a pace that would meet a new recurring charge of this size, and we would have no authority
to decline it. In practical terms it would potentially be paid out of police and fire staffing, street maintenance,
and the basic services our residents rely on most. This is a difficult moment for California cities to take on a
permanent, uncapped charge that the State has not funded. We would also have limited say in setting it: the
new eleven-member board seats five city council members for seven incorporated cities, so at least two cities
will have no representative on the body that decides what they pay.

We respectfully ask that you veto SB 675 and encourage the author to return in 2027 with legislation that
achieves the same objectives on an achievable timeline and with an identified funding source. The City of
[CITY] will participate in that process in good faith.

Thank you for your consideration.

WHAT SB 675 COSTS YOUR CITY

Five things every Imperial County city should know

1. Your city will be billed every year.
SB 675 lets the air district charge every city in Imperial County for whatever its own budget does not cover.
All seven cities pay, not just the ones with a seat on the board. Cities pay nothing today.

2. The air district board decides the amount. Your council does not vote on it.
Here is how your city’s bill gets set. Each year the air district adopts its budget. Whatever grants, permit fees,
and fines do not cover is the shortfall. The board then splits that shortfall among the seven cities and the
County according to population and bills each one. The bill calls this an “annual per capita assessment.”

The eleven-member air district board decides the shortfall amount, and therefore your city's share. Your
council does not vote on it, cannot reject it, and has no appeal. The only requirement is that the board hoid
one public hearing 30 days before imposing it. Nothing in SB 675 sets a ceiling on how large the assessment
can be.

3. The bill costs millions, and the State pays none of it.
The County's independent analysis puts one-time transition costs at $75,000 to $650,000 and ongoing annual
costs at $140,000 to $650,000, before facilities, and finds that building an independent IT system alone would
run into the millions. SB 675 provides no state funding for any of it.

Permit fees cannot close the gap. State law caps annual permit fee increases at 15 percent. The district
collected about $1.7 million in permit fees last year, so year one can add only about $250,000. And 79 percent
of the district's revenue is restricted grant money, spendable only on the programs it was awarded for. The
Legislature has adjusted that fee cap for other districts; South Coast is exempt outright, San Diego has set
fees closer to actual cost since 1998. Imperial got nothing, in a county with a fraction of San Diego's population
and twice its poverty rate. Whatever the County pays comes out of the same general fund that supports sheriff
patrols, roads, and public health in our rural, unincorporated communities.

And because the Air District’s only other option is raising permit fees, the cost also lands on the growers,
feedlots, and small businesses that make up Imperial County’s economic base. Those costs get passed on to
customers and employees. Every resident of Imperial County pays for SB 675 one way or another.

4. Here is the math for your city.
Take your population, divide by 10,000, and multiply. Based on a countywide population of 181,411:

If the district comes up short by... Your city pays, per 10,000 residents
$1 million a year $55,000 a year
$2.5 million a year $138,000 a year
$4.5 million a year $248,000 a year

Ex: a city of 20,000 residents pays about $110,000 a year if the shortfall is $1 million — and about $496,000 a year if it is $4.5 million.

5. Cities get 5 of 11 seats. Two cities get none.
The new board is one supervisor, five city council members, one irrigation district member, and four appointed
members. The five city seats are assigned one per supervisorial district. With seven cities and five seats, at
least two cities will have no one on the board that sets their bill.
WHAT WE ARE ASKING
SB 675 requires all of this to be done by July 1, 2027. An independent analysis concludes it would take at least
three years. San Diego’s change took from 2019 to 2021 and is still not finished.
The Governor must sign or veto SB 675 by September 30. The County has requested a veto so the bill can return
in 2027 with real funding and a workable deadline.
We are asking your city to send the Governor a letter requesting a veto. The County will provide the draft, ready
for your letterhead and signature.

Letters must reach the Governor’s Office by September 4, 2026.
