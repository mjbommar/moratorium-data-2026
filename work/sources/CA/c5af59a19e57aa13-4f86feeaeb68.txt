ORDINANCE NO. 2026-19


AN EXTENSION OF AN INTERIM URGENCY ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF ESCONDIDO, CALIFORNIA, PURSUANT TO CALIFORNIA GOVERNMENT CODE SECTION 65858, CONTINUING A MORATORIUM FOR TEN (10) MONTHS AND FIFTEEN (15) DAYS ON THE ESTABLISHMENT, EXPANSION, OR INTENSIFICATION OF DATA CENTERS AND OTHER SIMILAR HIGH-INTENSITY COMPUTING USES WITHIN THE CITY OF ESCONDIDO; AND FINDING THE ACTION TO BE EXEMPT FROM CEQAQA


WHEREAS, the City of Escondido, California, (“City”) is a general law municipal corporation, duly

organized and operating under the constitution and laws of the State of California, with the authority to

adopt and enforce land use regulations within its boundaries pursuant to its constitutional police powers

and the Planning and Zoning Law; and

WHEREAS, on June 7, 2023, the City adopted its Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy

(2023-2028) (“CEDS”) which identified and established, among other things, Fundamental 3 – Actively

Maximize Value of Land, which found that “it is crucial that land is put to its best use. Inefficient land use,

such as static storage for goods or cars should be discouraged. Land that is zoned for housing

(Fundamental 1) should do just that, and land that is zoned for creating jobs (Fundamental 2) should do

that… [L]and-scarce Escondido [should] get more people and jobs out of every acre.” CEDS at p. 56); and

WHEREAS, neighboring jurisdictions have received, and the City reasonably anticipates receiving,

applications, pre-applications, inquiries, and expressions of interest for the development of data centers

and other similar high-intensity computing uses within the City; and

WHEREAS, in general, a “data center” is a physical building or facility that houses and supports

the operation of computer servers, networking equipment, or similar electronic equipment and

information technology infrastructure for building, running and delivering applications and services, data

processing, storage transmission, cloud computing, or other similar high intensity computing activities;

and

WHEREAS, data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses may require extraordinary

electrical demand, substation or transmission improvements, substantial cooling systems, backup

generators, water and wastewater capacity, and 24-hour operations, and may thereby create or

exacerbate land use compatibility, noise, air quality, greenhouse gas, public health, public safety,

emergency response, and infrastructure capacity concerns; and

WHEREAS, the City’s existing General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning regulations, and other

applicable land use regulations do not currently define, classify, or comprehensively regulate data centers

and similar high-intensity computing uses, including with respect to their permissible locations, size and

intensity, utility demand, water consumption, cooling systems, backup generation, buffering, noise,

environmental performance, decommissioning, and community benefits; and

WHEREASa “data center” as a use is not expressly identified with the City’s Municipal Code;

,

however, similar such uses may be permitted within the General Industrial Zone via a Plot Plan Permit as

identified within Article 61 (Administration and Enforcement), Division 8 (Plot Plans); and

WHEREAS, a “data processing” use is expressly allowed within at least one specific plan area

identified within the City: the Escondido Research and Technology Center (“ERTC”); and

WHEREAS, the City's current Zoning Regulations do not contain development criteria specifically

for data center facilities; and

WHEREAS, the City Council intends within a reasonable time to study, consider, and potentially

adopt amendments to the City’s General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning ordinance, specific plans,

development standards, and other applicable regulations to address whether, where, and under what

conditions data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses should be allowed within the City; and

WHEREAS, approval of new data centers or similar high-intensity computing uses, or the

expansion or intensification of existing such uses, under the City’s current regulations during the period

in which the City is studying and considering such policy and regulatory changes could result in

developments that are inconsistent with or in conflict with the land use policies, zoning standards,

infrastructure planning, or other regulations being contemplated, and could thereby frustrate or

substantially undermine the effectiveness of any future updates; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that there is a current and immediate threat to the public health,

safety, and welfare because, absent a temporary moratorium, applications for such uses may be accepted,

processed, and approved under regulations that do not adequately address the potentially significant

infrastructure, environmental, operational, and land use compatibility issues associated with such uses;

and

WHEREAS, the City Council further finds that the approval of additional entitlements, permits, or

other approvals for such uses during the period of study would result in that threat to the public health,

safety, and welfare by allowing the commitment of land, utility capacity, water resources, and related

infrastructure before the City can determine and adopt appropriate standards and safeguards; and

WHEREAS, the City is unaware of any previously approved, newly proposed, or pending data

center projects or applications that may be affected by this interim ordinance or the implementation of

a temporary moratorium; and

WHEREAS, in 2025, the Governor signed into law Senate Bill 57 ("SB 57") also known as the

Ratepayer and Technological Innovation Protection Act, which allows the California Public Utility

Commission (“CPUC”) to assess the extent to which electrical corporation costs associated with new loads

from data centers result in cost shifts to other electrical corporation customers, of which the findings of

such shall be published publicly no later than January 1, 2027; and

WHEREAS, pursuant to SB 57, the California legislature found the quickly evolving development


of artificial intelligence requires large-format data centers that currently require extremely large loads of

electricity and water, and that if managed incorrectly, it could pose a serious threat to California’s climate

goals and, more importantly, leave existing ratepayers saddled with the enormous costs of stranded

assets built to support that industry; and

WHEREAS, whether or not an Applicant's proposed project requires local entitlement approvals,

there remain significant public safety-related concernsthat must be analyzed prior to any data center or

similar project moving forward within Escondido; and

WHEREAS, laws and regulations continue to be enacted within California and nationwide


surrounding data centers. Data center facilities create particularly unique water and energy demands. Data

center demands fueled by the Artificial Intelligence boom are yet to be fully studied and wide-reaching

effects still to be determined. However, current literature on data centers suggests impacts, such as

substantial water and electrical infrastructure demand thereby leading to increased climatic effects such

as drought and urban heat, along with encumbering land with low economically driving uses, has the

potential to create an adverse impact to the City. The City of Escondido has not had an adequate

opportunity to undertake a comprehensive study of the developing technologies and designs such that it

can publish appropriate development guidelines for these projects to ensure the public’s welfare and

safety; and

WHEREAS, California Government Code Section 65858 and 36937(b) authorize the City Council to

adopt an interim ordinance effective immediately and prohibiting any uses that may be in conflict with a

contemplated general plan, specific plan, or zoning proposal that the City is considering, studying, or intends to

study within a reasonable time; and

WHEREAS, the City Council further finds that this urgency ordinance is narrowly tailored, in both

scope and duration, by temporarily prohibiting the establishment, expansion, or intensification of data

centers and similar high-intensity computing uses while the City considers appropriate policy and

regulatory responses; and

WHEREAS, the City Council recognizes that data centers and similar uses may provide certain

economic benefits, but finds that it is in the public interest to temporarily pause new or expanded

approvals until the City can comprehensively evaluate whether and under what conditions such uses are

compatible with the City’s long-term land use, infrastructure, environmental, and community objectives;

and

WHEREAS, Government Code Section 65858 further providesthat such an urgency measure shall

be effective for only 45 days following its adoption, but may be extended beyond the initial 45-day period,

following compliance with that Section, for an additional 10 months and 15 days and subsequently for an

additional 12 months; and

WHEREAS, while this interim ordinance is in effect, the City intends to study and consider land

use development policies and standards related to data center facilities that should be added to the City's

General Plan and Zoning Regulations.

WHEREAS, the City Council has the authority under Government Code Sections 36934 and

36937(b) to adopt an ordinance that takes effect immediately if it is an ordinance for the immediate

preservation of the public peace, health or safety, contains a declaration of the facts constituting the

urgency, and is passed by a four-fifths vote of the City Council; and

WHEREAS, the City finds it is necessary for the City Council to adopt this interim ordinance as an

Urgency Ordinance, as set forth in the above recitals, which are incorporated herein by reference, and

pursuant to the powers under Government Code Sections 36934 and 36937(b), for the immediate

preservation of the public health and safety; and

WHEREAS, the City Council, at its meeting of August 26, 2026, passed by 5-0 vote to approve

Interim Urgency Ordinance No. 2026-18 to establish a 45-day moratorium and to prohibit the

establishment, expansion, or intensification of Data Centers and other similar high-intensity computing

uses within the City of Escondido; and

WHEREAS, the City Council, at its meeting of September 30, 2026, received a 10-day Report

Regarding Measures Taken to Alleviate Conditions which Led to Adoption of Urgency Ordinance No. 2026-

18, as required by California Government Code Section 65858(d); and

WHEREAS, at its meeting of October 7, 2026, the City Council held a public hearing to consider

the extension of Ordinance No. 2026-18 for an additional ten (10) months and fifteen (15) days through

Ordinance No. 2026-19.

NOW, THEREFORE, the City Council of the City of Escondido does ordain as follows:

SECTION 1. FINDINGS.


The City Council hereby finds and determines that the above recitals are true and correct and

incorporates them herein as substantive findings in support of this ordinance. In addition, the City Council

specifically finds:

A. Neighboring jurisdictions have received, and the City reasonably anticipates receiving,

applications, pre-applications, inquiries, and expressions of interest for new data centers and similar high

intensity computing uses, [as well as for the expansion or intensification of existing such uses], and such

proposals may be processed and approved under existing regulations in the absence of this interim

ordinance.

B. Existing General Plan and zoning provisions do not comprehensively address whether, where,

and under what conditions data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses should be permitted,

nor do they establish adequate standards concerning location, size, utility demand, water use, cooling

systems, backup generation, noise, buffering, environmental performance, public health and safety

protections, or related operational characteristics.

C. Data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses may create substantial and unique

impacts including, but not limited to, significant electrical demand, increased water consumption,

substantial mechanical cooling equipment, noise from cooling systems and generators, air emissions from

backup generation, fire and life safety considerations, and demands on public infrastructure and

emergency services.

D. There is a reasonable likelihood that approvals granted during the City’s study and

consideration of potential policy and regulatory changes would be in conflict with, or would substantially

undermine, contemplated amendments to the General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning ordinance, specific

plans, or other regulations regulating such uses.

E. There is a current and immediate threat to the public health, safety, and welfare because the

City’s current regulatory framework does not adequately address the potential land use, environmental,

utility, infrastructure, and operational impacts associated with data centers and similar high-intensity

computing uses.

F. The approval of additional subdivisions, use permits, variances, building permits, site

development permits, grading permits, or any other applicable entitlement, permit, or approval for the

establishment, expansion, or intensification of data centers or similar high-intensity computing uses

during the City’s study period would result in the threat described above by allowing such uses to proceed

before appropriate standards and safeguards are established.

G. A temporary moratorium on the establishment, expansion, or intensification of data centers

and similar high-intensity computing uses is necessary to maintain the status quo while the City completes

its studies and considers any proposed policy and regulatory changes.

H. This Ordinance has no effect on the exempted data centers identified herein, if any, and does

not impair any vested rights legally obtained prior to the effective date of this ordinance.

I. The adoption of this interim urgency ordinance is therefore necessary for the immediate

preservation of the public peace, health, and safety within the meaning of Government Code section

65858.

SECTION 2. MORATORIUM IMPOSED.


A. Pursuant to Government Code section 65858, the interim moratorium, established by

Ordinance No. 2026-18, is hereby extended by Ordinance No. 2026-19 to prohibit the approval of any

applications, permits, or entitlements for the establishment, expansion, or intensification of any data

center use within the City of Escondido, except as expressly provided in this ordinance. This ordinance

does not apply to the exempted data centers identified in Section 5.B below.

B. During the effective period of this ordinance (“Moratorium Period”), no City official, officer,

employee, department, board, commission, or agency shall accept as complete, process, approve, issue,

or grant any discretionary or ministerial permit, approval, license, clearance, or entitlement that would

authorize any of the following:

1. The establishment, construction, installation, occupancy, or operation of any new data

center use;

2. The conversion of any existing building, structure, or site to a data center use;

3. The expansion, enlargement, or intensification of any existing data center use including,

but not limited to, increases in gross floor area devoted to such use, server capacity, rack density,

electrical service capacity, cooling capacity, backup generation capacity, or water demand; or

4. Any subdivision, site development, grading, building, electrical, mechanical, or similar

permit to the extent it would facilitate or enable any activity prohibited by this ordinance.

SECTION 3. DEFINITIONS.


A. For purposes of this ordinance, “Data Center Use” means any facility, campus, building,

structure, or portion thereof used primarily or substantially for the housing, operation, or support of

computer servers, server racks, data storage devices, networking equipment, switching equipment, or

similar electronic equipment for data processing, storage, transmission, cloud computing, colocation

services, artificial intelligence model training or inference, high-performance computing, blockchain

operations, digital asset mining, cryptocurrency mining, or other similar high intensity computing

activities. “Data Center Use” includes, without limitation, hyperscale data centers, colocation facilities,

server farms, cloud computing facilities, artificial intelligence compute facilities, digital asset mining

facilities, blockchain mining facilities, and other substantially similar uses, whether or not such use is

separately defined or listed in the Municipal Code.

B. “Data Center Use” does not include any of the following:

1. Customary accessory server rooms, IT rooms, or equipment rooms that are clearly

incidental and subordinate to a primary permitted use and that serve only on-site business,

institutional, governmental, or facility operations;

2. Ordinary office, retail, school, hospital, or industrial computer equipment used solely to

support on-site operations;

3. Public safety, emergency communications, or emergency operations facilities; or

4. Telecommunications carrier facilities or public utility facilities necessary to provide

essential communications or utility services to the public, to the extent such facilities are not

principally used or designed as a commercial data center, colocation facility, cloud computing

facility, artificial intelligence compute facility, or digital asset mining facility.

SECTION 4. STUDY AND REVIEW.


During the Moratorium Period and any extension thereof, the City Manager, or designee, is

authorized and directed to:

A. Evaluate the potential land use, infrastructure, environmental, public health, and public safety

implications of data centers and similar high-intensity computing uses within the City, including but not

limited to electricity demand, water use, wastewater demand, cooling systems, backup generation, noise,

air quality, greenhouse gas emissions, emergency response, fire and life safety, compatibility with the

CEDS, and compatibility with surrounding land uses;

B. Review the City’s existing General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning regulations, specific plans, and

development standards to determine whether amendments are necessary to address data centers and

similar high-intensity computing uses;

C. Consult, as appropriate, with utility providers, water agencies, fire and emergency service

providers, public health agencies, environmental professionals, residents, businesses, and other

stakeholders regarding the potential impacts of such uses and possible regulatory responses;

D. Develop and present to the City Council for consideration any proposed amendments to the

General Plan, Municipal Code, zoning ordinance, specific plans, and other applicable regulations, including

but not limited to use classifications, permitted zoning districts, conditional use requirements,

development agreements, siting criteria, separation from sensitive uses, operational standards, utility and

water demand standards, noise standards, buffering and screening requirements, generator standards,

sustainability measures, and decommissioning requirements; and

E. Prepare and issue, prior to the expiration of this ordinance or any extension thereof, a written

report to the City Council describing the measures taken to alleviate the conditions that led to the

adoption of this Ordinance, consistent with Government Code section 65858.

SECTION 5. APPLICABILITY AND EXEMPTIONS.


A. This ordinance applies to all property within the City of Escondido, including any area

subsequently annexed to the City during the effective period of this ordinance, unless otherwise provided

by law.

B. The following are exempt from the moratorium established by this ordinance:

1. Data Center Uses for which all required discretionary approvals and ministerial permits

necessary to authorize construction and operation were finally granted prior to the effective date

of this ordinance, provided that the project is developed in substantial conformance with those

approvals;

2. Ordinary accessory server rooms, IT rooms, and similar facilities expressly excluded from

the definition of Data Center Use in Section 3.B;

3. Public safety, emergency communications, and emergency operations facilities;

4. Telecommunications carrier facilities and public utility facilities necessary to provide

essential public services, to the extent exempted by Section 3 .B; and

5. Minor repairs, maintenance, replacement in kind, or interior tenant improvements to an

existing lawful facility that do not expand the gross floor area devoted to a data center use and

do not increase server capacity, electrical service capacity, cooling capacity, backup generation

capacity, or water demand.

C. Nothing in this ordinance shall be construed to deprive any person of vested rights legally

obtained prior to the effective date of this Ordinance, as those rights may be determined under applicable

state law.

SECTION 6. PENALTIES.


The penalties for land use violations that are prescribed in Sections 33-1310 through 33-1313 of

the Municipal Code shall apply to violations of the provisions of this interim ordinance.

SECTION 7. SEVERABILITY.


If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, or portion of this ordinance is for any reason

held to be invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not affect

the validity of the remaining portions of this ordinance. The City Council hereby declares that it would

have adopted this ordinance and each section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, and portion thereof,

irrespective of the fact that any one or more sections, subsections, sentences, clauses, phrases, or

portions be declared invalid or unconstitutional.

SECTION 8. CEQA.


A. The City Council finds that this o ordinance is not a “project” under the California Environmental

Quality Act (“CEQA”) because it has no potential to result in either a direct physical change in the

environment or a reasonably foreseeable indirect physical change in the environment. In the alternative,

the ordinance is exempt from CEQA pursuant to State CEQA Guidelines sections 15060(c)(2) and

15061(b)(3), and, to the extent applicable, section 15308, because it is a regulatory action taken by the

City for the protection of the environment and there is no possibility that the adoption of this Ordinance

may have a significant effect on the environment.

B. The City Council hereby directs staff to prepare, execute, and file with the County of San Deigo

Clerk a Notice of Exception within five (5) working days of the adoption of this ordinance.

SECTION 9. CONSTRUCTION.


This ordinance must be broadly construed to achieve the purposes stated herein and consistent

with the Section 1 Findings. It is the City Council’s intent that the provisions of this ordinance be

interpreted and implemented by the City and others in a manner that facilitates the purposes set forth

herein.

SECTION 10. EFFECTIVE DATE; DURATION; EXTENSION.


A. This ordinance is adopted as an urgency measure pursuant to Government Code section

65858 as necessary for the immediate preservation of the public peace, health, and safety. Pursuant to

Government Code section 36937(b), this ordinance shall take effect immediately upon its adoption by at

least a four-fifths (4/5) vote of the City Council.

B. This interim ordinance is intended to supplement, not to duplicate or contradict, applicable

state and federal law and shall be interpreted in light of that intent.

C. Unless extended by City Council, this ordinance shall remain in effect until close of business

on August 22, 2027. Any extension of this ordinance shall require notice and a public hearing in

compliance with Government Code section 65858, and shall be supported by written findings that the

conditions warranting the Moratorium Period continue to exist.

D. The City Clerk is hereby directed to certify to the immediate passage of this interim ordinance

and to cause the same or a summary to be published one time within 15 days of its passage in a newspaper

of general circulation for the City of Escondido.
