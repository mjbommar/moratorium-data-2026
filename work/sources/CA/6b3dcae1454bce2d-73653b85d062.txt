AN INTERIM URGENCY ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF

HAYWARD PURSUANT TO GOVERNMENT CODE SECTION 65858, IMPOSING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON NEW DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT, AND FINDING THAT THE ACTION IS EXEMPT FROM ENVIRONMENTAL REVIEW


WHEREAS, the City of Hayward (City) has the power to regulate the use of land and

property within the City in a manner designed to protect and promote the public health,

safety, and welfare; and

WHEREAS, in order to protect the public safety, health, and welfare, California

Government Code section 65858 authorizes the City Council to adopt an interim urgency

ordinance prohibiting uses that may conflict with a contemplated general plan, specific plan,

or zoning proposal when necessary to protect against a current and immediate threat to the

public health, safety, or welfare while the City studies and evaluates what additional

regulations, limitations or other regulatory controls may be warranted to minimize potential

impacts related to additional data center development; and

WHEREAS, data centers are buildings designed and equipped to meet the needs of high

density computing equipment, such as server racks, used for data storage and processing and

require dedicated uninterruptible power supplies and cooling systems; and

WHEREAS, over the past several decades, the function, design and scale of data

centers have expanded along with technology. y. The recent expansion of the artificial

intelligence sector has led to a significant increase in the development of large-scale or

hyperscale data centers requiring significant levels of power, r, generally consuming large

amounts of water for cooling, and generating air quality and noise impacts related to on-site

equipment including backup diesel generators; and

WHEREAS, Hayward has four existing and approved data centers in close proximity

to PG&E’s Eastern Substation in the southwestern portion of Hayward’s Industrial Zoning

District; and

WHEREAS, the City was approached by a developer who disclosed that they are

planning to submit entitlement applications for two new data centers that would operate at 99

MW each in the vicinity of Arden Road and Corporate Avenue, near PG&E’s Eastern Substation

and within one-half mile of Hayward’s existing data centers; and

WHEREAS, it is necessary that this interim urgency Ordinance be enacted in order to

promote and protect the public health, safety and welfare, by pausing additional data center

approvals while City staff analyzes the cumulative impacts of existing and approved data

centers, and develops updated regulations and standards requiring monitoring, reporting

and imposing additional performance or operational standards to minimize potential

environmental and health risks associated with future data center development; and

WHEREAS, on September 15, 2026, the City Council held a public meeting to discuss an

August 12, 2026, City Council referral directing staff to explore options to further limit or ban

data centers and directing the Council Sustainability Committee to develop a report on

economic & environmental impacts of existing data centers in Hayward, and voted 7-0 directing

staff to prepare an urgency moratorium prohibiting future development of data centers while

staff conducts additional analysis and, if needed, develop stricter regulations or a ban on data

centers; and

WHEREAS, i in late September 2026, Governor Newsom signed bills addressing electric

infrastructure and rate payer protections (Senate Bill (SB) 886, SB 1168, Assembly Bill (AB)

2383); monitoring, disclosure and application requirements (AB 2469, AB 1577, AB 2619),

and addressing environmental review (SB 887); and

WHEREAS, California Government Code section 65858 authorizes the City Council, by

four-fifths (4/5) vote, to adopt an interim ordinance to prohibit uses that may be in conflict

with a contemplated general plan, specific plan, or zoning proposal that the City is studying

or intends to study within a reasonable time; and

WHEREAS, this Ordinance is intended to be of limited duration of 45 days unless

extended pursuant to existing law; and

WHEREAS, the City Council finds that, absent immediate action, applications for up to

two new data centers could be submitted under existing regulations before the City

completes the necessary study and adopts updated regulations specifically tailored to

address the unique operational, environmental, infrastructure, monitoring and public safety

impacts associated with such uses; and

WHEREAS, the City also intends to use the moratorium period to engage the

community who will likely be most impacted and to better understand and offer proposals

to address the concerns expressed by the community about the potential impacts related to

data centers to inform the City’s regulatory approach; and

WHEREAS, based upon all evidence contained in the administrative record, the City

Council hereby finds that this interim urgency ordinance is necessary to avoid current and

immediate threats to public health, safety, and welfare pending completion of the studies and

regulatory actions described herein; and

WHEREAS, on October 20, 2026, in accordance with California Government Code

Section 65858(a), a public meeting was held concerning the adoption of this Ordinance.

THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF HAYWARD DOES ORDAIN AS FOLLOWS:

Section 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this interim urgency ordinance is to establish a temporary moratorium on

the approval of any new data centers in the City to allow staff to further study the

environmental and economic impacts of such uses and to develop detailed, site-specific

regulatory standards and monitoring requirements to ensure that all impacts are measured,

disclosed and minimized to the extent possible.

Section 2. AUTHORITY

The City Council enacts this interim urgency ordinance under the authority granted to cities

by Article XI, Section 7 of the California Constitution, Section 65858 of the California

Government Code and Section 617 of the City Charter.

Section 3. DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this ordinance, the term defined below shall have the following meaning:

A. Data Center. A facility containing one or more large-scale computer systems used for

data storage and processing for off-f-site users. Typical supporting equipment includes

back-up batteries and power generators, cooling units, fire suppression systems, and

enhanced security features.

Section 4. FINDINGS

The City Council of the City of Hayward hereby finds:

A. Cumulative Environmental Impacts of Multiple Data Centers. There are four existing

and approved, and two anticipated data centers within approximately one-half mile

of each other in the southwestern portion of Hayward’s industrial crescent near an

existing PG&E substation. A moratorium on future data center development would

allow staff the time to develop performance standards, limitations and establish

ongoing monitoring and reporting requirements to ensure that impacts related to

data center development, particularly when that development is closely concentrated

in one area of the city, are disclosed and mitigated to the extent possible. The following

resource areas are of particular concern and are not currently addressed in the city’s

regulations related to data centers:

Electricity. y. Data centers generate a significant demand for power which could impact

future electrical infrastructure planning and utility capacity allocations.

Water. r. Data centers use a combination of air- and water-cooling systems with some of

the larger data centers facilities consuming hundreds of thousands of gallons of water

per day for evaporative cooling. A moratorium will provide time for staff to explore

various standards for cooling systems and to establish standards for systems that

minimize potential impacts on the City’s water supply.

Air Quality. y. While data centers connect to the grid for power, they must have

redundant on-site backup generation to maintain continuous power in the event of

grid failures. There are four existing and two anticipated data centers that are

operating, under construction, or planned within approximately one-half mile of each

Page 3 of 6

other, all of which have back-up generators that are being tested regularly and, in the

event of a grid failure, could be activated for an undetermined amount of time. While

such disruption is unforeseen, it is important for staff to understand the potential

cumulative air quality impacts from ongoing testing and use of generators and to

potentially update regulations to include limitations or performance standards for

generator testing and ongoing use.

Noise. While most data centers incorporate construction and soundproofing

techniques that ensure server noise is not audible outside of the building, air

conditioner compressors mounted on the roof or on ground that serve the facilities

can generate noise that carries across property lines. Per the City’s General Plan and

the Hayward Municipal Code (HMC), noise impacts for the industrial uses shall not

exceed 70 dBALeq measured at the property plane, and CEQA measures a noise impact

based on General Plan and Municipal Code standards. Data centers may generate

noise that can only be measured outside of the dBA scale however b because there is no

standard in the General Plan, Hayward Municipal Code or CEQA to reflect these other

potential noise impacts, it is necessary to research and develop a methodology and

potential limitations if these other noise levels in fact would pose an impact.

B. Emergency Response Capacity. y. Large-scale data centers contain substantial electrical

infrastructure, fuel storage facilities, hazardous materials such as fuel for on-site

backup generation, and battery storage systems that may present specialized fire

protection and emergency response challenges. It is essential for city staff to evaluate

if there are potential safety concerns or the need for additional fire resources if a

significant number of A Alternative Means and Methods of Protection Request (AMMR)

for developments that do not technically meet fire code are concentrated in a specific

geographic area.

C. Economic and Land Use Impacts of Data Centers. A As the main employment area of the

city, the City’s Industrial Technology & Innovation Corridor is an important economic

asset that must be protected for future industrial development. General Plan goals

and policies specifically reference maintaining a balance between manufacturing and

technology-based economy while supporting jobs-rich land uses:

• General Plan Goal LU-6: Enhance the Industrial Technology and Innovation

Corridor to expand the economic and employment base of Hayward and to achieve

a healthy balance between a manufacturing-based economy and an information

and technology-based economy.

• General Plan Policy LU-6.1, Land Uses: The City shall encourage employee

intensive uses, such as professional office, corporate campuses, research and

development, traditional and specialized manufacturing, throughout the

Industrial Technology and Innovation Corridor.

Data Centers generally generate fewer permanent jobs than other commercial,

industrial, manufacturing, and research-oriented uses warranting an evaluation of

land devoted to data centers to ensure that there is a balanced approach to land uses

permitted within the Industrial Corridor.

D. In September 2026, the State adopted seven data center-related bills addressing

electric infrastructure and ratepayer protections; monitoring, disclosure and

application requirements; and addressing environmental review. w. While these laws

will go into effect in January 2027, they primarily direct state agencies to develop

statewide standards and require local agencies to collect and manage ongoing

monitoring which will not immediately address issues related to monitoring and

disclosure of current impacts related to data centers. Further, they require local

jurisdictions to update entitlement and business license application forms to collect

requisite data.

Based upon the above-described facts and circumstances, and for these same reasons, the

City Council hereby finds that this interim urgency ordinance is necessary to avoid current

and immediate threats to public health, safety, and welfare pending completion of the studies

and regulatory actions described herein which are necessary to address those threats.

Section 5. APPLICABILITY

The regulations set forth in this Ordinance shall apply to entitlements of new data centers

and expansions of existing data centers pursuant to Chapter 10, Article 1 of the Hayward

Municipal Code. A temporary moratorium is not placed on the issuance of renewals of

currently valid business licenses and building permits for existing or approved data centers.

Section 6. REGULATION

No application for a data center, r, as defined herein, shall be approved and no use permit or

other applicable entitlements shall be issued unless the application was deemed complete

and approved prior to the adoption of this Ordinance. Notwithstanding any other provision

of the Hayward Municipal Code or any other ordinance or regulation of the City to the

contrary, no permit or entitlement for use or environmental document which either directly

or indirectly has as its result the approval or allowance of a new data center or expansion of

an existing data center, r, shall be approved, or granted while this Ordinance remains in effect.

Section 7. NO TAKING OF PROPERTY INTENDED


Nothing in this Ordinance shall be interpreted to effect an unconstitutional taking of the

property right of any person. If the City Council determines, based on specific evidence in the

administrative record, that the application of one or more provisions of this Ordinance to a

proposed project would effect an unconstitutional taking of a property right, the City Council

shall disregard such provision or provisions to the extent necessary to avoid such

unconstitutional taking.

Section 8. California Environmental Quality Act. Adoption of this Ordinance is exempt from

environmental review as the adoption of this Ordinance does not qualify as a “project”

pursuant to the California Environmental Quality Act (“CEQA”), under Public Resources Code

Section 21065 and CEQA Guidelines Section 15060(c)(2), 15378, and 15061(b)(3) as there

is no possibility that such action would cause either a direct, or reasonably foreseeable

indirect, physical change in the environment.
