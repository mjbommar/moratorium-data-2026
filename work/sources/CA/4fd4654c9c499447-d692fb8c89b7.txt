DEPARTMENTAL RECOMMENDATION:

The Department of Resource Management recommends that the Board of Supervisors:

1) Read the proposed ordinance by title only and waive further reading by majority vote;

2) Conduct a public hearing to consider a proposed 22 month and 15-day extension of Interim Ordinance 2021-1819U adopted as an urgency measure on April 6, 2021, prohibiting new commercial wind turbine generators and wind farms within the unincorporated areas of the County of Solano; and

3) Adopt Ordinance No. 2021-1819U-E to approve a 22 month and 15-day extension of Interim Ordinance 2021-1819U. (4/5 vote required).

SUMMARY:

The Board of Supervisors enacted Ordinance No. 2021-1819U prohibiting new commercial wind turbine generators and wind farms within the unincorporated areas of the County for 45 days (through May 21, 2021) following a public hearing conducted on April 6, 2021. The currently proposed extension ordinance (proposed Ordinance No. 2021-1819U-E) contains the same provisions as the original urgency ordinance with the added extension text in accordance with the provisions set forth in California Government Code section 65858, resulting in a total of two years that the urgency ordinance would be in effect if the extension is approved. The period of time the urgency ordinance will be in effect provides staff the necessary time to evaluate policies and implementation measures to reduce potential impacts on the radar systems at Travis Air Force Base Travis (TAFB) that could result from development of new commercial wind turbines and windfarms in the unincorporated area, including any measures needed to ensure implementation of the Travis Air Force Base Land Use Compatibility Plan.

FINANCIAL IMPACT:

The costs associated with preparing the agenda item and ordinance are nominal and absorbed by the Department’s FY2020/21 Adopted Budget.

DISCUSSION:

The importance of TAFB to national defense and the local economy has been well documented in the TAFB Sustainability Study and other economic studies conducted over the years. Other studies nationally have also concluded that wind turbines create interference that can degrade a radar’s capabilities in the immediate area of wind turbine developments.

Existing wind turbine development in the vicinity of TAFB has caused impacts to the TAFB traffic control system, which is also used to monitor private commercial air traffic in the Bay area. Other smaller airports in the County are also impacted by interference to the TAFB radar system This impact, which causes a wind farm to appear as aircraft entering the area on radar imagery has been acknowledged by the wind resources industry. TAFB has entered into several Cooperative Research and Development Agreements (CRADA’s) to address impacts of wind turbines on their radar system and, in addition, a Pilot Mitigation Program (PMP) has been initiated. Thus far the issue has not been solved, thus any further expansion of wind turbine projects in the vicinity of TAFB will further erode the radar system and be detrimental to TAFB operations. The 2015 TAFB Land Use Compatibility Plan adopted by the Airport Land Use Commission contains provisions to limit wind turbine development in the line of sight of the TAFB radar system. These provisions provide some protection against wind projects that may be detrimental to TAFB radar systems, but further County standards or restrictions may be necessary to address the range of potential projects that could be submitted in the future.

A recent example that County staff became aware of is a possible 75 turbine project with a proposed turbine height exceeding 800 feet north of Highway 12. Said project has not received FAA approval. A few years ago, a similar wind turbine project was contemplated in the same area, but the wind turbine height was in the range of 500 to 600 feet tall. This is also an area where there have been large scale property purchases by a single corporate entity for unknown purposes although large scale wind power development would seem to be a potential objective. Overall, it is likely that this area will continue to be a target of wind energy development, making it important for County standards and restrictions to be implemented that reinforce those in the TAFB Land Use Compatibility Plan. The potential impact of 800 feet high wind turbines in the vicinity or within the wind resource area would likely exacerbate the impacts to the TAFB radar system until technology evolves that would address effects of wind turbines on radar imagery.

The Travis Sustainability Study adopted by the Solano County City County Coordinating Council in 2018 was clear that the County and cities in the County need to be proactive to protect TAFB operations. Staff believes that the recommended action to extend the urgency interim ordinance establishing a prohibition on new commercial wind energy development in unincorporated Solano County provides the time needed to further assess potential necessary measures to protect TAFB radar systems from further interference from wind turbine development and possible impacts to TAFB operations.

Public Hearing Notice:

In accordance with Solano County Zoning Regulations, notice of a public hearing was published in local newspapers at least 15 days before the scheduled hearing.

ALTERNATIVES:

2. The Board could choose to consider further or different revisions to the proposed interim ordinance and provide direction to staff as necessary; or
