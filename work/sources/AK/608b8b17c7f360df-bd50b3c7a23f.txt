Ordinances

These are the city’s laws. They are introduced in writing, given a public hearing (with notice), and adopted by Council vote. Permanent, general ordinances are later codified into the Houston Municipal Code (HMC).

Typical uses: amending the municipal code (zoning, elections, purchasing, finance), adopting or changing land-use rules, and other lasting policy. They use the enacting clause “BE IT ORDAINED AND ENACTED BY THE CITY OF HOUSTON, ALASKA” and are numbered (e.g., Ordinance 25-14, Ordinance 23-04).

Resolutions

These are formal Council statements that express policy, authorize something, or take a position without changing the municipal code. They are still voted on and become official records.

Examples from Houston packets: authorizing the Mat-Su Borough to provide animal-care services inside the city, approving a union contract, certifying election results, or opposing a state land decision. The Planning & Zoning Commission also adopts its own resolutions (numbered like 26-PC-03).

Action Memorandums (AM / Action Memos)

These are staff request forms that ask Council to take a specific, usually administrative, action. Each AM has an originator, routing, fiscal-impact box, summary, and a recommended motion. Council votes to approve or deny them.

Common uses: confirming board/commission appointments, authorizing the mayor to sign a contract or lease, issuing a “statement of non-protest” on a liquor or marijuana license, or approving a particular expenditure. They are numbered (e.g., AM No. 25-48, Action Memorandum 26-08).

Houston also uses Informational Memorandums (IM) for items that need no vote—just background for Council.

Reports

These are supporting documents, not legislation. They inform Council before or during a meeting.

You will see:

Department reports (Public Works Report, Fire Chief’s Report, etc.)
Staff reports attached to ordinances (“City Council Legislation Staff Report”)
Stand-alone studies or plans (landscaping plan, comprehensive plan excerpts, budget documents, capital-improvement lists)

They appear inside the meeting packet so Council and the public can review the facts before a vote.
