GCPHD Monthly Update

1. Updates


GCPHD Leasing Space

GCPHD continues to explore opportunities to lease available office space within the Health Department to

public health-adjacent agencies. The goal is to utilize unused space to support organizations that align

with the social determinants of health model while improving community access to needed services.

As part of this process, GCPHD has scheduled a consultation with a local realtor next week to assess the

space and provide an estimated fair market lease value. This information will help guide future

discussions regarding potential leasing opportunities and ensure any agreements are aligned with current

market conditions.

New World Screwworm (NWS) in the US

The United States is actively monitoring the re-emergence of New World screwworm (NWS), a parasitic

fly whose larvae feed on the living tissue of warm-blooded animals. NWS primarily impacts livestock,

wildlife, and pets, though rare human cases can occur. Historically, the pest was eradicated from the U.S.

in the 1970s through aggressive surveillance and sterile fly release programs.

In June 2026, USDA confirmed new domestic detections in Texas and New Mexico, prompting a

coordinated federal response involving USDA, CDC, and state animal health partners. Current

containment efforts include surveillance, animal movement controls, rapid case reporting, and sterile fly

release to prevent further spread.

At this time, the immediate risk to Geary County remains low, as confirmed cases are concentrated in the

southern United States near the Texas-Mexico border. However, continued monitoring is warranted due

to the potential impact on livestock, pets, wildlife, and agricultural operations if spread occurs.

Recommended Actions:


• Maintain situational awareness through updates from USDA, KDHE, and KDA

• Encourage prompt reporting of suspicious wounds, infestations, or unexplained animal illness to

veterinary and agricultural authorities.

SECTION 4. EXEMPTIONS.


This moratorium shall not apply to:

A. Residential-scale solar installations serving individual homes, farms, or businesses;

B. Routine maintenance, repair, replacement, or upgrading of existing legally established

facilities;

C. Projects for which all required County land-use approvals were granted prior to the

effective date of this Resolution; and

D. Public utility maintenance activities that do not materially expand an existing facility.

SECTION 5. STUDY PERIOD.


During the moratorium period, the County shall:

A. Review existing zoning and land development regulations;

B. Evaluate potential impacts on infrastructure, agricultural land, public services, and natural

resources;

C. Assess road use, decommissioning, reclamation, and financial assurance requirements;

D. Conduct public meetings and solicit stakeholder input; and

E. Consider adoption of permanent regulations governing such facilities.
