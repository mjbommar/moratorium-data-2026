Christopher Ponte, a member of the Young Democratic Socialists of America, presented a petition on

behalf of the group opposing RCPD’s application for the Biden grant. He cited concerns about potential

Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) involvement, racial profiling, community safety, and public

trust, noting that the petition had collected more than 500 signatures.

Sharon Davis, a Manhattan Resident, and member of Rethinking Justice, a community-based organization

focused on justice reform, urged the Board to reconsider jail expansion and the acceptance of a $31 million

federal grant. She expressed concerns about potential local involvement in immigration enforcement and

deportation efforts, noting that many individuals (roughly 70%) detained by immigration authorities are not

criminal offenders. She also questioned the fiscal responsibility of accepting federal funding given the

national debt and the potential for federal requirements to influence local operations.

Davis encouraged the Board to consider whether investing in mental health services and other community

based resources would better address local detention needs than expanding jail capacity, citing Lawrence as

an example. She noted the importance of identifying local needs and developing appropriate solutions.

Gloria Greene, representing the Catholic Diocese of Salina and member of the Justice for Immigrants

Organization, spoke about immigrants’ contributions to local communities and expressed concern that

accepting the federal grant could subject Riley County to federal conditions related to immigration

enforcement that may not align with local values. Drawing on her 25 years of military service, she

discussed the effects of fear on families seeking safety and stability.

Greene urged the Board to assess the jail system’s demonstrated needs before accepting funds that could

create long-term obligations. She encouraged consideration of mental health services and residential

support as alternatives to expanding jail and administrative space. Citing Christian teachings on

compassion for marginalized individuals and newcomers, she asked the Board to decline the grant.

Monty Johnson, a Manhattan resident, member of Unitarian Universalist Fellowship of Manhattan

(UUFM), and community organizer, expressed concerns about the intended use of the Biden grant funds.

He questioned the allocation of more than half of the expenditures to the Real-Time Crime Center and

administrative offices, citing meeting notes indicating that Riley County’s crime rates were consistently

below state averages. He also questioned whether the grant would improve mental health services for

incarcerated individuals, explaining that an outreach officer had indicated that no funding would be

directed toward those services.

Johnson encouraged the Board to explore alternative funding options for jail and administrative facility

improvements, including previously suggested revenue sources, rather than accepting funding involving

ICE. He urged the Board to deny RCPD permission to accept the grant before it is offered by the

Department of Justice (DOJ).

Michelle Jeanette, a Manhattan resident, parent, and former teacher, urged the Board to reject the Biden

grant if offered. She emphasized the positive relationship between RCPD and the community, noting that

building public trust between law enforcement and residents requires sustained effort.

community collaboration.

Terwelp also opposed a proposed RCPD ICE detention facility, expressing concern about expanded

the long-term costs of operating and maintaining expanded facilities and systems. On behalf of DeFlock

Riley County, he asked the Board to reject future federal grants for an RCPD ICE detention facility and

cancel all surveillance camera contracts.

Philip Nell, a Manhattan resident, expressed concern that RCPD’s acceptance of a federal grant requiring

with the community. He explained that partnering with ICE could undermine public trust and community

safety, despite RCPD’s efforts to build positive relationships with residents. Nell urged the Law Board to

decline the grant if it is awarded, stressing the importance of preserving the community’s trust in RCPD.

Martha Sweeney, a Manhattan resident, acknowledged the need to modernize and expand RCPD facilities

expose the County to potential federal requirements.

and homelessness. However, she expressed concern about the potential effects of federal immigration

enforcement on vulnerable community members and urged the Board to decline the “free” DOJ grant and

avoid further ICE involvement in the community.

enforcement. She also noted that, based on a conversation with an RCPD officer at a recent Coffee Hour

related cases, and suggested that addressing mental health needs would be a more appropriate use of time

and resources.

P. Aggieville Road Restrictions: Major Hajek reviewed the history of Aggieville road


restrictions following their implementation during the first home football game on September 5, 2026. He

summarized discussions from the August, September, and October 2025 Law Board meetings. At the

August 2025 meeting, members of the Aggieville Business Association (ABA) raised concerns about

RCPD implementing road restrictions every Friday and Saturday night while school was in session, from

fall 2024 through spring 2025. The outcome of that meeting was to form a committee consisting of ABA,

Law Board, and RCPD representatives to discuss the issue.

At the September 2025 meeting, Member Beardsley, who served on the committee, reported that an initial

meeting had occurred, but further discussion was needed. Member James stated that he did not see

8.

Page 40 of 141

justification for road restrictions outside of home football games. The Chairperson also suggested working

with the City to install bollards to avoid using police vehicles and barricades to block roadways.

At the October 2025 meeting, Director Peete reported that the committee's discussions had been productive

and that he had issued Special Order 2025-004. The order established that Aggieville would remain

unrestricted by default, with restrictions implemented when warranted, such as home football games,

events expected to draw large crowds, and safety-related incidents. Member Beardsley commended the

collaboration between RCPD and ABA and described the committee's outcome as strong.

Major Hajek clarified an unintentional misstatement made by Director Peete during the Law Board

Meeting last month regarding the committee's agreement. Restrictions were to be implemented on

Saturdays during home football games, while restrictions on the preceding Friday night would be left to the

discretion of the supervising officer based on anticipated crowd size and activity. Major Hajek noted that

Director Peete had contacted ABA Executive Director Dennis Cook to clarify the misunderstanding.

Major Hajek reported that, following the committee's recommendations, road restrictions had been

implemented for Fake Patty's Day, the two remaining home football games in fall 2025, and the first home

football game on September 5, 2026. He stated that the committee's approach was intended to reduce the

arbitrary application of restrictions by establishing specific criteria and providing consistency for

businesses and ride share drivers. He recommended continuing to abide by the what was established by the

committee and implement road restrictions on home football games.

Dennis Cook, Executive Director, Aggieville Business Association, addressed the Board and confirmed

that road restrictions were in place for the first home football game but had not been used for the

safety in Aggieville but preferred increased police presence during busier weekends rather than restricting

road access. Cook explained that ABA had supported adding cameras in Aggieville, provided they were

highly visible and served as a proactive deterrent rather than solely as an investigative tool after incidents

occurred. He reiterated that ABA’s priority was public safety and stated that increased visibility of police

officers, rather than road restrictions, was the preferred approach.

Major Hajek thanked Dennis Cook for his comments and noted that the two had talked about the topic

and Saturday nights; however, staffing limitations mean that adding personnel is not always feasible. He

stated that the use of technology, including bollards installed specifically for this purpose, certainly helps.

until 11:00 p.m., regardless of kickoff time, to minimize impacts on Aggieville businesses.

risk-management measure to minimize vehicle-pedestrian conflicts during anticipated busy periods. He

acknowledged that he did not have available data did not establish a measurable difference in incidents

between weekends with and without restrictions.

restricting access for hotel guests.

MERITORIOUS SERVICE AWARD

Presented to

Officer Brek k Jag

Jager

The Riley County Police Department’s Meritorious Service Award is presented to Officer Brek Jager in recognition of his twenty ty

years of service as a member of the Department’s Tactical Team. Throughout his tenure, Officer Jager has demonstrated courage,

dedication, and devotion to duty.

Serving primarily on the entry element and frequently as the point officer, Officer Jager was oftften at the forefront of encounters

with violent offenders during tactical operations. His judgment, composure under pressure, and commitment to the safety of his is

fellow team members contributed to the success of numerous critical operations. His service on the Tactical Team reflected the

professionalism and dedication expected of its members.

In February 2023, Officer Jager was selected as Assistant Team Leader, taking on a leadership role focused on the development of

others. He provided instruction and mentorship in the safe resolution of high risk incidents, tactical movement, communication,

and use of force considerations. He also shared his experience with prospective team leaders, helping prepare them to lead during

complex operations. His commitment to teaching and mentoring others strengthened the Tactical Team and contributed to the

continued professionalism of the Department.

Through his service  and leadership, Officer Jager has made a lasting contribution to the Department. In recognition of his twenty

years of service and contributions to the Tactical Team, the Meritorious Service Award is presented to Officer Brek Jager.

Summary


The Riley County Police Department (RCPD) respectfully requests authorization from the Law Board to

pursue grant funding through the 2026 Patrick Leahy Bulletproof Vest Partnership (BVP) Program. If

awarded, these funds would reimburse 50% of the cost of ballistic vests purchased for RCPD sworn officers

in 2027.

Background


The Patrick Leahy Bulletproof Vest Partnership (BVP) Program, created under the Bulletproof Vest

Partnership Grant Act of 1998, is a U.S. Department of Justice initiative administered by the Office of Justice

Programs, Bureau of Justice Assistance. The program provides reimbursement of up to 50% of the cost of

body armor vests for eligible law enforcement agencies. A BVP program fact sheet is attached for reference.

As a federal matching grant, the BVP Program requires a 50% local match. RCPD meets all program

eligibility criteria, including the mandatory wear requirement. Compliance is established through RCPD

Policy 41.3.5, Protective Vests, which mandates vest wear for all uniformed patrol officers.

RCPD has participated in this program consistently since 2000 and has received annual awards in varying

amounts. Recent allocations include:

• 2025: $7,437.25

• 2024: $9,817.09

• 2023: $7,207.42

• 2022: $7,182.50

• 2021: $8,259.88

Specifics


For 2026, RCPD intends to request $7,752.25 in reimbursement funding. Total anticipated vest costs for

2027 are $15,504.50 for 22 vests. Under the match requirement:

• RCPD contribution: $7,752.25

• BVP reimbursement: $7,752.25

Recommendation


RCPD respectfully recommends that the Law Board authorize the agency to submit the 2026 BVP grant

application and accept the award if approved. This funding supports officer safety while reducing local

financial obligation through a well-established federal partnership.

Policy T Title: 41.3.5 Protective Vests


Policy & Procedures Approved On: 09-21-2026 Order: SO 2020 - 140


Approved:

Brian R. Peete,  Director Riley County Police Department CALEA Standard: Manhattan, KS 41.3.5


Chapter: 41 – Patrol - Equipment


1. Wearing Protective Vests Policy: It is Department policy to maximize officer safety through the use of


protective vests in combination with prescribed safety procedures. While a protective vest provides a

significant level of protection, it is not a substitute for the observance of officer safety procedures.

2. Definitions: Field Activities: Duty assignments and/or tasks  that place or could reasonably be expected


to place Sworn personnel in situations where they would be required to act in enforcement rather than

administrative or support capacities.

3. Procedures:


A. Issuance of Protective Vests:

1) The Department will provide to each sworn officer a protective vest as part of their initial

equipment issue.

2) The Department issued vests will be at a minimum Level II standard of protection. All vests

will be retained by the individual officer as an item of personal equipment. Members of the ERU

Tactical Team will be issued Level III or higher protective vests.

3) A protective vest that is worn out, damaged, or ill-fitting shall be replaced by the

Department upon completion of the appropriate form. Misuse or abuse of an issued vest shall

subject the officer to disciplinary action.

4) A Correction officer or Police Service Aide who conducts a transport may request through

their supervisor temporary issue of a protective vest.

5) Animal Control Officers will be issued a department ballistic vest and Correction personnel

will have access to riot vests and other equipment for emergencies occurring in the Jail.

B. Use of Protective Vests:

1) Sworn personnel shall wear only agency-approved protective vests.

2) Sworn personnel and Sergeants who are assigned to a uniformed function and sworn

Lieutenants serving as first line supervisors are required to wear their protective vest while

engaged in field activities except as follows:

Policy T Title: 41.3.5 Protective Vests


a. When an agency-approved physician determines that an officer has a medical

condition that would preclude wearing of a protective vest.

b. When a supervisor determines that an officer has a temporary condition that would

make the wearing of a vest extremely uncomfortable. (Two consecutive work periods

maximum)

c. When an officer is involved in undercover  or plain clothes work that a supervisor

determines would be compromised by the wearing of a protective vest; or

d. When the Director or designee determines that circumstances make it inappropriate

to mandate wearing a protective vest.

C. Inspections of Protective Vests: Supervisors are responsible to ensure protective vests are worn

and maintained as required by this policy through routine observation and inspections.

D. Care, Maintenance and Replacement of Protective Vests:

1) Sworn personnel shall routinely inspect their protective vests for signs of damage as well

as general cleanliness.

2) Sworn personnel are responsible for the cleanliness and care of their protective vest in

accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

3) Sworn personnel are responsible for reporting damage or excessive wear to the ballistic

panels or cover to their immediate supervisor.

E. Protective vests will be replaced in accordance with guidelines and protocols established by the

National Institute of Justice and the specific manufacturer.

APPENDIX A


FY 2026 ENFORCEMENT INITIATIVE DESCRIPTION BUDGET PROPOSAL (INVESTIGATIVE)


HIDTA: Midwest HIDTA

INITIATIVE TITLE: Topeka DEA Task Force

LEAD AGENCY(S): Drug Enforcement Administration

CO-LOCATION SITE: Topeka, KS

DESCRIPTION


Initiative Purpose

The Topeka DEA Task Force is a DEA led initiative that was created in 2015. The Initiative is a

cooperative partnership between federal and state and local law enforcement agencies in the

Topeka and Manhattan areas. The Initiative is comprised of a single enforcement group that

shares collocated space at the DEA’s Topeka Post of Duty. The task force focuses on mid to

upper level Mexican methamphetamine and poly-drug trafficking organizations (DTOs) operating

in its area of responsibility. All enforcement and investigative operations are coordinated

through the deconfliction services provided by the Midwest HIDTA ISC, which assist in enhancing

officer safety and increase efficiency by reducing duplication of effort.

Threat Priorities

International, regional and local DTOs affiliated with Mexican transnational criminal

organizations (TCOs) that distribute methamphetamine, as well as heroin/synthetic opioids and

other drugs are the primary threat in the Initiative’s area of responsibility. The task force initiates

investigations of DTOs operating in its area of responsibility, which primarily consists of the

greater Topeka and Manhattan areas.

Investigative Approach

The Topeka DEA Task Force utilizes a variety of investigative techniques that include traditional

undercover operations where controlled substances are purchased from investigative targets,

physical and electronic surveillance and Title IIIs. HIDTA funding is used to enhance the

Initiative’s efforts and consists of PE/PI, overtime for state and local investigators, investigative

supplies and various office supplies and services.

RILEY COUNTY POLICE DEPARTMENT POSITION ANNOUNCEMENT Director Riley County  Law Enforcement Agency  (Agency) is seeking  a highly qualified  law enforcement  professional to serve  as the next Director  of the Riley County Police Department.


The Riley County Police Department (RCPD) is a consolidated law enforcement agency serving

Riley County, Kansas, combining the functions of a county sheriff’s office and a municipal police

department. Riley County is a diverse and growing community located in the heart of the Flint

Hills. The county encompasses 610 square miles and includes the cities of Manhattan,

Leonardville, Ogden, Randolph, and Riley. M Manhattan is the largest city and h home to K Kansas State

University. It is located near the Fort Riley Army installation. RCPD is nationally accredited by

the Commission on Accreditation for Law Enforcement Agencies (CALEA), which ensures high

professional standards and promotes community advocacy. The Department also maintains state

accreditation through the Kansas Law Enforcement Accreditation Program (KLEAP). RCPD

serves approximately 74,000 residents of Riley County and is authorized 113 sworn law

enforcement  personnel, 47 corrections personnel, 24 dispatchers, and 43 professional staff

members.  RCPD oversees a consolidated dispatch center serving law enforcement, emergency

medical services (EMS), and municipal and rural fire services throughout Riley County.

Who We Are: RCPD is an evidence-based, intelligence-led  policing department whose mission


is to: “Reduce Crime and Improve the Quality of Life for the Citizens We Serve.” We are a law

enforcement department committed to being effective in our mission with procedural justice for

citizens being paramount to our success. As a department, we model service by fostering a caring

and supportive culture for our employees, so they are prepared to serve the community in the same

manner.

Minimum Requirements include: US Citizen; Minimum statutory requirements for certification


as a full-time Kansas law enforcement officer as set forth in the Kansas Law Enforcement T Training

Act and any additional requirements set forth in RCPD policy; Bachelor degree from a college or

university accredited by one of six regional accreditation agencies recognized by the Council for

Higher Education Accreditation; Minimum five years’ experience in the area of police

administration at the command level; Capable of being bonded for not less than $10,000.00 upon

appointment to the position; Commit to establishing residency i in accordance with current policies.

leadership and overall direction for the Department. As the Department’s chief executive officer,

the Director exercises direct and functional supervision over sworn and non-sworn personnel and

is responsible for all law enforcement operations, including patrol, investigations, and corrections

(jail operations). Pursuant to Kansas law, the Director is h hired d by, responsible to, and serves at the

written contract will be negotiated between the Director and the Agency. Terms of the written

contract s supersede t the written job description when the contract and job description conflict. Under

and operational duties including:
