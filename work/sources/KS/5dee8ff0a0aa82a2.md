## What is a data center?

The term data center is not defined in the Wichita-Sedgwick County Unified Zoning Code.

Generally, a data center is a facility that centralizes computing infrastructure for storing, processing, and managing digital data. These centers generally range from large, centralized "hyperscale" campuses to smaller, decentralized "edge" facilities. These can include facilities with technology that demands significant computing power, water, and energy resources.

## How does Wichita-Sedgwick County define or regulate a data center?

Because the Wichita-Sedgwick County Unified Zoning Code does not include a specific definition for data centers, any data center land use is evaluated on a case-by-case basis. Depending on their characteristics, they may be categorized under the following existing uses.

* Office, General, if the facility primarily supports administrative or non-industrial computer operations and has minimal infrastructure needs (e.g., standard commercial power and HVAC).
* Utility, Minor and Utility, Major, if the use includes substantial infrastructure without administrative operations, such as generators, cooling systems, security perimeters, or is critical to digital/utility networks.
* Storage, Outdoor, as a Principal Use, may apply in cases where large-scale equipment, servers, or containerized server farms (e.g., mobile crypto-mining units) are housed outdoors as the primary function of the site.

## What research has been done?

The Planning Department conducted a process of reviewing national information and contacting other communities to learn more about data centers and how they are regulated in other communities. This information was compiled into a white paper.

The Wichita-Sedgwick County Metropolitan Area Planning Department has contracted (through the City of Wichita) with Berkley Group, LLC to assist with this process. The Berkley Group has prepared a white paper with a review of existing regulations and their applicability to data centers; a review of related processes in other communities; industry practices and research related to data centers.
