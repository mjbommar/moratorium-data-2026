# Official Statement on Decision Regarding Citizen-Initiated Petition

source: http://www.eldoks.gov/m/NewsFlash/Home/Detail/665
fetched: 2026-09-23T12:20:55+00:00

Official Statements Posted on July 14, 2026

On June 22, 2026, the City of El Dorado received a citizen-initiated petition seeking the adoption of an ordinance prohibiting "high impact" data centers and Tier 2 and Tier 3 battery energy storage systems pursuant to K.S.A. 12-3013. The Butler County Clerk certified the petition as meeting the statutory requirements for a valid petition on June 25, 2026.

During a special meeting held on July 13, 2026, the City Commission voted unanimously to authorize the City Attorney to seek a declaratory judgement from the District Court of Butler County, Kansas, concerning the citizen-initiated petition.

The proposed ordinance prohibits “high impact” data centers defined as “server farms, colocation facilities, AI training facilities, cryptocurrency mining operations, and similar high-density computing facilities requiring a peak electrical load exceeding ten megawatts, or a daily water draw exceeding one hundred thousand (100,000) gallons.”

The ordinance also prohibits Tier 2 and Tier 3 battery energy storage systems. These systems are commonly referred to by the acronym “BESS.” The ordinance defines a Tier 2 BESS as a facility with “an aggregate energy storage capacity between 81 and 600 kilowatt-hours (kWh) or is comprised of more than one energy storage system technology in a room or enclosed area.” The ordinance defines a Tier 3 BESS as a facility with “an aggregate energy capacity greater than 600 kWh and, if in a room or enclosed area, consists of only one energy storage system technology.”

If adopted as written, the petition ordinance cannot be amended or repealed by the City Commission for ten years following its adoption, except by approval by voters of a ballot question amending or abolishing the adopted ordinance prior to that time.

By state law, the citizen-initiated petition process does not apply to administrative ordinances of municipal government. Kansas statutes and case law do not provide much guidance when attempting to determine what constitutes an administrative ordinance. Therefore, the City will seek a declaratory judgement from the District Court to ensure that a citizen-initiated petition is an appropriate mechanism to adopt the proposed ordinance. Should a judge find that the petition is legal, the City Commission will decide whether to adopt the citizen-initiated petition or provide for an election on the issue following the District Court’s decision.

Should the citizen-initiated petition be deemed invalid by the District Court, the City Commission discussed providing citizens with an opportunity to vote on an advisory ballot question to guide amendments to the Zoning Regulations specific to this subject. The City Commission would use the results of an advisory ballot to inform its decision on a path forward that represents the interests of the community.

The City will also seek an injunction from the timeline defined in K.S.A. 12-3013 requiring a decision by the City Commission within twenty days or an election within ninety days of the certified petition. This action merely “stops the clock” while a decision is rendered by the District Court.

In addition to the decision to seek a declaratory judgment, the City Commission also adopted a resolution providing a moratorium on the development of data centers and battery storage systems. The moratorium extends through November 30, 2026, and demonstrates the City Commission’s willingness to allow the process to proceed without considering a development proposal for the uses described in the petition ordinance.
