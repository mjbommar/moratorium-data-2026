Tami Robison, Finance Director, discussed the 2027 budget development worksheet. The development
worksheet reflects actual expenditures for 2025, the 2026 expenditure budget and the 2027 proposed
budget for the General fund by department. The transfers and appropriation requests are listed separately.
The 2026 General fund budgeted revenue and 2027 proposed budgeted revenue is reflected.
Appropriation requests by agency are also included. Based on presentation requests and other adjustments
made for a schedule change from a 37.5 hour to 40 hour work week in the Attorney’s office, a job
reclassification in Emergency Management, a wage adjustment in Public Works and an addition for the
Silver-Haired Legislature appropriation request, the total mills needed is 64.124 which equates to an
increase of 7.823 mills from the 2026 budget and exceeds the revenue neutral rate of 53.781, which the
2027 budget requests are over that rate by 10.343 mills. One mill equates to $330,141.00. The reason the
mill levy would go up is because the revenue sources decreased in the sheriff's department, previously
there were SRO grant revenue, the dispatch and jail agreements, other funding sources that adjusted with
the inmate medical costs. Other adjustments are estimated investments, sales tax and penalties that were
received last year because of the tax sale have decreased for 2027, the interest rates have dropped on the
investments and the checking account. The beginning cash has decreased over the last two years, the
budgets have been leaner over the years, but that does cut down on the carryover. In the past, there were a
number of vacant positions in the Sheriff's department and Public Works, but those are filled now, which
also cuts down on the carryover. Ms. Robison discussed the CIP transfer and how it is needed to keep up
with the purchase of equipment. Robison said most of the departments lowered their budgets or kept them
flat. Public Works came in higher, but they need to take care of the roads. The sheriff has done a lot to cut
his budget. Ms. Robison said this is going to be the toughest budget year the commission will have to deal
with since she has been here. Chairman Tremont said she appreciates the departments work on the
budgets. Tremont said with the budgets having 93% of their total is personnel, and the appropriations
need to be looked at. The county may have to look at cutting services, the public want their taxes lowered,
but they don’t want services cut. Ms. Robison talked about the unfunded state mandates, and how much
that costs the counties. The commission instructed Ms. Robison to send out an email to the departments
and get a list of the unfunded mandates the state has put on them and how much it costs. The fees from
Motor Vehicle do not pay to operate that department, the state is not reimbursing for the RNR notices
next year, which all costs the counties more. Ms. Robison said she thinks the commission should let the
County Clerk know the county will be going over the RNR.

Chairman Tremont states her goal is to be under the RNR, and some services might need to be cut.
Commissioner Giordano said that is going to be hard to achieve, and she doesn’t think it is possible in this

budget. Chairman Tremont asked Robison for her suggestions to lower the budget: she said they could
look at the CIP transfer, the budget reserves, decrease appropriation requests, decrease facility and bridge

e Reported the United States is actively monitoring the re-emergence of New World screwworm
(NWS) a parasitic fly whose larvae feed on the living tissue of warm-blooded animals.

NWS primarily impacts livestock, wildlife, and pets though rare, human cases can occur.
Historically, the pest was eradicated from the US in the 1970’s through aggressive surveillance
and sterile fly release programs. The immediate risk to Geary County remains low as confirmed
cases are concentrated in the southern United States, however, continued monitoring is warranted
due to the potential impact on livestock, pets, wildlife, and agricultural operations if spread occurs.
Recommended actions-maintain situational awareness through updates from USDA, KDHE and
KDA. Encourage prompt reporting of suspicious wounds, infestation or unexplained animal illness
to veterinary and agricultural authorities,
