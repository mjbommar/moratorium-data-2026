STAFF REPORT

Regulation Amendment

PETITION: #26-035

APPLICANT: Riley County Planning Board

110 Courthouse Plaza

Manhattan, KS 66502

REQUEST: Amend certain language of the Short Term Rental licensing

requirements of the Riley County Land Development Regulations.

BACKGROUND:

Riley County adopted Short-Term Rental (STR) criteria when the Land Development

Regulations went into effect on January 1, 2022. Those regulations were updated in May 2022,

and have been reviewed and discussed several times since to determine if additional changes are

needed.

After three work sessions with the BOCC, and examples from other entities inside and outside of

Kansas, they decided to propose a 500’ distance requirement between properties containing short

term rentals. That would mean there cannot be any new STRs within 500’ in any direction from

the property lines of a property already containing a licensed STR. Staff later had internal

discussions about this proposal and expressed concern that it could limit even larger properties

where traffic and parking are not an issue. It may be better to establish a setback between STR

units rather than STR properties. We request the Planning Board discuss this option and consider

any potential alternatives that would still meet the intent of limiting traffic and parking issues.

In early 2025, the Board of County Commissioners initiated discussions on several STR-related

items. In February 2026, the Board of County Commissioners adopted amendments to the Land

Development Regulations that included:

− No short-term rental unit may be located within 500 feet of another short-term rental unit;

the Department shall have the authority to waive this separation requirement based on the

evaluation of certain circumstances;

− A short-term rental unit must be current on all property taxes and county fees associated

with the property; and

− The responsible agent must affirmatively respond to complaints in person within an hour of

notification of such complaint.

PURPOSE:

The original intent of the STR regulations was very specific regarding obtaining and continuing

an STR license. Currently, the regulations mandate that:

− A rental license is issued to the specific owner(s) of a property and does not run with the

property.

Page 16 of 36

#26-035 Page 2

− The license shall not be transferred or assigned to another individual, person, entity, or

address, but may be managed by a third party on behalf of the owner.

− The owner of the short-term rental shall renew the license annually.

− Any existing licensed short-term rental unit within 500 feet of another existing licensed,

short-term rental unit may be allowed to continue, provided renewal of the license occurs

within twelve (12) months of license expiration.

The original intent was to allow a “grace period” for those renewing their STR license.

Consequently, this was misinterpreted to mean that if an STR was legally operating under a

required license, the grace period would also apply when the STR changed ownership, giving

time for the new owner to obtain a license. However, it was concluded by the County

Counselor’s staff’s review of the Land Development Regulations that, because the rental license

belongs to the owner(s) of an STR (does not run with the property) and the license is not

transferrable, the license terminates upon sale of the property with no grace period granted for

renewal.

Planning staff believes this was not the original intent of the STR regulations. Furthermore, with

a change in ownership immediately eliminating an “established” STR, a new license could

immediately be obtained for a new STR by a nearby property. Due to the application of the

recently adopted 500-ft separation requirement, the new owners of the “established” STR would

effectively be blocked from continuing. Staff believes this to be unreasonable, especially if the

whole point of purchasing the property was the investment backed expectation of continuing the

STR.

Staff suggests amending the language that would allow a reasonable amount of time (“grace

period”) for a new owner to acquire a license to continue a legally established STR, if desired,

but not an unreasonable amount of time as to prevent other property owners within the 500-ft

separation distance the opportunity to establish their own STR, if so desired.

Regulation Amendment

PETITION: #26-036

APPLICANT: Riley County

110 Courthouse Plaza

Manhattan, KS 66502

REQUEST: Amend certain language of the Driveway Standards for the

Unincorporated Area.

BACKGROUND:

In November 2021, Riley County adopted Driveway Standards as a standalone document

referenced within the Land Development Regulations. That document outlines the general

standards, when the standards apply, and who verifies that all criteria have been met.

Currently, the document specifies that the County Engineer or designee, or by the Emergency

Services Director, or designee, reviews and approves all p proposed driveways.

As defined by the Driveway Standards, the driveway is measured from the right-of-f-way (row) to

the principal structure. For reviewing purposes, the “driveway” portion is separate from the

“entrance” portion (the first 25 feet). The County Engineer/designee already reviews and

approves entrance permits separately.

Generally, there are not many new driveways created each year; however, the overall number

depends on the number of building permits issued and the type of development proposals

received, which can be unpredictable.

The Driveway Standards were created to provide for safe ingress and egress and adequate access

for emergency responders to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the community. There are

some driveways that are difficult, if not impossible, to traverse for emergency vehicles, which is

one of the reasons why the standards were put in place. Additionally, the Driveway Standards

were adopted to resolve certain addressing issues.

Public Works shared concerns over the availability of staff and time to conduct driveway

inspections. Staff from Public Works, Emergency Services, County Counselor, and Planning &

Development all agreed that the standards are important for emergency services and should

remain in place; however, it was also agreed that the review/approval responsibilities could be

shifted or shared.

Thus, following a series of work sessions and public hearings with the Riley County Planning

Board and the Manhattan Urban Area Planning Board, the Board of County Commissioners

adopted amendments to the Driveway Standards in the Unincorporated Area of Riley County,

Kansas on May 21, 2026, specifically regarding the Review Authority.

Unfortunately, it was discovered that these amendments provided only p partial relief by allowing

the County Engineer’s designee or the Emergency Services Director, or designee, to review a

“proposed” driveway, as shown on a plat or site plan.

The amendment did not include all relevant text needed that would allow for the County

Engineer or designee, or by the Emergency Services Director, or designee, to inspect and
