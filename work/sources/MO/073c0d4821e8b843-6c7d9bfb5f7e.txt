FY2025 CUSTOMER SERVICE

CUSTOMER SERVICE FY 2025

Introduction

The purpose of this report is to provide the City’s customer service results for FY 2025.

Results include customer feedback from October 2024 through September 2025.

Each team within the City is required to measure their level of customer service on a

regular basis. They measure proactively with surveys given out by the departments and

reactively by concerns filed with the Citizen Action Center. In this report you will find an

explanation of how the customer service scores are calculated as well as the final

results for each team and the City as a whole.

Methodology

Each year, as a proactive measure of customer service, each team identifies the

customers they serve and distributes surveys to them. The surveys ask customers to

grade the team or program on different aspects, such as professionalism,

responsiveness, and quality of service. The purpose of these surveys is to gather

information on how well each team is meeting their customers’ needs and to determine

where there is room for improvement

Since there is such great diversity in the services that each team provides, we use a

single, standard grading scale in order to allow us to compare the teams to one another.

The grading scale is as follows:

A = Total Satisfaction (100 points)

B = General Satisfaction (90 points)

C = General Dissatisfaction (50 points)

D = Total Dissatisfaction (0 points)

Using the above grading scale, each team is able to produce a percentage to rate their

customer service. The City’s goal is for every team to maintain a customer service

score of 90 percent or better. If a team falls below the 90 percent rating, they are

required to submit an action plan detailing how they will improve their customer service

in the future.

Proactive Customer Service Results

In FY 25, the overall customer service score for the City was 98 percent.

Appendices A and B show a breakdown of the scores.

Summary

The City has worked hard over the last twenty plus years to make the working

environment one that is a place where employees want to come to work and feel good

about the jobs that are done. This environment translates into outstanding service to

our residents. This is the 22nd consecutive year that the City has achieved an

outstanding level of service with an overall rating of at least 96%.

SECTION NO. 1. Findings of Fact. The recitals set forth above are hereby ratified and

incorporated as legislative findings of fact in support of this Ordinance.

SECTION NO. 2. Information Technology and Data Processing Facility Defined. For

purposes of this ordinance, “information technology and data processing facility” shall mean a

facility used primarily for the storage, management, processing, or transmission of digital

information and data for any purpose. Such facilities typically contain computers, computer

servers, digital storage devices, networking equipment, and associated infrastructure, including

mechanical and electrical systems such as backup power supplies, cooling systems, and security

controls. The term includes, but is not limited to, data centers, cloud computing facilities, web

hosting facilities, and colocation facilities. This term does not include facilities already existing

and operating within the City, wireless support structures, or facilities that contain only incidental

computer equipment used for internal business or administrative purposes, such as standard office

spaces, hospitals, small-scale computer repair shops, or similar operations.

SECTION NO. 3. Moratorium Imposed. During the time that this Ordinance is in effect

as specified in Section No. 6 of this Ordinance, there shall be a moratorium on the review and

approval of plans and development for properties requiring zoning amendments, site plans, special

use permits, and building permits that include requests for information technology and data

processing facilities.

SECTION NO. 4. Waivers. Any property owner seeking a waiver under this Section Four

shall file an application with the Board of Adjustment within ninety (90) days after the effective

date of this Ordinance. The Board of Adjustment, after hearing, may grant a waiver to the

moratorium and permit review of and approval of plans and of development on a specific parcel

where the Board of Adjustment determines, based upon substantial competent evidence, that the

plan or development adequately addresses public health concerns, will not be detrimental to the

public health, safety, or welfare of the residents of the City, and otherwise complies with the

requirements of the City Code. Except as otherwise set forth in this Ordinance, hearings conducted

pursuant to Section Four shall be conducted in the same manner as provided for in Section

405.780.H of the City Code.
