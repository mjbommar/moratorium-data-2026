A data center is a physical facility that is designed to store, manage, and disseminate data, making it a critical component of modern business infrastructure. This data is stored in servers within the data center, either for a single company or for multiple businesses to store, manage, and process data.

Data Center Moratorium

Recently, York County Council passed the third reading of a proposed Data Center Moratorium. As a result, the nine month moratorium is now in effect. Common questions, with responses, related to the moratorium are below.

Q: What does the proposed moratorium do?

A: The proposed data center moratorium would temporarily restrict the County from considering/accepting/approving the following activities related to new data centers for a period of nine months:

Application or issuance of special exceptions
Application or approval of civil site plan for conditional use
Any other related development approval

Q: Would current data centers be impacted?

A: If approved by the County Council, the moratorium would apply to all projects except those that have established vested rights.

QTS has nine buildings in various stages of development at its active site in York County. Because the site plan approvals occurred prior to the moratorium ordinance, those site plans are considered to have vested rights and may be developed accordingly.

Q: Why nine months, and what does the County and Council hope to accomplish during that time?

A: The proposed nine-month moratorium on data centers would provide time for the County to study identified issues related to this high-impact use, to review information from other Counties with existing data centers, and to consult with and/or retain industry experts. Those issues include, but are not limited to, impacts on utility rates, strain on energy resources, water usage, noise, pollution, and impact on adjacent and nearby properties. Once the issues have been studied, the County can make recommended changes to the existing ordinances to appropriately regulate this use.

If additional time is needed to finish a review or study, or if additional time is needed to finalize ordinance amendments, Council can approve a resolution to extend the moratorium.

Q: What will the County/Council do when the moratorium ends?

A: Consider recommended changes to the existing ordinance based on the studies and reviews performed during the moratorium period. Approved changes would be incorporated by amending the ordinance.

Q: Did the public have an opportunity to weigh in on the proposed moratorium?

A: The proposed moratorium passed third reading by the County Council on Monday, July 13, 2026. The public had an opportunity to provide feedback on this ordinance during the third reading and public hearing. Now that Council approved the ordinance, it will immediately take effect.

QTS and the company's facilities in York County

Below are common questions, with responses, about York County's only data center, which is being constructed by Quality Technology Services, or QTS.

Q: When did QTS come to York County?

A: Quality Technology Services (QTS) purchased land that was zoned for Industrial Development (ID) from the County on July 26, 2023. QTS also purchased land during this time from private landowners. All the QTS property purchased in 2023 was originally zoned industrial in 1986, with the adoption of the first zoning map in York County. There was no rezoning on this property necessary by the York County Council as data centers were a by-right use allowed in the Industrial Development District.

According to QTS, there are many factors that the company considers when looking at locations for data center development. Data centers require access to power (proximity to the Catawba Nuclear Station), connectivity and a skilled workforce.

Another important factor is existing infrastructure and appropriate zoning that can support data center development, which this site had previously established. Having zoning and infrastructure already in place minimizes environmental impacts and community disruption which is why QTS pursued and acquired this site.

QTS has recently purchased additional land from a private seller, some of which is also industrially zoned. The County was not involved in this purchase and has not approved rezoning for the portions of the newly acquired QTS land that are zoned agricultural or as a rural development district.

Q: What actions has York County taken regarding QTS?

A: York County staff vetted and researched the company, following the typical thorough process for significant economic development projects.

The sale of 173 acres of property from the County to QTS was approved by the Council following the prescribed process of three readings and a public hearing. A resolution was passed by the Council to commit to a Fees in Lieu of Taxes (FILOT) agreement with QTS. The FILOT agreement also followed the prescribed approval process for Council (three readings, including a public hearing). The FILOT was approved by the Council on September 18, 2023.

In 2025, QTS informed York County that technological advances, including cooling systems that require less water, necessitated additional building height over the existing maximum. York County staff visited a QTS site with similar dimensions to observe the technology and height firsthand. QTS also met with local fire department and York County Fire staff to review the potential increased height request and ensure public safety partners could sufficiently respond to and protect the data center. Council accommodated the request for additional height with a Zoning Code text amendment in June 2025, after the prescribed three readings and public hearing. Additionally, Council approved an agreement for road improvements. QTS received a site improvement grant from the State of South Carolina, of which York County served as the “pass through” organization.

All Council agendas related to QTS were publicly noticed and allowed for a public hearing. During early Council action, QTS was often referred to as “Project Cobra”.

York County’s Economic Development Department and the South Carolina Department of Commerce issued a press release announcing the agreement. You can view the press release here: QTS Data Centers establishing first South Carolina operations in York County - York County Economic Development.

Again, no rezoning was required.

Q: Why was QTS provided incentives?

A: FILOTs help make South Carolina competitive for major capital investment while supporting the long-term fiscal balance that counties rely on to serve growing populations. Offering these incentives to businesses, like QTS, benefits communities. Service needs (especially schools) expand with residential growth, while industrial users contribute more revenue per acre. Counties depend on industrial investment because residential growth does not pay for itself. For example, for every $1 billion in investment, QTS will pay approximately $5 million annually to the Clover School District in taxes and an additional $2 million annually to the community. This greatly reduces the tax burden on citizens.

Q: Why was QTS not referred to by name when being debated by Council?

A: South Carolina’s Department of Commerce withholds the identities of companies, negotiation details, and internal cost-benefit analyses during the preliminary stages of a deal. This allows South Carolina to remain competitive in securing and recruiting businesses to the area.

Q: Will this impact local residents’ electricity or water bills?

A: According to York Electric Cooperative (YEC), which will supply QTS with electricity, large, consistent energy loads (such as data centers) help stabilize demand, allowing YEC to purchase wholesale power more efficiently. QTS has committed to covering the cost of development and equipment required to serve the data center.

Additionally, QTS Data Centers is committed to sustainability and water conservation through their innovative closed-loop cooling system. This system saves over 48 million gallons of water annually per data center, which is equivalent to the water use from more than 2,200 U.S. homes per year. This approach not only conserves water but also supports local communities by replanting trees and sourcing energy from carbon-free sources. There are no planned wells for the QTS site. The development has approval to connect to York County water and sewer utilities. Any requests for wells, for irrigation or other purposes, are subject to review and approval by the South Carolina Department of Environmental Services.

QTS and the County remained in contact with utility and water providers to confirm they have the capacity and willingness to deliver service.

York Electric Cooperative recently published information about data centers and their role as an energy provide in their South Carolina Living magazine. You can read the digital edition here: February 2026: The Truth About Data Centers - York Electric Cooperative, Inc.

Duke Energy has created a resource hub related to data centers, which can be viewed here: Our Data Center Impact - Duke Energy

Q: Is there a conservation easement that impacts QTS property?

A: There was previously a flood easement from 1969, which gave Duke Energy the authority to flood the delineated easement area, if needed, while managing the power plant and dam on Lake Wylie. This easement was mislabeled as a conservation easement in previously recorded plats for the property, but was nonetheless abandoned in a 2024 recorded plat of the property.

Q: Are there any benefits to data centers in my community?

A: Yes. QTS will provide significant tax revenue to the Clover school district, and the district has been supportive of QTS and its plans. This funding offsets the tax burden on local citizens. QTS could attract further economic investments and indirectly support short and long-term job creation.

QTS has committed more than $8 billion in long-term investment in York County. Their phased development represents one of the largest private capital projects our community has seen. QTS has expressed a strong interest in being an active community partner. They have already begun conversations around charitable support, nonprofit involvement, and educational and workforce engagement. It is our expectation that QTS will remain an engaged community partner.

QTS plans to bring 200 permanent jobs to York County, with a median income of $80,000 per year, significantly higher than other industrial jobs. Additionally, the company expects to create up to 1,000 long-term construction jobs.

Data centers work best in communities with a talented work force and in a location in proximity to an energy provider. The facilities generate less traffic than other traditional industrial, and light industrial uses.

Q: Will QTS be subject to noise ordinance laws?

A: Yes. During construction and once operational, QTS will be subject to York County’s noise ordinance. County staff also required QTS to submit an engineered noise study. The resulting study demonstrated compliance with the noise ordinance, with proper mitigation measures in place.

You can report noise complaints and other code concerns to York County’s Code Enforcement at 803-909-7237.

QTS has been issued permits for blasting related to their construction. The permits were issued by the South Carolina Office of the State Fire Marshal. The QTS blasting date window runs through August of this year, with approved times between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. If residents have concerns about blasting noise, they should report it to the Office of the State Fire Mashal at 803-896-9800.

Q: Did the County require buffer yards, lighting specifications or other design restrictions?

A: The QTS site will adhere to County development and design standards. These standards include required buffer yards, foundation and parking lot landscaping, outdoor lighting limits, parking minimums, sign standards, and maintaining performance standards. Site and building plans must meet these standards to be approved, before construction projects can begin in York County. This was the case for QTS as well, with all requirements being met on submitted construction documents, photometric plans, lighting fixture details, architectural elevations, and landscaping plans during a thorough review process.

Q: What about QTS generators, how often will they be used?

A: QTS diesel generators are used for emergency backup purposes only and are not the main source of power for their data centers. Generators are tested once a month for a short period of time for routine maintenance. Additionally, their generators come equipped with high-efficiency filters to protect air quality and reduce emissions.

Q: Are the neighboring fire departments equipped and able to respond to potential issues at the QTS site?

A: Yes, leaders from neighboring fire departments have met with QTS and are confident in their ability to provide emergency response. Additionally, the buildings are protected with advanced and extensive fire detection and protection measures.
