Whereas the Pendleton Recreation Association 12U All-Star baseball team drew its players from a

tournament each year and reflects the players’ dedication, the leadership of Head Coach  Douglas Stettler

and Assistant Coaches Johnny Leopard and Nathan Leopard, and the support of their families and

community;

day of August 2026, does hereby congratulate the Pendleton Recreation Association 12U All-Star baseball

team upon its state championship and World Series appearance, commend the players and coaches for

making Pendleton history, and thank the families, volunteers, sponsors, and supporters who helped carry

the Bulldogs to the national stage.

WHEREAS, while House Bills 5286 and 5526 have not been passed, it is likely they will

Section 4-9-30 of the South Carolina Code of Laws, 1976, as amended; and

WHEREAS,  the South Carolina Local Government Comprehensive Planning Enabling


Act of 1994, Section 6-29-310 et seq., of the South Carolina Code of Laws, 1976, as amended,

authorizes the County to enact or amend its land development regulations to guide development

in accordance with existing and future needs and to protect, promote and improve public health,

safety, and general welfare; and

WHEREAS,  Anderson County Council is currently working with Code Wright as a


consultant to help develop, modify, and review land use development ordinances and zoning

ordinances to ensure such ordinances are effective, efficient, and serving the best interest of the

residents of Andeson County. The County Councils finds it is prudent to request to and allow a

response from Code Wright regarding the regulation of data centers; and

WHEREAS, inadequately regulated development of multiple data centers may have


negative impacts on neighboring property owners, and the operation of such centers are dependent

on adequate capacity of regulated utilities; and

WHEREAS,  the exponential growth of data center facilities, particularly large-scale


campuses, could potentially create an unprecedented strain on the County’s essential public

resources, including without limitation, the electric power grid, water supply, and local

infrastructure; and

WHEREAS,  Anderson County recognizes the County currently does not have a


regulatory framework or zoning ordinance structure that is sufficient to properly manage the scale

and impact of this industry, necessitating a comprehensive, independent study to protect the public

health, safety, and welfare of Anderson’s citizens; and

WHEREAS, The Council has determined that it may be necessary to review the areas


within the County where Data Centers are allowed, and the conditions under which Data Centers

may be developed, and to further consider county-wide amendments to the provisions of the land

use and zoning regulations concerning Data Centers if necessary and prudent for the security,

general welfare, and convenience of the County or for preserving health, peace, order, and good

government therein.

NOW, THEREFORE,  be it ordained by the Anderson County Council in meeting duly


assembled that:

1. Temporary Moratorium on New Data Centers. By and through the enactment of this

Ordinance, the Council hereby imposes a temporary moratorium on the consideration of

development applications for new data centers, and the issuance or approval of other land use

applications or approvals, as applicable, for the development of new data centers within the

County for a period of six (6) months from the date of the enactment of this Ordinance. The

County staff, the Planning Commission, and the County’s Board of Zoning Appeals are hereby

directed to hold in abeyance any development applications involving new data centers that are

received after the first reading of this Ordinance.

2. Definition of Data Centers. For the purpose of this Ordinance, data centers are defined as

a facility comprised of one or more buildings that house computer systems and associated

components, including telecommunications and data processing or storage systems, and whose

primary function is the storage, management, and processing of data. This definition applies to

stand-alone structures or a distinct portion of a building where the principal use is electronic data

processing and storage, but excludes minor, ancillary server rooms, or IT closets that support a

different primary business on the same premises.

3. Extension of Temporary Moratorium by Resolution. The Council hereby finds and

determines that the Moratorium imposed hereby may be extended by resolution of the Council.

The resolution must instruct County Administration and Staff to investigate, examine, and create

a regulatory framework around which data centers will be regulated. The resolution should

identify the criteria and considerations for County Administration and Staff to consider when

creating the regulatory framework. Any such resolution extending the moratorium must expire

on or before December 31, 2027.

4. Pending Ordinance Doctrine. This Ordinance is subject to the Pending Ordinance

Doctrine.

County, South Carolina not specifically affected hereby remain in full force and effect.

SUBJECT: UNDISCOVERED  SC GRANT  APPLICATION—DOLLY COOPER PARK


Staff requests permission to proceed with development and submittal of an application for

funding through SCPRT’s Undiscovered SC grant program. If awarded, funds will be used

to support Saluda River access improvements at Dolly Cooper.

This project involves the development of a 10-wide concrete path which will expand the

utility of the family-friendly Lazy River system at the park. In addition, the trail is

designed to accommodate less active folks, kids, strollers, bikes, and disc golfers. Its  design

also lends itself to easier management of inner tubes.

By adding an additional access point to the river, the proposed scope of work will yield a

total of three river access locations for Dolly Cooper Park's signature lazy river amenity

,

which makes it possible families to spend ample time on the river without committing to a

long trek down the Saluda to Piedmont. It will also provide additional access for major

Saluda River Blueway events.

Access to the river will be provided by a protected staircase to the water. A ten-foot wide

concrete path will extend 1000' from existing path infrastructure down to the natural

riverside trail and then to the water's edge. The path's location high on the  ridge avoids the

existing wooded natural trail, helping to protect the grove of trees and improving

construction access. The existing natural trail will remain intact for adventurous folks.
