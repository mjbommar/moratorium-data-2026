LAND USE MATTERS AND REZONING REQUESTS
(1) RZ-26-03, 1420 Pinewood Rd. – Second Reading & Public Hearing
Presenter: Helen Roodman, Planning Director
Summary: A request to rezone approximately 60.56 acres from Residential-15 (R-15) to Agricultural
Conservation (AC). Planning staff advised that a substantial portion of the property lies within a
special flood hazard area and that the AC designation is more consistent with the site constraints
and the Comprehensive Plan. The Planning Commission recommended approval.
Applicant/property owner Michael “Chad” Carson stated that he may seek future conditional-use
approval to relocate his concrete-finishing specialty contracting business to the developable portion
of the property.
Public Hearing Results: Chairman McCain opened the public hearing. Michael “Chad” Carson
spoke in support of the request. No other speakers came forward, and the public hearing was
closed.
Action: Motion was made by Vice Chairman Byrd and seconded by Councilmember Greene to
approve Second Reading of RZ-26-03.
Vote: Unanimously carried by Council.


         MINUTES | AUGUST 11, 2026 | SUMTER COUNTY COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING
(2) RZ-26-04, 18-30 Callen Dr. – Second Reading & Public Hearing
Presenter: Helen Roodman, Planning Staff
Summary: A request to rezone four approximately one-acre parcels from Agricultural Conservation
(AC) to Residential-9 (R-9). The applicant proposes four single-family attached structures, totaling
eight dwelling units. Planning staff advised that the R-9 designation is supported by the Suburban
Development Planning Area of the Comprehensive Plan, and the Planning Commission
recommended approval.
Public Hearing Results: Chairman McCain opened the public hearing. Buddy Ward, on behalf of
B&E Investments, spoke in support of the request. Ben McMurtry, a Callen Drive resident, expressed
concerns regarding additional housing, traffic, and speeding on the neighborhood street. The public
hearing was closed.
Action: Motion was made by Vice Chairman Byrd and seconded by Councilmember Baker to
approve Second Reading of RZ-26-04.
Note: Following the vote, the applicant agreed to meet with concerned residents regarding the
proposed development.
Vote: Unanimously carried by Council.

Presenter: Johnathan Bryan, County Attorney

COMMITTEE REPORTS
(1) Land Use Committee Meeting Held On August 11, 2026 At 4:30 PM
Committee Chairman Edens reported that the Committee continued its review of proposed
development standards for data centers. The Committee recommended forwarding the proposed
ordinance to the Planning Commission for review and input. Chairman Edens emphasized that
additional Committee and Council review would occur before final consideration.
Action: Motion and a second from Committee to forward the proposed data center ordinance to the
Planning Commission was unanimously carried by Council.
Chairman Edens also reported continued discussion regarding rural land-use standards and options
intended to better direct residential development in rural areas. No action was taken on that matter.
(2) Public Safety Committee Meeting Held On August 11, 2026 At 5:15 PM
Committee Chairman Washington reported that the Committee continued discussion regarding
nuisance and roaming animal concerns, including issues raised in the Sans Souci neighborhood.
Additional work and discussion will continue before the matter returns to Council. No action was
taken.

PUBLIC COMMENT
The following citizens addressed Council during the public comment period:
   Lori Cook-Briggs, representing the Sumter SPCA, discussed free-roaming cats and dogs, animal
   overpopulation, public-safety concerns, disease risks, and the role of the SPCA in accepting

         MINUTES | AUGUST 11, 2026 | SUMTER COUNTY COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING
   stray and unwanted animals. She offered Council information concerning humane trap-neuter-
   return practices and related studies.
   Ms. Wright, a Sumter resident and retired Air Force veteran, raised concerns regarding animal-
   control staffing, transparency, public reporting, facility conditions, stray-animal response, and
   budget accountability, and requested stronger public reporting and updated animal-control
   policies.
   Tanya Eddins addressed Council regarding proposed data-center regulations and encouraged
   review of experiences in other communities, including Virginia, before adopting permanent
   standards.
   Caleb Bonds spoke in opposition to data-center development, citing concerns regarding water
   consumption, electrical demand, infrastructure, and impacts on the community.
   Tony DiBartolo spoke regarding the need to regulate data centers and raised concerns about
   noise, utility usage, and potential impacts on residents.
   Michelle Richardson spoke in opposition to data centers, citing concerns regarding noise, utility
   costs, families, animals, and quality-of-life impacts.
   Margaret Dancy spoke in opposition to data centers, citing concerns regarding electricity and
   water usage and the number of jobs such facilities may provide.
   Lisa Hall, a former Windy Ridge Rescue volunteer, discussed the stray-cat population and
   animal-rescue efforts and stated that local advocates are working toward a collaborative
   proposal for addressing animal-welfare concerns.
   Katina Dreamer presented support for a low-cost spay and neuter clinic and referenced a
   community petition supporting such services.
   Chandler Schwede discussed animal-control staffing and facility resources and stated that
   local animal-welfare advocates intend to develop recommendations involving trap-neuter-
   return, affordable spay/neuter services, breeder regulation, animal registration, and additional
   animal-control staffing and training.
   Matthew Stewart, representing his family in Paxville, spoke in opposition to data centers and
   raised concerns regarding farmland, agriculture, wildlife, water use, heat, noise, and
   environmental impacts.
   Lorenzo Rivers, a volunteer at the South Sumter Resource Center, raised concerns regarding
   facility maintenance, security improvements, neighborhood infrastructure, and abandoned
   manufactured homes.
   Sharon Sisson continued her comments regarding animal registration and suggested
   registration and enforcement mechanisms as tools to measure and address animal-control
   concerns.
   Kylie Cordell shared a personal experience involving stray cats and the difficulty of obtaining
   assistance with removal of a deceased animal from a public area.
   Sondra Ruff, a Windy Ridge Rescue foster volunteer, discussed the burden on foster volunteers,
   trap-neuter-return efforts, and the continuing number of abandoned and stray cats.
   A District 7 resident identified in the transcript only as Bryce spoke in opposition to data-center
   development, particularly regarding water usage, and commented on the stray-cat issue.
