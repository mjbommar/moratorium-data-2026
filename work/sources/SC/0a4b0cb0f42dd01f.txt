                                  RESOLUTION 2026-048

                             Proclaiming September 22, 2026

                              as National Scenic Byway Day

WHEREAS, scenic byways preserve and celebrate the unique natural beauty, historic
signiﬁcance, cultural heritage, archaeological resources, recreational opportunities, and
scenic landscapes that enrich communities across the United States; and

WHEREAS, the Savannah River National Scenic Byway serves as an important
transportation corridor and destination, connecting residents and visitors to the stories,
landscapes, and traditions that deﬁne our region; and

WHEREAS, scenic byways strengthen local economies by supporting tourism, small
businesses, hospitality services, outdoor recreation, and community development; and

WHEREAS, scenic byways encourage stewardship of our nation's treasured places through
education, preservation, and appreciation of local history, culture, and natural resources;
and

WHEREAS, the volunteers, organizations, businesses, local governments, and citizens who
support the Savannah River National Scenic Byway play an essential role in protecting and
enhancing the quality of life in our communities; and

WHEREAS, National Scenic Byway Day provides an opportunity to recognize the
contributions of designated scenic byways nationwide and to encourage residents and
visitors to explore and enjoy these special roads and the communities they serve; and

WHEREAS, the Savannah River National Scenic Byway, designated in 1998, extends 110
miles from Oakway, S.C. to Clarks Hill, S.C. showcasing South Carolina’s Fresh Water
Coast; three major Army Corps of Engineers lakes with historic dams; towns and state
parks; diverse outdoor recreational opportunities; and the nationally recognized Green
Pond Landing and Event Center, all serving as a source of pride for residents and visitors
alike; and

NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved this 18th day of August, 2026 that the Anderson County
Council does hereby proclaim September 22, 2026 as NATIONAL SCENIC BYWAY DAY in
Anderson County, South Carolina and encourages all citizens to celebrate the scenic,


historic, cultural, recreational, archaeological, and natural treasures found along the
Savannah River National Scenic Byway and throughout our Country’s Byways.

                                  Resolution 2026-049
                         Savannah River National Scenic Byway

                                      August 18, 2026


WHEREAS, the Savannah River National Scenic Byway (“the Byway”) serves as an
important scenic corridor extending along Lake Hartwell in Anderson County and further
connecting travelers to the stories, landscapes, and traditions that deﬁne our region; and

WHEREAS, “the Byway” supports tourism in the form of outdoor recreation and hospitality
services, especially at the Legendary Green Pond Landing and Event Center and along
Anderson County’s 40-mile stretch of South Carolina’s Freshwater Coast, home to park
sites, boat launches, ﬁshing spots, swimming holes, picnic areas, and campgrounds; and

WHEREAS, Anderson County encourages stewardship of our treasured places through
education, preservation, and appreciation of local history, culture, natural resources, and
recreation through promotion and support of “the Byway” which was nominated for
inclusion as an American National Scenic Byway in 1998, complete with a Corridor
Management Plan; and

WHEREAS the reactivation of “the Byway” is a recommendation adopted in the 2026
Anderson County Comprehensive Plan with the County Council, Administration, Visit
Anderson, the County Museum, and the Planning Department named as Policy
Champions; and

WHEREAS, Anderson County encourages travelers to explore and enjoy the Byway
extending 110 miles from Oakway, S.C. to Clarks Hill, S.C. showcasing South Carolina’s
Fresh Water Coast; three major Army Corps of Engineers lakes representing three decades
of historic dam building; charming small Southern towns; diverse outdoor recreational
opportunities; and the nationally recognized Green Pond Landing and Event Center, all
serving as a source of regional pride; and

NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved this 18th day of August, 2026 that Anderson County
Council does hereby recognize the Savannah River National Scenic Byway and will
promote the scenic, historic, cultural, recreational, archaeological, and natural qualities
found along this inspiring touring route.

        AN ORDINANCE AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION AND DELIVERY OF A
        FEE IN LIEU OF TAX AND SPECIAL SOURCE CREDIT AGREEMENT BY AND
        BETWEEN ANDERSON COUNTY, SOUTH CAROLINA AND ARTIFLEX, LLC
        AND RED BULLET 25, LLC, FORMERLY KNOWN TO THE COUNTY AS
        PROJECT FLAT ROCK, WITH RESPECT TO CERTAIN ECONOMIC
        DEVELOPMENT PROPERTY IN THE COUNTY, WHEREBY SUCH
        PROPERTY WILL BE SUBJECT TO CERTAIN PAYMENTS IN LIEU OF
        TAXES, INCLUDING THE PROVISION OF CERTAIN SPECIAL SOURCE
        CREDITS; AND OTHER MATTERS RELATED THERETO.

        WHEREAS, Anderson County, South Carolina (the “County”), acting by and through its County
Council (the “County Council”), is authorized and empowered under and pursuant to the provisions of Title
12, Chapter 44 (the “FILOT Act”), Title 4, Chapter 1 (the “Multi-County Park Act”), and Title 4, Chapter
29 of the Code of Laws of South Carolina, 1976, as amended (the “Act”) and Article VIII, Section 13 of the
South Carolina Constitution (i) to enter into agreements with qualifying companies to encourage investment
in projects constituting economic development property through which the economic development of the
State of South Carolina (the “State”) will be promoted by inducing new and existing manufacturing and
commercial enterprises to locate and remain in the State and thus utilize and employ manpower and other
resources of the State; (ii) to covenant with such industry to accept certain fee payments in lieu of ad
valorem taxes (“FILOT”) with respect to such investment (“Fee Payments”); (iii) under the Multi-County
Park Act, to create multi-county industrial parks with one or more contiguous counties and include certain
properties therein, and, in its discretion, include within the boundaries of these parks the property of
qualifying industries, and under the authority provided in the Multi-County Park Act, the County has created
previously a multi-county park with Greenville County, South Carolina (the “Park”); and (v) to make and
execute contracts of the type hereinafter described pursuant to Section 4-9-30 of the Code of Laws of South
Carolina, 1976, as amended; and

         WHEREAS, the County is authorized by Section 4-1-175 of the Multi-County Park Act to provide
special source credits against payments in lieu of taxes to provide reimbursement to companies in respect of
investment in infrastructure enhancing the economic development of the County, including improvements to
real estate and personal property including machinery and equipment used in the operation of a
manufacturing or commercial enterprise, within the meaning of Section 4-29-68, Code of Laws of South
Carolina 1976, as amended (“Infrastructure”); and

       WHEREAS, ArtiFlex, LLC a company formerly known to the County as Project Flat Rock (the
“Company”) does intend to acquire, construct, and install land, buildings, improvements, fixtures,
machinery, equipment, furnishings and other real and/or tangible personal property to constitute an
expansion to its manufacturing facility in the County (collectively, the “Project”); and

        WHEREAS, the Company has represented that the Project will involve an investment of
approximately $28,188,720 in the County and the expected creation of approximately 63 new, full-time jobs
at the Project, all within the Investment Period (as such term is defined in the hereinafter defined Fee
Agreement; and

        WHEREAS, the County has determined on the basis of the information supplied to it by the
Company that the Project would be a “project” and “economic development property” as such terms are
defined in the FILOT Act, and that the Project would serve the purposes of the FILOT Act; and

        WHEREAS, pursuant to the authority of Section 4-1-170 of the Multi-County Park Act and Article

        (d)      The purposes to be accomplished by the Project, i.e., economic development, creation of

                                             RECITALS

        1.      Title 12, Chapter 44 (the “FILOT Act”), Code of Laws of South Carolina, 1976,
as amended (the “Code”), authorizes the County to (a) induce industries to locate in the State; (b)
encourage industries now located in the State to expand their investments and thus make use of
and employ manpower, products, and other resources of the State; and (c) enter into a fee
agreement with entities meeting the requirements of the FILOT Act, which identifies certain
property of such entities as economic development property and provides for the payment of a fee
in lieu of ad valorem tax with respect to such property.

        2.      Sections 4-1-170 and 12-44-70 of the Code authorize the County to provide special
source revenue credit (“Special Source Revenue Credit”) financing secured by and payable solely
from revenues of the County derived from payments in lieu of ad valorem taxes for the purposes
set forth in Section 4-29-68 of the Code, namely: the defraying of the cost of designing, acquiring,
constructing, improving, or expanding the infrastructure serving the County and for improved and
unimproved real estate, and personal property, including but not limited to machinery and
equipment, used in the operation of a manufacturing facility or commercial enterprise in order to
enhance the economic development of the County (collectively, “Infrastructure”).

       3.     The Company (as a Sponsor, within the meaning of the FILOT Act) desires to
provide for the acquisition and construction of the Project (as defined herein) to constitute a
manufacturing facility in the County.

        4.      Based on information supplied by the Company, the County Council has evaluated
the Project based on relevant criteria that include, but are not limited to, the purposes the Project
is to accomplish, the anticipated dollar amount and nature of the investment, employment to be
created or maintained, and the anticipated costs and benefits to the County. Pursuant to Section
12-44-40(H)(I) of the FILOT Act, the County finds that: (a) the Project is anticipated to benefit
the general public welfare of the County by providing services, employment, recreation, or other
public benefits not otherwise adequately provided locally; (b) the Project will give rise to no
pecuniary liability of the County or any incorporated municipality therein and no charge against
their general credit or taxing powers; (c) the purposes to be accomplished by the Project are proper
governmental and public purposes; and (d) the benefits of the Project are greater than the costs.

                                  ORDINANCE NO. 2026-037
     AN ORDINANCE IMPOSING A MORATORIUM ON THE ACCEPTANCE,
  PROCESSING, OR APPROVAL OF DEVELOPMENT APPLICATIONS FOR DATA
  CENTERS AND ALL OTHER LAND USE OR DEVELOPMENT APPROVALS FOR
        DATA CENTERS; AND OTHER MATTERS RELATED THERETO.
       WHEREAS, the South Carolina General Assembly, in its 126th session, introduced
House Bills 5286 and 5526, which are drafted to restrict counties from granting approval for
applications or permits related to new data centers until January 1, 2028, or until comprehensive
oversight and approval processes can be established by the General Assembly; and
         WHEREAS, while House Bills 5286 and 5526 have not been passed, it is likely they will
pass or be reintroduced at the beginning of the 127th legislative session. The County Council
finds it is prudent to wait for the General Assembly to speak on this matter by enacting
legislation which could regulate data centers or counties’ approval of such data centers; and
        WHEREAS, Anderson County Council is empowered pursuant to Title 4, Chapter 9,
Section 4-9-25 of the South Carolina Code of Laws, 1976, as amended, to adopt regulations,
resolutions, and ordinances not inconsistent with the Constitution and general law of the State of
South Carolina (the “State”), including the exercise of these powers in relation to health and
order in the County or respecting any subject as appears to the Council necessary and proper for
the security, general welfare, and convenience of the County or for preserving health, peace,
order, and good government therein, to include providing for land use regulations pursuant to
Section 4-9-30 of the South Carolina Code of Laws, 1976, as amended; and
        WHEREAS, the South Carolina Local Government Comprehensive Planning Enabling
Act of 1994, Section 6-29-310 et seq., of the South Carolina Code of Laws, 1976, as amended,
authorizes the County to enact or amend its land development regulations to guide development
in accordance with existing and future needs and to protect, promote and improve public health,
safety, and general welfare; and
       WHEREAS, Anderson County Council is currently working with Code Wright as a
consultant to help develop, modify, and review land use development ordinances and zoning
ordinances to ensure such ordinances are effective, efficient, and serving the best interest of the
residents of Andeson County. The County Councils finds it is prudent to request to and allow a
response from Code Wright regarding the regulation of data centers; and
       WHEREAS, inadequately regulated development of multiple data centers may have
negative impacts on neighboring property owners, and the operation of such centers are dependent
on adequate capacity of regulated utilities; and

        WHEREAS, the exponential growth of data center facilities, particularly large-scale
campuses, could potentially create an unprecedented strain on the County’s essential public
resources, including without limitation, the electric power grid, water supply, and local
infrastructure; and


        WHEREAS, Anderson County recognizes the County currently does not have a
regulatory framework or zoning ordinance structure that is sufficient to properly manage the scale
and impact of this industry, necessitating a comprehensive, independent study to protect the public
health, safety, and welfare of Anderson’s citizens; and

        WHEREAS, The Council has determined that it may be necessary to review the areas
within the County where Data Centers are allowed, and the conditions under which Data Centers
may be developed, and to further consider county-wide amendments to the provisions of the land
use and zoning regulations concerning Data Centers if necessary and prudent for the security,
general welfare, and convenience of the County or for preserving health, peace, order, and good
government therein.

      NOW, THEREFORE, be it ordained by the Anderson County Council in meeting duly
assembled that:
1.      Temporary Moratorium on New Data Centers. By and through the enactment of this
Ordinance, the Council hereby imposes a temporary moratorium on the consideration of
development applications for new data centers, and the issuance or approval of other land use
applications or approvals, as applicable, for the development of new data centers within the
County for a period of six (6) months from the date of the enactment of this Ordinance. The
County staff, the Planning Commission, and the County’s Board of Zoning Appeals are hereby
directed to hold in abeyance any development applications involving new data centers that are
received after the first reading of this Ordinance.

2.       Definition of Data Centers. For the purpose of this Ordinance, data centers are defined as
a facility comprised of one or more buildings that house computer systems and associated
components, including telecommunications and data processing or storage systems, and whose
primary function is the storage, management, and processing of data. This definition applies to
stand-alone structures or a distinct portion of a building where the principal use is electronic data
processing and storage, but excludes minor, ancillary server rooms, or IT closets that support a
different primary business on the same premises.

3.      Extension of Temporary Moratorium by Resolution. The Council hereby finds and
determines that the Moratorium imposed hereby may be extended by resolution of the Council.
The resolution must instruct County Administration and Staff to investigate, examine, and create
a regulatory framework around which data centers will be regulated. The resolution should
identify the criteria and considerations for County Administration and Staff to consider when
creating the regulatory framework. Any such resolution extending the moratorium must expire
on or before December 31, 2027.

4.     Pending Ordinance Doctrine. This Ordinance is subject to the Pending Ordinance
Doctrine.

5.    All other terms, provisions, sections, and contents of the Code of Ordinances, Anderson
County, South Carolina not specifically affected hereby remain in full force and effect.


6.     Should any part or provision of this Ordinance be deemed unconstitutional or
unenforceable by any court of competent jurisdiction, such determination shall not affect the
remainder of this Ordinance, all of which is hereby deemed separable.

7.     This Ordinance shall take effect from and after the public hearing and the third reading in
accordance with the Code of Ordinances, Anderson County, South Carolina.

                                         RITA DAVIS, FINANCE MANAGER

        SUBJECT:                         UNDISCOVERED SC GRANT APPLICATION—DOLLY COOPER PARK

        Staff requests permission to proceed with development and submittal of an application for
        funding through SCPRT’s Undiscovered SC grant program. If awarded, funds will be used
        to support Saluda River access improvements at Dolly Cooper.

        This project involves the development of a 10-wide concrete path which will expand the
        utility of the family-friendly Lazy River system at the park. In addition, the trail is
        designed to accommodate less active folks, kids, strollers, bikes, and disc golfers. Its design
        also lends itself to easier management of inner tubes.

        By adding an additional access point to the river, the proposed scope of work will yield a
        total of three river access locations for Dolly Cooper Park's signature lazy river amenity,
        which makes it possible families to spend ample time on the river without committing to a
        long trek down the Saluda to Piedmont. It will also provide additional access for major
        Saluda River Blueway events.

        Access to the river will be provided by a protected staircase to the water. A ten-foot wide
        concrete path will extend 1000' from existing path infrastructure down to the natural
        riverside trail and then to the water's edge. The path's location high on the ridge avoids the
        existing wooded natural trail, helping to protect the grove of trees and improving
        construction access. The existing natural trail will remain intact for adventurous folks.
