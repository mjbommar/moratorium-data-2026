DEPARTMENT OF PLANNING & DEVELOPMENT


MEMORANDUM


TO: Town Council


Julia Henze, Town Manager

FROM: Julie Erdman, Director of Planning and Development DATE: October 26, 2026 SUBJECT: Data Center Moratorium Extension


On June 1, 2026, the Town Council adopted a 180-day moratorium on the siting, permitting, construction,

or operation of new, enlarged, or expanded data centers with an electrical capacity of one megawatt or

greater (Attachment 1). The moratorium was adopted to provide the Town with time to evaluate the

potential impacts of these facilities and consider appropriate regulations. Areas of concern include demands

on electrical and water infrastructure, noise, and other potential environmental impacts.

On August 3, 2026, the Town Council held a workshop to discuss data centers and potential approaches to

regulating these facilities. At that meeting, staff presented a preliminary draft of potential zoning regulations

for the Council’s initial consideration. The draft was intended to illustrate one possible regulatory

framework and facilitate discussion of key policy questions, including whether and to what extent data

centers should be allowed in Brunswick and what facility sizes, if any, may be appropriate.

Council discussion at the workshop indicated that a majority of Councilors favored an outright prohibition

of major data centers rather than an approach focused on regulating their development and operation. The

Town Attorney advised staff that pursuing a prohibition would require an in-depth analysis of the potential

impacts of these facilities to support the Town’s approach. Such analysis could include a grid-impact study

prepared by a qualified energy professional. To date, planning staff have not identified a consultant

available to perform this analysis.

The existing moratorium was established to provide time for the Town to evaluate data center impacts and

develop an appropriate regulatory framework. However, the Council’s discussion on August 3 identified a

potential policy direction that requires additional technical review and legal consideration beyond the initial

regulatory approach contemplated by staff.

Extending the moratorium would provide the Town with additional time to:

• Identify and retain a qualified consultant to evaluate potential electrical grid impacts and other

relevant infrastructure considerations.

• Further assess the potential environmental, infrastructure, and community impacts associated with

major data centers.

• Work with the Town Attorney to evaluate the legal considerations associated with a potential

prohibition and determine what analysis is necessary to support the Town’s approach.

• Develop and present options for Council consideration that reflect the Council’s policy direction

and are informed by appropriate technical and legal review.

Staff recommends that the Town Council schedule a public hearing for November 16, 2026 to consider

extending the existing data center moratorium to allow sufficient time for the Town to obtain the necessary

approach for Council consideration.

FIRST EXTENSION OF AN EMERGENCY MORATORIUM REGARDING DATA CENTERS Town of Brunswick, Maine


WHEREAS, the Town Council of the Town of Brunswick, Maine, after due consideration,

find pursuant to 30-A M.R.S. § 4356 as follows:

1) There has been a recent increase in p proposals to develop large-scale data centers within

the State of Maine;

2) Data centers consume disproportionate amounts of water and electrical energy, often

equivalent to that used by tens of thousands of homes;

3) Data centers are known to strain local energy grids and result in an increase in residential

utility costs;

4) Data centers can create noise pollution, heat islands, and have significant environmental

impactsrequiring large amounts of water to function;

,

5) The development of data centers within the Town, without appropriate ordinance and

regulatory standards, is likely to result in an overburdening of Town resources and

facilities;

6) The Brunswick’s Land Use Ordinance and other available Town Codes and regulations

are inadequate to p prevent the serious public harm that may result from the development

of data centers in the Town or p provide for the effective review and regulation of the

location and operation of data centers in the Town;

7) The Town requires at least 180 days to gather information regarding the potential effect

of data centers on the local energy grid and environment and to develop and implement

ordinances and regulations necessary to address these development pressures and protect

the public health and safety and welfare of the Town’s residents; and

8) The Town Council finds that the foregoing facts necessitate the imposition of a temporary

moratorium on data centers.

NOW THEREFORE, be it ordained by the Town Council of the Town of Brunswick as

follows:

1) This Data Center Moratorium (“Moratorium”) is hereby extended pursuant to the

authority granted to Town of Brunswick under 30-A M.R.S. § 3001 and 30-A M.R.S. §

4356 and, in furtherance thereof, the Town Council hereby declares a moratorium on the

siting, permitting, construction and operation of data centers within the Town of

Brunswick;

2) Where the Town Council intended to use the Moratorium period to adopt ordinances

governing the siting, permitting, construction and operation of data centers and additional

time is needed to complete that work, this extension shall take effect immediately upon

the Data Center Moratorium’s expiration on November 28, 2026 and shall remain in

effect for one hundred and eighty (180) days from its effective date, unless extended by

order of the Town Council or repealed or modified by ordinance, for the express purpose

of drafting an amendment to the Brunswick Land Use Ordinance and other Town codes

and regulations to establish appropriate standards for the siting, permitting, construction

and operation of data centers within the Town of Brunswick.

3) During the effective period of this Moratorium, no official, officer, board or agency of the

Town of Brunswick shall acceptprocess, approve, deny or in any other way act upon any

,

application or request for any type of permit or approval relating to the siting, permitting,

construction or operation of a new, enlarged or expanded data center with electrical

capacity of 1 megawatt or greater within the Town of Brunswick.

4) For the purposes of this Moratorium, "data center" means any facility which may be a

freestanding structure or a facility within a larger structure, that primarily contains

electronic equipment used to process, store and transmit digital information that uses

environmental control equipment to maintain the proper conditions for the operation of

electronic equipment.

5) Notwithstanding 1 M.R.S. § 302, or any law to the contrary, and regardless of the date of

its enactment, this Moratorium shall apply retroactively to any and all proceedings or

applications filed on or after date of enactment.

6) If any data center is opened or operated within the Town in violation of this Moratorium

Ordinance, such operation shall constitute a violation of this Moratorium and each day of

such violation shall constitute a separate violation of this Moratorium. The Town’s

officers, officials and employees are hereby authorized to institute any and all rights

available to it pursuant to 30-A M.R.S. § 4452, including fines and penalties, injunctive

relief, and its reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs in prosecuting any such violation; and

7) Any of the Town’s ordinances that are inconsistent or in conflict with the provisions of

this Moratorium are hereby repealed to the extent applicable for the duration of the

moratorium imposed by this Moratorium. If any section or provision of this Moratorium

is declared by a court of competent jurisdiction to be invalid, such a declaration shall not

invalidate any other section or provision.

Emergency Declaration


In accordance with Section 212 of the Charter of the Town of Brunswick, Maine, and any other enabling

legislation, for the reasons set forth in the preamble above, the Town Council declares the existence of an

emergency affecting life, health, property, or public peace. This Moratorium shall be in effect

1Section 212.

- Emergency ordinances.

(a) To meet a public emergency affecting life, health, property or the public peace, the council may enact one

or more emergency ordinances, but such ordinances may not levy taxes, grant, renew or extend a franchise,

or authorize the borrowing of money.

(b) An emergency ordinance shall be plainly designated an emergency ordinance and, after the enacting clause,

it shall contain a declaration stating the existence of an emergency, which shall be described in clear and

specific terms.

(c) An emergency ordinance may be enacted with or without amendment or rejected at the meeting at which it

is introduced, but the affirmative vote of at least 6 councilors shall be required for enactment. After its

enactment the text of the ordinance shall be published and printed in a newspaper having a general

circulation in the community, where it is reasonable to do so in the opinion of the council, and posted in at

least 2 public places. Otherwise, the notice must contain a reasonable summary of the enacted ordinance. It

shall become effective upon enactment, but it shall automatically stand repealed as of the 50th day

following the date on which it was enacted unless it had been enacted as a regular ordinance according to

sections 210 and 211 of this article at the time it was adopted as an emergency ordinance.

(d) An emergency ordinance may be repealed by the enactment of a repealing ordinance in the same manner

specified in this section for the enactment of emergency ordinances. An emergency ordinance may become

a regular ordinance by its reenactment according to sections 210 and 211 of this article.
