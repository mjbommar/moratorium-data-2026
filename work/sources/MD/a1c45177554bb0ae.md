## Data Centers

Calvert County Government is not currently pursuing or exploring data center development.

On Aug. 18, 2026, the Board of County Commissioners adopted a temporary six-month moratorium on site plan approvals for data center development projects. During the moratorium, the county will not accept site plan applications for data center development or approve pending or new site plans proposing a data center.

The moratorium provides the county time to review potential land-use, infrastructure and environmental impacts and consider related regulations and zoning text amendments. These considerations include land disturbance, electrical and mechanical equipment, backup generators, cooling systems, stormwater and wastewater, noise, heat and water demand.

Read [Ordinance 42-26](https://www.calvertcountymd.gov/DocumentCenter/View/57423/42-26) for more information.

## Calvert County Zoning

On March 1, 2025, Calvert County’s updated zoning ordinance went into effect following a two-year public process. Prior to this update, data centers were already permitted in areas where warehouses were allowed, though they were not specifically defined in the previous ordinance.

The 2025 zoning update formally defined data centers and established restrictions on where they may be located. The update was part of a broader effort to align county zoning regulations with the Comprehensive Plan, Calvert 2040, and establish clear standards for the siting and development of data centers.

Under the current Zoning Ordinance, data centers must comply with use standards addressing setbacks, design and noise ([§ Z-18-11F](https://ecode360.com/46249563Z?)). Agricultural and rural preservation remain priorities, with data centers restricted to areas near existing industrial infrastructure to protect farmland and open space. Additional information about the zoning requirements for data centers is available in the county’s [Data Center Development Requirements and Review Process](https://www.calvertcountymd.gov/DocumentCenter/View/54067/Data-Centers_PZ-Zoning-Ordinance) document.

![Map of Calvert County showing I-1 and I-2 Industrial Districts](https://www.calvertcountymd.gov/ImageRepository/Document?documentID=53517 "Map of Calvert County showing I-1 and I-2 Industrial Districts")

### Data centers are only permitted in industrial zones

* In the Heavy Industrial District (I-2), data centers are permitted with conditions.
* In the Light Industrial and Mixed Use District (I-1), data centers require conditional use approval, including a Special Exception from the Board of Appeals.
* **It is important to note that parcels are not always buildable or conducive for data center construction**; some are used for parking, stormwater management, utilities or other purposes

### Public Engagement

* Prior to Planning Commission consideration, the applicant must conduct a public meeting within the district where the project is proposed.
* Meetings must be advertised once per week for at least two consecutive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation within the county. The first advertisement shall be no more than 30 days before the public meeting.
* A sign-in sheet and minutes from the meeting must be submitted to the Planning Commission Clerk at least two weeks before the scheduled Planning Commission meeting.

### Noise Mitigation

* All noise generated by data center operations, including accessory mechanical equipment, must comply with Chapter 80 of the Calvert County Noise Control Ordinance.
* Calvert County adopts Maryland’s noise standards (COMAR 26.02.03) with local modifications enforced by the Sheriff’s Office and Planning & Zoning, with the County Attorney prosecuting violations. Noise limits include 75 dBA during the day and 70 dBA at night in industrial zones. Violations carry civil fines up to $1,000 for the first offense and $10,000 for repeat offenses. For reference, 75 dBA is similar to a vacuum cleaner from 10 feet away, a dishwasher or traffic inside a car at highway speeds.

### Setbacks

* Data centers must be located at least 200 feet from all property lines.
* A minimum setback of 400 feet is required from any lot or parcel containing a residential building or active residential building permit, except for adjacent properties under common ownership. For perspective, 400 feet exceeds the length of a standard football field (360 feet).

### Building Design

* Primary building facades shall include at least two of the following treatments:
* Fenestration (windows, doors or openings)
* Recesses in the building facade to avoid a continuous unbroken plane
* Changes in building material, pattern, texture, color or accent materials

[What is a data center?](#)

A data center is a facility that houses computer systems and related equipment, including telecommunications and storage systems. It plays a crucial role in storing and managing data for various applications and services, such as online banking, video streaming platforms, healthcare systems, and payment platforms like Square used at farmers markets and local businesses. Much of the world’s internet traffic flows through this digital infrastructure.

[Are there any active Non-Disclosure Agreements related to data centers in Calvert County?](#)

There are currently no active Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) related to data centers in Calvert County. An NDA limits the sharing of sensitive or proprietary information and is a standard tool counties and businesses use to handle confidential information responsibly. It is also common practice for one authorized individual to sign an NDA on behalf of a larger group. This approach ensures clarity, prevents conflicting obligations and reflects how governments routinely execute legal agreements. Authorization is defined by county policy and may include the county administrator, county attorney, board president or another designated official.

In data center projects, NDAs are commonly used during early discussions that involve confidential details such as potential site locations, infrastructure needs, energy planning concepts, security considerations or preliminary financial information.

Data center developers often require NDAs because the industry is highly competitive and early disclosure could affect land acquisition, utility negotiations, cybersecurity or market position. NDAs allow counties to receive detailed information needed to evaluate potential impacts while protecting information that is not yet public or finalized.

Signing an NDA does not approve a project, commit the county to any agreement or bypass public process requirements. It allows for early information-sharing. Any future data center proposal would still be subject to all zoning requirements, environmental reviews, public hearings and formal approvals required by law.

Once a proposal moves forward for county consideration, transparency requirements take precedence. Project information is shared publicly through required meetings, filed permits and published materials, and the community is formally invited to participate.

While NDAs may apply during early, preliminary discussions about a potential project, they do not eliminate public access to information to limit public participation once a project enters the formal review process. NDAs are typically narrow in scope and apply only to specific confidential business details shared before any application is submitted.

[Does Calvert County Government have an Exclusive Negotiating Rights Agreement?](#)

There are currently no active Exclusive Negotiating Rights Agreements (ENRA) related to data centers in Calvert County. ERNAs are used by government entities when working with a developer interested in a specific project. An ENRA allows the developer, for a defined period of time, to perform their due diligence while both parties evaluate the project.

An ENRA does not obligate the county to approve a project or enter into a final agreement. Instead, it establishes a framework and allows both parties to fully evaluate the project’s feasibility.

The most recent ENRA with Calvert County Government related to data centers expired on May 17, 2026.

[How do data centers manage cooling and water use, and how are local aquifers protected?](#)

Modern data centers increasingly use closed-loop, hybrid or air-based cooling systems that reduce or eliminate water use. When water is required, projects are reviewed to ensure water sources, treatment and discharge are isolated from residential systems and meet all regulatory standards.

[How is noise from data centers controlled?](#)

Facilities must comply with the Calvert County Noise Ordinance. Noise mitigation measures may include setbacks, sound-reducing design, buffering and staggered generator testing schedules. Some modern data center designs include open interior areas where backup generators are located, with the building itself helping to reduce noise.

Enforcement of the Noise Control Ordinance is carried out by sworn officers of the Calvert County Sheriff’s Office. Violations are treated as civil infractions and may result in fines of up to $1,000 for a first offense and up to $10,000 for each subsequent offense. View the full [Calvert County Noise Control Ordinance](https://ecode360.com/15520602 "https://ecode360.com/15520602") to learn more.

To report a concern or file a complaint, please call the Sheriff's Office non-emergency line at 410-535-2800.

[Will a data center increase my electric bill?](#)

Calvert County is part of the PJM regional power grid, which serves 13 states and Washington, D.C. Electricity planning and pricing occur at the regional level, not by individual county, and large energy users anywhere within PJM contribute to overall demand. Growth across the region influences electricity trends.

A data center’s power needs depend on its size, design and technology, and its specific impact can only be determined after a formal proposal is reviewed by utility providers and state regulators. While a data center does not typically directly increase residential electric bills, large facilities add demand to the regional grid. Over time, this increased demand could contribute to rate adjustments, depending on how utilities and regulators allocate the costs of power generation and infrastructure upgrades.

Electric rates are set by utility providers and approved by state regulators; the Board of County Commissioners and Calvert County Government do not control these decisions. It is also important to note that electricity costs are rising nationwide due to factors such as inflation, aging infrastructure, fuel prices, population growth, electrification and expanding technology use.

If a data center is built in another region within the PJM grid, Calvert County residents could still experience rate pressures without receiving local benefits such as jobs, tax revenue, or infrastructure investment. If a project is proposed here and approved through the full public process, the resulting local revenue could help offset impacts, invest in infrastructure and strengthen the county’s financial position.

[What types and how many permanent jobs does a data center provide?](#)

Permanent job creation can be variable depending on the type and size of the data center. Single user data centers typically employ approximately 100 permanent jobs after construction, depending on their size and design.

* Microsoft data centers hire about 50 full-time employees and vendors for the initial building. As they build out the subsequent buildings, additional staff is hired and generally employs 50 full time staff and vendors per building. [View Microsoft data center FAQs.](https://local.microsoft.com/blog/frequently-asked-questions-about-our-datacenters/)
* Meta campuses, such as the one located in Beaver Dam, WI, employ 100 direct operations jobs per campus.
* Google campuses, such as the one located in Fort Wayne, IN, employ approximately 200 jobs per facility.
* AWS campuses, such as the one located in Wilmington, OH, employ 100 permanent jobs, with multi-site campuses reporting up to 1,000.

Jobs typically include:

* Facilities and operations technicians
* IT and network professionals
* Electrical and mechanical technicians
* Security and site operations staff

Local trades are often used during construction, and local hiring is encouraged. Many communities also develop job training programs to support and prepare the local workforce.

[Why are data centers included in the Calvert County Zoning Ordinance?](#)

Data centers were added to the Zoning Ordinance to proactively manage their potential future presence. The ordinance establishes clear standards for setbacks, design, noise and location. Data centers are permitted only in Industrial zones (I-1 and I-2), with conditional approvals required in certain districts ([view ordinance, use standards § Z-18-11F](https://ecode360.com/46249563Z?)).

The Calvert County Zoning Ordinance Update process began in April 2023 and was approved by the Calvert County Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) at a public hearing on Tuesday, Feb. 18, 2025. The process included extensive opportunities for public input. There were 13 work sessions held with the BOCC, the Planning Commission (PC) and in joint sessions. In addition, there were nine open public comment periods, followed by nine public forums. An additional public comment period was held for the second public draft, which was reviewed by residents, the BOCC and the PC before being sent to the County Attorney for final review.

Following this comprehensive review process, the ordinance was approved at the public hearing. Citizen input was carefully considered throughout the process and highlighted through a social media series that showcased how the new zoning ordinance benefits Calvert County residents. Information was also shared in the *Planning Perspective* newsletter. Residents can view past editions and sign up at [www.calvertcountymd.gov/Newsletters](https://www.calvertcountymd.gov/Newsletters).

[Can the county control where data centers are located?](#)

The county does not control decisions made by private developers. However, Calvert County regulates where data centers may be located through zoning, land-use restrictions and environmental protections to preserve rural character and sensitive areas.

[How close can a data center be to residential properties?](#)

Under the county’s Zoning Ordinance, a data center must maintain a 200-foot setback from all property lines and be at least 400 feet away from any lot or parcel with an existing residential building or an active residential building permit.

[How might data centers affect Calvert County’s rural character?](#)

If the opportunity for a data center in Calvert County presents itself, the county will prioritize managing growth in a way that preserves our rural character and focuses data centers in areas with existing infrastructure to minimize any impact on agricultural land and open spaces. Tax revenue generated by data centers makes it possible to maintain low residential growth while having minimal impact on traffic, schools and public safety services.

[What economic benefits do data centers provide?](#)

Data centers can:

* Generate significant local tax revenue to support county services such as schools, roads and public safety.
* Create permanent technical and facilities jobs
* Support workforce development and skilled trades
* Attract related high-tech industries

[What environmental protections apply to data centers?](#)

Data centers must comply with all county, state and federal environmental regulations, including:

* The Calvert County Zoning Ordinance
* Forest Conservation and Critical Area rules
* Noise Control Ordinance (Chapter 80)
* Air, water and wastewater permitting

Best-practice designs are used to reduce noise, heat, light pollution and resource use.

[Will data centers increase network availability?](#)

Data centers require fiber infrastructure, which can attract new investment and lower the cost of future broadband expansion for businesses, homes and underserved areas.
