AN ORDINANCE EXTENDING THE TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON ACCEPTANCE, PROCESSING, AND APPROVAL OF APPLICATIONS FOR NEW TELECOMMUNICATIONS AND DATA STORAGE FACILITIES WITHIN THE CITY OF CHARLOTTE, NORTH CAROLINA AND ITS EXTRATERRITORIAL JURISDICTION PURSUANT TO N.C.G.S § 160D-107


WHEREAS, pursuant to N.C.G.S. § 160D-107, local governments may adopt temporary

moratoria on any development approvals required by law; and

WHEREAS, pursuant to N.C.G.S. § 160D-107, no moratorium may be subsequently

renewed or extended for any additional period unless the local government has taken all

reasonable and feasible steps proposed to be taken in its ordinance establishing the

moratorium to address the problems or conditions leading to imposition of the moratorium

and unless new facts and conditions warrant an extension; and

WHEREAS, the Charlotte Unified Development Ordinance (“UDO”) broadly defines

“telecommunications and data storage facilities (“data centers”) as “a facility, accessed

only by employees, that houses computers systems and associated components, related

to the transmittal and receiving of information, including but not limited to,

telecommunications systems, telecommunication and telephone switching systems,

cloud storage systems, and server farms”; and

WHEREAS, the Charlotte City Council desires to thoroughly consider the unique

development, infrastructure, and environmental impacts of data centers; and

WHEREAS, the Charlotte City Council at its May 26, 2026, business meeting held a

legislative hearing in accordance with N.C.G.S. § 160D-601; and

WHEREAS, the Charlotte City Council at its June 8, 2026, business meeting passed a

150-day moratorium in accordance with N.C.G.S. § 160D-107; and

WHEREAS, the Charlotte City Council believes that the 150-day moratorium should be

extended in accordance with N.C.G.S. § 160D-107(e); and

WHEREAS, the Charlotte City Council at its October 12, 2026, business meeting held a

legislative hearing to impose a moratorium extension until October 18, 2027, in

accordance with N.C.G.S. § 160D-601; and

WHEREAS the Charlotte City Council believes such a moratorium extension will protect

citizens of Charlotte and its ETJ while the City continues to assess the unique

development, infrastructure, and environmental impacts of data centers and adopts any

necessary regulations to address the same.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE C CITY OF CHARLOTTE CITY COUNCIL:

Section 1. The June 8, 2026, temporary moratorium is hereby extended commencing on


November 6, 2026, and expiring no later than October 18, 2027, or upon City Council

approval of new regulations and/or policies regarding data centers, whichever comes first.

The moratorium includes the City of Charlotte and its Extraterritorial Jurisdiction.

This moratorium shall not apply to facilities whose primary purpose is supporting the

internal operations of an institution, government entity, healthcare provider,

telecommunications provider, or other permitted principal uses so long as such data

processing activities are not offered as a primary service to off-f-site users. Projects

classified as exempt in accordance with N.C.G.S. § 160D-107(c) are also excluded from

this moratorium as a matter of law.

Section 2. In compliance with the requirements of N.C.G.S. § 160D-107, the City of


Charlotte makes the following statements:

(1) Certain data centers can require considerable amounts of electricity, land, and

water. The City of Charlotte is in the process of researching these complex and

unique land use types and developing appropriate standards and mitigation

methods based on the particular impacts. The City has looked for alternative

solutions to a moratorium but found none. The City continues to be constrained

from modifying certain land use requirements in the UDO due to Session Law

2024-57 (SB 382) which limits the ability of local governments to down-zone

property without the written consents of all affected property owners.

(2) This moratorium applies to the acceptance, processing and approval of

applications for new telecommunications and data storage facilities as defined in

the Unified Development Ordinance. The conditions leading to the moratorium are

the emergence of data centers, in particular hyperscale facilities, and the need to

study and possibly address the impacts of their operation. Additionally, the City

needs additional expertise and time to develop the tools required to ensure

responsible data center development. A moratorium on data center approvals will

provide the City of Charlotte with the opportunity to conduct meaningful research

on the effects of these uses; develop appropriate regulations as are necessary to

ensure organized and responsible growth without sacrificing progress towards

comprehensive plan goals and policies; and determine the best regulatory

framework given Charlotte’s existing treatment of data centers as a monolithic land

use.

(3) The moratorium shall begin on November 6, 2026, and shall end on October 18,

2027, or upon the adoption of specific standards addressing the uses of data

centers, telecommunications and data storage facilities, and associated uses and

any necessary amendments, whichever comes first. The moratorium is necessary

to allow sufficient time for the City to engage a subject matter expert to analyze the

unique development, infrastructure and environmental impacts of data centers,

provide t technical research, a and make recommendations for appropriate regulatory

and policy changes to address those impacts.

(4)  The first 6-8 months of the moratorium will be spent continuing to study the impacts

of data centers to develop policy recommendations and propose regulatory

changes with the assistance of a subject matter consultant to be procured during

that time. In the final 3-5 months, any necessary City Code amendments or policy

updates will be considered which will follow the appropriate legislative process for

adoption. The time needed for research, policy development, and the legislative

adoption process necessitate a moratorium extension until October 18, 2027.

Section 3. In accordance with N.C.G.S. § 160D-107(e) the City of Charlotte shows the


following:

The City of Charlotte has taken all reasonable steps outlined in the June 8, 2026, 150-

day moratorium to address the problems and conditions leading to the imposition of the

moratorium. City staff has begun researching the effects of telecommunications and data

storage facilities, has created a Data Center Task Force, and undertaken extensive

community engagement. The City has determined that an extension is necessary in order

for City staff to continue researching the effects of data centers with  assistance from

subject matters experts, as well as input from the Task Force and the community, and so

that relevant policy and ordinance changes may be brought to the governing body for

consideration. T The City may also use the moratorium extension to engage and/or partner

with outside regulatory agencies to address the impacts of data centers that may be within

the authority of those agencies.
