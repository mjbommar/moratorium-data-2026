### Your Biweekly City Update: August 21, 2026

## Bo's Bulletin

* * *

I am pleased to welcome Stephanie Mardis to Durham as [our next police chief](https://www.durhamnc.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/4241) beginning in early September.

We know how important this role is for our community, and I want to thank everyone who shared input early in the search process. Your feedback helped us identify the values, priorities, and leadership qualities that matter most to Durham, and those insights directly shaped our selection.

Chief Mardis joins us from the Greensboro Police Department, where she has been serving as an assistant chief. She brings more than 24 years of experience, and her career reflects the kind of principled, community-centered leadership that residents told us they want.

Her work includes:

* Developing programs that strengthen investigative performance
* Expanding support for families affected by homicide
* Reinforcing transparency and accountability within the department

## Violence Reduction Strategy

Chief Mardis steps into this role as Durham continues advancing our community safety efforts, including the recommendations from the recent [Summit on Saving Lives](https://www.durhamnc.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/4238). Our violence reduction strategy emphasizes focused support and accountability, strengthened community-based outreach, place-based partnerships in areas most impacted by violence, and trauma-informed approaches at every level.

Chief Mardis is ready to help lead this work forward in strong partnership with our community.

## Upcoming Opportunities to Connect

Chief Mardis will begin her service on September 8. Shortly after she arrives, there will be opportunities for residents to meet her, including at [National Night Out](https://www.durhamnc.gov/3715/National-Night-Out) on October 6. Please keep an eye on the City’s website for additional meet-and-greet events this fall.

I also want to thank Interim Chief Walter Tate, who continues leading the department as interim chief with professionalism and steady guidance until Chief Mardis’ arrival next month.

Thank you for your continued partnership as we work together to strengthen safety throughout Durham.

**\- City Manager Bo Ferguson**

## Stage 2 Water Shortage Response Remains in Place

Drought conditions continue for our community, and we remain under Stage 2 of our Water Shortage Response.

Rain in July helped to briefly ease our drought status, but August has returned to very little rain in our watershed.

## Community Response

We are grateful to our community for reducing water demand 12-14% since the implementation of Stage 2 in mid-June. We must continue these efforts until we receive sufficient rain.

## Stage 2

Reminder that under our Stage 2 Water Shortage Response:

* Spray irrigation is prohibited.
* Drip irrigation and hand watering are allowed, but only with a hose attachment that has an automatic shutoff.
* Car washing is limited to commercial car wash facilities.

Water-saving tips, details on what is or isn't allowed, and how you can report violations can all be found [on our website](https://www.durhamnc.gov/1061/Durham-Saves-Water).

## Durham Awards Over $75,000 in Mini-Grants to 13 Resident-Led Local Projects

We have selected recipients of the [2026 Community Engagement Mini-Grants](https://www.durhamnc.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/4251)! These projects, totaling more than $75,000, help to build skills, strengthen connections, and create impact in Durham neighborhoods.

This annual program, organized through our [Community Partnerships & Engagement Department](https://www.durhamnc.gov/5412/Community-Partnerships-Engagement), provides community groups and organizations with grants ranging from $2,000 to $10,000. These awards are funded through a combination of City general funds and federal American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) dollars.

We received 250 applications for this year’s cycle and 13 projects were selected for funding after a competitive review process. Selected projects reflect the program’s core goals of community engagement, skill-building, and clear community benefit.

Most of the selected projects are expected to be complete by December 2026. We expect to open our next Community Engagement Mini-Grants cycle next spring.

## Introducing Durham Answers: Addressing Durham's Big Questions

We are excited to launch ['Durham Answers](https://www.youtube.com/live/S9-uDDuM2P0?si=QNNF0OmJaKaNnTS3)', a new video series where local experts address the top issues facing our community and answer questions directly from residents.

In [our premiere episode](https://www.youtube.com/live/S9-uDDuM2P0?si=QNNF0OmJaKaNnTS3), we tackle a critical question: What are we doing to reduce homelessness?

When neighbors wait months or years for stable housing, health, employment, and family stability all suffer. But the core issue isn't just a lack of shelter—it's a system that wasn't built to respond fast enough. In Durham, we’re treating homelessness as a solvable system failure, not an endless crisis.

Through a new, unified Homelessness Strategic Framework, the City of Durham and Durham County—alongside community partners—are building a proactive response. By securing housing options in advance, removing financial barriers, and using real-time data, our goal is to cut the time from outreach to a signed lease down to 30 days.

[Watch the premiere episode on our YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/live/S9-uDDuM2P0?si=QNNF0OmJaKaNnTS3).

## Resident Data Center Survey Now Available

City Council passed a data center moratorium which restricts new data center development until May 3, 2027.

Per state law, this pause is only allowed in order to grant staff the time to draft appropriate regulations.

We are collecting information on resident perspectives as we research potential standards. Please take our [Durham Resident Engagement Survey on Data Centers](https://forms.cloud.microsoft/g/BG9Kb7wBDu) to give your opinions on how we should craft these regulations.

## Back to School Road Safety

New school year, new all-way stop near Club Boulevard Elementary!

This safety feature:

* Helps reduce vehicle speeds at the intersection of West Club Boulevard & Glendale Avenue
* Helps lower the risk of turning crashes
* Creates more predictable driver behavior & shorter cross distances which can improve pedestrian safety
* Slows vehicle approaches and increase driver awareness of nearby cyclists

As the school year begins, please remember to fall all road safety rules around schools and buses.
