Library Update


The Chairman recognized Library Director Marlan Brinkley for an update on Library activities.

Mr. Brinkley spoke about his strategic planning process that had been underway for several months.

He noted that the process has involved many stakeholders and included a day-long session on the

same day.

Mr. Brinkley also spoke (Ellis and Allison) regarding a book by mail program. He noted that the

program as designed would be set up for disabled individuals and would be free due to a USPS

program. Mr. Brinkley asked that the program be implemented as well as a waiver of any late fees that

would occur due to difficulties in books being returned.

Commissioner Ellis motioned to approve the program (including the waiver of late fees), second

by Commissioner Allison. By a vote of 5-0 the motion passed.

Cryotocurrency Ordinance Legislative Hearing


Chairman Walker recognized Ronald Harmon for an overview of the ordinance. Mr. Harmon

noted that the ordinance as written would prohibit the establishment of cryptocrrency mines.

Chairman Walker declared the meeting into a Legislative Hearing pursuant to NC General

Statute 160D-601.

Glenn Kern provided comments in favor of the ordinance as it was written. Mr. Kern noted that

a facility in Cherokee County has been very detrimental to that community and he does not feel that

McDowell County would suitable for these facilities due to the noise impacts and its impact on the

natural resources of the county. He also spoke about his concern to its impact on the electric grid.

Diane Slater spoke in support of the ordinance due to her concerns related to the impact to the

electric grid.

Chris Price spoke in opposition to the ordinance due to his concerns about governmental

regulation of private enterprise. He said he would support an ordinance regulating the activity, but not

prohibiting it.

Neil Schunke stated his opposition to the activity to cryptocurrency due to the impacts of the

activity on the electrical grid.

Commissioner Ellis motioned to go out of the legislative hearing. Commissioner Greene

seconded the motion and the vote to close the legislative hearing was unanimous.

Chairman Walker asked for Commissioner comments. Vice Chairman Brown stated his general

agreement with the comments presented. Chairman Walker noted that the draft ordinance allows

residential cryptocurrency mining.

The County Manager noted that the Board is free to adopt the ordinance with an effective date

of 1/8/24, which would also end the moratorium.

Vice Chairman Brown made a motion to adopt the ordinance as presented, second by

Commissioner Ellis. By a vote of 5-0 the motion passed.

Pisgah National Forest Update


Grandfather District Ranger Nicholas Larson updated on various activities. The meadow loop

trail has opened. He also noted the Catawba Falls Trail will hopefully be open in the late spring.

Mr. Larson noted that the Old Fort Picnic Grounds are being improved with improved shelters

and restrooms.

2

Mr. Larson also noted that there had been significant work throughout the national forest in

improving the health of the forest through prescribed burns, removal of non-native species, and by

improving water quality.

Chairman Walker asked Mr. Larson to provide an update on any prescribed burns. Mr. Larson

noted that there is a prescribed burn planned near Coats American in the spring as well as Singecat

Ridge in the same timeframe.

Commissioner Ellis asked for an estimate on when Catawba Falls would be opened. Mr. Larson

said he hoped by late spring or early summer the access would be fully opened.

Mr. Larson noted that the Pisgah National Forest is the most visited national forest in the

country that does not have a ski slope.

Chairman Walker thanked Mr. Larson for his presentation and encouraged him to return.

Strategic Planning Process


The County Manager noted that the strategic planning process continues. He noted that there

had been a meeting in early December with the Strategic Planning Workgroup. Mr. Wooten noted that

the NC Department of Commerce staff had collected survey information about the community’s

Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats. He noted that the results will be distributed to the

Commissioners. It was also noted that the next step in the process will be the development of a vision

statement. A public meeting will be held in a few months, and the entire process should be completed

in the late spring, according to Mr. Wooten.

Building Projects


The County Manager noted that there were several ongoing building projects of note.

DSS Building

Mr. Wooten presented a recommendation for moving forward with a replacement facility for the

Department of Social Services. He noted that the Commissioners had discussed such a facility for

several years and had looked at State funding for the project. The County’s legislative delegation had

encouraged the development of renderings and site plans in order to help with the advocacy.

Mr. Wooten presented a proposal from the County’s selected architect Little. The proposal

would allow Little to initiate some preliminary planning which would result in a starting point for site

development. The cost of the proposal is $38,000. Mr. Wooten recommended paying for the proposal

out of the American Rescue Plan Act interest fund if the Commissioners chose to move forward.

Commissioner Ellis made a motion to accept the proposal, second by Commissioner Greene.

By a 5-0 vote the motion passed.

Recreation Center

towards the end of January. The goal is to have bids presented to the Commissioners for approval at

their March meeting.

Water System Update


The County Manager advised of several ongoing water projects:

Nebo

- Work has begun on Phase 1, which comprises NC 126, Wildlife Road, Lonesome Pine Drive,

Jack Corpening Road, Sunny Acres, and Bentwood Drive. The line on Lonesome Pine Drive is

essentially complete.

- Hoppy Tom Hollow has been permitted and is ready for bid. The grant will pay for the plumbing

for these residences. Therefore, letters are being prepared to be mailed to the property owners

advising them of this news and asking for their interest to be confirmed before work begins.

- Nebo Phase 2 is under final design and will be submitted for permitting soon. Part of this work

involves the relocation of the US 70 master meter towards the City limits. A site has been

identified immediately west of Beaman Road. The property owner David Patenaude has asked

for two water taps in exchange for the meter being placed on the property.

Commissioner Greene motioned to approve granting two water taps to Mr. Patenaude in

exchange for the easement, second by Commissioner A Allison. By a vote of 5-0 the motion passed.

- The grant for the East End project along NC 126 was awarded. The engineering contract has

been signed and the initial survey and design will begin soon. A meeting with property owners

has taken place.

- An Appalachian Regional Commission grant to extend water around Exit 90 has been

awarded. Staff has selected an engineering firm as being the most qualified. The selected firm

is preparing a contract for Board approval.

Providence Area/West Marion Elementary Area

- The Appalachian Regional Commission grant for $500,000 to extend water towards West

Marion Elementary was awarded.

- The State budget granted $3.88 million to McDowell County for the project to extend water

around the community near West Marion Elementary. We will be able to move forward with next

steps once the State sends the formal letter to proceed. The letter has been requested, but not

received as of 1/2/2024.

- The Board of Education has voted to allocate $500,000 over several years to the water project.

- Staff will initiate the engineering selection once the State letter has been received.

Broadband Update


The County Manager provided an update on the broadband grant process, noting that the

Commissioners have previously authorized the submission of a Completing Access to Broadband

(CAB) grant. Staff has been working with the NC Broadband Office on the initial application steps.

Mr. Wooten noted that it appears that after all of the planned grants there will be approximately

prioritize one community over another for future grants.

Sheriff Redaction Request


The County Manager reminded the Board that Sheriff Buchanan had previously requested that

Law Enforcement Officer’s personally identifiable information be removed. Mr. Wooten noted that two

prior discussions had resulted in the rejection of the request. He also noted that he had sent a letter to

the County’s legislative delegation asking for assistance in providing guidance on the issue since NC

General Statute 153A-98 (c4) is vague.

Commissioner Greene expressed his reservations about granting the request since he did not

believe the referenced statute explicitly required the redaction. He said he had concern about leaving

out other County departments and agencies.

Commissioner Ellis asked Commissioner Allison why he requested the item be revisited without

having received guidance from the General Assembly staff. Mr. Allison responded that he had spoken

to Sheriff Buchanan and staff since the last vote and wanted to reconsider the item.

Commissioner Greene motioned to reject the Sheriff’s request, second by Commissioner Ellis.

By a vote of 2-3 the motion failed.

Commissioner Allison motioned to approve the Sheriff’s request, second by Commissioner

Brown.

The County Manager asked for a clarification on the intention of the motion as to whether the

redaction would be available to all Law Enforcement Officers who reside in the county or to only

McDowell County Sheriff’s Office LEOs. It was stated that the intent of the motion was for the request

to only cover MCSO sworn staff.

By a vote of 3-2 the motion passed.
