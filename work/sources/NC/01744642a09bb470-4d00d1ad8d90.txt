Proposed Data Center Policy & Community Discussion

As data center facility interest grows across the nation, Cumberland County has been evaluating potential policy and zoning considerations related to data center development.

Beginning in early 2026, the Cumberland County Board of Commissioners initiated discussions to proactively examine how data centers may fit within the County’s long-term planning efforts and strategic growth goals. As part of this process, the Board held work sessions and public hearings to gather information and community feedback regarding potential benefits and considerations associated with data center development. Topics discussed have included economic development opportunities, infrastructure demands, land use compatibility, utility needs, noise, environmental considerations and potential impacts on surrounding communities.

This page serves as a central location for information related to data center discussions, meeting materials, frequently asked questions and opportunities for public engagement.

What We Heard

Cumberland County gathered public input on the proposed data center ordinance through two community input sessions and an online survey. The Data Center Ordinance Public Input Survey & Sessions Report summarizes the comments, concerns, questions, opportunities and potential development standards identified through the input process.

Common themes included water and electrical capacity, location and compatibility, noise, environmental protection, and transparency, monitoring and enforcement.

Community input was provided to Planning staff and the Joint Planning Board Codes Subcommittee for consideration during development of the draft ordinance.

How Other Communities Regulate Data Centers

As Cumberland County develops a proposed data center ordinance, Planning staff researched how other communities regulate data center development. The research found approaches vary by community. Some jurisdictions allow data centers in certain zoning districts, while others require a special use permit, conditional zoning or additional development standards

Common approaches include:

Limiting data centers to certain commercial or industrial zoning districts.
Requiring special use permits or conditional zoning.
Establishing development standards specific to data centers.
Regulating site design, parking and other development requirements.
Using different requirements based on the size or type of facility.

Planning staff reviewed regulations from communities including Buncombe, Chatham, Edgecombe, Madison and New Hanover counties, as well as Durham, Greenville, Holly Springs and Morrisville.

Buncombe County Permitted in certain districts with supplemental standards
