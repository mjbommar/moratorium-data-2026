2023.63 Proclamation – Designating June as Elder Abuse Awareness Month


WHEREAS,  in 2006, the International Network for the Prevention of Elder Abuse, in support of


the United Nations International Plan of Action, proclaimed a day to recognize the

significance of elder abuse as a public health and human rights issue; and

WHEREAS,  Henderson County's vulnerable and elder adults deserve to live safely with dignity; and WHEREAS,  vulnerable and elder adults of all socio-economic, racial and ethnic backgrounds


may be targets of abuse, neglect or exploitation which occurs in fafamilies, long term

care facilities and communities; and

WHEREAS,  Henderson County Adult Protective Services received 579 reports in 2022 with 339


screened in for evaluation; and

WHEREAS,  we know that elder abuse and mistreatment are underreported according to a study by the World Health Organization that found only 1 in 24 cases of elder abuse is


reported; and

WHEREAS,  recognizing that it is up to all of us, to ensure that proper social structures exist so


people can retain community and societal connections, reducing the likelihood of

abuse; and

WHEREAS, protecting Henderson County's vulnerable and elder adults is a


community responsibility and it is imperative that communities work

together to help reduce and prevent abuse, neglect and exploitation; and

WHEREAS, all residents should watch for signs of abuse such as physical trauma,


withdrawal, depression, anxiety, fear of family members, friends or

caregivers and report suspected abuse or exploitation to the

Department of Social Services.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT PROCLAIMED that the Henderson County Board of


Commissioners does hereby declare June as Elder Abuse Awareness Month in Henderson

County, and encourages the community to work together to help reduce and prevent abuse,

neglect and exploitation of our elders.

Adopted this the 1st day of May, 2023.

Commissioner Hill l made the motion that the Board adopt the Proclamation to declare June as

Elder Abuse Awareness Month in Henderson County. All voted in favor and the motion carried.

Facility Use Policy Exemption – American Legion


The County has received a request from the American Legion Auxiliary Unit 77 to set up a

poppy display in the Historic Courthouse Rotunda. The request would be now through Memorial

Day. Part of the would include a donation cup, which would involve solicitation.

2023.62 PUBLIC HEARING –  – To Consider a Moratorium on “Cryptocurrency Mining”


A Public Hearing has been scheduled on a draft Ordinance Imposing a Moratorium on

Cryptocurrency Mining.

Chairman McCall made the motion to go into Public Hearing. All voted in favor and the motion

carried.

County Attorney Russ Burrell gave the Board an update on what cryptocurrency mining meant

from a practical standpoint. It uses tremendous amounts of electricity, and is extremely loud. He

stated that the request was to impose a 60-day moratorium on cryptocurrency mining to allow the

Planning Board to recommend some regulations in the form of a draft Ordinance for the Board’s

consideration.

Public Input


There was none.

Commissioner Andreotta stated that he had done some research, and noted that one of the other

concerns was about the impact that cryptocurrency mining has on water quality.

Chairman McCall made the motion to go out of Public Hearing. All voted in favor and the motion

carried.

Vice-Chair Edney made the motion to adopt an ordinance impose a 60-day moratorium on

cryptocurrency mining in Henderson County. All voted in favor and the motion carried.

Budget Amendment –  – Motorola Body Cameras


The Board had a discussion at the March 15th meeting regarding a request from the Sheriff’s Office

to approve a fifive-year contract to allow for a switch to Motorola/Watchguard body cameras. The

change is necessary due to the reoccurring failures of our current cameras, for integration with the

vehicle car cameras and for officer safety. Sheriff Griffin stated that when we started the Body

Camera program, the vendor selected was Intrensic, but with the necessary in-camera upgrades,

the upgrade for integrated body cameras seems to make sense timing wise.

The Sheriff’s Office has included a Budget Amendment to pay $33,077 from the FY23 budget to

allow them to receive the cameras and start the service. The remaining amounts per the contract

will be payable according to the contract schedule. The total cost of the contract is $379,985 which

includes the cameras, video evidence storage, setup, no fault warranty on the cameras, transfer

stations, batteries, docking stations, and training.

Following discussion, C Commissioner Lapsley made the motion to “approve an appropriation from

the Automotive Supplies funds in an amount of $33,077 and the necessary budget amendment(s),

and to authorize the Sheriff’s Office to purchase the body cameras.” All voted in favor and the

motion carried.
