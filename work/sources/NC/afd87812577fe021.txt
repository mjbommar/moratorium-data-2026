     Adrienne Gardner, Development Services Director, presented
research from Development Services Staff on Data Center
Development and related Moratoriums throughout the State.

     Howard Jones, County Attorney, advised options for the
Board related to land use ordinances for future data center
development consideration. One option proposed by the County
Attorney was to adopt a Moratorium to allow the proper time for
research and amendments associated with the Surry County land
use ordinance.

     Adrienne Gardner, Development Services Director, read the
proposed draft Moratorium aloud:

     ORDINANCE IMPOSING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON DEVELOPMENT
APPROVALS FOR DATA CENTERS AND RELATED HIGH-IMPACT FACILITIES IN
ALL AREAS SUBJECT TO SURRY COUNTY ZONING AUTHORITY AND
JURISDICTION

     WHEREAS, the Surry County Board of Commissioners (the
"Board") is responsible for promoting the health, safety, and
general welfare of the County's residents;

     WHEREAS, North Carolina General Statute § 160D-107
authorizes local governments to adopt temporary moratoria on
development approvals under certain conditions;

     WHEREAS, the Board has observed a recent increase in
inquiries and potential applications for high-impact facilities,
specifically data centers within the unincorporated areas of the
County;

     WHEREAS, these types of facilities present unique
challenges and potential impacts not adequately addressed by the
County's current Development Ordinance;

     WHEREAS, the County's existing regulations were not
developed with these specific types of high-impact uses in mind,
and the County needs time to develop appropriate, comprehensive
standards to ensure that any future development of such
facilities is consistent with the County's long-term land use
plan and protects the public interest;

     WHEREAS, a temporary moratorium on accepting and processing
new applications for such facilities is a reasonable and
                                                              14450


necessary measure to allow County staff and the Planning Board
sufficient time to study the issues, engage with stakeholders,
and draft appropriate amendments to the Development Ordinance;

     WHEREAS, the Board finds implementation of this moratorium
is necessary to protect the public interest and welfare of the
County’s residents until appropriate regulations regarding the
development of data centers can be adopted.

     NOW, THEREFORE, be it now ordained by the Surry County
Board of Commissioners as follows:

     Section 1.     Moratorium Imposed. A temporary moratorium
is hereby imposed for a period of sixty (60) days from the
effective date of this Ordinance. During this period, the County
shall not accept, process, or approve any applications for the
following development approvals: Special Use Permits; Zoning Map
Amendments; Building Permits (except for existing lawful uses
for interior renovations that do not expand capacity or impact
exterior appearance/noise); and any other development approval
required by law; for the establishment, construction, erection,
alteration, or expansion of any data center.

     Section 2.     Effective Date, Duration and Termination.
This Ordinance shall be in full force and effect from its
adoption on and June 1, 2026 and shall last for a period of
sixty (60) days therefrom, unless earlier terminated, or
modified by the Board in accordance with North Carolina law.
The stated duration is reasonably necessary to address the
problems identified herein.

     Section 3.     Application and Definition. For the
purposes of this ordinance, “data center” is defined as any
building or group of buildings containing one or more large-
scale computer systems or networked server computers or other
similar or related equipment, for data storage, data processing,
web hosting, program development, application hosting, streaming
services, artificial intelligence, artificial intelligence
training, quantum computer development, or other related or
similar uses, processes, work, or services. Typical supporting
equipment may include mechanical and electrical equipment,
redundant power supply connections, redundant data
communications connections, power generators, cooling units, and
enhanced security features.

     For the purposes of this Ordinance, the term “data center”
shall also include “cryptocurrency mining” and other comparable
land uses, and the terms shall be used interchangeably. The
terms “cryptocurrency mining,” as well as “cryptomining,” or
“cryptomining operation” shall be defined as any building or
group of buildings containing one or more large-scale computer
systems or networked server computers for computationally
intensive applications such as blockchain technology, validating
blockchain transactions, cryptocurrency mining, and other
similar functions.

       This definition and ordinance shall not apply to use of
data processing equipment which is clearly incidental and
subordinate to a permitted principal use which is currently
clearly identified and listed in the Table of Uses in the Surry
County Development Ordinance and is intended solely to support
only the on-site operations of such principal use. Such
principal uses may include, without limitation, hospitals,
                                                             14451


medical facilities, financial institutions, offices, educational
institutions, or similar uses, provided such data processing
activities are not offered to, or used by, off-site users, or
off-site locations.

     Section 4.     Statement of the Problems or Conditions and
Alternatives Considered.

     a.   Adverse issues associated with data centers. Data
centers present serious problems which may negatively impact
Surry County and the welfare of its citizens, including but not
limited to:

    i.   Extremely high energy consumption and strain.

     ii. Significant noise generation from cooling systems and
backup generators, affecting adjacent residential areas and
general quality of life.

    iii. Substantial water usage for cooling purposes.

     iv. Environmental impacts, including hazardous material
storage and electronic waste disposal.

     v.   Land use compatibility concerns, particularly in or in
the vicinity of schools, places of worship, places of public
recreation, and other sensitive locations, and in or in the
vicinity of rural, residential, or agricultural zoned districts
and/or uses.

     vi. Negative impacts to the rural, scenic and bucolic
assets and way of life which make Surry County unique.

     b.   Necessity for Development Ordinance amendments. Data
centers are not included in the permitted uses listed in the
Table of Uses in Section 154.007 of the Surry County Development
Ordinance, meaning data centers are permitted for the use listed
in the Table of Uses that most closely generates the same land
use impacts. There is no single use listed in the Table of Uses
that uses the amounts of water and electricity and generates the
combines environmental impacts as data centers. The Board finds
that, in addition to amendment of the Development Ordinance to
specifically address data centers, different zoning and
development controls, such as a special use permit or
development agreement, may be necessary and appropriate for data
centers and should be fully explored, and that a moratorium is
needed to pause development approvals to allow the County time
to accomplish the same.

     c.   Alternative Course of Action considered. The Board
has considered alternative courses of action, such as relying on
the existing Development Ordinance, existing general use
permits, or a hastily considered zoning text amendment, but has
determined these are inadequate and that the County needs
additional time to research and develop specific performance
standards (e.g., noise limits, utility capacity requirements,
setbacks) needed to mitigate the unique impacts of data centers
effectively. Data centers, their impacts and the measures
needed to protect County citizens, present complex issues and
require time to assess. For these reasons, the Board finds that
there is not a viable alternative to a moratorium.

     Section 5.     Plan of Action and Schedule of Actions to be
taken During the Moratorium.

     a.   Now through June 8, 2026: Development Services is
directed to further their research and drafting of amendments to
the Development Ordnance to address data centers.

     b.   At the next regularly scheduled Planning Board meeting
on June 8, 2026: Development Services shall provide the
Planning Board with the results of their research and analysis
and also the then current preliminary draft of possible text
amendments to the Development Ordinance related to data centers.
The Planning Board shall conduct review and discussion of the
same. The Planning Board shall then advise the Director of
Development Services whether the Planning Board believes that
the amendments are ready for formal planning board review at the
next available date for a public hearing, which is July 13, 2026
and if the Planning Board believes it has sufficient information
to conduct a formal review on July 13, 2026. If the Planning
Board believes that additional time is needed and the moratorium
needs to be extended to provide the same, the Planning Board
shall explain how much additional time the Planning Board needs,
and what it would hope to accomplish during said additional
time.

     c.   At the Next regularly scheduled Board of Commissioners
Meeting on June 15, 2026: the Board of Commissioners shall
review the state of the draft amendments at that time and the
advice and counsel of the Director of Development Services and
the Planning Board and determine whether it is prudent to
proceed with public hearings on the then available draft text
amendments to the Development Ordinance, before the Planning
Board on July 13, 2026, and the Board of Commissioners on July
20, 2026, or, instead, direct staff to schedule a hearing for an
extension of the moratorium on data centers so that additional
due diligence can be done by the Planning Board, and staff.

     Section 6.     Scope and Exemptions. This moratorium shall
apply to all unincorporated areas of Surry County subject to
County zoning authority. Exemptions include any development
that has received valid development approval in accordance with
G.S. 160D-107(c) prior to the effective date of this ordinance.

     Section 7.     Conflict with Other Ordinances. The
provisions of any other ordinance of Surry County which conflict
with the terms of this Moratorium are temporarily superseded by
this Ordinance while this Moratorium is in effect, but not
thereafter.

     Section 8.     Severability. If any section or part of
this Ordinance is declared invalid or unenforceable by a court
of competent jurisdiction, such invalidity shall not affect the
validity of the remaining sections or parts.

     This Ordinance is adopted by the Surry County Board of
Commissioners after conducting a duly noticed legislative
hearing on June 1, 2026.

Adopted this the 1st day of June, 2026.
