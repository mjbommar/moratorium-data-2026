Call to Order


Chairman Brown called the meeting to order at 11:31 a.m.

Cryptocurrency Mining Public Hearing


Commissioner Ellis made a motion to go into a public hearing at 11:32 a.m. regarding

Cryptocurrency Mining, seconded by Commissioner Allison. By a vote of 3-0, the motion

passed.

Ashley Wooten explained that Cryptocurrency Mining (Cryptomining) is essentially

linking a multitude of computers together in a mining farm causing a large energy drain and a

cause for major noise concern.

Vice-Chairman Walker entered the meeting at 11:35 a.m.

Staff suggested placing a moratorium on Cryptomining for the time being.

Citizen Chris Price asked if the moratorium was being placed just because of the sound

issues it would cause. Mr. Wooten and Vice-Chairman Walker informed Mr. Price that the

moratorium was being placed more so as to give staff more time to research in greater detail

what cryptocurrency mining is and what it can do to the community.

Vice-Chairman Walker made a motion to go out of the public hearing at 11:39 am

seconded by Commissioner Ellis. By a vote of 4-0, the motion passed.

1

Vice-Chairman Walker made a motion to approve the two-month recommended

moratorium as presented by staff, seconded by Commissioner Ellis. By a vote of 4-0, the motion

passed.

DSS Update


DSS Director Bobbie Sigmon reviewed her monthly memorandum of understanding

(MOU) with the Board. Mrs. Sigmon is still working on recruiting employees to fill current

vacancies from surrounding colleges, which the Board appreciates.

DSS staff is working on providing heating and air for clients in severe need. Staff is also

waiting on direction from the state for Medicaid expansion.

Mrs. Sigmon updated the Board on finding placements for children who have severe

behavioral needs and continues to seek guidance and support from legislative representatives.

Finance/HR Items


HR Director Anna Lee presented NEOGOV, which is a public sector HR software and

management solution. Mrs. Lee informed the Board that in 2022, there were 94 total employee

terminations and 74 of those terminations were eligible for additional payouts such as vacation

balances and compensatory time.

Commissioner Ellis requested that staff look into paying vacation out for an employee

leaving voluntarily.

Mrs. Lee also discussed the employee turnover rate. Commissioner Ellis inquired about

the reasoning for the number of turnovers and he was informed that there are various reasons

such as a change of career, transitioning to the private sector, pay, etc. By the year 2023, based

on age, 33% of the workforce will be eligible to retire.

There are current challenges with staffing, assessment, data, time, and outdated filing

and file retention since there isn’t adequate software for the County. All of these could lead to

fines due to avoidable penalties. The total cost for year one of NEOGOV is $70,105.66.

Vice-Chairman Walker made a motion to approve NEOGOV and to take the money out

of ARPA interest for one year followed by including the cost in the budget for years after,

seconded by Commissioner Ellis. By a vote of 4-0, the motion passed.

Mr. Wooten informed the Board that most of the County’s bank accounts are currently at

First Citizens. First Bank approached the County Finance Director, Alison Bell, and offered

higher interest rates. For the operating bank account at First Citizens, the County earns 0%

interest, but everything flows through a reserve account, which accrues 0.7%. At First Bank

there will be a rate of 2%.

Vice-Chairman Walker made a motion to approve the staff’s recommendation and move

accounts from First Citizens to First Bank, seconded by Commissioner Allison. Commissioner

Ellis asked if there were any other banks that were looked into and Mrs. Bell informed him that

First Bank was the best rate. By a vote of 4-0, the motion passed.
