Data Center Moratorium Overview


Mr. Wooten gave an overview of the data center moratorium public hearing. The

purpose of the special meeting is to receive comments on a proposed moratorium for

data center development. Data centers house computer servers that store, manage,

and process data for users ranging from tech and social media companies to banks,

cloud providers, and AI models. They range from small on-site server rooms to shared

“colocation” facilities to campus-sized “hyperscale” centers built by companies like

Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft.

A moratorium is a temporary pause on development activities authorized by NC

General Statute 160D-107. A moratorium is not a ban on a specific activity, but it

institutes a pause on activity while the Commissioners and their designees determine

what type of regulation, if any, is needed. On a national and state level, there are

common themes and concerns being expressed by communities.  Those concerns tend

to focus on several areas, such as electric and water demand, noise, and emissions,

among others. The Commissioners have stated they want time to study these issues

and to develop regulations that are appropriate for McDowell County.

Mr. Wooten presented the staff ff recommendations as follows:

● The Board of Commissioners should vote to approve an ordinance implementing

a moratorium.  The Board has the discretion to have a moratorium of any

reasonable length, but the statewide standard has been 12 months.

● Research existing data center development elsewhere in the state, including

regulations other counties have implemented, and learn more about data centers

that have been built in the last several years in response to concerns regarding

electricity, water, sound, etc.

● Develop parameters and development requirements that need to be in a

regulatory ordinance and direct the Planning Board to develop an ordinance that

addresses the concerns expressed by the Commissioners and the public

● Receive public comment on the proposed ordinance

● Receive Commissioner comment on the proposed ordinance

● Make any needed changes to the proposed ordinance after receiving public and

Commissioner comments

● Adopt ordinance, discontinue moratorium, and begin regulating data center

development

Mr. Wooten explained the process for the public hearing to follow. The Board will

hold a public hearing to receive comments on the proposed moratorium. Speakers are

allowed three minutes for comments. The hearing is not designed to be interactive; the

Commissioners will hear comments on the proposed moratorium, but will not answer

questions during the hearing. At the conclusion of the hearing, the Board will then adopt

an ordinance implementing the moratorium for their preferred duration.

Board Action on Proposed Data Center Moratorium


Chairman Brown introduced the discussion surrounding the proposed data center

moratorium action.

Commissioner Walker moved to enact a 6-month moratorium to give staff ff and the

Planning Board time to review and bring back a solid document related to water

concerns, the power grid, health concerns, develop parameters and requirements for

the ordinance, and let the Board review the ordinance in 6 months. This motion was

seconded by Vice-Chair Greene. Commissioner Garner asked Mr. Wooten about the

possibility of data centers being banned. Mr. Wooten referenced legal guidance from the

County Attorney and others, and noted that you cannot ban any legal activities by

ordinance or otherwise. An ordinance would set up guidelines as to what could limit or

allow a data center to come to the community. By a vote of 5-0, the motion passes.
