Agenda Title: Adoption of Amended Mid-East RPO MOU

Summary of Information:  The Mid-East Rural Planning Organization (RPO) is one of 18 RPOs in North

Carolina, serving Beaufort County, Martin County, and the areas of Pitt County outside the planning

boundaries of the Greenville Urban Area Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO). Pursuant to N.C.G.S.

136-210-213, the Mid-East RPO coordinates, assists, and develops local and regional transportation plans;

provides a forum for public participation in the transportation planning and implementation process; provides

transportation-related information to local governments; and develops and prioritizes aviation, bicycle &

pedestrian, ferry, highway, public transit, and rail projects that it believes should be included in the State

Transportation Improvement Program (STIP).

All RPOs in the State operate based off an adopted Memorandum of Understanding (MOU). The MOU

specifies the purposes, responsibilities, membership, and structure of the RPO, and is required to be signed by

each member county, the lead planning agency (LPA), and the North Carolina Department of Transportation

(NCDOT). The original MOU for the Mid-East RPO was signed on February 18, 2002, and was amended on

February 6, 2006; May 20, 2010, and November 19, 2018. On July 23, 2026, the Mid-East RPO’s governing

board (Transportation Advisory Committee) voted unanimously to endorse an amended MOU.

In order for the new MOU to become effective, it must be adopted by the Beaufort County Board of

Commissioners, Martin County Board of Commissioners, Pitt County Board of Commissioners, Mid-East

Commission Board of Directors, and signed by the N.C. Secretary of Transportation. The governing boards of

participating municipalities may sign a municipal statement of adoption; however, this is optional, and not

required for the MOU to become effective.

Submitter Recommendation/Motions:  Staff recommends aopting the amended Mid-East RPO MOU.

Section I. Establishment of the RPO

It is hereby agreed that the Parties intend to establish and participate in a Rural Transportation Planning

Organization created for the general purposes and responsibilities outlined as follows:

1. Develop sound, short, and long-range local and regional multi-modal transportation plans in

cooperation with the North Carolina Department of Transportation.

2. Provide a forum for public participation in the rural transportation planning process.

3. Develop and prioritize suggestions for transportation projects that the RPO believes should be

included in the State Transportation Improvement Program (STIP).

4. Provide transportation-related information to local governments, and other interested

organizations and persons.

5. To conduct transportation-related studies and surveys for local governments, and other interested

entities or organizations.

6. To perform other related transportation planning activities that shall be agreed upon between the

RPO and the North Carolina Department of Transportation.

Section II. Lead Planning Agency

It is hereby further agreed that the transportation plans and programs, and land use policies and programs

for the RPO shall be coordinated by the Mid-East Commission, an agency selected on behalf of

participating local governments and the North Carolina Department of Transportation, to be the

administrative entity and to serve as the lead local planning agency for coordinating rural transportation

planning in the RPO’s three county region. The RPO hereby authorizes the Mid-East Commission to be

the recipient of any funds appropriated to the RPO by the North Carolina Department of Transportation

pursuant to North Carolina General Statute 136-213(c), or otherwise obtained by the RPO.

Summary of Information:  The GREAT Grant Brightspeed Projects current construction schedule for

completion is 12/30/2026 however there is concern there will not be sufficient time to submit the final report

and for the county to issue payment if we wait until the final report is submitted. Because the County must

disburse its committed project funds by December 31, 2026, the original provision tying payment of the

County contribution to project completion or receipt of the final Construction Period progress report is no

longer workable. An amendment to Exhibit J has been proposed by Brightspeed. Recall that the County

contribution is treated as part of the match. To date, Brightspeed has incurred more than $600,642.50 in

Match-Eligible Expenditures, approved by NCDIT, and they are working another reimbursement submission.

Those expenditures exceed the County’s total contribution of $500,000.

The attached proposed amendment would:

• permit payment of the County contribution of $500,000 before the end of the year based on the

documented match-eligible expenditures exceeding the county contribution to the match;

• apply the County contribution toward the project’s required match before Brightspeed’s remaining

match contribution;

• preserve the County’s existing protections under 3 circumstances:

o if the final eligible project costs are lower than anticipated;

o the County’s contribution would exceed the amount permitted by law (no more than 50%), or

o Brightspeed does not complete all contracted locations.

o

The amendment does not increase the County’s original commitment or change the project scope. It only

revises the payment timing and supporting documentation so the County can disburse its committed funds

before the end of 2026 based on expenditures that have already been approved by NCDIT.

The second amount to Charter for $148,169 is for their portion of this project which has been completed.

Submitter Recommendation/Motions:  Approve the disbursement of funds to Brightspeed ($500,000) and the

amendment as well as the Charter payment($148,169) for the Beaufort County GREAT Project

AMENDMENT TO EXHIBIT J TO AGREEMENT NO. 2000066879

Beaufort County GREAT Project

This Amendment revises and clarifies the County payment structure set forth in Exhibit J to the Growing

Rural Economies with Access to Technology grant agreement among the North Carolina Department of

Information Technology ("NCDIT"), Beaufort County ("County"), and Connect Holding II LLC d/b/a

Brightspeed ("Brightspeed").

RECITALS

WHEREAS, NCDIT, the County, and Brightspeed entered into the Growing Rural Economies with Access

to Technology Agreement, Agreement No. 2000066879, effective April 10, 2023 (the "Agreement"), for

the construction of broadband infrastructure in Beaufort County;

WHEREAS, under Exhibit J to the Agreement, the County agreed to contribute Five Hundred Thousand

Dollars ($500,000.00) in matching funds for the Project;

WHEREAS, Exhibit J currently provides that the County will pay the County Contribution within thirty (30)

days after the County receives the final progress report for the Construction Period from NCDIT;

WHEREAS, NCDIT is willing to extend the Construction Period for GREAT projects through June 30,

2027, and the parties to the Agreement are finalizing the related amendment;

WHEREAS, the County desires to disburse its committed Project funds before completion of the

extended Construction Period; and

WHEREAS, the Parties desire to revise the timing of the County Contribution payment because

Brightspeed has already incurred and documented Match-Eligible Expenditures substantially exceeding

the County Contribution, while preserving the County’s repayment and reconciliation protections.

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises contained herein, the Parties agree as

follows:

1. Defined Terms

Capitalized terms not otherwise defined in this Amendment have the meanings assigned to them in the

Agreement.

"Construction Completion Date" means June 30, 2027, or such later date as may be approved through a

written amendment to the Agreement.

"County Contribution" means the amount of matching funds actually paid by the County for the Project

under the Agreement and this Amendment, not to exceed $500,000.00.

The County Contribution is subject to the limitation in Section 2.3.a of the Agreement that the County

shall be applied first to support the County Contribution before additional Match-Eligible Expenditures

are credited toward Brightspeed’s remaining match obligation.

This allocation governs the attribution of Match-Eligible Expenditures toward the required cash match.

It does not alter the date on which an expenditure was incurred, the party that initially paid the

expenditure, or the eligibility requirements applicable to the expenditure. No expenditure may be

counted more than once toward satisfaction of the required cash match.

County Contribution of $500,000.00 within thirty (30) days after execution of this Amendment, subject

cumulative Match-Eligible Expenditures in an amount at least equal to the County Contribution.

8. Final Match Reconciliation and Fifty-Percent Limitation

Following Project completion and final closeout, the Parties shall reconcile the County Contribution

against Brightspeed’s Final Match Contribution and the final Match-Eligible Expenditures accepted by

NCDIT.

In accordance with Section 2.3.a of the Agreement, the County Contribution retained for the Project

shall not exceed fifty percent (50%) of Brightspeed’s Final Match Contribution. If the Agreement or

applicable law imposes a more restrictive limitation, the more restrictive limitation shall control.

If the County Contribution actually paid exceeds fifty percent (50%) of Brightspeed’s Final Match

Contribution, Brightspeed shall repay the excess to the County within sixty (60) days after written notice

of the final reconciliation amount.

The repayment amount shall be calculated as follows:

County Match-Limit Repayment = County Contribution Actually Paid minus fifty percent (50%) of

Brightspeed’s Final Match Contribution

No repayment shall be due under this Section if the result is zero or a negative number.

Example: If Brightspeed’s Final Match Contribution is $1,000,000 or more, the full $500,000 County

Contribution complies with the fifty percent (50%) limitation. If Brightspeed’s Final Match Contribution

is $900,000, the maximum permissible County Contribution would be $450,000, and Brightspeed would

repay $50,000 to the County.

9. Repayment for Unsupported County Match

If final Match-Eligible Expenditures available to support the County Contribution are less than the

County Contribution actually paid, Brightspeed shall repay the unsupported amount to the County

within sixty (60) days after written notice of the final reconciliation amount.

Example: If final Match-Eligible Expenditures available to support the County Contribution total only

$475,000, Brightspeed would repay $25,000 to the County, even if the fifty-percent limitation in Section

8 is otherwise satisfied.

Any repayment required under this Section shall be coordinated with the repayment required under

Section 8 so that the County receives the greater applicable repayment amount, without duplicate

recovery.

No repayment shall be required solely because the final total Project cost is less than the original

Project Budget, provided that the County Contribution complies with the fifty-percent limitation, is fully

supported by final Match-Eligible Expenditures, and Brightspeed has satisfied the Project requirements.

ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE

ACCEPTANCE, PROCESSING, AND APPROVAL OF APPLICATIONS FOR DATA

CENTER DEVELOPMENTS IN BEAUFORT COUNTY, NORTH CAROLINA

WHEREAS, the Beaufort County Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) is charged with

protecting the public health, safety, welfare, natural resources, and economic well-being of the

citizens of Beaufort County; and

WHEREAS, Beaufort County's economy is founded upon agriculture, manufacturing,

commercial fishing, tourism, small businesses, and other industries that depend upon reliable

public infrastructure, abundant natural resources, and responsible long-term planning; and

WHEREAS,  North Carolina General Statute § 160D-107 authorizes local governments to adopt

temporary moratoria on development approvals under certain conditions; and

WHEREAS, large-scale data centers have expanded rapidly throughout North Carolina and the

United States in response to increasing demand for cloud computing, artificial intelligence, digital

storage, and advanced computing technologies and the Board believes potential applications for

these high-impact facilities may be forthcoming within the unincorporated areas of the County;

and

WHEREAS, these types of facilities present unique challenges and potential impacts not

adequately addressed by the County's current zoning regulations, including but not limited to:

• Extremely high electricity consumption and strain on existing utility infrastructure;

• Significant noise generation from cooling systems and backup generators, affecting adjacent

residential areas and general quality of life;

• Substantial water usage for cooling purposes;

• Potential environmental impacts, including hazardous materials and electronic waste disposal;

and

• Land use compatibility concerns, including in rural or agriculturally zoned districts; and

WHEREAS, The Board further finds that approving such developments before completing a

comprehensive review of these issues could result in irreversible impacts to Beaufort County's

infrastructure, natural resources, agricultural economy, utility systems, and quality of life; and

WHEREAS, the Beaufort County Board of Commissioners finds that additional study is necessary

to ensure that future Data Center development is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies

of the Beaufort County Comprehensive Plan, including the protection of agricultural resources,

preservation of rural character, responsible infrastructure planning, environmental stewardship,

and sustainable economic development; and

WHEREAS,  the Board has considered alternative courses of action, such as relying on existing

general use permits, but determined this was inadequate due to the lack of specific performance

standards (e.g., noise limits, utility capacity requirements, setbacks) needed to mitigate the unique

impacts of data centers effectively; and

72

WHEREAS,  a temporary moratorium on accepting and processing new applications for such

facilities is a reasonable and necessary measure to allow County staff and the Planning Board

sufficient time to study the issues, engage with stakeholders, and draft appropriate amendments to

the zoning regulations.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED  by the Beaufort County Board of Commissioners as

follows:

Section 1. Moratorium Imposed

A temporary moratorium is hereby imposed for a period of one (1) year from the effective date of

this Ordinance. Effective immediately upon adoption of this Ordinance, Beaufort County shall not

accept, process, or approve applications for the establishment, construction, expansion, or

operation of a Data Center, including but not limited to:

1. Building permits;

2. Site development approvals;

3. Land disturbance permits;

4. Utility extension approvals;

5. Infrastructure agreements;

6. Development agreements; and

7. Any other County-issued authorization necessary for construction or operation.

Section 2. Definition

For purposes of this Ordinance, a Data Center shall be defined as:

A building, facility, campus, or group of buildings whose principal purpose is the storage,

processing, management, transmission, or hosting of digital data utilizing computer servers and

associated electrical, cooling, telecommunications, backup power, and supporting infrastructure.

A facility shall be considered a Data Center if it meets one or more of the following criteria:

1. Requires an electrical service demand of one (1) megawatt or greater;

2. Occupies five (5) acres or more for data center operations and supporting infrastructure;

3. Includes dedicated cooling systems, substations, emergency power generation, or electrical

infrastructure designed primarily to support commercial data center operations; or

4. Is intended for cloud computing, artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, colocation

services, enterprise hosting, cryptocurrency processing, or other commercial high-density

computing operations.

73

The term Data Center shall not include:

1. Server rooms or information technology facilities accessory to hospitals, medical facilities,

schools, colleges, universities, or governmental facilities;

2. Telecommunications switching facilities regulated as public utilities;

3. Facilities owned and operated by electric utilities for system control, communications, or

grid reliability;

4. Commercial, industrial, agricultural, or office facilities where computer servers are

incidental to the principal business conducted on the property; or

5. Public safety communications centers, emergency dispatch facilities, or other emergency

communications infrastructure.

Section 3. Plan of Action and Schedule

During the moratorium period, the County Manager, County Attorney, Planning staff, Engineering

staff, Emergency Management, Fire Marshal, Public Works, and other appropriate agencies or

departments shall take the following actions:

1. Phase One: Evaluate the potential impacts data centers and similar facilities may have on

the following:

a) Electrical generation, transmission, distribution, and substation capacity.

b) Utility infrastructure improvements and associated public costs.

c) Protection of existing electric service reliability and affordability.

d) Groundwater availability and protection.

e) Public water supply capacity and sustainability.

f) Surface water protection.

g) Agricultural impacts and preservation of prime farmland.

h) Emergency response capabilities.

i) Fire protection requirements.

j) Hazardous materials management.

k) Fuel storage and emergency power generation.

l) Noise mitigation.

m) Air quality impacts.

n) Stormwater management.

o) Wetlands and floodplain protection.

p) Transportation infrastructure impacts.

q) Construction impacts on surrounding communities.

r) Fiscal impacts upon County government.

s) Long-term economic benefits.

t) Environmental sustainability.

u) Decommissioning requirements.

v) Financial assurance or bonding requirements.

w) Compatibility with existing communities, industries, and agricultural operations.

74

x) Potential impacts on future economic development opportunities.

y) Any additional matters determined necessary to protect the public health, safety,

welfare, and economic interests of Beaufort County.

2. Phase Two: Draft land use ordinance and/or amendments and performance standards to

regulate these facilities, including appropriate regulations, setback requirements, noise

limitations, and utility capacity conditions.

3. Phase Three: Planning Board review of the draft amendments.

4. Phase Four: The Board of Commissioners will hold the necessary public hearings to

consider and potentially adopt the final ordinance or applicable amendments.

Section 4. Scope and Exemptions

This moratorium shall apply to all unincorporated areas of Beaufort County. Exemptions include:

1. Any development that has received valid development approval in accordance with G.S.

160D-107(c) prior to the effective date of this ordinance; and

2. Routine maintenance, repair, replacement, modernization, or expansion of existing

facilities that does not increase electrical demand beyond previously approved levels.

Section 5. Duration

This moratorium shall remain in effect for twelve (12) months beginning September 14, 2026 until

September 14, 2027, unless earlier terminated, extended, or modified by the Board in accordance

with North Carolina law. The stated duration is reasonably necessary to address the identified

problems due to the unique and relatively complex nature of data centers.

Section 6. Severability

If any provision of this Ordinance is held invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, such

determination shall not affect the validity of the remaining provisions, which shall remain in full

force and effect.

Adopted this 14th  day of September, 2026.

Agenda Title: Authorize Staff to Draft Ordinance Prohibiting Virtual Currency Kiosks

Summary of Information:  Criminal activity involving scams occurring across the United States as well our

State have seen a drastic increase over the past few years. Beaufort County isn’t an exception to this rise in

crimes involving scams. More recently, scammers have involved the use of virtual currency kiosks to steal

money from innocent people that are being scammed. The Beaufort County Sheriff’s Office has

investigated 42  criminal scam cases involving digital currency in the past three years.

In 2025, the FBI reported more than $333 million had been stolen by scammers through the use of virtual

currency kiosks. Also 2025, North Caolina saw 491 complaints of scams using virtual currency kiosks with a

combined loss of $12.6 million (report attached). Residents of Beaufort County have fallen victim through the

use of virtual currency kiosks. Since 2025, the Beaufort County Sheriff’s Office has investigated 19  cases in

which residents have been instructed to withdraw cash from their bank account and carry it to a virtual

currency kiosk and transfer it to digital currency. Once a person inserts their cash into a virtual currency kiosk

and transmits it to any account other than their own, they can no longer recover that money. Their money is

gone forever. Our residents have lost a total of $159,000.00  through the use of these kiosks.

Through the enactment of HP 920 (attached), the North Carolina Legislature have recently launched

legislation to help prevent the use of virtual currency kiosks from being used to scam people. In Section 1 of

HB 920, it states "§ 53-461. Local government authority not preempted. (a) No Preemption of Local Authority.

– This Article does not preempt, restrict, or otherwise limit the authority of a city or county to regulate virtual

currency kiosks through zoning, land-use controls, development regulations, or other ordinances adopted

pursuant to Chapter 160D of the General Statutes, provided the ordinances do not conflict with the express

provisions of this Article.” As part of this law, our legislature recognized the right for local governments to

regulate such instruments. Our county currently has 9  virtual currency kiosks.

This request is not to prohibit anyone from lawfully purchasing or trading non-fiat currency. There are many

platforms or apps that allow people who wish to buy or sell virtual currency such as CashAPP, Paypal, Venmo,

Binance, Coinbase, and many others. However, because virtual currency kiosks are easily used in the

commission of a crime without practical safeguards, the Beaufort County Sheriff’s Office is requesting that

Commissioners authorize county staff to draft an ordinance prohibiting virtual currency kiosks in a manner

that complies with HB920 and Chapter 160D.

"§ 53-441. Short title.

This Article may be cited as the Virtual Currency Kiosk Consumer Protection Act.

"§ 53-442. Definitions.

The following definitions apply in this Article:

(1) Bank Secrecy Act. – The Bank Secrecy Act, 31 U.S.C. § 5311, et seq., and its

implementing rules and regulations, as amended and recodified from time to

time.

(2) Blockchain. – A distributed digital ledger or database which is chronological,

consensus-based, decentralized, and mathematically verified in nature.

(3) Blockchain analytics. – A software service that uses data from various virtual

currencies and their applicable blockchains to provide a risk rating specific to

digital wallet addresses from users of virtual currency kiosks.

(4) Commissioner. – – The Commissioner of Banks of the State of North Carolina.

(5) Digital wallet. – Hardware or software that enables individuals to store and

use virtual currency.

(6) Digital wallet address. – An alphanumeric identifier representing a destination

on a blockchain for a virtual currency transfer that is associated with a digital

wallet.

(7) Dollar. – – The United States dollar.

(8) Existing virtual currency customer. – A person whose first transaction with a

licensee to transmit virtual currency occurred more than 30 days ago.

(9) Fiat currency. – A medium of exchange that is authorized or adopted by the

United States government as part of its currency and is not backed by a

commodity.

(10) Individual. – A natural person.

(11) New virtual currency customer. – A person that has no previous transactions

with a licensee to transmit virtual currency or a person whose first transaction

with a licensee to transmit virtual currency occurs within the past 30 days.

(12) NMLS. – The Nationwide Multistate Licensing System and Registry

developed by the Conference of State Bank Supervisors and the American

Association of Residential Mortgage Regulators and owned and operated by

the State Regulatory Registry, LLC, or any successor or affiliated entity, for

the licensing and registration of persons in financial services industries.

(13) Prevailing market value. – The value, in dollars, to buy or sell a particular

virtual currency, as applicable, at the time of the transaction, as quoted on a

and having sufficient transaction volume to reflect the prevailing market p price

virtual currency at the time of the transaction.

Providing Appropriate Tools Required to Intercept and Obstruct Terrorism

(16) Virtual currency. – Any type of digital unit that is used as a medium of

payment system technology. Virtual currency includes digital units of

exchange that (i) have a centralized repository or administrator, (ii) are

decentralized and have no centralized repository or administrator, or (iii) may

be created or obtained by computing or manufacturing effort. Virtual currency

does not include digital units that are used (i) solely within online gaming

platforms with no market or application outside of the gaming platforms or

(ii) exclusively as part of a consumer affinity or rewards program and can be

applied solely as payment for purchases with the issuer or other designated

merchants but cannot be converted into or redeemed for fiat currency.

(17) Virtual currency kiosk. – Any fully automated electronic machine owned,

operated, or used by a virtual currency kiosk operator that enables the owner

or operator to facilitate the transfer of fiat currency to a public key that is

recorded on the blockchain, as directed by the customer.

(18) Virtual currency kiosk operator. – A corporation, limited liability company,

limited liability partnership, or foreign entity qualified to do business in the

State t that o operates a virtual currency kiosk within the State. The term does not

include a business location, property owner, lessee, or other person that

provides space for a virtual currency kiosk manufactured or distributed by a

third party.

IC3 Cryptocurrency Kiosk Complaint Data By State

As a supplement to the Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) 2025 Annual

Report, the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) provides the following data

involving cryptocurrency kiosks at the state level.

Cryptocurrency kiosks  are ATM-like devices or electronic terminals that allow users

to exchange cash and cryptocurrency. Criminals may direct victims to send funds

via cryptocurrency kiosks. According to IC3 data, the use of cryptocurrency kiosks

to perpetrate fraudulent activity against the US population is increasing. In 2025,

the IC3 received more than 13,400 complaints reporting the use of cryptocurrency

kiosks, with losses over $388 million — a 23% increase in complaints and a 58%

increase in losses from 2024. More than half of the complaints involved individuals

over 50, with losses over $302 million.

In typical IC3 complaints involving cryptocurrency kiosks, criminals give detailed

instructions to individuals, including how to withdraw cash from their bank, how to

locate a kiosk, and how to deposit and send funds using the kiosk.

Agenda Title: Bunyan Volunteer Fire Department – Acknowledgment of Public Hearing and Tax-Exempt

Apparatus Financing

Summary of Information:  Bunyan Volunteer Fire Department, Inc. (“Bunyan VFD”) borders the City of

Washington and protects one of the County’s more densely populated fire districts, including numerous

waterfront communities. Those areas contain large, elevated homes and properties that are difficult to access

and that, in many cases, require greater-than-typical water flow to extinguish a working fire.

Like other North Carolina fire departments, Bunyan VFD works to be a good steward of district tax dollars and

to return value to property owners by maintaining the lowest insurance rating possible. Water supply

accounts for approximately 40 % of a department’s insurance rating. A department’s ability to transport water

is therefore a critical part of protecting that rating. Bunyan last purchased a pumper/tanker in 2015 and has

not purchased a dedicated tanker since 2000. The Department is now finalizing the purchase of a 3,000-gallon

tanker at a purchase price of $640,000. Bunyan VFD intends to finance $300,000 of that cost through a tax

exempt municipal lease-purchase offered by United Financial, a division of HomeTrust Bank. The quoted rate

is 4.17 percent for a five-year term, with an annual payment of $67,710.29.

United Financials’ application process requires the Department to advertise and conduct a public hearing so

residents of the district may speak in support of or opposition to the financing. That hearing is also required

under Section 147(f) of the Internal Revenue Code if the financing is to qualify for tax-exempt interest

treatment. Notice of the hearing was posted on the official Beaufort County website beginning September 14,

2026, and remained posted through the hearing date. The hearing was held at the Bunyan Fire Department,

8264 Broad Creek Road, Washington, N.C., on September 24, 2026 at 7:00 p.m. No community members

attended, and no opposition was expressed to the Department. A written report of the hearing is attached.

Why is the County involved in the application process?

This item is the public approval required by Internal Revenue Code § 147(f) so that interest on Bunyan VFD’s

lease-purchase may be tax-exempt to the lender. It is not a County borrowing, appropriation, co-signature, or

guarantee. The lessee and sole named obligor will be Bunyan Volunteer Fire Department, Inc. The Board of

Commissioners is the applicable elected representative of the Local Government. As used in the lender

295

certificate, “Local Government” means Beaufort County, and “District” means Bunyan VFD’s assigned fire

response district, which the Department serves under its existing written fire-protection contract with the

County. That district is the area that is not otherwise provided with primary firefighting service. Mutual aid

and automatic aid remain available in the ordinary course.

Why United Financial is a practical, lower-cost option versus a conventional bank vehicle loan:

• Specialist lender. United Financial has financed fire apparatus, equipment, and stations for municipal

and volunteer departments in North Carolina and neighboring states for more than 30 years and is

familiar with North Carolina Local Government Commission and volunteer-department

documentation.

• Tax-exempt rate. A properly structured municipal lease-purchase allows the lender’s interest income

to be exempt from federal income tax. The quoted 4.17 percent, five-year rate is below a typical

taxable commercial vehicle loan of comparable size and term.

• Lower transaction cost. United Financial is a direct lender, reports no origination fee on these

transactions, and does not insert a broker commission. Conventional vehicle loans frequently carry

origination or documentation fees.

• Terms matched to a cash-funded down payment. Bunyan is financing $300,000 of a $640,000 tanker

over five years at a fixed 4.17 percent ($67,710.29 per year). A typical community-bank vehicle loan is

taxable and often shorter or fee-loaded in a way that raises the all-in cost.

• Fits volunteer-department structure. Volunteer fire corporations may use this tax-exempt “on behalf

of” structure under the Internal Revenue Code when a public hearing is held and the applicable

elected official of the served government approves the plan of finance. That is the certificate United

Financial has asked the Chairman to sign.

Submitter Recommendation/Motions:  The Office of Emergency Services respectfully requests that the Board

of Commissioners, as the applicable elected representative of Beaufort County, approve the plan of finance

described in the attached certificate and authorize the Chairman to execute the Certificate of Approval

(Schedule H), and any substantially similar acknowledgment United Financial may require, confirming that:

(1) notice of a public hearing on financing a new tanker apparatus was posted on the official Beaufort County

website for at least seven consecutive days from September 14, 2026 through September 24, 2026;

(2) a public hearing was conducted at Bunyan VFD on September 24, 2026 at 7:00 p.m.; and

(3) Beaufort County is aware of the Department’s planned purchase. This action approves the plan of finance

for Internal Revenue Code § 147(f) purposes only. It does not create a County debt, appropriation, or

guarantee, and it does not name the County as a party to the lease.

Agenda Title: Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy Committee Appointment

Summary of Information:  The Mid-East Region Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy is requesting

an elected official be appointed to the advisory Committee.

Simply put, a CEDS is a strategy-driven plan for regional economic development. A CEDS is the result of a

regionally owned planning process designed to build capacity and guide the economic prosperity and

resiliency of an area or region. A CEDS is required for an economic development district to be eligible for EDA

funding.

This advisory committee will meet monthly over the course of a 1-year planning period, with the first meeting

occurring in mid-to-late October. They are tentatively planning to meet in person and share lunch at the first

and last meetings (likely at the Telecenter in Williamston) and meet virtually for the other meetings. Once the

list of members is finalized, the group will be polled to see what meeting schedule works best.
