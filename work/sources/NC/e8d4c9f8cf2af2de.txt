7.   Public Hearing - Brian Barnett

     a.   Public Hearing Comment Rules

     b.    Ordinance of the Davie County Board of Commissioners Imposing a One-Year
           Moratorium on the Development of Data Centers in Davie County, North Carolina
County Manager Brian Barnett presented the statutory authority for and purpose of the
proposed data center moratorium. He explained that the proposed 12-month moratorium
would provide the County time to study the potential impacts of data center development,
develop appropriate zoning regulations, and conduct three public hearings throughout the
review process. Mr. Barnett stated that neither Davie County staff nor the Board of
Commissioners had entered into any non-disclosure agreements with data center developers.
While county staff have been approached by companies seeking information regarding zoning
regulations and utility availability, no formal applications for a data center have been
submitted. He further explained that the County's current zoning ordinance does not define a
data center, making the moratorium necessary to allow time to establish a clear definition and
appropriate land use regulations. Mr. Barnett also noted that developers typically meet with
utility and development services staff during the preliminary stages of evaluating a potential
project before submitting a formal application.

The public hearing opened at 6:33 PM regarding the proposed one-year moratorium on data
center development within the County's zoning jurisdiction.

We will start with the 8 individuals who signed up for public comment.
Chair Finney asked if anyone was against the moratorium to raise their hand; no one did so.
County Attorney Ed Vogler reviewed the rules governing the public hearing and reminded
speakers to limit comments to the proposed moratorium and maintain appropriate decorum.

she had researched the issue and believes the revenue generated would not provide a
significant benefit to the County.

Breanna Tilley stated that she hopes the community will stand together to keep data centers

John Fuller (154 Cherry Hill Road, Mocksville, NC) read an excerpt from the 2019 Davie County
Comprehensive Plan regarding future growth and development. He stated that potential data

citizens who could provide recommendations to the Board.

advisory board and conduct independent studies.


Jeff Plott urged the Board to approve the proposed moratorium.

Chair Finney thanked everyone for their research and everyone who came out tonight.

Commissioner Jones made a motion to close the public comment section of the public hearing.
Commissioner Poindexter seconded the motion. All were in favor, and the motion passed 5-0
(Mark Jones, Benita Finney, Richard Poindexter, Terry Renegar, M Brent Shoaf)

Commissioner Jones stated that a 600-acre industrial park should not be proposed. He stated
that 100 data centers in North Carolina are either being proposed or developed right now.
Commissioners asked questions regarding the proposed timeline, the definition of a data
center, the current zoning ordinance, and the process by which commercial developers
communicate with County staff.
 Ed Vogler stated that all authority comes from the State of NC, and they passed a law, Chapter
106 D of the NCGS law, which provides that local governments may implement a temporary
moratorium. Authority is given to extend a moratorium if it meets the requirements that are
stated in the law. We can only have a moratorium for a reasonable time period, which is a year,
according to looking at other countys. Once the year is close to being up, if the findings are
made, we can extend it.

Commissioner Jones made a motion to strike the second sentence of section 1, item 2, (The
Davie County Board of Commissioners (Board) shall be permitted to terminate this moratorium
early upon the passage of a resolution by a majority vote of the board). Commissioner Shoaf
seconded the motion. The Chair announced there was a motion to adopt the one-year
moratorium on the development of Data Centers in Davie County, minus the second sentence.
All were in favor, and the motion passed 5-0 (Mark Jones, Benita Finney, Richard Poindexter,
Terry Renegar, M Brent Shoaf).

Cooperative Extension
•      Extension Hosts Summer Intern: N.C. Cooperative Extension, Davie County Center, is one of 47 County Extension offices in the
       state selected to host interns for the NC State Extension 2026 Summer Internship Program. The program received a record
       setting number of 274 applications from college students representing 44 institutions across the country. The internship
       program exposes students to the challenges and awards of community engagement work and helps them consider careers in
       agriculture, food systems, nutrition, youth development, family and consumer sciences, natural resources and environmental
       science. Cayleigh Shaw from Mocksville, a former Davie 4-Her and FFA member, is serving as this year’s Davie Extension intern.

•     Cooleemee Heritage Day: The Davie Livestock Agent worked with an area dairy farmer to teach Cooleemee Elementary first
      grade students about dairy farming. Students fed hay to a dairy cow and learned about how differently cattle digest food. The
      Cooleemee Historical Association sponsors this event for first graders to learn what life was like in the 1930s in the mill town.
•     Pesticide Disposal Day: The Davie Extension Center in partnership with NCDA&CS hosted a free pesticide collection for
      homeowners and farmers at the Davie Center on May 6. The collection event resulted in the proper disposal of 135 pesticide
      containers totaling 1,516 pounds of material. By removing these products from homes and farms, the program reduced
      potential environmental hazards and helped avoid an estimated $7,600 in disposal costs. Participants expressed appreciation
      for the opportunity to safely dispose of hazardous materials that are otherwise difficult for homeowners, landscapers and
      farmers to manage.
•     Volunteer Appreciation & 4-H Achievement Night: On May 14, Extension volunteers, 4-H’ers and their families gathered at
      the Davie Senior Center to recognize the incredible contributions and accomplishments of our volunteers and youth
      throughout 2025. Cherry Hill Farm was recognized as the 2025 Extension Contributor of the Year, and Marissa Brzescinski as
      the 2025 Extension Volunteer of the Year. Karen Martin with the Davie County Public Library received the Friend of 4-H Award.
      Several 4-H youths also received awards for 2025. Levi Williams was recognized the 4-H Volunteer of the Year Award, and
      Nadilyn Einloth as the 4-Her of the Year. Alex Ortiz received the ‘I Dare You’ Award, while Leah Frick received the Head and
      Heart Award.
