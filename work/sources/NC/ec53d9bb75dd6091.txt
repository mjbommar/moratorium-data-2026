  ORDINANCE IMPOSING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON DEVELOPMENT
  APPROVALS FOR CERTAIN LARGE-SCALE DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT

        WHEREAS, Alamance County is authorized under Chapter 160D of the North Carolina
General Statutes to regulate land development within its planning and development-regulation
jurisdiction; and

       WHEREAS, N.C. Gen. Stat. § 160D-107 authorizes a local government to adopt a
temporary moratorium on development approvals required by law when the moratorium is
reasonably necessary to address identified conditions; and

        WHEREAS, the Board of Commissioners finds that large-scale data center development
may present land-use, infrastructure, utility, environmental, public-safety, and community-
compatibility impacts materially different in scale and character from other commercial and
industrial development, including substantial electrical demand; electrical transmission,
substation, and distribution-facility needs; water demand and wastewater generation; backup-
generation equipment, noise, air-emissions, and fuel-storage impacts; building mass and site-
design concerns; stormwater and watershed impacts; road and emergency-service impacts; and the
need for coordinated review with utility providers and other affected agencies; and

        WHEREAS, the Board further finds that a data center designed for or reasonably
anticipated to require ten (10) megawatts or more of aggregate maximum electric demand may
create or materially contribute to the foregoing conditions; and

       WHEREAS, the County’s existing development regulations do not adequately address the
foregoing conditions; and

       WHEREAS, the Board has considered alternatives to a temporary moratorium, including
application of the County’s existing uniform development ordinances, subdivision, building, and
other development regulations on a case-by-case basis, but finds those alternatives inadequate
because the existing regulations contain no definition or use-specific standards, do not provide for
review of material utility and infrastructure impacts, and would require applications to be
considered under standards not tailored to this use; and

        WHEREAS, a temporary moratorium on the development approvals identified below will
allow the County a reasonable and limited period to study the identified conditions and to consider
and, if appropriate, adopt amendments to its development regulations addressing large-scale data
center development; and

       WHEREAS, during the moratorium, County staff will undertake the actions identified in
Section 5 according to the schedule stated therein; and

       WHEREAS, the Board has conducted a legislative hearing after notice given in accordance
with N.C. Gen. Stats. §§ 160D-107 and 160D-601.

      NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Board of Commissioners of Alamance
County, North Carolina, that:

Section 1. Purpose and findings.

The foregoing recitals are incorporated as findings of fact. The Board finds that this temporary
moratorium is reasonably necessary to address the conditions stated above; that its duration is no
longer than reasonably necessary to address those conditions; and that it is adopted for the purposes
stated herein and not for the purpose of regulating residential uses.

Section 2. Definitions.

For purposes of this Ordinance, “Data Center” means a facility or group of facilities primarily used
to house, operate, or maintain computer servers, data-storage equipment, networking equipment,
or other information-technology equipment for the processing, storage, transmission, or
distribution of digital data. The term includes associated buildings, cooling systems, backup-
generation equipment, electrical substations or switchyards located on the same development site,
and other appurtenant facilities.

“Large-Scale Data Center” means a Data Center that is designed, proposed, or reasonably
anticipated to require aggregate maximum electric demand of ten (10) megawatts or more,
measured at the point or points of electrical service or interconnection.

Aggregate maximum electric demand includes the demand of all buildings, equipment, cooling
systems, backup and supporting facilities, and expansions located on the same or contiguous parcel
or parcels under common ownership, common control, or a common development plan, whether
proposed for construction in one phase or multiple phases.

“Data Center” does not include:

   1. Information-technology equipment that is accessory to the principal use of a residential,
      office, educational, medical, governmental, retail, manufacturing, telecommunications, or
      similar facility; or
   2. A telecommunications facility whose principal purpose is the transmission of voice, video,
      or data communications, rather than the operation of a data-processing or data-storage
      facility.

Section 3. Temporary moratorium imposed.

Beginning on the effective date of this Ordinance and continuing through 11:59 p.m. on February
17, 2027, the County shall not accept, process, approve, conditionally approve, issue, or otherwise
grant a development approval for a new Large-Scale Data Center or for the expansion of an
existing Large-Scale Data Center within the County’s development jurisdiction.

The development approvals subject to this moratorium include, to the extent applicable to a Large-
Scale Data Center, text amendments initiated by or on behalf of an applicant, special use permits,

conditional siting approvals, site-plan approvals, building permits, subdivision approvals, and any
other County development approval required by law.

The moratorium will address the conditions identified in Section 1 by preventing approval of
Large-Scale Data Center development under regulations the Board finds may be inadequate to
address the use’s potential land-use, infrastructure, utility, environmental, and public-safety
impacts while the County completes the review and regulatory work described in Section 5.

Section 4. Statutory exemptions and scope.

Except in the event of an imminent threat to public health or safety, this Ordinance shall not apply
to any existing facility, nor to any facility exempted by N.C. Gen. Stat. § 160D-107(c), including:

   1. A project for which a valid building permit is outstanding;
   2. A project for which a special use permit application was accepted as complete before the
      call for the hearing on this Ordinance;
   3. Development set forth in a site-specific vesting plan approved under N.C. Gen. Stat. §
      160D-108.1;
   4. Development for which substantial expenditures have been made in good-faith reliance on
      a prior valid development approval; or
   5. A preliminary or final subdivision plat accepted for review before the call for the hearing
      on this Ordinance. A preliminary plat accepted for review before the call for the hearing,
      if subsequently approved, may proceed to final plat approval without being subject to this
      Ordinance.
   6. If a complete application for a development approval was submitted before the effective
      date of this Ordinance, N.C. Gen. Stat. § 160D-108(b) shall apply when processing of that
      application resumes following expiration or termination of this Ordinance.

Nothing in this Ordinance prohibits the County from conducting ministerial work necessary to
determine whether a proposed project is subject to an exemption in this Section.

Section 5. Work program.

During the moratorium period, the County shall undertake the following work:

   1. Within sixty (60) days after the effective date, Planning Staff shall prepare an inventory of
      existing County regulations and identify the development approvals, infrastructure
      considerations, and regulatory gaps implicated by Large-Scale Data Center development.
   2. Within ninety (90) days after the effective date, Planning Staff shall consult with affected
      County departments, municipalities, utility providers, emergency-service providers, and
      other appropriate agencies regarding electrical demand, electrical-service and transmission
      needs, water and wastewater service, road access, emergency response, backup generation,
      noise, stormwater, and other relevant impacts.
   3. Within one hundred twenty (120) days after the effective date, Planning Staff shall present
      proposed regulatory options to the Planning Board and Board of Commissioners. The
      options may include a use definition; locational standards; dimensional, buffering,

      screening, lighting, noise, and design standards; utility and infrastructure information
      requirements; emergency-operations and backup-generation standards; and appropriate
      approval procedures.
   4. Before expiration of the moratorium, the County shall consider whether to adopt any
      recommended amendments to its development regulations.

Section 6. Duration and extension.

This Ordinance shall expire automatically at 11:59 p.m. on February 17, 2027. The Board finds
that the six-month duration is reasonably necessary to allow County staff to complete the
regulatory inventory; obtain and evaluate information from affected County departments, utility
providers, municipalities, emergency-service providers, and other agencies; prepare regulatory
options; obtain Planning Board review and recommendation; and present proposed amendments
for Board consideration through the applicable notice, hearing, and adoption procedures. The
Board further finds that a shorter period would not reasonably permit completion of these
sequenced tasks, while a longer initial period is unnecessary in light of the work program and
schedule set out in Section 5.

Section 7. Severability.

If any provision of this Ordinance or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid,
the invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect without the
invalid provision or application.

Section 8. Effective date.

This Ordinance is effective upon adoption.

Adopted this 17th day of August, 2026.
