complete band, if possible, on the construction of data centers in Pasquotank County. She said not

only are these centers bad for the communities they reside in, but they are actively contributing to

resource depletion. According to the Environmental and Energy Study Institute, large data centers

bills. She said data centers in Pasquotank County will have financial, environmental, and physical

impacts on the community. She asked the Board to take this into consideration and prevent our

lovely community from being another rural area destroyed.

ORDINANCE OF THE PASQUOTANK COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS INSTITUTING A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE PERMITTING OF DATA CENTERS, DATA PROCESSING FACILITIES, CRYPTOCURRENCY MINING OPERATIONS, AND ANY USE ASSOCIATED WITH DATA PROCESSING FACILITIES IN PASQUOTANK COUNTY, NORTH CAROLINA PURSUANT TO N.C.G.S. §160D-107


WHEREAS, pursuant to N.C. General Statute §160D-107, Local Governments may adopt an


ordinance authorizing a temporary moratorium on any development approval required by law;

and

WHEREAS, the Pasquotank County Zoning Ordinance does not define cryptocurrency mining,


data processing, and other uses associated with data centers as specific uses; and

WHEREAS, the Pasquotank County Board of Commissioners at its May 18, 2026 regular


meeting held a legislative hearing regarding this moratorium in accordance with N.C. General

Statute §160D-601.

WHEREAS, the Pasquotank County Board of Commissioners believe such a moratorium will


protect the public interest and welfare of the residents of Pasquotank County until such

regulations regarding the aforementioned uses are adopted.

BE IT ORDAINED, by the Pasquotank County Board of Commissioners as follows:


Section 1: A temporary moratorium is hereby imposed commencing on May 18, 2026, and

expiring no later than May 17, 2027, or upon approval of Zoning Ordinance amendments

regarding data centers, data processing facilities, cryptocurrency mining operations, and uses

associated with data processing facilities not yet defined in the Pasquotank County Zoning

Ordinance. This moratorium specifically includes all land uses that involve cryptocurrency

mining in the unincorporated areas of Pasquotank County.

For the purposes of this moratorium, a data center, data processing facility, or cryptocurrency

mining operation is a building, a dedicated space within a building, or group of buildings

housing computer systems and associated components, such as telecommunication and data

processing systems, to be used for remote storage, processing, or distribution of large amounts of

data. Examples of such data include, but are not limited to, computationally intensive

applications such as cryptocurrency mining, artificial intelligence (A.I.) computing, weather

modeling, genome sequencing, application hosting, cloud storage, video and technical streaming

services, etc. Such facilities may include air handlers, power generators, water cooling and

storage facilities, utility substations, and other infrastructure to support operations.

This moratorium shall not apply to data processing equipment or server rooms that are clearly

incidental and subordinate to a permitted principal use and are intended solely to support the on

site operations of such principal use. Such principal use may include, but is not limited to,

medical facilities, financial institutions, offices, educational institutions, or similar uses, as long

as such data processing activities are not offered as a primary service to off-f-site users.

Section 2: In compliance with the requirements of N.C. Gen. Stat. §160D-107, Pasquotank

County makes the following statements:

(1) Data centers, data processing facilities, cryptocurrency mining operations, and any other uses

associated with data processing facilities require considerable amounts of electricity and water

usage, which can result in greenhouse gas emissions, as well as additional pollution, e-waste,

noise, and other local impacts to residents and communities living near the facilities. The

Pasquotank County Planning Department is in the process of developing zoning standards and

mitigation methods to address these intensive land use types that may cause detrimental harm to

the natural environment and the quality of life of County residents. The County seeks time to

develop such standards. The County has looked at alternative solutions to a moratorium but

found none. The County has determined that to simply allow this intensive land use to be

permitted without regard to location, height, size, density, population, industry, residence or other

purposes would be counter to the stated goals of the Pasquotank County 2023 Land Use Plan.

(2) The Table of Permitted uses in the Zoning Ordinance currently includes “Computer Services

and Data Centers” as a permitted use in the C-1, Commercial, I-1, Industrial, and O&I, Office &

Institutional zoning districts. However, the use is not defined and there are no supplemental

performance standards. The County seeks to update the definition section of the Zoning

Ordinance to align with current industry practices, and to specifically define “cryptocurrency

mining” as a specific land use, separate and apart from data processing facilities or data centers,

and to research other associated uses which would more align with the intensity of

“cryptocurrency mining.” Therefore, the County seeks to place a moratorium on the use of

property within the unincorporated jurisdiction of Pasquotank County and outside any municipal

extraterritorial jurisdictions for data centers, data processing facilities, cryptocurrency mining

operations, and any uses associated with data processing facilities for a period of (12) months or

until such time that specific land use standards for these uses can be developed. All development

approvals for data centers, data processing facilities, cryptocurrency mining operations, and any

uses associated with data processing facilities are subject to the moratorium.

(3) The moratorium shall begin on May 18, 2026, and shall last until May 17, 2027, or upon a

Zoning Ordinance amendment addressing the land uses of data centers, data processing facilities,

cryptocurrency mining, and other uses associated with data processing facilities, whichever

comes first. This twelve (12) month moratorium is necessary to allow Pasquotank County

Planning Staff sufficient time to study these uses and their impacts, to consider zoning standards

and mitigation methods, and to prepare an amendment to the Ordinance to address data centers,

data processing facilities, cryptocurrency mining, and other uses associated with data processing

facilities prior to the expiration of the moratorium.

(4) Pasquotank County Planning Staff will study the impacts of data centers, data processing

facilities, cryptocurrency mining, and any other uses associated with data processing facilities on

communities. Planning Staff will also investigate how other communities in North Carolina and

across the United States have addressed these impacts through zoning regulations. Planning Staff

will develop the land use regulations required to mitigate the negative impacts associated with

land uses described as data centers, data processing facilities, cryptocurrency mining, and any

other uses associated with data processing facilities. Subsequently, ordinance amendments will

be proposed which will then follow the legislative process for adoption. Public input will be

sought throughout the process. The research, policy development, and legislative processes

necessitate an adequate moratorium time limit of twelve (12) months.

Section 3: If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase of this ordinance is, for any

reason, held to be invalid, such decision shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions of

this ordinance. The Board of Commissioners hereby declares that it would have passed this

ordinance, and each section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase thereof irrespective of the

fact that anyone or more sections, subsections, sentences, clauses, or phrases be declared invalid.

Section 4: All ordinances or parts of ordinances in conflict herewith are hereby repealed to the

extent of such conflict.

Section 5: This Ordinance shall be in full force and effect beginning on May 18, 2026.

Adopted, this the 18th day of May, 2026.

WHEREAS: In 2006, the International Network for the Prevention of Elder Abuse, in support

of the United Nations International Plan of Action, proclaimed a day to recognize

the significance of elder abuse as a public health and human rights issue; and

WHEREAS: 2026 marks the 20th Annual World Elder Abuse Awareness Day. Its recognition

will promote a better understanding of abuse and neglect of older adults; and

WHEREAS: The National Center on Elder Abuse (NCEA), Albemarle Commission Area

Agency on Aging and Pasquotank County recognize the importance of taking

action to raise awareness, prevent and address elder abuse; and

WHEREAS: As our population lives longer, we are presented with an opportunity to think

about our collective needs and future as a nation; and

WHEREAS: Ageism and social isolation are major causes of elder abuse in the United States;

and

WHEREAS: Recognizing that it is up to all of us, to ensure that proper social structures exist

so people can retain community and societal connections, reducing the likelihood

of abuse; and

WHEREAS: Preventing abuse of older adults through maintaining and improving social

supports like senior centers, human services and transportation will allow

everyone to continue to live as independently as possible and contribute to the life

and vibrancy of our communities; and

WHEREAS: Where there is justice there can be no abuse; therefore, NCEA urges

all people to restore justice by honoring older adults.

WHEREAS: Join us in our engaging and empowering movement and putting an end to abuse.

THEREFORE; The County of Pasquotank hereby proclaims June 15, 2026, as World Elder

Abuse Awareness Day in Pasquotank County and encourage all of our communities to recognize

and celebrate older adults and their ongoing contributions to the success and vitality of our

country.

Dated on this day of May 18, 2026

e. Adoption of Proclamation for Older Americans Month 2026

Every May, the Administration for Community Living (ACL) leads the celebration of Older

American’s Month (OAM). The Albemarle Commission Area Agency on Aging is requesting

adoption of the following proclamation, proclaiming May 2026 as Older Americans Month in

Pasquotank County:

Older Americans Month 2026


A PROCLAMATION


WHEREAS, May is Older Americans Month, a time for us to recognize and honor Pasquotank

County’s older adults and their immense influence on every facet of American society; and

WHEREAS, our communities benefit when people of all ages, abilities, and backgrounds can

participate fully with the highest level of independence; and

WHEREAS, older Americans improve our communities through intergenerational relationships,

community service, civic engagement, and many other activities; and

WHEREAS, Pasquotank County must ensure that as we age, we have the resources and support

needed to stay involved in our communities, reflecting our commitment to inclusivity and

connectedness; and

WHEREAS, Pasquotank County recognizes the valuable role of caregivers and the need to

promote programs and services that support and foster their well-being;

Now, therefore, Lloyd Griffin, Chairman of the Pasquotank County Board of Commissioners do


hereby proclaim May 2026 as Older Americans Month. This year's theme, “Champion Your

Health,” focuses on prevention, wellness, and personal responsibility as cornerstones of healthy

aging. It encourages taking an active role in managing your own health, advocating for yourself,

accessing preventive care, and making informed decisions that support independence.

I call upon all residents to join me in recognizing the contributions of our older citizens and

promoting programs and activities that foster connection, inclusion, and support for older adults.

f. Approval of Reclassifying Planning Position

The County’s Planning Technician/Code Enforcement Officer position has been vacant since last

June. Although the position was previously advertised and several interviews were conducted, staff

did not think any of the potential candidates were a good fit fofor the job duties and responsibilities

that were required. The department then began to focus on hiring for the Building Inspector

position that was also vacant with the hope that some of the code enforcement duties could be

directed to that position. However, this has not worked out due to the volume of inspections that

the department has been experiencing.

Planning and Inspections Director Shelley Cox has requested that the position be reclassified to a

Planner/Code Enforcement Officer position, with the intention that the department can attract an

applicant who has a Planning degree, or a degree in a closely related field. Currently, when Shelley

is out of the office or away from her desk, there is no one that can answer planning related

questions. This often presents an inconvenience to our customers who need or expect an answer

immediately. The position will also assist in developing internal talent to potentially fill the

Director position when Shelley retires within the next five years.

The current position is currently Pay Grade 21 ($48,628 to $68,556) and the Finance Committee

recommends the reclassified position be set at Grade 24 ($53,343 to $75,357).

Chairman Griffin called on Kim Hamby, Engineer with Timmons Group to present and answer

any questions regarding the sketch plan. Ms. Hamby summarized the sketch plan. She noted that

the subdivision will be accessed by a road that was basically dedicated for access to this property

because of the bypass cutting off all access to this property. She explained that it is currently

improved as a 16-18’ wide gravel road, which is somewhat irregular. She said the plan is to pave

it, widen it, and make sure it has adequate shoulders. She believes the most narrow point is 50’,

but it does have variable widths that run parallel to the Highway 17 right of way.

Along with purchasing this property, they also purchased a single lot that is unimproved in the

adjacent subdivision. It has frontage on Shellie Drive. They are proposing to bring the water in

off of Shellie Drive though an easement. Ideally, they thought about installing an access directly

off of Shellie Drive for a road but didn’t think that the residents of the neighborhood would

appreciate that. She said she has been thinking about the fact that when they bring the water line

in, they have room to come across the back of lot 21 and provide an emergency fire access. They

will propose putting a gate at the entrance to prevent unauthorized use. It can be provided with a

lock that the fire department could open in the event the existing road were blocked or destroyed.

Ms. Hamby said from a stormwater standpoint, the property is one of the better ones she has

worked with in the fact that it has about 2’ of fall from the woods at the north end to the tip at the

southern point where they would be discharging the stormwater pond into the large DOT ditch.

For purposes of doing the drainage analysis, they have surveyed the ditch and gone all the way

down to the interchange where there are major pipes that go under the interchange out to the Little

River. She noted that there is currently a ditch along the backs of the lots on Shellie Drive, but

they will be draining all of their lots forward, utilizing swells so they will not be contributing to

that drainage. She noted that there is also an intermediate pond that the developer wanted to

provide for aesthetic value, which will also h help with regard t to meeting the stormwater ordinance.

Ms. Hamby said they had a flow test performed about 10 months ago when the developer was

doing his due diligence. They have already constructed a water cad model to show that they can

provide the required domestic and fire protection.

didn’t want to fight that battle and have all the other residents angry with them.

Chairman Griffin asked what the diameter is of the cul-de-sacs. Ms. Hamby answered, it should

Commissioner Overman said it is not typical for the Board to respond to public comments, but he

asked Ms. Cox if she would stay because this is a question w we get asked all the time because some

counties around do allow people to set up their RVs while they are building their house. He asked

Ms. Cox to speak to the reason why the County holds to that building code. Ms. Cox said the

Zoning Ordinance specifically says that we cannot reside in a camping trailer either permanently

or temporarily. There are several reasons, but mainly because they don’t meet building code. The

North Carolina State Building Code specifically says under their definition of dwelling unit that

RV’s, campers, and work trailers are not permitted onsite while homes are built and shall not be

permitted for use as a dwelling unit as defined in the ordinance. That is the reason why our Zoning

Ordinance says this and that is the reason why they are telling the public that they cannot live in

an RV or camping trailer either t temporarily or permanently. She said she understands Mr. Pureza’s

dilemma and the situation he is in, but it would not be a good idea to go against the North Carolina

State Building Code.

Commissioner Overman said in the western part of the state, some counties allow it on a local

basis, as long as they have an active building permit. If the building permit expires and they are

still living in the RV, who is going to kick them out. He noted that it is happening locally as well.

He said if a hurricane comes, it could be a very bad day. Ms. Cox said as far as a variance goes;

this Board would not issue a zoning variance. That is something the Board of Adjustment does,

but “use” variances are illegal in North Carolina. Therefore, the Board of Adjustments could not

legally issue a variance from that provision. The only alternative to get that changed would be to

take it through the text amendment process and get the ordinance amended. She would not

recommend amending the ordinance, because it is in the building code that we should not be doing

that.

Commissioner Meads noted that he was just in the Inspections Office in Perquimans County and

they have a big sign that specifically spells that out. Commissioner Davis said there are some

places in the state that advertise, buy your lot in the mountains and you can camp here until you

build your house. How they do that, he does not know. Ms. Cox said there are some coastal

counties that have an allowance in emergency situations where they will allow it temporarily.
