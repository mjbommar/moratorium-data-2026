          BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF LYNN HAVEN,
FLORIDA:
          SECTION 1. Article 1 of Chapter 42 of the City’s Code of Ordinances related to Parks
and Recreation shall be amended as follows (strikethrough text is deleted; underlined text is
added):
Chapter 42 - PARKS AND RECREATION

ARTICLE I. - IN GENERAL

Sec. 42-1. - Certain operations prohibited on Davis Lake.
It shall be unlawful for any person to place or operate in or upon those waters known as Davis
Lake, such lake lying south of 9th Street, west of Iowa Avenue, and east of Pennsylvania Avenue,
and such lake being within the corporate limits of the city, any motorboat, outboard motor, or any
other boat, vessel or watercraft propelled by any internal combustion engine or otherwise, except
as permitted by this section. It shall be lawful for any person to place or operate in or upon the
waters any boat propelled by any electric trolling motor.

Sec. 42-2. - Fees.
(a) The city may charge a reasonable fee for the use of park and recreational facilities, including
the city library. Such fees shall be set from time to time by resolution of the city commission and
published in Appendix A of this Code.
(b) Reserved and scheduled use of any city park or recreational facility must be accompanied by
a facility use agreement approved by the department of leisure services.

Sec. 42-3. - Vehicles and parking facilities.
(a) No person shall park any vehicle, camper, trailer or any towed conveyance in any area not
specifically authorized or designated.
(b) There shall be no overnight parking of any vehicle, camper, trailer, or any towed conveyance
in any area unless otherwise posted.

                                                                                        Page 1 of 6


                                                                                               Page 23 of 109


(c) No motorized vehicle shall be allowed on any portion of the recreational facility other than
designated roads or parking.
(d) No person shall park a single vehicle in more than one single marked space.

Sec. 42-4. - Camping facilities.
It shall be unlawful to camp in any city park or recreational facility unless otherwise authorized
and posted.

Sec. 42-5. - Alcoholic beverages.
It shall be unlawful to consume, sell, or cause to be sold, any beer, wine, liquor, or alcoholic
beverages of any kind, in any city park or recreational facility.

Sec. 42-6. - Sanitation.
(a) Failure to dispose of all garbage and refuse, including papers, cans, bottles, waste materials,
and rubbish, in designated containers or failure to remove such garbage and refuse from the site
or area constitutes a violation of this chapter.
(b) No person or company shall use a city park or recreational facility refuse container for dumping
of household or commercial garbage, refuse, or trash brought from private property.

Sec. 42-7. - Use of an e-cigarette where smoking is prohibited.
       (a) It shall be unlawful to Smoke or Vape in any City Parks except as follows:
           i.       Smoking unfiltered cigars.
           ii.      Smoking or Vaping in any smoking area clearly marked and designated by the
                    City Manager.
           iii.     Smoking or Vaping by a person in a parked personal vehicle on City Parks
                    property.
           iv.      When permitted by the City Manager as part of a Special Event.
       (b) The use of an e-cigarette is prohibited at all locations on city-owned property at
           which smoking is prohibited under F.S. ch. 386.
       (c) For purposes of this section, the following words shall be defined as follows:
           i.       City Parks shall mean and include Porter Park, Sheffield Park, Kinsaul Park
                    (including bark park), James Rogers Park, 17th St. Pocket Park, Cain Griffin

Section 1 — Introduction, Purpose, and Authority
1.1 Purpose
This Cybersecurity Policy Manual ("Policy") establishes the City of Lynn Haven's baseline requirements for
protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the City's data, information technology, and
information technology resources. It is intended to give every employee, elected official, contractor, and
vendor with access to City systems a clear, consistent set of expectations, and to give the City Manager,
department directors, and the City's IT function a defensible framework for administering and enforcing
those expectations.

1.2 Statutory and Framework Authority
This Policy is adopted to satisfy the City's obligations under Section 282.3185, Florida Statutes (the "Local
Government Cybersecurity Act"), which requires every Florida county and municipality — regardless of
size — to adopt cybersecurity standards that safeguard data and IT resources, consistent with generally
accepted best practices including the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity
Framework. Lynn Haven, as a municipality with fewer than 25,000 residents, fell under the January 1,
2025, compliance deadline established for smaller jurisdictions.

This Policy also reflects:

    ●   Section 282.318, Florida Statutes (the state-level Florida Cybersecurity Act), whose training and
        incident-severity framework Section 282.3185 incorporates by reference;
    ●   Section 282.3186, Florida Statutes, which prohibits the city from paying or otherwise complying
        with a ransomware demand;
    ●   The NIST Cybersecurity Framework's five core functions — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and
        Recover — which organize the individual policies in Sections 3 through 14 of this manual;
    ●   Chapter 119, Florida Statutes (Public Records Act), which continues to govern the retention and
        disclosure of City records regardless of the medium in which they are stored, and which must be
        considered alongside any data-protection or incident-response action taken under this Policy.

1.3 Scope
This Policy applies to all City of Lynn Haven elected officials, employees, contractors, consultants, interns,
volunteers, and temporary workers (collectively, "Users") who access, use, or manage City data, networks,
applications, or computing devices, whether City-owned, leased, or personally owned. It applies to all City
departments, including Finance, Human Resources, Planning and Community Development, Public Works,
Utilities, Parks and Recreation, Police, and Fire, and to any third party that connects to City systems or
handles City data on the City's behalf.

1.4 Governance Structure

Section 3 — Acceptable Use Policy
Purpose
To define acceptable and prohibited use of city computing equipment, networks, and electronic resources
so that city systems, data, and reputation are protected, and so employees understand the standard of
conduct expected of them.

Scope
Applies to all users of city-owned, leased, or personally owned devices used to conduct city business or
connect to city networks or business systems.

Policy Statements
    ●     City IT resources are provided for the conduct of city business. Incidental personal use is permitted
          only where it does not interfere with job duties, consume significant resources, or create legal,
          security, or reputational risk to the city.
    ●     Users must lock or log off any device before leaving it unattended, and must not share login
          credentials or allow another person to use their account.
    ●     Users may not install unauthorized software, disable security controls (antivirus, firewall, screen-
          lock policies), or connect unapproved devices or removable media to city networks without IT
          authorization.
    ●     Users may not use city systems to engage in any activity that is illegal under local, state, or federal
          law, including accessing, storing, or transmitting obscene, harassing, or discriminatory material.
    ●     Users may not use city email or systems to conduct outside business, political campaigning
          inconsistent with applicable law, or unauthorized solicitation.

    ●   Department directors and the IT function may exempt specific staff from individual restrictions
        where required for legitimate job duties (e.g., systems administrators disabling a compromised
        host's network access).
    ●   Violations are handled as any other allegation of employee misconduct, up to and including
        termination, and may be referred for criminal prosecution where applicable law is implicated.


Section 4 — Access Control (Minimum Necessary Access) Policy
Purpose
To ensure users are granted only the access required to perform their job duties (the principle of least
privilege / minimum necessary access), reducing the city's exposure if any single account is compromised.

Policy Statements
    ●   Access to city systems and data is granted based on documented job function and approved by
        the user's department director and the IT function before an account is created.
    ●   Administrative or elevated privileges are limited to IT staff and other specifically authorized
        personnel and are reviewed at least annually.
    ●   Access is reviewed and adjusted immediately upon a change in job role, and disabled immediately
        upon separation, following a standard offboarding checklist coordinated between Human
        Resources and the IT function.
    ●   Access to highly sensitive systems (e.g., financial/utility billing systems, police records
        management, HR/payroll, SCADA/utility control systems) requires additional documented
        approval and is logged.
    ●   System-use notices (login banners) are displayed on city systems where feasible, advising users
        that the system is for authorized city business use, that use may be monitored, and that
        unauthorized access is prohibited.
    ●   Remote access to city networks is permitted only through IT-approved, encrypted methods (e.g.,
        VPN or equivalent) and is subject to the same authentication requirements as on-site access.


Section 5 — Identification, Authentication, and Password Policy
Purpose
To ensure that every user accessing city systems is uniquely identified and authenticated, and that
credentials are strong enough to resist common attack methods.

Policy Statements
    ●   Every user is assigned a unique account; shared or generic logins are prohibited except for limited,
        IT-approved service accounts with documented compensating controls.
    ●   Passwords must meet a minimum length of 12 characters (or the longest practicable length
        supported by a given system) and must not be reused across the user's last 10 passwords.

   ●   Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for all remote access, email, and any system storing
       highly sensitive data, and is strongly encouraged for all other city accounts as systems are
       upgraded to support it.
   ●   Default and vendor-supplied passwords must be changed prior to a system being placed into

   ●   Accounts are locked after a limited number of failed login attempts and require IT or help-desk

   ●   Passwords must never be written down in an unsecured location, shared verbally or in writing

   ●   Where a city system supports passphrases or password managers, users are encouraged to use an

Section 6 — Mobile Device and Remote Access Policy
Purpose
To extend the city's security requirements to laptops, smartphones, tablets, and remote-work
connections, which present a higher risk of loss, theft, or unmonitored access.

Policy Statements
   ●   City-owned mobile devices must use full-disk or device-level encryption, a screen-lock timeout of
       no more than 10 minutes, and IT-managed security updates.
   ●   Personally owned devices used to access city email or data (a "bring your own device"
       arrangement) must be enrolled in the city's mobile device management solution, where one is in
       use, and are subject to remote wipe of city data if lost, stolen, or upon separation.
   ●   Lost or stolen devices that contain or can access city data must be reported to the IT function and
       the employee's supervisor immediately, and no later than within the same business day of
       discovery.
   ●   Use of public or prohibited Wi-Fi to access city systems is discouraged and, where unavoidable,
       requires use of the city's approved VPN.
   ●   Utility SCADA/control-system access from mobile or remote connections is prohibited unless
       specifically authorized in writing by the Utilities Director and the IT function, given the elevated
       risk to critical infrastructure.




Section 7 — Email, Internet Use, and Phishing Defense Policy
Purpose

To reduce the city's exposure to phishing, business email compromise, and malicious web content, which
are consistently the leading causes of ransomware and financial-fraud incidents affecting Florida local

Policy Statements
    ●   Users must not open attachments or click links from unknown or unexpected senders and must
        report suspected phishing emails to the IT function using the city's designated reporting method
        before taking any other action.
    ●   Any request to change payment or banking instructions for a vendor, contractor, or employee —
        however received — must be independently verified by phone using a previously known, verified

    ●   Web filtering is applied to city networks to block known malicious, high-risk, and inappropriate

    ● All sensitive (tier 3) information sent via email shall be encrypted.

Section 8 — Data Classification and Protection Policy
Purpose
To ensure City data is handled in a manner appropriate to its sensitivity, and that Users can readily identify
which data requires heightened protection.

Policy Statements
    ●   City data is classified into three tiers: (1) Public — records generally subject to disclosure under
        Chapter 119, F.S.; (2) Internal — non-public operational data not otherwise restricted by law; and
        (3) Highly Sensitive — data protected by law or posing a heightened risk if disclosed, including
        personnel and HR records containing personal identifying information, law enforcement records
        restricted under Chapter 119, financial account and payment-card data, utility customer account
        data, and critical infrastructure/SCADA configuration data.
    ●   Highly Sensitive data must be encrypted at rest and in transit where technically feasible, and
        access must be logged and limited to users with a documented business need.
    ●   Highly Sensitive data must not be stored on personal devices, personal cloud storage, or
        removable media without specific IT authorization and encryption.
    ●   Department directors are responsible for identifying which data sets within their department fall
        into the Highly Sensitive tier and communicating that to the IT function.

Section 9 — Third-Party and Vendor Risk Management Policy
Purpose
To ensure that vendors, contractors, and other third parties who connect to city systems or process city
data on the city's behalf meet the same baseline security expectations as city staff.

Policy Statements
    ●   The IT function maintains an inventory of third-party vendors with access to city networks,
        systems, or data, including the type of access granted and its business justification.
    ●   Contracts involving access to city data or systems must include, where practicable, provisions
        addressing data protection, incident notification obligations, and the vendor's responsibility to
        cooperate with the city's incident response and any statutorily required reporting.
    ●   Vendor remote access accounts are subject to the same access-control, authentication, and
        logging requirements as employee accounts, and are disabled immediately when no longer
        needed.
    ●   Before a new cloud service, software-as-a-service platform, or IT vendor is engaged, the
        requesting department must coordinate with the IT function to confirm the vendor meets
        minimum security expectations (encryption, breach-notification terms, and — for financial or
        utility systems — relevant industry standards).


Section 10 — Change and Patch Management Policy
Purpose
To ensure that software and systems are kept current with security patches and that changes to
production systems are made in a controlled, documented manner.

Policy Statements
    ●   Critical and high-severity security patches are applied to city systems as soon as practicable, and in
        general no later than 30 days after release, unless testing identifies a conflict requiring a delayed
        or compensating approach.
    ●   Systems that can no longer receive security updates from the manufacturer (end-of-life systems)
        are identified by the IT function and prioritized for replacement or isolated with compensating
        controls.
    ●   Changes to production systems, network configuration, or firewall rules are documented and,
        where feasible, tested in a non-production environment before deployment.

    ●   The IT function conducts or arranges periodic vulnerability scanning of city networks and systems,
        consistent with the vulnerability testing expectations of Subsection 282.3185, F.S., and tracks
        remediation of identified findings.


Section 11 — Backup and Disaster Recovery Policy
Purpose
To ensure the city can restore critical data and systems following a ransomware attack, hardware failure,
natural disaster, or other disruptive events — particularly given Lynn Haven's exposure to hurricanes and
severe weather.

Policy Statements
    ●   Critical city systems and data (financial systems, utility billing, permitting, police and fire records,
        email) are backed up on a documented schedule appropriate to their criticality, with at least one
        backup copy stored offline or otherwise isolated from the production network so it cannot be
        encrypted in a ransomware event.
    ●   Backup restoration is tested at least annually to confirm backups are viable, and that recovery
        time objectives are realistic.
    ●   The city maintains a basic disaster recovery/continuity of operations plan identifying which
        systems must be restored first, who is responsible for restoration, and how city services will
        continue (including manual workarounds) while systems are unavailable.
    ●   Given the statutory prohibition on paying ransomware demands (Subsection 282.3186, F.S.),
        reliable, tested backups are the city's primary means of recovering from a ransomware event and
        must be treated as a priority, not an afterthought.


Section 12 — Incident Response and Statutory Reporting Policy
Purpose
To ensure the city detects, contains, and responds to cybersecurity incidents quickly, and meets its
mandatory reporting obligations under Florida law.

Policy Statements
    ●   Any user who suspects a cybersecurity incident — including a phishing click, malware alert, lost
        device, unauthorized access, or suspicious system behavior — must report it immediately to the IT
        function and their supervisor. Users must not attempt to independently investigate, "clean up," or
        power down a potentially compromised system without IT guidance, as doing so can destroy
        evidence needed for response and reporting.
    ●   The IT function, in coordination with the city manager, determines the severity level of a
        confirmed incident consistent with the severity framework referenced in Subsection
        282.318(3)(c), F.S., (ranging from Level 1, minimal impact, to Level 5, severe impact affecting
        public health or safety).

Section 13 — Cybersecurity Training Policy
Purpose
To satisfy Subsection 282.3185, F.S.'s mandatory training requirements and to build a workforce that can
recognize and avoid common attack methods.

Policy Statements
   ●   Every city employee with access to the city's network must complete basic cybersecurity
       awareness training within 30 days of hire (or of being granted network access) and annually
       thereafter.
   ●   IT staff, technology professionals, and any employee with access to Highly Sensitive information
       (as defined in Section 8) must additionally complete advanced cybersecurity training within 30
       days of hire and annually thereafter, consistent with the curriculum referenced in Subsection
       282.318(3)(g), F.S.
   ●   Members of the Cybersecurity Incident Response Team (CSIRT) complete incident-specific training
       addressing their role in a response.
   ●   Training completion is tracked by Human Resources in coordination with the IT function, and
       department directors are responsible for ensuring their staff complete required training on
       schedule.
   ●   The City will draw on training resources made available by the Florida Digital Service, the Florida
       Center for Cybersecurity at the University of South Florida (Cyber Florida), and the Florida League
       of Cities to meet this requirement cost-effectively.

Purpose
To protect servers, network equipment, and other IT infrastructure from unauthorized physical access,

Section 15 — Enforcement, Exceptions, and Policy Review
15.1 Enforcement
Violations of this Policy are treated as any other allegation of employee misconduct and are addressed
through the city's standard disciplinary process, up to and including termination. Contractors and vendors
who violate this Policy are subject to remedies available under their contract, including termination of
access or termination of the contract. Conduct that violates state or federal law, including Chapter 815,
Florida Statutes (Computer-Related Crimes), may be referred to law enforcement.

15.2 Exceptions
Any request for an exception to this Policy must be submitted in writing to the city manager, describing
the business need and any compensating controls, and is documented and reviewed at least annually.

15.3 Policy Review
This Policy is reviewed at least annually by the City Manager's Office and the IT function, and following any
Severity Level 3, 4, or 5 incident, to incorporate lessons learned, changes in the NIST Cybersecurity
Framework, and changes in Florida law. Material revisions are presented to the City Commission for
adoption by resolution.

Appendix A — Definitions
Cybersecurity Incident: An event that violates, or imminently threatens to violate, this Policy or standard
computer-security practices, consistent with NIST SP 800-61.
Highly Sensitive Data: City data protected by law or presenting heightened risk if disclosed, as described in
Section 8 of this Policy.
Least Privilege / Minimum Necessary Access: The principle that a user is granted only the access required
to perform their job duties, and no more.
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Authentication requiring two or more independent credentials (e.g., a
password plus a one-time code).
Ransomware Incident: A cybersecurity incident involving malicious software that restricts access to city
systems or data, typically accompanied by a demand for payment.
Severity Level: The impact classification (Level 1 through 5) assigned to an incident consistent with F.S.
282.318(3)(c), used to determine reporting obligations.
User: Any elected official, employee, contractor, consultant, intern, volunteer, or temporary worker who
accesses city data, networks, or systems.

FINAL ORDER

The City Commission of the City of Lynn Haven (the “Commission’”), having heard

testimony at a quasi-judicial hearing on August 26, 2025, and having reviewed the exhibits
presented, hereby makes the following Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law:

Findings of Fact

On April 9, 2025, (the “Applicant”) submitted an application for a certificate of
concurrency and development order of property located at Aberdeen Loop, Parcel
#11528-551-000.

On August 5, 2025, the Planning Commission of the City of Lynn Haven (the
“Planning Commission”) considered and recommended approval of the Application.

On August 26, 2025, the City Commission considered the Planning Commission
recommendation, the staff report, and testimony from the Applicant.

This matter was properly noticed, the witnesses were sworn, and the City
Commission disclosed any ex parte communications. The City was represented by
Vicki Harrison, Planning Specialist, and the Applicant was represented by Robert
Carroll, P.E., McNeil-Carroll Engineering. Affected parties were present, and all
parties offered testimony.

Approval of this project is conditioned upon completion of the obligations set forth in
the Development Order, and payment to the City of all applicable impact fees, as
provided in Chapter 86, Article V of the Code, including, but not limited to, the
stormwater system impact fees. All other applicable fees will be assessed during the
building permit process.

Approval of this project is conditioned upon completion of the obligations set forth in
the Development Order.
Conclusions of Law

I. Report in Brief:

This Change Order for Jim House and Associates, in the amount of $ 151,275.00 is to replace
additional equipment that cannot be retrofitted due to out dated, corroded and obsolete controls.
This Change Order would fund this project through completion and is budgeted for in the the FY
26 budget.

FY 2026 Budget Impact, 402535-546009-25002 R&M Infrastructure. (budgeted)


Background:

On March 25, 2025 the City entered into an In-kind agreement with the Florida Department of
Environmental Protection by way of Consent Order OGC File No. 23-0911; Bay County which
required the city to upgrade all lift stations with a Supervisory Control And Data Acquisition
(SCADA) element to detect and notify staff in advance of a lift station failure that could lead to a
possible Sanitary Sewer Overflow (SSO).
