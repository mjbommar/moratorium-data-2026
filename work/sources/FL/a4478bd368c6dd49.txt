                           ~PROCLAMATION~
       WHEREAS, National Disability Independence Day commemorates the signing of the Americans
with Disabilities Act (ADA) on July 26, 1990. The ADA provides protection from employment
discrimination as well as better access to goods, services, and communications for people with disabilities;
and
        WHEREAS, the day not only celebrates the anniversary of the ADA, but it also serves several
other purposes. The law first broke down barriers that individuals with disabilities faced every day, and
over time, common areas became accessible to wheelchairs, included braille signs and crosswalks for the
vision impaired, improving mobility and safety; and
       WHEREAS, beyond structural changes, the Americans with Disabilities Act motivated designers
to enhance technology that often restricted a person’s ability to access legal or health information with
new assistive technologies making it easier to obtain the necessary information; and
         WHEREAS, as accessibility improves, the world continues to change in other ways. Many of
today’s brick and mortar businesses are ADA compliant. However, as more and more businesses switch
to online versions, their websites need to include technology that incorporates multifunctional approaches
to fit every ability; and
        WHEREAS, we encourage all employers and designers to consider how accessible their business
is to people with disabilities. The ADA empowers and encourages people with disabilities to stand up for
their rights and to give shout-outs to accessible businesses and organizations by using
#DisabilityIndependenceDay and sharing on social media.
       NOW, THEREFORE, by the virtue and authority vested in me as Mayor of the City of Lynn
Haven, on behalf of the City Commission and all citizens of Lynn Haven, I do hereby declare by
proclamation AS FOLLOWS:
       1.) That the City Commission of the City of Lynn Haven calls upon our citizens, public and private
           institutions, businesses, and schools to affirm the principals of equality and inclusion for
           persons with disabilities, to celebrate the freedom the ADA inspires with forward thinking
           design and technology, and to bring forth the promise of hope and freedom that is envisioned
           by the passage of the ADA; and
       2.) That the City Commission and all citizens of Lynn Haven do hereby recognize July 26, 2026,
           the 36th anniversary of the Americans with Disabilities Act that passed in 1990 as
                              National Disability Independence Day
        IN WITNESS WHEREOF, do I, Mayor Dave Lowery, hereby set my hand and cause the seal of
the City of Lynn Haven to be affixed, this 28th day of July 2026.

Agenda Title: Discussion and possible approval to accept the annual renewal of Employee
Benefits Plans and associated costs. (Assistant City Manager/HR Director)


Requested Action: Commission to discuss and approve recommendations.

I. Report in Brief:

Abentras Benefits Administrators work on behalf of the City to negotiate competitive employee
medical, health, and supplemental benefits. Through the continued efforts of employees to keep
health costs and claims manageable, Abentras was able to negotiate a very favorable renewal
plan. This year, to help with rising premium costs across the board, Abentras negotiated the
addition of a cost-effective Open Access HMO plan which will meet the needs of many of our
employees, while keeping the employee/employer premiums at a much lower rate. Abentras and
staff also researched the premium loads and have determined adjustments are warranted.


Background:

As part of the employment package offered by the City, employees are offered medical, health,
and supplemental benefits for themselves and their family. In the past, the city provided benefits
to those employees who worked at least 21 hours/week. The premiums were not adjusted for
their part-time role. Consequently, the city was paying the same premium price for a part-time
employee as a full-time employee. Also, the city has been paying a higher premium percentage
for spouse, children, and family coverage, which has greatly increased the total city cost. This
year, adjustments to these areas as well as the introduction of a more cost-effective plan are
introduced and recommended.

Also, as part of the employee health benefit package, additional supplemental coverages are
being provided at 0% increases across the board.

Recommendation:

1. Staff recommends that the Commission approves the addition of the more affordable Open

Access HMO as a plan option for employees. This plan will have the lowest premium charges to
both employee and employer while still providing excellent medical coverage.

2. Staff recommends that the Commission approves the overall rate changes as presented.

    4.1 General

            The Contractor shall furnish all labor, equipment, materials (unless otherwise specified), and supervision necessary to
            complete culvert removal, installation, and site restoration, including all work described herein. All work shall comply with
            applicable local, state, and federal regulations; traffic control shall conform to applicable Florida Department of
            Transportation Standard Plans; and installation, bedding, and compaction shall conform to FDOT Standard Specifications
            and/or applicable ASTM standards unless otherwise approved by the City.

    4.2 Work to be performed

            The Contractor shall perform culvert replacement services, at a minimum, as follows:

4.2.1 Site Preparation and Demolition
            Includes all work required to access the site, maintain safety and drainage, and remove existing culvert infrastructure.
            • Mobilize personnel, equipment, and materials to the project site and establish appropriate traffic control and site safety
                 measures.
            • Secure the work area and maintain public safety throughout construction.
            • Excavate as necessary to expose the existing culvert pipe.
            • Remove and dispose of the existing culvert pipe and associated materials.
            • Maintain positive drainage during all phases of construction.

            4.2.2 Installation of New Culvert Pipe
Includes all work required to install City-furnished culvert pipe to the specified line, grade, and structural requirements.
    •   Install new culvert pipe furnished by the City.
    •   Prepare trench and bedding in accordance with applicable industry standards and City specifications.
    •   Place, align, and connect pipe to achieve proper line, grade, and joint integrity.
    •   Backfill and compact in lifts using suitable material to prevent settlement and ensure structural stability.


            4.2.3 Restoration
Includes all work required to return disturbed areas to pre-construction condition or better with proper grading and
    stabilization.
    •   Restore all disturbed areas to equal or better condition than existed prior to construction.
    •   Restoration may include, but is not limited to, roadway, shoulder, ditch, embankment, sod, and other surface
    features.
    •   Ensure final grading promotes proper drainage and site stabilization.


            4.2.4 Spoil Handling and Disposal
            Includes all work required to handle, transport, reuse, and dispose of excavated and demolished materials.
            • Load, transport, and deliver all excavated materials designated for reuse to a City-designated location.
            • Dispose of excess or unsuitable materials in accordance with all applicable local, state, and federal regulations.
            • Maintain haul routes in a clean and safe condition.

rebuilding. The FEMA funds for the City Parks projects have been exhausted, which would

diminish General Fund reserves. The Invitation to Bid (ITB) was advertised on June 8, 2026, on

contractors to construct James Rogers Park. Bids were due on July 9, 2026. A total of eight (8)

solicitation. Panhandle Engineering confirmed all prices received and reviewed the bids to
ensure compliance with the scope of work, identifying Gulf Coast Utility Contractors, LLC
(GCUC) as the apparent low bidder with a bid amount of $797,850.00. Staff has determined that
GCUC is a responsible contractor capable of performing the required work.

none of the project and reject all bids. Direction will be provided at this meeting and staff will

DATE: July 13, 2026

General Scope-Of-Work

The Project generally consists of three phases of improvements, modifications, and additions to: streetscape,
electrical lighting, landscape and irrigation, hardscape, roadway improvements, minor utilities, signage, and site
furnishings construction as indicated on drawings and as specified roughly between 7th Street and 10th Street along
Florida Avenue; and between 7th Street and 12th Street along Ohio Avenue; and 7th Street through 10th Street
connectors between Florida Avenue and Ohio Avenue. Work is generally limited to within the Right-Of-Way of the
designated areas. This Project is federally funded and the federal contract provisions, including but not limited to,
Davis-Bacon Certified Payroll Reporting, and HUD Section 3 requirements.

The work involves multiple licensed subcontractors and generally includes work from storefront to storefront of
businesses that must remain open during construction. This effort requires intense subcontractor and work
scheduling, and the constant movement of temporary barriers and controls, and the full-time supervision of an
experienced superintendent managing multiple trades and the project schedule to keep the public safe while
maintaining business operations.

Issue
The purpose of this Memorandum is to substantiate the Architect’s Bid Award Recommendation Letter and the line
of thinking used in its preparation. An apparent low bidder on a CDBG-funded public construction project does not
hold a Florida general contractor license, as required by the bid documents. The City may be concerned that
rejecting the bid on this basis may trigger a bid protest. Set forth below is the statutory, regulatory, and case-law
authority establishing that (1) licensure is a mandatory, non-waivable requirement independent of the bid
documents themselves (which requires a licensed General Contractor), and (2) a bidder who fails to meet this
requirement is non-responsive and therefore lacks standing to protest the award.

1. State Law Independently Requires Award to a Licensed General Contractor
Fla. Stat. § 255.20(1): A municipality “must competitively award to an appropriately licensed contractor” any public
construction project estimated to cost more than $300,000 ($75,000 for electrical work). Bids on this project ranged
from approximately $4.67 million to $7.5 million, well above the $300,000 threshold, so the mandatory prong of §
255.20(1) squarely applies. The City has no legal discretion to award to an unlicensed contractor — doing so would
violate state law on its face, regardless of cost savings.

2. Unlicensed Contracting is Independently Unlawful
Fla. Stat. § 489.127(1)(f): It is a first-degree misdemeanor (a third-degree felony on a repeat violation) for any
person to “engage in the business or act in the capacity of a contractor” without being duly certified or registered
under Chapter 489. Contractor as defined by Florida Statutes means a contractor whose services are unlimited as
to the type of work which he or she may do.

Fla. Stat. § 489.128: Any contract entered into by an unlicensed contractor is unenforceable, in law or equity, and
no lien or bond claim arises in his favor. Even if awarded the contract, this non-responsive bidder would have no
enforceable rights against the City nor the City against the unlicensed contractor — a risk the City is not obligated to
accept.

3. Federal CDBG Procurement Rules Incorporate the Same Requirement
2 C.F.R. § 200.318(a): Grantee procurement procedures for federally funded projects “must be consistent with
State, local, and Tribal laws,” which pulls Florida’s licensing statutes directly into the CDBG compliance framework.

2 C.F.R. § 200.318(h): The grantee “must award contracts only to responsible contractors,” considering integrity,
public policy compliance, and technical resources. Lack of a legally required license is an independent, defensible
basis for a non-responsibility determination under the federal Uniform Guidance, separate from state law.

4. The Bidder is Non-Responsive and Has No Standing to Protest
Under well-settled Florida procurement practice, a bid that fails to meet a licensing requirement stated in the
solicitation is treated as a material deviation, rendering the bid non-responsive rather than merely non-responsible.
A non-responsive bidder generally lacks standing to protest an award to a different, responsive bidder — he was
never legally eligible for the award in the first place, so he suffers no cognizable harm from losing it.

5. Controlling Case Law
Wester v. Belote, 103 Fla. 976, 138 So. 721 (1931) — Florida’s foundational competitive-bidding decision. The
Florida Supreme Court held that competitive bidding requirements exist to protect the public against favoritism and
unfair advantage, and that a public contract awarded in violation of applicable bidding requirements is void,
conferring no enforceable rights on the awardee. This forecloses any argument that the City may simply waive the
licensing requirement to accommodate the low bidder.

6. Licensed Subcontractors Do Not Cure the Prime's Lack of Licensure
The bidder has represented that he is compliant because the subcontractors performing the individual trades hold
their own trade licenses. This does not satisfy the requirement that the prime bidder himself be an appropriately
licensed contractor.

Fla. Stat. § 489.105(3): A “contractor” is defined as the person who “undertakes to, submits a bid to, or does
himself or herself or by others construct” a building or structure. The phrase “by others” shows the statute already
contemplates that a contractor performs work through subcontractors — that is the normal way a contractor
operates, not an exception to licensure. The bidder, not his subs, is the one who submitted the bid and would hold
the prime contract with the City.

7. The "It's Horizontal Work, Not a Structure" Argument Does Not Exempt This Project
The bidder has also argued that because the project is largely site work, roadwork, landscaping and irrigation, and
electrical for street lighting rather than vertical building construction, § 489.105(3)’s reference to a “building or
structure” means no General Contractor’s license is required.

Fla. Stat. § 489.103(1): Chapter 489 does exempt contractors doing work on “bridges, roads, streets, highways, or
railroads, and services incidental thereto” — this is why heavy-highway contractors are often qualified through
FDOT prequalification (§ 337.14) rather than a CILB license. This is a real, narrow exemption, not a frivolous claim.

Fla. Admin. Code R. 61G4-12.011: The board and the Department of Transportation have defined “services
incidental thereto” for purposes of that exemption to mean work on bridges, roads, streets, highways, and railroads
— but the rule expressly excludes building construction and excludes the Division II specialty contractor categories
listed in § 489.105(3)(d)-(q), which include underground utility and excavation, plumbing, and mechanical work. So
even on a qualifying road job, those specialty scopes still require their own license; the exemption does not sweep
in the whole project.

Electrical work falls outside the exemption entirely. Electrical contracting is regulated under Part II of Chapter
489 (Secs. 489.501-489.552), a wholly separate licensing scheme administered by the Electrical Contractors’
Licensing Board. The § 489.103(1) road/bridge exemption is a Part I exemption and does not touch Part II. Street
lighting electrical work on this project therefore requires a licensed electrical contractor regardless of how the
roadway portion of the scope is characterized.

The project here is mixed-scope, not a pure road/bridge/highway job. Because it combines site and road work
with landscaping and irrigation, and electrical for street lights, the narrow § 489.103(1) exemption does not extend
to the electrical scope or to any specialty trade work captured by § 489.105(3)(d)-(q). At minimum, the electrical
component alone defeats the “no license required” position.

9. Holding An Underground Utility and Excavation Contractor License Does Not Make the City's GC
Requirement Unlawful
The bidder asserts that because he personally holds a valid Underground Utility and Excavation Contractor license
(§ 489.105(3)(n)), it is illegal for the City to require bidders to hold a General Contractor license for this project. This
argument is not correct, for several independent reasons.

Fla. Stat. § 489.117(4)(a)4: This is almost certainly the provision behind the claim. It states that “a local government
may not require a license as a prerequisite to submit a bid for public works projects if the work to be performed does
not require a license under general law.” This provision — part of the 2021-2024 state preemption of local
occupational licensing (HB 735 and its amendments) — exists to stop cities and counties from inventing their own
local licenses for trades the state does not regulate at all, such as painting, flooring, cabinetry, or handyman work. It
does not apply here, because the work on this project unquestionably does require licensure under general law: the
electrical scope requires a Part II electrical contractor license, and the utility/excavation scope requires precisely the
underground utility license the bidder holds. Because the work requires licensure under general law, the §
489.117(4)(a)4 exception does not apply, and the statute does not speak to which specific Chapter 489 license
classification the City may require of its prime bidder.

“489.117     Registration; specialty contractors.—

(4)(a)1. A person whose job scope does not substantially correspond to either the job scope of one of the
contractor categories defined in s. 489.105(3)(a)-(o), or the job scope of one of the certified specialty contractor
categories established by board rule, is not required to register with the board. A local government, as defined in
s. 163.211, may not require a person to obtain a license, issued by the local government or the state, for a job
scope which does not substantially correspond to the job scope of one of the contractor categories defined in
s. 489.105(3)(a)-(o) and (q) or authorized in s. 489.1455(1), or the job scope of one of the certified specialty
contractor categories established pursuant to s. 489.113(6). A local government may not require a state or local
license to obtain a permit for such job scopes. For purposes of this section, job scopes for which a local
government may not require a license include, but are not limited to, painting; flooring; cabinetry; interior
remodeling when the scope of the project does not include a task for which a state license is required; driveway
or tennis court installation; handyman services; decorative stone, tile, marble, granite, or terrazzo installation;
plastering; pressure washing; stuccoing; caulking; and canvas awning and ornamental iron installation.”


Whether the City may require the GC classification specifically is a separate question, governed by ordinary
bid-specification law, not the preemption statute. Florida courts give public bodies broad discretion to set bid
specifications and qualification criteria, so long as they are rationally related to a legitimate public purpose and are
not arbitrary, capricious, or used to unfairly restrict competition. A “general contractor” is specifically the license
classification whose “services are unlimited as to the type of work” and who “may contract for any activity requiring
licensure under this part” (§ 489.105(3)(a)) — in other words, it is the classification the Legislature created precisely
for prime contractors who must coordinate multiple, otherwise separately licensed trades under one contract. Given
that this project combines underground utility/excavation work, electrical work, paving, and landscaping and
irrigation, requiring the prime to hold the one license classification designed for multi-trade coordination is a
reasonable, defensible specification, not an arbitrary one.
