TO: Honorable Mayor & Members of the North Port Commission

FROM: A. Jerome Fletcher II, ICMA-CM, MPA, City Manager

TITLE: An Ordinance of the City of North Port, Florida, Establishing a Temporary Moratorium on the Acceptance, Processing, Review, Approval, or Issuance of Building Permits, Rezoning, Site Plans, Conditional Use Approvals, Variances, Comprehensive Plan Amendments, or Other City Approvals for the Construction, Establishment, Expansion, or Intensification of Data Centers, Data Center Facilities, Large-Scale Data Centers, and Large Load Customer Facilities Within the Municipal Limits of the City of North Port; Providing Exceptions; Directing Study and Evaluation of Data Center Impact; Providing for Enforcement; Providing for Notice; Providing for Findings; Providing for Conflicts; Providing for Severability; and Providing an Effective Date.

Recommended Action for Second Reading

Commission policy decision.

City Commission Options

Option 1: Approve Ordinance No. 2026-25 as presented and continue to second reading on September 8, 2026.

• Pros: Furthers the will of the Commission expressed during the City Commission Regular Meeting held on July 21, 2026.

• Cons: Opens the pathway to legal challenges against the City, especially with a pending Data Center development application.

Option 2: Don’t approve Ordinance No. 2026-25 as presented and continue to second reading on September 8, 2026.

• Pros: Avoids legal challenges, including any challenge posed by the pending data center development application.

• Cons: Does not further the will of the Commission expressed during the City Commission Regular Meeting held on July 21, 2026.

City Commission Options for Second Reading

Option 1: Approve Ordinance No. 2026-25 as presented.

• Pros: Furthers the will of the Commission expressed during the City Commission Special Meeting held on August 31, 2026.

• Cons: Opens the pathway to legal challenges against the City, especially because the pending data center development application has not been fully exempted from the moratorium.

Option 2: Don’t approve Ordinance No. 2026-25 as presented.

• Pros: Avoids legal challenges, including any challenge posed by the pending data center development application.

• Cons: Does not further the will of the Commission expressed during the City Commission Special Meeting held on August 31, 2026.

Background Information

During the City Commission Regular Meeting held on July 21, 2026, Agenda Item 26-0985 went before Commission for the Discussion and Possible Action Regarding Data Center Moratorium. City Attorney Fuino introduced the item including allowable uses in light/heavy industrial zoning, state law consideration, moratorium processes and compliance, effective date options, Ordinance examples, potential legal issues, options, hurricane affected lands, inclusion of process for aggrieved party, and next steps.

Commission questions and discussion took place regarding formal applications received to date, Unified Land Development Code (ULDC) language, ability to modify ULDC and/or Comprehensive Plan, legal ability to mandate mitigation of environmental factors, public notice to enact moratorium and pending Ordinance adoption impact on legal standing, data center definitions and types, hyperscale data centers, job opportunities, environmental impacts, discussion at City Commission Workshop, community engagement, draft Ordinance, Noise Ordinance update, timeline, and tax break incentives.

City staff and the Commission learned during the discussion that a Data Center development application had been submitted.

A motion was made by Commissioner Stokes, seconded by Commissioner Petrow, to direct the City Manager to immediately issue a public notice and to schedule an agenda item to act on an Ordinance imposing an 18-month moratorium retroactively on accepting and approving any application for any data centers in the City of North Port excluding those properties under SB180 affected by Hurricanes Debbie, Helene, and Milton.

During the City Commission Special Meeting held on July 29, 2026, the Commission directed the City Attorney to create an agenda Item for first reading of the Data Center Moratorium for the City Commission Special Meeting scheduled for August 31, 2026, at 6:00 p.m., with a second reading for the City Commission Regular Meeting scheduled for September 8, 2026, at 10:00 a.m.

The attached Ordinance No. 2026-05 was presented to the Commission for first reading during the August 31, 2026, City Commission Special Meeting. At that time, the Ordinance created an 18-month moratorium on the acceptance, processing, review, approval, or issuance of building permits, rezonings, site plans, conditional approvals, variances, comprehensive plan amendments, or other City approvals for the construction, establishment, expansion, or intensification of data centers. The moratorium applied to all applications received after the effective date of Ordinance No. 2026-05 as well as any applications pending on the effective date - including the application that City staff and the Commission learned of during the City Commission Regular Meeting held on July 21, 2026.

Ordinance No. 2026-05 further directed City staff to review the City’s ULDC and related development regulations, and prepare proposed amendments addressing the siting, design, review, compatibility, infrastructure capacity, environmental impacts, water resource impacts, emergency service impacts, public safety impacts, and public facility impacts of Data Centers. Finally, Ordinance No. 2026-05 allowed for exceptions to an applicant who believes the impact of the moratorium would create an extraordinary hardship or whom has been affected by Hurricanes Debbie, Helene, or Milton.

The Commission passed Ordinance No. 2026-05 during the August 31, 2026, City Commission Special Meeting with one amendment: the pending Data Center application would be exempted from the acceptance, processing, and review portion of the moratorium, but not the approval or issuance of development approval portion. That amendment is reflected in new Section 5.04. So, while the Data Center application can be accepted, processed, and reviewed by City staff, it cannot be approved without further action by the Commission. The Commission also moved the second reading of the Ordinance to September 22, 2026.

Ordinance 2026-25 has been reviewed by the City Attorney and is legally correct as to form.

The Business Impact Statement has been prepared and posted publicly on July 31, 2026, in accordance with Florida Statute Section 166.041.
