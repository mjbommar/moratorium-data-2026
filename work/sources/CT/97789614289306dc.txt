RESOLUTION TC31-61:

WHEREAS, the three fire districts in the Town of Trumbull, CT respond to three major limited
access state highways that run through the Town of Trumbull. There are approximately ten (10)
miles of Route 25, eight (8) miles of Route 15, and three (3) miles of Route 8; and

WHEREAS, the number of responding fire apparatus units is based on the location and nature of
the incident. The Long Hill and Nichols Fire Departments have the closest access to the
highways based on highway entrances and the state route intersections. The distance between
access ramps requires there to be one vehicle stationed on the west side of town and the other on
the east side of town for optimal response time; and

WHEREAS, the Trumbull Fire Departments regularly place a piece of apparatus in a “blocking”
position on divided highways to allow fire, EMS, police and tow operators to render aid at motor
vehicle accidents or to extinguish car fires; and

WHEREAS, the Fiscal Year 2027 Legislative appropriation contains a $300,000 grant to the
Town of Trumbull for the purpose of the Nichols Fire District and Long Hill Fire District, which
will use the funds to purchase and equip two (2) highway blocking attenuator trucks; and

WHEREAS, it is anticipated that the full cost of the two (2) highway blocking attenuator trucks
as equipped will exceed the grant amount of $300,000;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, That Vicki A. Tesoro, First Selectman, or her
designee, be and the same is hereby authorized to sign the Notice of Grant Award from the
Office of Policy and Management and all subsequent contracts, amendments, reports and related
documents in order to administer and implement the Grant for the Nichols Fire District and Long
Hill Fire District, with the condition that the Nichols Fire District and the Long Hill Fire District
will pay any additional costs to purchase and outfit the two (2) highway blocking attenuator
trucks above the amount of the grant as well as any additional costs associated with the grant.

WHEREAS:

1. In 2023, the State of Connecticut enacted a tire extended producer responsibility law, codified at Conn. Gen.
   Stat. Ann. § 22a-905i, including as amended, (“Tire EPR Law”) requiring certain entities to establish and
   implement a “Program” (defined below) for the collection and recycling of certain tires.

2. CTS was formed to establish and implement the Program under the Tire EPR Law.

3. CTS desires to enter into this Agreement for the purpose of collecting certain tires under the Program,
   among other services;

4. Covered Entity desires to enter into this Agreement to participate in the Program by collecting certain tires at
   its “Drop-Off Sites” (defined below), among other services.

5. The Parties wish to enter this Agreement, which provides the terms and conditions under which Covered
   Entity will participate in the Program as a Drop-Off Site.

Resolution TC31-65:
WHEREAS the Town of Trumbull is committed to protecting the public health, safety, and
welfare of all residents, businesses, and visitors; and

WHEREAS, the Trumbull Town Council believes that a sufficient time is required to research
and evaluate the potential environmental, infrastructure, and public safety impacts associated
with the development of any new data centers within the Town of Trumbull; and

WHEREAS, it is in the best interests of the Town of Trumbull and its residents, businesses and
visitors to adopt a moratorium on the establishment of any new data center.

NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED AND ORDAINED THAT THE TRUMBULL
MUNICIPAL CODE BE AND THE SAME IS HEREBY AMENDED BY INCLUSION OF
THE FOLLOWING:
  A. The Town hereby adopts a two-year moratorium on the establishment and development
     of any new data centers within the Town of Trumbull.
  B. It is specifically understood that this moratorium shall have no impact or effect on any
     data centers currently existing within the Town of Trumbull.
  C. For the purposes of this moratorium, the term DATA CENTER shall mean a facility,
     structure, or campus of structures primarily used for the storage, processing, and
     distribution of digital data and information, including cloud-based services and network
     operations.
  D. This moratorium shall take effect upon the effective date of this ordinance and shall
     sunset twenty-four months from said effective date.

Discussion on Pavement Preservation
Mr. Estrada thanked those who came to the orientation to share some operational experiences.
The Pavement Preservation Program has been in place for the last three years beginning with
ARPA funding. It is now carried in the Capital budget. This has become one of the most
important components in extending the life of the roads and will provide a more programmatic
extension of the life cycle of Town roads.

Ms. McHugh requested Mr. Estrada to schedule additional orientations of the Facilities
Department and Parks and Rec.

Mr. Infante discussed the program highlighting the following:
    1. This program uses various techniques to preserve the durability, longevity, and
        drivability of the roadways.
    2. In 2014, mix designs of asphalt changed that did not provide longevity. The Town began
        to use these mixes designed by UCONN. Preservation techniques were developed and
        they now work in conjunction with the mix designs allowing for stability and longevity.
    3. Standard Paving Program was milling and paving with minimal crack sealing.
        Approximately $18.00 per sq yard but could increase to $54.00. Only working on the
        worst roads.
    4. Started crack sealing in 2023 with ARPA funds for pavement preservation. This is ﬁlling
        the cracks in the road by ﬁrst cleaning them out and then ﬁlling them with polymer
        modiﬁed asphalt (ﬁber reinforced). Biggest problem for the roads is freezing in winter
        with water and cold temperatures. 71 roads and 26.12 miles covered. Cost is $.80 to
        $1.80 per sq yard; $18.00 to mill and pave. Inexpensive and eﬀective process. Qualifying
        roads are 5-15 years old.
    5. Started chip sealing in 2024. This is cleaning the road by sweeping, clearing any
        vegetation and spraying a hot emulsion on it with an aggregate that covers the entire
        surface taking out imperfections and cracks. They crack seal ﬁrst and then chip seal over
        the top. One additional liquid asphalt layer is applied to complete the process.
        Qualifying roads are 16-20 years old. Six to eight years extension.
    6. Experimented with fog sealing with sand ﬁnish on two roads. It is the same process with
        the oil after cleaning and putting sand down for traction. Qualifying roads are 5-12 years
        old.
    7. 2024 – crack sealing - 30 roads covering 9.27 road miles; chip sealing - 13 roads covering
        2.27 road miles and fog seal with sand ﬁnish - 2 roads covering .64 road miles.


                                                2
                                                                                                17


   8. In 2024, found a process for ﬁlling pot holes by an air injected liquid emulsion that layers
       aggregate into the pot holes. Specialized truck is available most of the year for this
       process and is rented due to cost. One truck/one week - 6 roads covering 7.92 road
       miles.
   9. Total 51 roads covering 20.1 road miles in 2024.
   10. Continued to use the same methods in 2025 totaling 42 roads covering 20.13 road miles.
   11. Process is not perfect with traﬃc concerns and lose stones, but the value is noticeable.
   12. Adding in a mastic application; Board already approved the mastic trailer. This
       application covers cracks over 1.5 inches that cannot be addressed by crack sealing. It is
       liquid asphalt with stones mixed in. Will be able to capture more roads. Very eﬀective.
   13. Coverage over three years – crack sealing covered 37.68 miles; chip sealing covered 5.63
       miles and the pot hole truck covered 22.4 miles (concentrating on main roads).
   14. Looking into other preservation methods. All current methods of preservation are
       recyclable.

RESOLUTION TC31-26:

RESOLUTION APPROPRIATING $710,000 FOR THE PLANNING, DESIGN,
DEMOLITION, CONSTRUCTION, RENOVATION, EQUIPPING AND FURNISHING
OF A NEW TRUMBULL VETERANS AND FIRST RESPONDERS CENTER AND

RESOLVED:

        Section 1.      The sum of $710,000 is hereby appropriated by the Town of
Trumbull, Connecticut (the "Town") for the planning, design, demolition, construction,
renovation, equipping and furnishing of a new Trumbull Veterans and First Responders
Center, as more fully set forth in that certain Budget Report, dated as of January 23,
2026, prepared by Downes Construction Company, LLC, as amended from time to time,
and related costs, including, but not limited to: expenses related to architectural and
advisory services; engineering; oversight; permitting; demolition of existing structures
earthwork, excavation and site work; topographic surveys, environmental, geological and
geotechnical testing; and site remediation; installation of concrete, masonry, structural
and steel improvements, thermal and moisture protection, doors, windows and finishes,
kitchen equipment; installation of fire protection and sprinkler systems, plumbing
infrastructure, heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems, electrical systems, site
lighting, utilities, communications, technology, data, life-safety and security systems;
ADA and other code compliance improvements; and related site and interior
improvements, including roofing, hardware, hazardous-materials assessment, testing and
abatement; acquisition and installation of materials, furniture, fixtures, and equipment;
and construction, reconstruction and improvement of parking areas, sidewalks,
driveways, storm drainage facilities and sanitary sewerage systems, landscaping, and
temporary parking and storage; and legal, consulting, licensing, advertising,
administrative, printing, governmental fees and expenses and costs of issuance related
thereto (the "Project").

        Section 2.      To meet said appropriation, $710,000 bonds of the Town or so
much thereof as shall be necessary for such purpose, shall be issued, maturing not later
than the maximum maturity permitted by the General Statutes of Connecticut, Revision
of 1958, as amended from time to time (the "Connecticut General Statutes"). Said bonds
may be issued in one or more series as determined by the First Selectman and the Town
Treasurer, provided that the total amount of bonds to be issued shall not be less than an
amount which will provide funds sufficient, with other funds available for such purpose,
to pay the principal of and the interest on all temporary borrowings in anticipation of the
receipt of the proceeds of said bonds outstanding at the time of the issuance thereof, and
to pay for the administrative, financing, legal and other costs of issuance of such bonds.
