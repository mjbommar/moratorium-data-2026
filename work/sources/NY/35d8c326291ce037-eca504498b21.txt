BACKGROUND

The Town of Islip is proposing a local law establishing a moratorium for Data Centers that will

F. Exclusions. This section shall not apply to routine maintenance! repair, or replacement

STAFF ANALYSIS

As noted in the past by the Suffolk County Planning Commission, a moratorium on development

is a local law or ordinance that suspends the right of property owners to obtain development

approvals while the community takes time to consider, draft or adopt land use plans or rules to

respond to new or changing circumstances not adequately dealt with by its current law. A

moratorium preserves the status quo for a reasonable time. A moratorium is, from one

perspective, the most extreme land use action that a municipality can take because it suspends

completely the rights of owners to use their property. Such development moratoria may be general

or specific. A general imposes a ban on all development within a community (allowing for certain

hardship exemptions), while a specific moratorium may prevent development approvals in a

specific geographic area or of a certain type of development.

Staff find that this proposed moratorium is intended to be specific and should not restrict all

development on any or all parcels within the Town; rather, it is understood that the proposed

moratorium is limited to AI data centers only, and for a period of about 18 months.

Staff notes that the Town is proposing the moratorium extension to allow time to review its zoning

code and develop regulations to adequately address the unique issues present with data center

development. The legislative intent of the local law states the following:

The Town Board finds that the development of data centers presents significant land use,

infrastructureenvironmental, and other considerations, including but not limited to:

,

electrical transmission capacity and grid reliability. water consumption and wastewater

infrastructure. noise generated by mechanical equipment and emergency generators, air

emissions associated with backup power systems, traffic impacts during construction and

operation, compatibility with surrounding land uses, emergency services and public safety

considerations, and long-term economic and community impacts.

In New York State as of May 2026, there were nearly 12 gigawatts of data center load requests;

about two-thirds (8 gigawatts) were requested in 2025. There are no current applications for data

centers within the Town of Islip.

Proponents of data centers cite the need for these facilities to support the data needs of emerging

technologies, such as artificial intelligence requiring large amounts of data storage. These

facilities are argued to encourage grid upgrades through new investments as businesses build

data centers. In the short term, they can support construction jobs. Long term jobs are potentially

well-paying tech roles.

Opponents of data centers cite many of the issues above such as water and power usage. Noise

from cooling fans and other mechanical components, as well as potential pollution from water

usage are also cited as issues. The promise of high-tech jobs may also be overstated.

RECOMMENDATION

Staff recommends Approval of a proposed Local Law of the Town of Islip for a moratorium on

the issuance of permits and/or approvals of data centers, subject to the following condition and

comments:

Condition:

The moratorium extension shall be limited to an initial twelve (12) month period. The Town

shall return to the Suffolk County Planning Commission at the end of the twelve (12) month

period (i.e. July 2027) to report on their progress with updates to their code for data centers,

and to discuss if an additional six (6) month period extension of the moratorium is warranted

.

Reason:

Comments:

1. The Town should immediately review all state materials and reports related to data centers

as they are released. New York State Executive Order 62 includes timelines for reports

from various state agencies (DEC, etc.). The Town should contact these agencies for any

additional information as it becomes available.

2. The Town should confirm that the temporary moratorium will only apply to data center

related applications and not deem a larger proposed land use application unfit for review

if a portion of such proposal includes a data center facility, rather that data center portion

of such a proposed application may not move forward but allow the remainder of said

application to continue to be reviewed by the Town.

3. The Town is encouraged to review and coordinate with other Long Island municipalities

on their review of updates to local, state and national regulations that may relate to data

centers, which could help the Town better understand applicable appropriate regulations

that would allow for the development of these facilities while adhering to the Town’s public

safety, land use planning, infrastructure, and other concerns.
