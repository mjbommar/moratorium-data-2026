COMMON COUNCIL CITY OF PEEKSKILL


A LOCAL LAW TO AMEND CHAPTER 575 OF THE CODE OF THE CITY OF PEEKSKILL TO

ADD SECTIONS 12.1 AND 12.2 AND TO AMEND SECTION 33.G(2) CONCERNING

“PAYMENT IN-LIEU OF PARKING” IN THE DOWNTOWN PARKING DISTRICT


A LOCAL LAW to amend Chapter 575 of

the Peekskill City Code concerning the

“Zoning” to add Sections 12.1 and 12.2

and to amend Section 33.G(2) concerning

Payment-in-Lieu-of-Parking.

BE IT ENACTED  by the Common Council of the City of Peekskill as follows:


Section 1: Chapter 575 of the Peekskill City Code entitled “Zoning” is hereby amended by adding Section 12.1


relating to “Payment-in-lieu-of-parking” as follows:

§575-12.1 Payment-in-lieu-of-parking


.

A. Purpose. To allow an alternate means for a property owner to provide required parking for residential

units including upper floor dwelling units that are  newly created through new construction or the

repurposing and renovation of existing space within  the Downtown Parking District as shown on Schedule

___ to the City of Peekskill Zoning Code.

B. Applicability and Requirements. Property owners that obtain site plan approval to create residential u units

in new buildings or create more than two (2) units i in renovated buildings in the Downtown Parking District

may make a one-time ‘Payment-In-Lieu-Of-Parking’ (“PILOP”) to the City of Peekskill Downtown

Parking Fund, rather than providing required parking spaces on-site for new residential units. In addition

to the PILOP, the property owner is required to purchase the required number of municipal parking permits

for residents residing in the new residential units, and to pay an annual maintenance fee for each permit

parking space. The PILOP and maintenance fee will allow the City of Peekskill to acquire land, finance

,

design, construct and carry out capital repairs and d perform other necessary and desirable actions to provide

municipal parking facilities as well as on-street parking and to allow for other transportation and

pedestrian improvements in the Downtown Parking District. During site plan  review, the Planning

Commission will also consider the need for a loading zone in close proximity to the new residential units.

C.  No permanent waiver. For all residential units created through construction and for more than two (2)

residential units created through renovation in the  Downtown Parking District, no permanent waiver of

off-street parking requirement shall apply.

10 November 24, 2025

D. Maximum on-site parking. If greater than 40 off-street parking spaces are required by the City of Peekskill

City Code for a proposed residential project, a maximum of 60% of required parking may be permitted on

site, with the remainder subject to the PILOP. The applicant may submit a request to the Planning

Commission to provide greater than 60% of parking spaces on site. To permit greater than 60% of required

parking on site, the Planning Commission must find one of the following conditions is satisfied based on

the applicant’s statement of findings:

i. site conditions are conducive to providing parking spaces on-site; or

ii. the occupants of the proposed residential units have unique needs that require on-site parking;

or

iii. other unique conditions exist that require on-site p parking.

E. Temporary Waiver of Permit Purchase. Five years after issuance of the final residential Certificate of

Occupancy, a property owner may apply to the Planning Commission to reduce the number of permits

required to be purchased. In support of such application, the property owner must submit an affidavit to

the Planning Commission affirming the number of vehicles that each residential unit possesses during the

immediately preceding three-year period. If the Planning Commission finds that the three-year average

of the total number of vehicles is less than the required number of municipal permits, then the Commission

may issue a temporary waiver for the purchase of permits and payment of maintenance fees for the number

of spaces not needed. Further reduction of permits m may be applied for every three years based on data that

identifies the average number of vehicles that the r residential units possess during the preceding three-year

period, as described above. The original PILOP fee will not be refunded for temporarily waived spaces.

F. Revision of Temporary Waiver. The Planning Commission may request vehicle ownership data from a

property owner at any time to consider decreasing the number of temporarily waived spaces. The property

owner may purchase additional permits for residents  at any time.

G. Establishment of Fees. The amount of the one-time P PILOP fee and the annual permit fee and maintenance

fee per parking space shall be in accordance with the fees identified in the Peekskill Consolidated Fee

Schedule that may be periodically revised by the Common Council. In setting such fees, the Common

Council shall take into account the costs of acquiring property, carrying out suitable site development, and

maintaining municipal parking facilities.

H.  Timing and Method of Payments

(a) Prior to the issuance of a final Certificate of Occupancy for the residential units:

[1] One-hundred percent (100%) of the required PILOP fee shall be paid by the property owner to

the City of Peekskill.

[2] The property owner must purchase a parking permit for each required municipal space annually

for five years from the date of issuance of the Certificate of Occupancy. These permits must be

11 November 24, 2025

renewed annually thereafter (or for the number of permits as determined by the Planning Commission

as per Section 575-12.1.E), at the 24-hour rate at the time the permits are purchased.

[3] The Certificate of Occupancy issued for the property shall identify that a PILOP is in effect to

inform any new owners about the existence of the PILOP. In addition, the PILOP will be a condition

of site plan approval.

[4] The property owner must submit a form of covenant or deed restriction confirming the obligation

to provide off-site parking spaces in a municipal fafacility, and file and record such covenant or deed

restriction with the Westchester County Department of Land Records, in a form to be approved by the

City Attorney or Corporation Counsel.

(b) At the time the permits are purchased, the property  owner shall pay the maintenance fee on an annual

basis as long as the residential use remains, regardless of the property’s ownership status. The first

maintenance fee payment will be pro-rated.

(c) Maintenance fees may be paid up to five years in advance for the net-present value of the fees, per

agreement by the Director of Planning;

(d) In the event the property changes ownership, the new property owner shall pay the annual maintenance

fee and shall purchase annual parking permits as specified herein as long as the residential use remains.

(e) All payment in-lieu fees and maintenance fees shall  be placed in the City of Peekskill Downtown

Parking Fund. The funds can only be used for parking creation and maintenance or other

transportation and pedestrian improvements in the downtown. Use of the Downtown Parking Fund

must be approved by the Common Council.

I. Grandfathering. The amendments set forth herein shall apply to all current and future applications that

have not received site plan approval as of the effective date of this amendment.

WHEREAS, the City recognizes that Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) within the City of


Peekskill (the “City”) are a key component to the viability and promotion of renewable energy sources; and

WHEREAS, since the technology of the BESS is still in its infancy, there is a significant amount of


public concern regarding the potentially volatile nature of lithium-ion batteries, which stems from fires at

three different BESS facilities in the State of New w York since May 2023.

WHEREAS, the City of Peekskill Common Council, the City Planning Commission, the City Zoning


Board of Appeals and the City Building Department require additional information and assurances about the

safety of BESS facilities, including but not limited to best practices for fire suppression and emergency response,

ensuring the technology and availability of equipment necessary for local fire departments should a fire occur,

precautions to take related to gas emissions and deterring any necessary fire suppression chemicals from

impacting groundwater, and the adequacy of location  and other standards for siting of BESS in the City; and

d

WHEREAS, a public hearing was previously held on March 24,  2025, to consider a local law


implementing a moratorium on large-scale BESS systems in the City of Peekskill, at which time all members of

the public had an opportunity to comment on the proposed moratorium; and

WHEREAS, following the March 24, 2025 public hearing, the Common Council adopted a local


law approving a six (6) month moratorium prohibiting the review and approval of applications  and

issuance permits for large-scale BESS in the City to address concerns about the safety and location of

BESS systems and to gather information on these issues to implement appropriate regulations and address

any outstanding concerns; and

14 November 24, 2025

WHEREAS, this local law did not apply to small-scale BESS w which have an aggregate energy


capacity less than 80kWh, which are scaled for residential use and given their limited energy capacity y do not

pose the same associated risks to the community and  residents of the City as large-scale BESS; and

WHEREAS, the local law adopting the moratorium was later filed wiwith the Secretary of State on


May 5, 2025 and expired on November 5, 2025; and

WHEREAS, the City is continuing to study and analyze the safefety and security of these BESS, including


thermal runaway, off gassing and toxicity, stranded d energy, ways to prevent fires, prevent by-product

contamination, and ensure emergency responders have  the necessary training and information to prepare and

deploy resources in the event of a fire; and

WHEREAS, the City also continues to be concerned about locating BESS in proximity to residential


properties and in other areas where fires in such fafacilities could result in substantial harm to residents and damage

to properties in the City; and

WHEREAS, the City also continued to be concerned about other adverse environmental impacts BESS


may have with respect to locating BESS on or in proximity to environmentally sensitive properties and with

respect to adjacent properties; and

WHEREAS, as a result, the Common Council has determined that further extending the moratorium an


additional six (6) months p prohibiting the review and approval of applications  and issuance permits for large-scale

BESS in the City of Peekskill  is necessary to address concerns about the safety and location of BESS systems and

to gather additional information on these issues; and

WHEREAS, a public hearing was held on November 24, 2025, at which time all members of the public


had an opportunity to comment on the proposed further extension of the moratorium on large-scale BESS in the

City of Peekskill .

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT


RESOLVED, that the Common Council of the City of Peekskill approves Local Law No. 14 of 2025, in


the form set forth below, further extending the moratorium on large-scale BESS over 80 kHw, for an additional

six (6) months; and be it further

Section 3: Legislative findings.


This ordinance is adopted to impose an additional six (6) month moratorium on the issuance of permits

and/or approvals for large-scale Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”). While the City recognizes that

BESS are a key component to the viability and promotion of renewable energy sources, since the

technology of these systems is still in its infancy, there is a significant amount of public concern regarding

the potentially volatile nature of lithium-ion batteries, which stems from fires at three different BESS

facilities in the State of New York since May 2023.

In response to these BESS fires, New York State Governor Kathy Hochul found it necessary to form an

Inter-Agency Fire Safety Working Group tasked with ensuring the safety of BESS across New York. On

February 6, 2024, Governor Hochul released initial recommendations from the Inter-Agency Fire Safety

Working Group, outlining enhanced safety standards for battery energy storage systems. The draft

18 November 24, 2025

recommendations include potential updates to the Fire Code of New York State as well as a list of

additional opportunities for defining and implementing best practices. If adopted, the changes will codify

enhanced safety standards and continue to position New York as a national leader in responsible and

reliable battery energy storage development.

As a result, the Common Council of the City, the City Planning Commission, the City Zoning Board of

Appeals and the City of Building Department require  additional information and assurances about the

safety of BESS facilities, including but not limited to best practices for fire suppression and emergency

response, ensuring the technology and availability of equipment necessary for local fire departments should

a fire occur, precautions to take related to gas emissions and deterring any necessary fire suppression

chemicals from impacting groundwater, and the adequacy of location and other standards for siting of

BESS. The City intends to study the safety and security of these energy storage systems, including thermal

runaway, off gassing and toxicity, stranded energy,  ways to prevent fires, prevent by-product

contamination, and ensure emergency responders have  the necessary training and information to prepare

and deploy resources in the event of a fire.

In addition, the City is concerned about locating BESS in proximity to residential properties and in other

areas where fires in such facilities could result in substantial harm to residents and damage to properties in

the City. The City is also concerned about other adverse environmental impacts BESS may have with

respect to locating BESS on or in proximity to environmentally sensitive properties and with respect to

adjacent properties.

A local law adopting a moratorium was previously adopted on March 24, 2025 and was filed with the

Secretary of State on May 5, 2025 and expired on November 5, 2025. This local law enacting an additional

six (6) month limited moratorium on large-scale BESS will not apply to small-scale BESS which have an

aggregate energy capacity less than 80kWh. This is due to the fact that such small-scale BESS are scaled for

residential use, are primarily installed directly on a homeowner’s property, and given their limited energy

capacity do not pose the same associated risks to the community and residents of the City as large-scale

BESS.


In short, the City has concerns about the potential  effects that large-scale BESS may have on the health,

safety, welfare, character and quality of life of the residents and citizens of the City and requires time to

adequately study the potential impacts from large-scale BESS and adopt ordinances addressing these

concerns as determined to be appropriate. Given these concerns and the current lack of relevant data and

information on these issues, it is proposed that the Common Council of the City enact an additional six (6)

19 November 24, 2025

month limited moratorium prohibiting the review and  approval of applications and issuance permits for

large-scale BESS in the City while these issues are  further considered and the City Code is adequately

revised to address any outstanding concerns.

Section 4: Moratorium.


Pursuant to the statutory authority vested in the City to regulate and control land use and to protect  the

health, safety and welfare of its residents, the Common Council of the City hereby declares an additional

six (6) month moratorium on the review and approval  of new as of yet unfiled and any and all pending

applications for any and all land use approvals, including site plan approvals, subdivision plats, building

permits, special use permits and variances (hereinafter “Land Use Approvals”) for all large-scale Battery

Energy Storage Systems within the City. During the period of this local law:

1. The Planning Commission shall not accept any application, grant any approval to, or continue the

review of a subdivision plat, site plan, special use permit or other permit that includes the

permitting, construction and/or development of a Large-Scale Battery Energy Storage System

within the City.

2. The Zoning Board of Appeals shall not accept any application or grant any approval for a variance

or other permit that would have as a result the permitting, construction and development of a

Large-Scale Battery Energy Storage System within the City.

3. The Building Department shall not accept any building permit application or grant any Certificate

of Occupancy or Certificate of Compliance for a Large-Scale Battery Energy Storage System

within the City.

4. Projects currently under construction and projects that have received final approvals are not

subject to this moratorium.

Section 5: Term.


The moratorium imposed by this Local Law shall be in effect for a period of six (6) months from the

effective date of this Local Law.

11/24/2025

William Cannady & Melissa Cannady

501 Roosevelt Ave

Peekskill, NY 10566

Mayor and Members of the Common Council,

Our names are William Cannady and Melissa Cannady. We have lived at 501 Roosevelt

Avenue—within close proximity to 710 Washington Street—for almost 14 years. We have reviewed the

November 6, 2025 Planning Department memorandum, the Grand Street Cannabis Odor Mitigation

Plan dated September 12, 2025, the Twin Oaks consulting drawings, and the CPL engineering review

dated October 8, 2025.

We are not arguing against cannabis as a product. Our concern is that the specific building and

odor-control design proposed for this location is not technically reliable enough to protect the

neighborhood, and several elements of the plan remain incomplete or insufficiently supported.

1. Non-standard filtration specification.

The Odor Mitigation Plan relies on “MERV 20” filtration as a core control measure. ASHRAE’s MERV

rating scale—the standard reference for air filters—runs from MERV 1 to MERV 16. Filters above that

are classified differently. Because “MERV 20” is not a recognized rating, the City has no objective basis

to verify the claimed filtration performance or whether the proposed fans can operate with such

resistance. ASHRAE’s published guidance for health-care facility installations demonstrates the proper

engineering standards for high-efficiency filtration and does not rely on unsupported metrics. We

recommend the City evaluate this system against the standards described here: https://www.ashrae.or

g/file%20library/technical%20resources/covid-19/i-p_a19_ch09_health_care_facilities.pdf

2. Carbon filter capacity and life not supported by calculations.

The plan states that activated carbon filters can “absorb their molecular weight of contaminants” and

proposes a fixed three-month replacement interval. Industry references show vapor-phase activated

carbon generally adsorbs tens of percent of its own weight—not 100 percent

(https://www.scribd.com/document/210523271/Activated-carbon-for-VOC-pdf, page 2). The application

provides no mass-balance calculations tying the amount of carbon installed, the facility’s expected

odor/VOC load, and the proposed changeout interval. Without these calculations, the City has no

quantitative assurance that filters will not saturate and allow breakthrough during peak odor periods

such as flowering, trimming, or harvest.

3. Unusually low reliance on outdoor air compared to common practice.

According to the materials, outside air is introduced only four times per day, and the air-exchange table

shows approximately one air change per hour (ACH). Many industrial and commercial facilities rely on

four or more ACH of outdoor air for contaminant control, and cannabis facilities frequently exhaust

substantial outside air through carbon filtration. This proposal minimizes outdoor air and instead

circulates air internally through ROS and carbon systems. That increases the risk that unfiltered

leakage—through loading doors, exits, or the building envelope—will allow odors to escape to the

surrounding neighborhood. The submittal does not model, quantify, or mitigate that risk.

4. Inconsistent airflow numbers and incomplete mechanical design.

The Planning memo states that each approximately 1,200-square-foot flower room will have 10,000

CFM of air filtration with negative pressure and AirROS technology. That flow rate corresponds to

roughly 40–50 air changes per hour and would require multiple large filtration units per room. However,

the Twin Oaks drawings do not include an equipment schedule showing how the system achieves

10,000 CFM per room, and the air-exchange table simultaneously shows only about one ACH of

outdoor air. CPL’s review appropriately describes these as “technically applicable concepts” that will

require a full PE-sealed mechanical design before any approval. The numbers currently cited are not

supported by a complete engineering package.

5. Monitoring only partially reflects CPL’s recommendations.

CPL recommends VOC sensors located before and after the final carbon treatment, with continuous

monitoring, alarm thresholds, and data logging integrated into a computer management system. The

Planning memo notes that the applicant proposes two VOC sensors, but the application does not

identify sensor locations, calibration procedures, alarm thresholds, or how alarms will trigger corrective

actions. The applicant’s monitoring plan still relies heavily on staff detecting odors manually. If the City

intends to enforce a “no off-site odor” condition, it should require the complete monitoring design

outlined by CPL.

6. Outstanding building-code issues and change of use.

The November 6 memorandum also notes that the Building Department issued a Notice to Remedy on

October 29, 2025 after a fire inspection found that the existing multi-industrial configuration constitutes

a change of use. Planning and Buildings are still assessing the ramifications of that Notice. Approving a

long-term special-use permit for a cannabis manufacturing operation in an older mixed-use building

before the underlying use-classification and code-compliance issues have been resolved would be

premature.

Given these unresolved technical issues, inconsistencies in the mechanical design, and ongoing

building-code concerns, we do not believe the current record provides a reliable or enforceable basis

for ensuring that odors and other impacts will be controlled over the life of the facility. We respectfully

request that the Council deny the special permit at this time.

Thank you for your consideration.
