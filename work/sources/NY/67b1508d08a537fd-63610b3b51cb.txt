PUBLIC HEARING – 6:30 PM

Proposed Local Law No. 2 of 2026 – “Override Tax Cap for Fiscal Year

2027”

Supervisor Giebelhaus called the Public Hearing to order at 6:33 PM and took roll call

attendance. Present were Supervisor Giebelhaus, Councilmember Bunzey,

Councilmember Duncan, Councilmember la Cour, and Councilmember Miller,

constituting a quorum. Also present were Town Clerk De Oliveira and Deputy

Supervisor Doolin.

Supervisor Giebelhaus asked everyone to stand for the Pledge of Allegiance.

Supervisor Giebelhaus stated that the Public Hearing had been duly noticed concerning

the proposed local law to override the tax cap limit for fiscal year 2027. He explained

that the proposed action was being considered proactively based upon information

received to date regarding the upcoming budget and anticipated projects and expenses.

He then presented a PowerPoint presentation concerning financial trends, statistics,

and increased costs of services contributing to the potential need for a tax cap override.

PUBLIC COMMENT

Prior to opening public comment, Supervisor Giebelhaus invited comments from the

Town Board regarding the presentation. Deputy Supervisor Doolin encouraged

residents to attend Town Board meetings and participate in public discussion.

Councilmember Bunzey commented on the percentage of the overall budget allocated to

the Transfer Station and the need to address those costs.

James Rapp questioned the amount of revenue returned to the Town from recycling at

the Transfer Station. Supervisor Giebelhaus stated that approximately $9,000 is

received. Mr. Rapp also suggested exploring whether the Town could obtain a DEC

permit for burning wood

.

Marie Lounsbury complimented the Town Park and commented that, based on

events she attended during the summer, it was apparent that money had been spent on

the Park. She also questioned why a forensic audit had not been completed in

connection with the Town's financial deficit.

Elfi Smith expressed concern regarding the effect of potential tax increases on retired

residents and others living on fixed incomes. She also commented on financial pressures

facing farmers and concerns that farmers may leave the Town if they are unable to

remain fifinancially viable.

1

Catherine Ansbro referenced comments attributed to Supervisor Giebelhaus in a

January article in The Altamont Enterprise, including a statement concerning “righting

the ship.” She stated that she hoped the Town's elected representatives would represent

the interests of the residents as it is five votes for the town compared to 60% for the

school budget vote.

Jim Willis stated that he was relatively new to the Town and found the presentation

helpful. He suggested that the Town conduct a further review of its financial situation to

fully understand the circumstances contributing to the Town's current financial

position

.

Michael Vincent commented that he was pleased to see residents attending the

meetings and participating in public discussion. He expressed concern regarding

potential tax increases for residents on fixed incomes and encouraged the Board to

consider reductions across Town government in an effort to limit tax increases.

Peggy McQuade, representing the Helderberg Lake Association, stated that the

Association had requested establishment of a special tax district in 2022 and was

considering making a similar request. She asked whether, if established, such a district

would fall within the two-percent tax levy limit from a municipal perspective.

Dennis Barber, referencing his prior experience serving on the Town Board in Knox,

offered suggestions for reducing expenditures. His suggestions included consideration

of compensation for the Planning Board and Zoning Board, reducing Town Clerk office

hours, conducting dog enumeration, reducing Animal Control Officer compensation,

and reducing Transfer Station hours.

Greg Terrell expressed concern regarding references to the COVID-19 pandemic in

discussions concerning the Town's financial condition. He stated that the Town should

focus on its current financial circumstances rather than continuing to attribute current

conditions to events occurring approximately six years ago.

Joseph G. Roger shared a statement concerning current economic conditions,

including concerns regarding inflation and capitalism “fighting the math.”

Joe Burke discussed a meeting he had with Supervisor Giebelhaus and commented

that there are many aspects of running the Town that residents do not see. He thanked

Supervisor Giebelhaus for taking the time to explain Town operations, including the

Town's bank accounts.

Patty Swasey questioned why a forensic audit had not been completed. She also asked

whether the proposed purchase of the Masonic Lodge was no longer under

consideration and sought clarification regarding approximately $564,000 in bonded

funds associated with highway trucks.

2

Joel Willsey brought concerns regarding highway conditions on Long and Sawmill

Road to the attention of the Town Board and the public. He requested that the Town

consider declaring a state of emergency and closing the roadway, citing concerns

regarding potential liability. Mr. Willsey also expressed concerns regarding the working

relationship between the Town Supervisor and Highway Superintendent Stempel and

referenced the Board's consideration of an RFP. He further expressed concerns

regarding the Board's priorities.

Brandon Clark expressed his hope that the Town Board would provide Highway

Superintendent Stempel with the support he requires to perform his duties.

Trevor Stempel, referencing his family's five generations in the Town of Berne, spoke

about the Town's history, founding principles, and the rights of residents. He expressed

dissatisfaction with what he characterized as a substantial increase in taxes over prior

years and described his concerns as resulting from a continuing series of decisions and

circumstances.

ADJOURNMENT OF PUBLIC HEARING

Public Hearing on the proposed local law concerning the tax cap levy override for fiscal

year 2027.

SECTION 6. SEVERABILITY

If any clause, sentence, paragraph, section, or part of this Local Law is adjudged by a

court of competent jurisdiction to be invalid or unconstitutional, that judgment shall not

affect, impair, or invalidate the remainder of this Local Law, but shall be confined in its

operation to the clause, sentence, paragraph, section, or part directly involved in the

controversy in which the judgment was rendered.

This Local Law shall take effect immediately upon filing with the New York State
