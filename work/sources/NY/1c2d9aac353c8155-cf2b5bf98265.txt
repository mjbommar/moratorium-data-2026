TO: Common Council of the City of Peekskill FROM: Eric L. Gordon, City Attorney RE: Proposed Local Law Extending Moratorium on Large-Scale Battery Energy Storage Systems DATE: November 6, 2025


This memorandum proposes the extension of the moratorium on the issuance of

permits and/or approvals for large-scale (having an aggregate energy capacity greater

than 80kWh) Battery Energy Storage Systems (“BESS”) within the City of Peekskill

(the “City”).

As the City Council was previously advised, while the City recognizes that BESS are a

key component to the viability and promotion of renewable energy sources, since the

technology of these systems is still in its infancy, there is a significant amount of

public concern regarding the potentially volatile nature of lithium-ion batteries, which

stems from fires at three different BESS facilities in the State of New York since May

2023.

In response to these BESS fires, New York k State Governor Kathy Hochul found it

necessary to form an Inter-Agency Fire Safety Working Group tasked with ensuring

the safety of BESS across New York State. On February 6, 2024, Governor Hochul

released initial recommendations from the Inter-Agency Fire Safety Working Group,

outlining enhanced safety standards for battery energy storage systems. The draft

recommendations include potential updates to t the Fire Code of New York State as well

as a list of additional opportunities for defining and implementing best practices. If

adopted, the changes will codify enhanced safety standards and continue to position

New York as a national leader in responsible and reliable battery energy storage

development.

As a result, the Common Council of the Cityty, the City Planning Commission, the City

Zoning Board of Appeals and the City Building Department require additional

information and assurances about the safety of BESS facilities, including but not

limited to best practices for fire suppression and emergency response, ensuring the

technology and availability of equipment necessary for local fire departments should a

fire occur, precautions to take related to  gas emissions and deterring any necessary fire

suppression chemicals from impacting groundwater, and the adequacy of location and

other standards for siting of BESS in the City. The City intends to study the safety and

security of these energy storage systems,  including thermal runaway, off gassing and

toxicity, stranded energy, ways to prevent fires, prevent by-product t contamination, and

ensure emergency responders have the necessary training and information to prepare

and deploy resources in the event of a fire.

In addition, the City is concerned about locating BESS in proximity to residential

properties and in other areas where fires in such facilities could result in substantial

harm to residents and damage  to properties in the City. The City is also concerned

about other adverse environmental impacts BESS may have with respect to locating

BESS on or in proximity to environmentally sensitive properties and with respect to

adjacent properties.

The City Council previously enacted a Local Law adopting a moratorium on large-scale

BESS in April 2025, which became effective o on May 5, 2025. The moratorium expired

on November 5, 2025. However, various City agencies are still in the process of

developing proposed zoning g regulations to address the above-mentioned safety

concerns and to determine the appropriate size and location of f BESS systems within

the City. Therefore, an extension of the previously adopted moratorium is required at

this time to maintain the status quo.

It should be noted that this local law enacting an additional six (6) month limited

moratorium on large-scale BESS will not apply to small-scale BESS which have an

aggregate energy capacity less than 80kWh. This i is due to the fact that such small-scale

BESS are scaled for residential use, are primarily installed directly on a homeowner’s

property, and given their limited energy capacity do not p pose the same associated risks

to the community and residents of f the City as large-scale BESS.

In short, the City continues to have significant concerns about the potential effects

large-scale BESS may have on the health, safetyty, welfare, character and quality of life

of the residents and citizens of the City and r requires additional time  to adequately study

the potential impacts from large-scale BESS and adopt ordinances  addressing these

concerns as determined to be appropriate. Given these concerns and the current lack

of relevant data and information on these issues, it is proposed that the Common

Council of the City enact a further six (6) month limited moratorium prohibiting the

review and approval of applications and issuance permits for large-scale BESS in the

City while these issues are further considered  and the City Code is adequately revised

to address any outstanding concerns.

The Local Law being proposed for consideratition continues to maintain the status quo

by temporarily suspending the processing of land use approvals as provided therein
