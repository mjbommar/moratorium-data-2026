Public Hearing: Spectrum Franchise Agreement

Supervisor Palow briefed the audience on the current Franchise Agreement that has

been in place since 1991, was revisited in 2005 but still the original agreement stands.

The Town receives 3% of annual gross sales from Spectrum Enterprises which is a

charge incurred to each resident the Town receives around $18,000 annually.

Michael Vincent- Long time resident and planning board member spoke with

concerns about limited broadband access for some residents and the impact on

education and work-from-home connectivity

.

Jeff Marden discussed his opinion that it has been beneficial for the Town that the

contract had not been renegotiated and he urged the Town Board not to sign the new

contract. The structure of the current and proposed Spectrum franchise agreement has

implications for subscribers and that current contract terms require service expansion

only when a certain household-per-mile threshold is met.

Public Hearing Proposed Local Law 1-2025 Solar Law Energies & BESS

Hyde Clark from Young Sommers representing TJA on Canaday Hill and Switzkill Road

who has years in the works discussed frustrations with the fact that in 2022 the Town of

Berne adopted a very well composed Solar Law. Although it was agreed that battery

storage was not covered, he urged the Board to allow current solar projects to carry on

and instill a BESS Local Law to alleviate concerns.

Micheal Vincent- discussed concerns raised about battery storage associated with solar

farms, particularly related to fire safety and environmental considerations as a local

volunteer fireman he understands the potential danger.

US Light Energy- proposed early stages project on Helderberg Trail whose project falls

under compliance with the 2022 Local Law on Solar Energies. With a decade in the

field, he felt the law is very well written and understood if the Town Board felt the need

to address standalone solar or battery storage. Revenue for the Town and taxpayers was

also discussed.

1

Steven Khoury- Town of Berne Planning Board Chairman stated that the solar law is an

instrument and guide. He expressed concerns of fire protection and the need for road

ways for the fire department battery storage and logical consideration as well as

drainage and the impact of hydrology on the Town.

Weston Hillegas- PPV Solar on Jansen Lane suggested a special use permit for battery

storage and that there were no batteries proposed in their plan. He asked for the letter

from Amy Lauterbach and the letter from the homeowner on Jansen Lane be included

in the record( these will be attached as meeting minute attachments.) He discussed the

potential benefits for tax entities such as the Town and school as well as the

homeowners who would have the opportunity to opt into community solar projects and

receive a discount on their national grid bill.

Peter Sogrio – represents 8 different Towns in Western New York as Town Attorney and

understands and respects the process. His clients set up a meeting with the Fire

Department following the suggestion of putting in a roadway, a SPDEs permit has

already been addressed for drainage. In all fairness he stressed the application has been

in motion since 2024 and would hope if the moratorium passed that current applicants

would be exempt.

Supervisor Palow made a motion seconded by Councilmember Doolin to close the public

hearing, Supervisor Palow, Councilmembers Doolin, laCour, Giebelhaus all voted aye.

accepted, nor permits, certificates of occupancy or approvals issued, which would

authorize development within the Town for land uses relating to solar energy and/or

BESS as described above.

SECTION 4. TERM.

This moratorium shall be in effect for a period of twelve (12) consecutive months

from its effective date.

This Local Law shall be subject to renewal for a cumulative period of up to an

additional Twelve (12) months, if necessary, by Resolution(s) of the Town Board.

SECTION 5. EFFECT ON OTHER LAWS.

To the extent that any law, ordinance, rule or regulation, or parts thereof, are in

conflict with the provisions of this Local Law, including all provisions of Article 16 of the

New York State Town Law concerning special use permit, site plan, building permit and

certificate of occupancy procedure and requirements, this Local Law shall control and

supersede such law ordinance, rule or regulation.

SECTION 6. WAIVER.

Owing to the limited scope and duration of this moratorium, there is no provision

being made in this Local Law for any waivers to its applicability. However, the Town

Board may, but is not obligated to, promulgate regulations by a Resolution of the Board

authorizing a hardship waiver process to this moratorium.
