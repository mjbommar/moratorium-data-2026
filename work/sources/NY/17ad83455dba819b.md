**TOWN OF ORANGETOWN**

**LOCAL LAW NO. \_5\_\_ OF THE YEAR 2026**

**IMPOSING A TEMPORARY TOWN-WIDE MORATORIUM ON THE APPROVAL, CONSTRUCTION, AND INSTALLATION OF BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEMS AND DATA STORAGE FACILITIES FOR A PERIOD OF ONE-HUNDRED EIGHTY (180) DAYS**

BE IT ENACTED by the Town Board of the Town of Orangetown as follows:

**Adoption of a new Article XX, of Chapter 43 (Zoning), of the Code of the Town of Orangetown, to**

**be entitled “Temporary Town-Wide Moratorium – Battery Storage Systems and Data Storage**

**Facilities”.**

SECTION 1.

* 20-1. Title

This local law shall be known and cited as the Town of Orangetown Local Law No. \_5\_ of 2026 entitled “Temporary Town-Wide Moratorium-Battery Energy Storage Systems and Data Storage Facilities (Data Centers)”.

SECTION 2.

* 20-2. Purpose and Intent.

The Town of Orangetown adopted a new Comprehensive Plan in 2023 which identified the need to better define and regulate land uses within the Town to modernize the code to reflect new uses and changes to the interpretation of certain existing uses, not previously contemplated. The purpose of this local law is to protect the public health, safety and welfare of the residents of the Town of Orangetown by temporarily suspending the processing of land use approvals and the granting of approvals for certain battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities for a period of 180 days from the effective date of this local law while the Town Board studies whether amendments to the Zoning Code are necessary regarding battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities.

The Town Board wishes to review the current zoning laws related to data storage facilities and battery energy storage facilities and provisions as currently found in the Town of Orangetown Zoning Code to clarify applicability requirements; to eliminate any inconsistencies in its Zoning Code; and determine whether to add any standards and requirements relating to siting, noise, fire, energy use, and otherwise to further the objectives of the Town of Orangetown Comprehensive Plan.

The Town of Orangetown has residential neighborhoods in proximity to traditional industrial areas as well as commercial corridors, where battery energy storage systems and data centers have sought to locate within the Town. The placement of battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities in too close proximity to residential areas raises issues of concern for the health, safety and welfare of Town residents.

Battery Energy Storage Systems

The Town of Orangetown acknowledges that the battery energy storage systems play an important role in reducing demand and costs associated with power grid infrastructure, add capacity while lessening the burden on existing infrastructure, and reduce emissions, putting the Town closer to its sustainability goals identified in the Comprehensive Plan. While the Town Board remains dedicated to those goals, significant public health and safety concerns relative to the potentially volatile nature of lithium-ion batteries and battery storage operations have emerged, especially with regard to locating mega-watt scale facilities in close proximity to established residential areas. Indeed, several fires at battery energy storage facilities across the State of New York have significantly raised concerns by the Town Board and the emergency and first responders, who must manage these occurrences and ensure continued public safety. The Town Board has therefore decided that it is necessary to review the Zoning Code and consider potential revisions to address the safety concerns raised by battery energy storage systems.

The Town Board intends to study the safety and security of these energy storage systems, including thermal runaway, off gassing and toxicity, stranded energy, ways to prevent fires, prevent by-product contamination, and ensure that emergency responders have the necessary training and information to prepare and deploy resources in the event of a fire. The Town Board also intends to study in which areas of the Town it may be appropriate to locate such systems.

Data Storage Facilities

The Town of Orangetown acknowledges that data storage facilities play an important role in supporting the digital economy, cloud computing services, telecommunications infrastructure, artificial intelligence applications, and other critical technology functions that benefit residents, businesses, and government agencies. These facilities can also contribute to economic development through capital investment, job creation, and enhancement of the Town’s technology infrastructure. While the Town Board recognizes the benefits associated with data storage facilities and technological innovation, significant public health, safety, environmental, and community character concerns have emerged, particularly with regard to the siting of large-scale facilities in proximity to established residential neighborhoods in the Town. Such concerns include, but are not limited to, noise generated by cooling and backup power systems, air emissions associated with emergency generators, visual impacts of large industrial-style buildings, increased energy and water consumption, traffic associated with construction and operations, and the potential strain on local utility infrastructure. The increasing size and operational demands of modern data centers have raised concerns among the Town Board, residents, and emergency service providers regarding their compatibility with surrounding land uses and their long-term impacts on the community.

The Town Board has therefore determined that it is necessary to review the Zoning Code and consider potential revisions to address the land use, environmental, infrastructure, and public safety concerns associated with data storage facilities.

The Town Board intends to study the operational characteristics of data storage facilities, including energy consumption, water use, backup power generation, noise generation, air quality impacts, sustainability measures, utility infrastructure requirements, and potential effects on neighboring properties and community character. The Town Board also intends to evaluate the extent to which such facilities are compatible with surrounding land uses and to determine in which areas of the Town it may be appropriate to locate these facilities while protecting the health, safety, and welfare of Town residents.

SECTION 3.

* 20-3. Moratorium Provisions.

1. The Town Board hereby finds that pending the completion of the necessary review, studies, meetings, hearings, environmental review and other actions necessary and incident to the proper consideration and adoption of local law amendments to the Town of Orangetown Zoning Code regulating the installation of battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities within the Town, reasonable measures must be taken to maintain the status quo in order to protect the public interest and ensure that proper regulation of the installation and approval of battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities.
2. During the effective period of this Local Law, the Town Board, the Planning Board, the Zoning Board of Appeals, and the Office of Building Planning Zoning Administration and Enforcement (OBZPAE) shall not accept, process, interpret, deliberate upon, decide, approve or issue any permits or certificates of occupancy for any application for the installation of battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities, except as set forth below.
3. The moratorium shall apply to all applications, including for building permits or certificates of occupancy, for the installation of such battery energy storage systems and data storage facilities. Any applications submitted to the Town of Orangetown for data storage facilities prior to July 1, 2026, may proceed with the approval process including but not limited to for a building permit and/or certificate of occupancy.

SECTION 4.

* 20-4. Hardship.

1. Should any owner of property affected by this Local Law suffer any unnecessary hardship in the way of carrying out the strict letter of this Local Law, then the owner of said property may apply to the Town Board of the Town of Orangetown in writing for a waiver from strict compliance with this Local Law. The submission shall include proof of such unnecessary hardship. For the purposes of this Local Law, unnecessary hardship shall not be the mere delay in being permitted to make an application or waiting for a decision on the application for a special permit, site plan, subdivision, variance or other permit during the period of the moratorium imposed by this Local Law.
2. Procedure.
3. Upon submission of a written application to the Town Clerk by the property owner seeking a waiver from this Local Law, the Town Board shall, within thirty (30) days of receipt of said application, schedule a public hearing on said application upon five (5) days’ written notice in the official newspaper of the Town. Notice of said hearing by regular mail, shall be provided to abutting property owners at the address shown on the tax rolls.
4. At said public hearing, the property owners and any other parties wishing to present evidence with regard to the application shall have an opportunity to be heard, and the Town Board shall, within fifteen (15) days of the close of said public hearing, render its decision either granting or denying the application for variation from the strict requirements of this Local Law3. If the Town Board determines that a property owner will suffer an unnecessary hardship if the Local Law is strictly applied to a particular property, then the Town Board shall waive the application to this Local Law to the minimum extent necessary to provide the property owner relief from strict compliance with this Local Law.

SECTION 5.

* 20-5. Penalties for Offenses.

Any person, firm, entity or corporation that shall construct, erect, enlarge or alter any building or structure, in violation of the provisions of this Local Law or shall otherwise violate any of the provisions of this Local Law shall be guilty of a violation and subject to a fine of not less than $500.00 or more than $7,500.00. Each day that the violation continues shall be a separate offense.

SECTION 6.

* 20-6. Conflicting laws superseded.

All local laws, ordinances, or parts of local laws and ordinances, of the Town of Orangetown that are in conflict with the provisions of this Local Law are hereby suspended to the extent necessary to give this Local Law full force and effect during the effective period of the moratorium.

SECTION 7.

* 20-7. Authorization for adoption.

This Local Law is hereby adopted pursuant to Municipal Home Rule Law §10(l)(i) and §10(l)(ii)(14) and Statute of Local Governments §10(6) of the State of New York.

SECTION 8.

* 20.8. Severability,

The provisions of this Local Law are separate and if any provisions, clause, sentence, subsection, word or part thereof is held illegal, invalid or unconstitutional, or inapplicable to any person or circumstance, such illegality, invalidity or unconstitutionality, or inapplicability, shall not affect or impair any of the remaining provisions, clauses, sentences, subsections, words or parts of this Local Law or their application to other persons or circumstances. It is hereby declared to be the legislative intent to the Town Board of the Town of Orangetown that this Local Law would have been adopted if such illegal, invalid or unconstitutional provision, clause, sentence, subsection, word or part had not been included therein, and if such person or circumstance to which the Local Law or part thereof is held inapplicable had been specifically exempted therefrom.

SECTION 9.

* **20.9**. **Term**.

This Local Law shall remain in force for a period of one-hundred eighty (180) days from its effective date. This Local Law is subject to review and renewal by the Town Board of the Town of Orangetown for an additional one-hundred and eighty (180) day extension by Town Board resolution upon a finding by the Town Board of a need for such extension.

SECTION 10.

* **20-10. Effective date.**

This Local Law shall take effect upon its adoption and filing with the Secretary of State in accordance with the provision of §27 of the Municipal Home Rule Law.
