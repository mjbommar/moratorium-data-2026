STAFF ANALYSIS

As noted in the past by the Suffolk County Planning Commission, a moratorium on development

is a local law or ordinance that suspends the right of property owners to obtain development

approvals while the community takes time to consider, draft or adopt land use plans or rules to

respond to new or changing circumstances not adequately dealt with by its current law. A

moratorium preserves the status quo for a reasonable time. A moratorium is, from one

perspective, the most extreme land use action that a municipality can take because it suspends

completely the rights of owners to use their property. Such development moratoria may be general

or specific. A general imposes a ban on all development within a community (allowing for certain

hardship exemptions), while a specific moratorium may prevent development approvals in a

specific geographic area or of a certain type of development.

Staff find that this proposed moratorium is intended to be specific and should not restrict all

development on any or all parcels within the Town; rather, it is understood that the proposed

moratorium is limited to AI data centers only, and for a period of about 18 months.

Staff notes that the Town is proposing the moratorium extension to allow time to review its zoning

code and develop regulations to adequately address the unique issues present with data center

development. The below is from the stated purpose of the local law:

In order to address development of Data Centers within the Town of Brookhaven, the

Town Board heretofore directed the Commissioner of Planning, Environment and Land

Management to undertake in the development of a comprehensive zoning ordinance as

to form spspecific rules for the location and installation of Data Centers in an effort to guide

development of these facilities. The scale, operational characteristics, and infrastructure

requirements distinguish Data Centers from typical commercial and industrial uses. It is in

the public interest to establish comprehensive land use regulations that will serve to

protect the Town’s natural resources; ensure compatibility with surrounding land uses;

promote sustainable and resilient development practices; mitigate potential impacts

related to noise, heat, visual character, water and energy consumption; consider

opportunities for energy efficiency, renewable energy integration, and resource

management; and promote safe, secure, and efficient facility operation. ThThere is a need

for further development of the issue of the impacts of Data Centers, and the Town must

take reasonable measures, temporarily, to protect the public interest and welfare until a

comprehensive zoning ordinance is adopted.

In New York State as of May 2026, there were nearly 12 gigawatts of data center load requests;

about two-thirds (8 gigawatts) were requested in 2025. In the Town of Brookhaven, there is

currently a proposal for a data center to occupy three vacant warehouses in Yaphank; a total of

549,000 square feet on 71 acres.

Proponents of data centers cite the need for these facilities to support the data needs of emerging

technologies, such as artificial intelligence requiring large amounts of data storage. These

facilities are argued to encourage grid upgrades through new investments as businesses build

data centers. In the short term, they can support construction jobs. Long term jobs are potentially

well-paying tech roles.

Opponents of data centers cite many of the issues above such as water and power usage. Noise

from cooling fans and other mechanical components, as well as potential pollution from water

usage are also cited as issues. The promise of high-tech jobs may also be overstated.
