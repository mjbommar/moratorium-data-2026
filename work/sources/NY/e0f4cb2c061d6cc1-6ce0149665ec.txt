Resolution:

A motion was made by M. Robbins and seconded by P. Goodman to adopt a resolution for a

proposed local law placing a moratorium on all types of data centers.

Page 2

T. Wood inquired about a timeline.

Attorney Gailey responded: Steps are to prepare a moratorium local law, then a public hearing,

and then adoption. Moratorium is typically 12-18 months, but can be extended.

• Public Comment (Data Centers):

M. Vrydaghs: Supports a moratorium.

Suggested to the Board to act quickly to do as soon as possible.

Protect the rural community character.

Plan for legal challenges, loopholes, and workarounds from large corporations, including

temporary facilities.

T. Jackson-Seitel: Supports a moratorium.

Suggests the Board to protect the rural character.

Require a site-specific independent study.

Examine potential impacts to groundwater, wells, air quality, noise and environmental and

community effects.

F. Coviello:  Supports a moratorium.

Requested the Board oppose hyperscale data centers because of substantial water and energy

demands.

Potentially consider smaller enterprises regulated based on its size, physical footprint and

energy consumption.

Prevent clustering of smaller facilities that could combine into a large operation.

L. Otto (President of the BKAA):  Supports a moratorium.

Supports enterprise data centers serving businesses such as banks and medical facilities, noting

their lower energy use and use of air cooling rather than water.

Asked the Town to consider permanently prohibiting larger data centers that exceed specified

electricity or water-cooling thresholds.

Suggests temporary moratorium on micro/enterprise centers until zoning regulations can

establish limits and prevent expansion, clustering, or linking.

Page 3

• Public Comment (Data Centers) Continued:

L. Snitzer (Treasurer of the Bashakill Area Association): Supports a moratorium

Hopes that a moratorium will ultimately lead to a permanent ban.

Concerned with Mamakating’s irreplaceable resources such as the forests, wetlands,

groundwater, aquifers, and the Bashakill.

Data centers can require enormous amounts of electricity and water, create ongoing noise and

light pollution, require significant infrastructure, and occupy large amounts of land.

Questioned the economic benefit and job creation of data centers compared with their

potential impacts.

J. Pape-Laucella: Supports a moratorium

Summarized a recent article from the NY Times in regards to diesel spillage into the

Hackensack River, emphasizing the potential environmental consequences of similar

incidents locally.

Proposes that the Town Board ban data centers.

Urged the Community to come together to protect Mamakating’s environment and

beauty for future generations.

M. Zimmerman:  Supports a moratorium

Spoke of a lawsuit currently in Memphis Tennessee and Missisippi concerning alleged

health and environmental impacts associated with data centers.

Expressed concerns about potential contamination of land, air, and water.

Raised concerns about the fire and safety risks of large battery storage systems, referencing the

use of hazmat crews to handle damaged Tesla batteries after a Los Angeles fire.

W. Pishtey:  Supports a moratorium

Supports a pause for research and planning, not an outright ban.

Believes the Town should determine whether different types and sizes of data centers

can be responsibly regulated.

Recommends studying water use, aquifer protection, and electrical-grid impacts, including

closed-loop cooling and power limits.

Supports setting size or power thresholds and adopting regulations based on the findings.

If impacts cannot be adequately mitigated, a ban could then be considered.

Page 4

• Public Comment (Data Centers) Continued:

M. Monahan:  Supports a moratorium

Cited reported impacts near a large Meta data center in Louisiana, including noise, higher utility

and energy costs, and water advisories.

Concern that large corporations may leave communities with financial, environmental, and

health burdens.

C. Longo-Arment:

Urges the Town Board to be very cautious about potential legal loopholes.

Warns that large corporations with experienced attorneys may find ways around regulations.

A. Poitras (Town of Thompson resident): Supports a moratorium

Requested the Town to take as much time as needed to carefully evaluate the issue.

Wants the policy to be thorough and resistant to loopholes that could allow harmful

development.

Believes the area’s natural beauty and community character should be protected.

Doesn’t believe that large data centers would provide enough economic benefit to average

residents to justify their potential impacts.

Encourages residents to raise similar concerns with other municipalities throughout Sullivan

County and New York State, as the issue extends beyond Mamakating.

D. Diorio: Supports a moratorium

Opposes data centers due to concerns about noise, light, water, wildlife, property values, and

environmental impacts.

Warns that impacts may be difficult to regulate once facilities are established.

Wants to preserve Mamakating’s natural beauty and community character rather than risk

overdevelopment.

B. Morthsen: Opposes Data Centers

Cited concerns about water quality, noise, higher utility costs, and affordability.

Worried that environmental damage, particularly to water resources, could be difficult or

impossible to reverse.

Urged the Town Board to thoroughly research the issue and reject data-center development,

arguing that financial incentives should not outweigh potential community impacts.

Page 5

• Public Comment (Data Centers) Continued:

C. Roth: Supports a permanent moratorium

Cited a reported 5,000-gallon diesel spill in New Jersey as an example of potential

environmental risks.

Stated data-center policy should align with Mamakating’s master plan and focus on ecotourism.

Expressed concern that other development and infrastructure decisions could harm rivers, land,

scenery, and natural resources (examples: Bloomingburg and Phillipsport).

Urged the Town to prioritize protecting Mamakating’s environment and community character

to support its long-term ecotourism goals.

N. Bartels:

Spoke on behalf of the Bashakill preserve and wildlife, urging the Town to consider ecological

impacts before approving data centers.

Warned that 24/7 industrial noise could disrupt wildlife by masking communication, increasing

chronic stress, and interfering with nocturnal species.

Asked the Board to review environmental and noise studies from other municipalities and

develop proactive wildlife and ambient-noise protections, including strict decibel limits.

Offered a municipal precedent and policy framework memorandum to help the Board prepare

regulations.

RESOLUTION OF THE TOWN BOARD OF

THE TOWN OF MAMAKATING

IN SUPPORT OF THE NEW YORK

ASSOCIATION OF TOWNS BILL OF RIGHTS

WHEREAS, town government provides services essential to the vitality of New York State,

including but not limited to maintaining and repairing roads, protecting neighborhoods and

businesses, guiding economic development, and serving as the first line of defense,

communication, and safety for residents every day of the year; and

Page 9

WHEREAS, the 933 towns of New York State are home to more than 9.1 million New Yorkers and

are diverse in population, geography, and demographics, such decisions affecting daily life are

best made at the local level by the government that best understands each community’s

character, challenges, and needs; and

WHEREAS, the towns of New York are partners in shaping the State’s future, and a true

partnership between the State and its local governments will propel New York State forward to

the benefit of all New Yorkers; and

WHEREAS, the New York Association of Towns has adopted a Bill of Rights articulating the core

principles that should govern the relationship between the State and its towns; and

WHEREAS, the Town of Mamakating shares and affirms those principles, namely:

1. Protection and defense of municipal home rule, recognizing that towns, as the level

of government closest to the people, are best positioned to balance competing

interests and make decisions that shape daily life;

2. Local control of land use and zoning, free from statewide zoning rules that override

the voice of resident taxpayers and the officials they elect;

3. Freedom from unfunded mandates, so that local taxpayers are not made to bear the

cost of orders imposed by State and Federal authorities without the resources to carry

them out;

4. Modernized transparency, accessibility, and engagement, including reform of

outdated public-notice requirements that consume scarce municipal dollars on print

notices often missed by the public; and

5. A meaningful voice in State decisions affecting the hometowns that towns govern,

so that town government is treated as a true partner rather than an afterthought or

a hurdle to clear; now, therefore, be it

RESOLVED, that the Town Board of the Town of Mamakating hereby endorses and adopts the

New York Association of Towns Bill of Rights and affirms its commitment to the principles set

forth therein; and be it further

RESOLVED, that the Town Board calls upon the Governor of the New York State Legislature to

respect and uphold these principles in the enactment of legislation and the administration of

State programs affecting towns; and be it further

RESOLVED, that the Town Clerk is hereby directed to transmit certified copies of this resolution

to the Governor of the State of New York, the Temporary President of the Senate, the Speaker of

the Assembly, State Senator Peter Oberacker, Assembly Member Paula Elaine Kay, and the New

York Association of Towns.
