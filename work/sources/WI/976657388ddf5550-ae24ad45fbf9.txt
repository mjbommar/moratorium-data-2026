WHEREAS, a “Data Center” is one or more facilities that house computing and networking

equipment, along with storage and management systems, to support the storage, processing, retrieval, and

distribution of digital data and applications; and

WHEREAS, data centers typically feature high-performance servers, storage arrays, networking

equipment, cooling systems, and power backup solutions to ensure uninterrupted operations; and

WHEREAS, small to medium enterprise data centers typically range from server rooms and small

facilities to mid-sized data centers, often occupying less than 10,000 square feet or up to approximately

25,000 square feet, with power requirements commonly below 20 megawatts and frequently in the range of

less than 5 megawatts for smaller facilities, and may house from a few dozen to several hundred servers

depending on organizational needs; and

WHEREAS, small to medium enterprise data centers are commonly operated by corporations,

healthcare institutions, financial institutions, and other organizations to support internal information

technology operations, including hosting proprietary data, business applications, and systems designed for

redundancy, backup, and continuity of operations; and

WHEREAS, such small to medium enterprise data center uses are consistent with existing

operations by institutions such as corporations, hospitals, and financial organizations in communities like

the Village of Ashwaubenon; and

WHEREAS, hyperscale data centers are large-scale facilities designed to support cloud computing

and data-intensive applications, typically housing thousands to tens of thousands of servers and requiring

substantial electrical capacity often measured in tens of megawatts, with many facilities operating in the

range of 20 megawatts or more; and

WHEREAS, such hyperscale facilities are commonly developed in large buildings or campuses

that may exceed 100,000 square feet and can extend to significantly larger footprints as operations scale;

and

WHEREAS, the Village of Ashwaubenon Planning and Zoning Code does not contain language

addressing the location, construction, and operation of hyperscale data centers; and

WHEREAS, the Village of Ashwaubenon has concerns about potential health and safety issues

related to the construction and operation of hyperscale data centers, including electrical connections,

electrical and magnetic fields, impacts on air quality and water resources, water use, electricity demand, and

fire hazards; and

WHEREAS, the Village of Ashwaubenon is concerned that the construction and operation of

hyperscale data centers will result in an overburdening of public services and infrastructure in the Village

of Ashwaubenon, including electrical and water utilities, village roads and highways, and fire and

emergency services; and

WHEREAS, the Village of Ashwaubenon has concerns about the potential secondary impacts of

the construction and operation of hyperscale data centers, including impacts on property values, noise and

visual impacts, and impacts on the public health, safety and general welfare of residents, businesses, and

visitors; and

WHEREAS, the construction and operation of hyperscale data centers may not be consistent with

existing land use p plans and ordinances including the V Village of Ashwaubenon Comprehensive Plan; and

WHEREAS, pursuant to Wis. Stat. § 61.34 (1), 61.35, and 62.23(7) the Village of Ashwaubenon

Board of Trustees may enact a moratorium that is not a “development moratorium” as that term is defined

in Wis. Stat. § 66.1002(1)(b); and

WHEREAS, a moratorium would allow the Village to fully explore, analyze, and research the

environmental, economic, health, and safety impacts of data centers and develop consistent zoning and

regulatory standards to be applied to h hyperscale data centers;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE VILLAGE BOARD OF THE VILLAGE

OF ASHWAUBENON:

SECTION 1. Chapter 17, A Article 10, Hyperscale Data Center Moratorium, is hereby created to read:

17-10-100: Moratorium

(a) For the purpose of this section, “Data Center” means one or more facilities that house

computing and networking equipment, along with storage and management systems, to

support the storage, processing, retrieval, and distribution of digital data and applications.

(b) For the purpose of this section, “Hyperscale Data Center” means a data center, including

campus-style developments with multiple buildings, that has either a total c combined f floor

area of 100,000 square feet or more; or a planned or installed electrical capacity of 20

megawatts or greater.

(c) Pursuant to Wis. Stat. § 61.34 (1), 61.35, and 62.23(7) the Village of Ashwaubenon enacts

the following moratorium: Notwithstanding any other provision in this Chapter, there

shall be a moratorium on the receipt of applications and the granting of all permrmits or

approvals for the placement or construction of hyperscale data centers in the Village of

Page 2 of 4

Ashwaubenon. The purpose of the moratorium is to allow the Village of Ashwaubenon

time to research the possible environmental, economic, health, and safety impacts of

hyperscale data centers and develop consistent zoning and regulatory standards to be

applied to data centers in the Village of Ashwaubenon and to allow the Village Plan

Commission to conduct hearings and make recommendations to the Village Board

regarding amendments t to this Chapter or other Village Ordinances. The moratorium shall

be in effect for a period of 12 months from the date of this ordinance is published or until

the Village Board adopts amendments to this ordinance or rescinds this moratorium, or

both. The moratorium enacted under 17-10-100 is not intended to be, and does not,

constitute a “development moratorium” as that term is defined in Wis. Stat.

§66.1002(1)(b);

SECTION 2. Effective date. This ordinance shall take effect upon publication.
