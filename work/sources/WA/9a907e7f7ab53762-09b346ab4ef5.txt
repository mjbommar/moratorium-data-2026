Executive Summary


Issue Title: Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) Briefing


Meeting Date: 10/20/2026 Time Required: 45 minutes


Attendees: Jim Rogers, Long Range Planning Supervisor


Action Requested at this Meeting:  No action; briefing only


Background:


This briefing is meant to update the Planning Commission on the status of Battery Energy

Storage System (BESS) code development. We will discuss what BESS systems can look

like, the status of the moratorium, important issues to address, experts we have met with,

significant issues to address, what’s next and the remaining schedule for code adoption.

What is a BESS?

A BESS is a facility that stores electricity in rechargeable batteries—currently lithium-ion is

favored—for use when energy demand is high or electricity generation is low. BESS

facilities help stabilize the electrical grid, support renewable energy sources such as solar

and wind, and provide backup power during outages. These systems range from small

installations serving individual buildings to large, utility-scale facilities containing multiple

battery units, inverters, transformers, and associated safety equipment.  Larger utility

scale systems are often sited near utility substations for ease of development and transfer

of power, and lower costs and line maintenance.

The moratorium

DCD received an application for a 170 Megawatt BESS in north Kitsap in mid-2024. The

application prompted a moratorium, with one extension granted, and another being

sought:

• A one-year moratorium was established on June 11, 2025;

• a six-month extension to the moratorium was approved on May 11, 2026; and

• a second six-month extension to the moratorium is being requested by DCD which puts

the final date by which a BESS ordinance must be adopted at June 5, 2027.

Key issues

There several issues to consider when allowing any BESS to be sited:

• Definitions that separate BESS facilities into different classes; standardized approach

and conditions for different class-sizes of BESS.

• Compliance with “outside” code requirements, eg, Underwriters Laboratories,

National Fire Protection Association.

• Compatibility with the surrounding neighborhood and consistency with the intent of the

zone being used, eg, noise, lights, viewshed impacts.

• Hazard mitigation and emergency response to and training for BESS fires.  Code must

consider environmental consequences of containing and eliminating fire, eg, plume

toxicity, water-introduced toxins.

• Conditional Use Permit standards and conditions.

• Decommissioning guarantees and project bonding.

Experts counseled to date

DCD has met with several industry experts including:

• Puget Sound Energy staff, including Chris Green a renowned fire and life safety expert.

• Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, who has advised on narrow issues and zoning

controls.

• Sandia National Laboratories (US Department of Energy), a major federal research

authority on BESS, particularly on safety, reliability, and performance.

• The Electric Power Research Institute, an independent research organization that

conducts research on electricity generation, transmission, distribution, and energy

storage. It is one of the leading organizations researching BESS safety and failures.

• Industry guidance from WA Dept of Commerce, National Association of Counties

(NACo), and Municipal Research and Services Center (MRSC).

• Green energy advocates such as Greenlight America, Clean and Prosperous, and

Renewable NW.

• Several counties and cities, largely to understand approach, concerns, and zoning

controls.

• A site visit to Seaside BESS facility in North Portland, OR that has a power capacity of

200 MW.

Major issues for the PC to consider

The more significant challenges for the County will largely rest in two areas. While DCD

feels that it can treat many of the issues identified above in zoning code, there are

challenges:

• Where should the different sizes of BESS facilities be allowed and in which zones?

• What is the permissibility of different classes of BESS? We will likely have 4 classes of

uses, with an accessory use being permitted outright (eg, Telsa Powerwall residential

storage) to a Type III Conditional Use Permit before the Hearing Examiner (eg, 200 MW

or larger).

DCD will provide recommendations and zoning documents to assist the Planning

Commission in those discussions.

What’s next?

DCD needs to start packaging a draft BESS ordinance for the Planning Commission (eg,

zones, standards and conditions, and permissibility). DCD will be conducting outreach for

the County, perhaps as early as November. DCD will be asking experts to attend meetings

where they can, since many of the issues are deeply technical and beyond typical staff

knowledge.
