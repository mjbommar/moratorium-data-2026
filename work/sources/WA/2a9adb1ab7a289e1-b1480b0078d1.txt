CHAPTER 17.28 DATA CENTERS


17.28.8.010 Purpose and scope.


The purpose of this chapter is to establish standards for the location, development, construction, expansion, and

operation of data centers within the City of Port Angeles.

These regulations are intended to:

A. Support responsible technological investment, economic development, and the continued operation of

businesses requiring digital infrastructure.

B. Establish a regulatory framework that distinguishes small accessory information technology facilities from

commercial and industrial-scale data centers.

C. Ensure that data center development is appropriately located and compatible with surrounding land uses,

infrastructure capacity, and the City's Comprehensive Plan.

D. Protect the reliability, affordability, and long-term sustainability of municipal electrical, water,

wastewater, stormwater, and transportation infrastructure.

E. Ensure that costs attributable e to data center development, including necessary infrastructure

improvements, utility capacity expansions, and ongoing utility service, are borne by the applicant t and not

shifted to existing utility customers to the extent authorized by law.

F. Minimize impacts associated with water consumption, electrical demand, noise, air emissions, emergency

power generation, stormwater runoff, and other environmental considerations.

G. Establish predictable development standards and meaningful public review for data centers of a scale that

may substantially affect the community.

H. Preserve the City's ability to evaluate cumulative impacts and deny or condition development proposals

that cannot adequately mitigate identified impacts.

Permits issued under this ChChapter are subject to the requirements of PAMC 18.02, Administration of Project Permit

Applications and Review Procedures.

4. Potential impacts on system reliability, capacity, power quality, and existing utility customers.

5. Estimated capital costs, ongoing operating expenses, maintenance costs, and potential impacts on

the City's electrical utility rates and power supply obligations.

6. Anticipated impacts on the City's Bonneville Power Administration (BPA) Tier 1 power allocation and

potential requirements for Tier 2 or other higher-cost power purchases.

7. Proposed strategies for reducing peak electrical demand and improving energy efficiency.

1. The applicant, owner, or operator of a Class B B or Class C C data center will  be responsible for the full

2. Such improvements include, but are not limited to, substations, transformers, electrical transmission

and distribution lines, poles, conduits, switching equipment, protective devices, metering equipment,

system controls, and other necessary infrastructure.

3. The applicant's financial responsibility will  extend to all required on-site and off-site infrastructure

improvements, including improvements located outside the development property or required

elsewhere within the City's electrical utility system as a result of the development.

4. The applicant t will also cover all costs for studies or infrastructure required to procure and/or transmit

required additional power and auxiliary services to meet power supply and transmission contractual

5. Required improvements must t be designed, constructed, inspected, and accepted in accordance with

6. No development is s entitled to electrical service until all required infrastructure improvements have

7. The City is not t obligated to finance, construct, or subsidize infrastructure improvements necessary to

accommodate a Class B B or Class C C data center, except as otherwise expressly authorized by applicable

law.

C. Protection of Existing Utility Customers.

1. The development, operation, and expansion of a Class B B or Class C C data center will  not result in

project-attributable electrical infrastructure costs, increased wholesale power procurement costs, or

other identifiable incremental utility costs being shifted to existing residential, commercial, or

industrial utility customers, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law.

2. The applicant, owner, or operator is responsible for all additional electrical utility costs directly

attributable to the development, including increased power procurement costs, infrastructure

improvements, system capacity requirements, and other identifiable expenses associated with

serving the facility.

3. This responsibility will remain in effect throughout the operational life of the data center and will not

terminate upon completion of construction, issuance of a certificate of occupancy, transfer of

property ownership, or commencement of operations.

4. Where the City determines through a documented cost-of-service analysis, applicable power supply

agreement, or other reasonable utility methodology that the operation of a data center has resulted

in increased electrical utility costs, the applicant, owner, or operator will  pay such attributable costs

through applicable rates, charges, or utility service agreements.

5. The City may require periodic reassessment of project-attributable costs and corresponding

adjustments to utility rates, charges, or contractual payment obligations.

17.28.170 Administration and Enforcement


.

A. The Community and Economic Development Director, or designee, will  administer this chapter in

coordination with the City's utility departments, Fire Marshal, and other applicable reviewing agencies.

B. No building permit, certificate of occupancy, or authorization for utility service will  be issued for a Class

enforcement and penalties under PAMC 2.90.

D. The City may pursue corrective action, penalties, or other remedies authorized by law for failure to

comply with approved development or operational requirements.

E. Nothing in this chapter limits ts s the City's authority to enforce applicable building, fire, utility,

environmental, or public safety regulations.

established in PAMC 17.28.060 will  require immediate action by the responsible applicant, owner, or

operator to protect the City's electrical utility system and restore compliance. Where immediate

permanent correction is not practicable, the responsible party will  implement interim protective

measures approved by the City's electrical utility and complete permanent corrective action within the

timeframe established by the City.

G. The applicant, owner, or operator is s responsible for all lawfully imposed fees, penalties, additional

power procurement charges, testing expenses, infrastructure repair costs, and other recoverable

expenses directly attributable to a violation, including applicable charges assessed against the City by

BPA or another power supplier.

operational restrictions, suspension of electrical service where authorized by law, or other remedies

available under the Port Angeles Municipal Code and applicable state law.
