# Pierce County Council Passes Emergency Ordinance Prohibiting New, Expanded Data Centers

source: https://www.piercecountywa.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/7241
fetched: 2026-09-23T12:33:09+00:00

County Council Posted on August 26, 2026 | Last Updated on September 03, 2026

The Pierce County Council passed an emergency ordinance on Tuesday, Aug. 25, that prohibits filing, processing, or approval of permits for new and expanded data centers exceeding 10 megawatts in unincorporated Pierce County for up to one year. A public hearing on the ordinance is scheduled for Tuesday, Oct. 20.

Emergency ordinances require a supermajority, or five of seven votes, to pass. [Emergency Ordinance No. 2026-577](https://online.co.pierce.wa.us/cfapps/council/iview/proposal.cfm?proposal_num=O2026-557) passed by a 6-1 vote.

**What: Public hearing on an emergency ordinance to prohibit new and expanded data centers in Unincorporated Pierce County.
When:** Tuesday, Oct. 20, 2026, at 3 p.m.
**Where:** Pierce County Council Chambers, 1501 Market Street, Suite 140, Tacoma, WA 98402.

You can also participate in the public hearing remotely through Zoom by [using this link](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/4516/Council-Meetings) or calling (253) 215-8782 and using Webinar ID: 976-6178-7423.

“Any type of development moratorium on private business is something to really take a hard look at. Some people would say that government doing that is exercising king-like authority,” said Council Vice Chair Paul Herrera (District 2). “I have concerns about having moratoriums on private business, but I'm willing to support this moratorium; the county is already working on policy, and a formal pause is something I can live with, allowing time for that policy to develop.”

There are currently no hyperscale data center applications for unincorporated Pierce County, and the Planning & Public Works Department is actively developing data center regulations.

The sole dissenting vote on the Council was Councilmember Dave Morell (District 1), citing Quincy, WA as a positive case study for data centers, sharing that the poverty rate was halved, the tax base grew from $6.13B to $54.2B assessed value, and arguing no genuine emergency exists to justify bypassing the normal legislative process.

“This emergency ordinance bypasses the normal legislative process, where we get to debate pros and cons with public comments through multiple meetings. I think this is an abuse of the emergency because there is no emergency. The world isn't going to melt down if we don't allow the current process to happen, which, as indicated by the planning director, is underway,” said Morell. “I would support an ordinance that went through the normal process, but I will not support a made-up emergency this way.”

The emergency moratorium takes effect once the Executive signs it and will remain in place for one year, unless extended or lifted by the Council. During this period, no new applications for data center development will be accepted, processed, or approved within Unincorporated Pierce County.

The Council emphasizes that this action is a temporary, precautionary measure intended to give county staff time to fully assess the impacts of large-scale data center growth on the county. A comprehensive review will continue during the moratorium.

You can find more information and participate in the review process at [Data Center Regulations | Pierce County, WA - Official Website](https://www.piercecountywa.gov/9112/Data-Center-Regulations).
