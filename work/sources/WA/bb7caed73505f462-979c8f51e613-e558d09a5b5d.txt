6. NEW BUSINESS


A. Approval of Ordinance – Interim Fee Waiver Program:

Mr. Sexton requested approval of Ordinance 3939, establishing a

temporary fee waiver for affordable housing. In January the City

received a Connecting Housing to Infrastructure Program (CHIP) grant

from the State of Washington in support of Community Action’s Kulshan

View development. This is a temporary program to meet the

requirements of grant, keep the Community Action project on track to

break ground in June, and allow the City to revisit the program  and

discuss waivers further later this year.

Councilmember Hudson moved to approve Ordinance 3939, authorizing an interim fee waiver program. Motion seconded by Councilmember Tercero. Motion passed 6-0


B. Approval of Resolution – T Transportation Benefit District (TBD):

Mr. Phillips requested approval of Resolution 1097, calling for a general

election ballot proposition for the renewal of the 0.2% sales and use tax

within the boundaries of the Mount Vernon Transportation Benefit

District.

The 0.2% TBD sales and use tax approved by voters in 2016 will expire

on April 1, 2027. With Council approval a measure to renew the tax for

an additional ten years will go on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

As part of the process, the City will put out public notice seeking

members to serve on committees both for and against the proposition.

Residents are invited to visit the Public Works website to review TBD

funded projects from 2017 to present.

Councilmember Tercero moved to approve Resolution 1097 continuing the Transportation Benefit District. Motion seconded by Councilmember Hudson. Motion passed 6-0

Number of Parking Spaces: MVMC 17.84.030(N) requires 1 parking space for every 250 sf of net

floor area. Net floor area for the purposes of calculating the number of parking spaces is defined

in MVMC 17.06.060 as the main areas of the building that are occupied. It does not include

accessory areas ordinarily used by the occupant such as restrooms, stairs, shafts, wall thickness,

corridors and mechanical rooms.

Using the definition for net floor area, the applicant has demonstrated that the proposed 26,612

s.f. building is reduced to 24,759 s.f., which when divided by 250 equals a requirement to create

99 off-street parking s stalls. The project proposesto provide a total of 123 off-street parking spaces,

Size of Parking Spaces:  MVMC 17.84.060 requires parking stalls arranged at 90-degrees to be a

minimum of 9 feet wide by 19 feet deep and shall have an adjacent access aisle not less than 60

inches. Where two adjacent spaces are provided, the access aisle may be shared between the two

spaces. Per MVMC 17.84.060, 25% of parking stalls may be compact, allowing a size reduction to

8 feet wide by 16 feet deep. With 99 parking stalls required, no less than 75 may be smaller than

the standard dimension stated above.

stalls. The site plan currently shows 76 standard stalls, each measuring 9 feet wide by 19 feet deep,

and 47 compact stalls, each measuring 8 feet wide by 19 feet deep. Therefore, the proposal

complies with the requirements MVMC 17.84.060. To ensure non-compact vehicles are not

parking in compact stalls staff has added a suggested condition requiring all compact stalls have

the word COMPACT painted on them.

accessible (ADA) parking spaces for outpatient medical care facilities classified as IBC Occupancy

Group I-1 or I-2. The proposed building is classified as a Business Occupancy (B) and must comply

with the number of accessible parking spaces specified in Table No. 1 of MVMC 17.84.065, the

Washington State Building Code (Chapter 11), RCW 19.27.550 and RCW 70.92. With a total of 123

parking spaces, the project is required to provide five accessible spaces. The proposal includes five

accessible spaces, and therefore complies with the accessible parking requirements.

research and technical assistance to develop a record and (through the record) recommendations

from City staff on proposed development regulations to mitigate potential impacts from BESS.

3. That the City needs to thoroughly evaluate the public safety impacts from BESS including

impacts resulting from a BESS failure. As noted by Matthew Paiss, a Pacific Northwest National

Laboratory (PNNL) battery safety expert, “A“As with any complex electrical appliance or piece of

equipment, failures occasionally occur…while this is rare, it must be considered in the planning

process.”1 Following is an illustrative list of potential impacts from BESS:

a. Thermal runaway, fire propagation, toxic off-gassing and explosion.

b. Safety of First Responders. First responders may need specialized training, equipment

and chemical-specific extinguishing agents to safely suppress large-scale lithium-ion

battery fires.

c. Contamination from toxic runoff, contaminated fire suppression water and potential air

quality impacts.

d. Zoning Considerations. Noise pollution and adverse odor and visual impacts.

or develop a battery energy storage system that is intended or likely to be used to primarily store and

supply electrical power to the electrical grid within the City.

COMPREHENSIVE PLAN GOALS, OBJECTIVES AND POLICIES


On September 14, 2016 the Mount Vernon City Council approved Ordinance 3690 adopting a revised and

updated Comprehensive Plan for the City consistent with  RCW 36.70A. The subject rezone has been found to

be consistent with the City’s Comprehensive Plan.

The following are Goals, Objectives and Policies that are specific to and support the requested redesignation:

Land Use Goal 1 is to enhance the quality of life found in the City of Mount Vernon as a place to work, live and

recreate. Objective 1.1 is to balance residential, commercial, industrial and public land uses within the City.

Policy 1.1.3 indicates that annually the City should provide adequate capacity for the City’s projected residential

growth and provide enough commercial/industrial areas within the City to balance residential growth. As these

three lots have remained undeveloped since platted in 2003 while the others were developed, a rezone to a

district that allows multifamily housing is an effort to rebalance capacity for residential growth.

“Goal LU-51: Enhance and improve the quality of multi-family living environments throughout the City that

provide areas that offer a larger range of housing options in the form of multi-family units.”

“Housing Goal 1: Achieve a mix of housing styles in Mount Vernon that are appealing and affordable to a

diversity of ages, incomes and cultural backgrounds.”

Additional housing options will be available with approval of the requested amendment. The area is

characterized by a mix of different residential uses and the proposal would allow for additional density in an

area already characterized by many housing types. Furthermore, the City Comprehensive Plan recognizes that

scattered multifamily use will exist within single family  areas. The amendment will allow the development of a

multi-family project in a mixed density neighborhood, thereby assisting in achieving a mix of housing styles.

“Objective LU-51-1 Ensure that development in the multi-family residential designations are designed to

provide quality homes and neighborhoods for residents and to mitigate impacts to existing neighborhoods as

new ones develop.”

“Objective HO-1.1 In City plans and zoning regulations,  accommodate a variety of housing types, attractive and

compatible in design, and available to all economic segments of the community.”

“Objective HO-1.2 Promote infill housing that is compatible with abutting housing styles and with the character

of the existing neighborhood.”

The City’s zoning standards (e.g., building height) and design standards will ensure that the future multi-family

development will be constructed with quality site and home design. Additionally, the abutting professional

office structures and their parking at similar scales as would be expected from a future multifamily project.

“Policy LU-51.1.6 New multi-family residential projects should demonstrate provisions for an environment that

contributes to a high quality of life for future  residents, regardless of income level.”

As a multi-family infill project subject to the City’s design standards, the amendments will provide for additional

housing options available to lower income levels.

“Policy HO-1.2.1 The City should encourage infill housing on vacant or underutilized parcels having adequate

The site is underutilized but has urban services. As a multi-family infill project subject to the City’s design

neighborhood.

SUBJECT:  2026 BUDGET AMENDMENT


RECOMMENDED ACTION:


A request for Council to approve an ordinance to amend the 2026 Adopted City Budget.

INTRODUCTION/BACKGROUND:


The 2026 Budget Amendment includes the following:

1. The Parks and Recreation Fund (F103) requires an increase of expenditures in the amount

of $672,562. The City entered into an agreement with Northwest Playground Equipment

for playground equipment for Hillcrest Park in the amount of $1,172,562. The 2026 budget

was approved for only $500,000 because there wasn’t a definite answer on how much

could be completed in 2026. The City will transfer $672,562 of 2026 General Fund (F001)

property tax revenues into the Parks and Recreation Fund to offset the additional expense.

This expenditure agrees with the use of the additional property tax created from the voter

approved levy lid lift which was approved in 2025.

2. The following Park’s Capital Funds need an increase in 2026 appropriations to carryover

budgeted 2025 funds for projects that did not get completed until 2026.

a. Parks Capital Projects Fund (F115) requires an increase in the amount of $20,000

for Bonnie Rae Park Improvements and $12,000 for playground development,

replacement and upgrades in various city parks.

b. Little Mountain Parks Fund (F107) requires an increase in the amount of $72,928

for the completion of the Little Mountain vault toilet, and $8,000 for new trail and

educational signs.

c. Park Impact Fees Fund (F311) requires an increase in the amount of $50,000 for

Bonnie Rae Park improvements.

3. The Fire Debt Service Fund (F210) requires an increase in budgeted appropriation in the

amount of $105,667 to make the debt service payments for the financing of the new

Ladder Truck. The Fire Capital Fund (F306) will need an increase in appropriation to

transfer $105,667 to the Fire Debt Service Fund to fund the payments.

4. The  Affordable Housing Sales & Use Tax Fund – HB1590 (F124) requires an increase in

budgeted appropriation in the amount of $343,441. In 2025 Skagit Community Action was

awarded an Affordable Housing Grant from the City in the amount of $500,000. This was

a 2025 budgeted expenditure. Their project was not fully completed in 2025 so this

amendment is carrying over $343,441 into 2026 for current year expenditures.

5. The City also requests the following 2026 Salary Schedule and position adjustments:

a. Fire – eliminate the Office Specialist position, grade nr18  - $4,394.65 - $5,783.05

b. Fire –  – eliminate the Nurse Educator position, grade nr29  - $7,516.33 - $9,890.98

c. Fire – create EMS Captain/Admin, $11,100.79  - $11,552.28

d. Police –  – Retitle Evidence Tech to Senior Evidence Tech – no change in salary range

e. Police –  – Retitle Records Specialist to Evidence Tech – no change in salary range

f. Police –  – Retitle Records Manager to Records Supervisor –  – no change in salary range

g. Police –  – Parking Enforcement Officer moved from Protec, $3,805.00 - $5,007.12 to

Police Non-Commissioned, $3,651.75 - $4,805.46.

h. Wastewater – Requesting an additional Seasonal Employee - $16,800

FINDINGS/CONCLUSIONS:


As these increases in appropriations were not  included in the adopted 2026 budget, these budget amendments are necessary to complete and properly record these transactions. All listed funds have available funding for these expenditures.

City staff is requesting Council to approve ordinance 3942 to amend the 2026 Adopted

The  City has  entered into  an agreement  with  Systems  Design West as the new  provider  for  EMS

billing  services.  Finance has  the responsibility to capture  individual transport  revenue  and

reconcile with our monthly  bank  reconciliation.  Systems  Design West suggested,  and the City

agrees,  that we  open  a  separate bank  account  to capture all these  individual payments.  This  will

simplify  reconciliations of these  electronic deposits.  This  new account  would be at the  City’s

current  bank, Columbia Bank.

RECOMMENDATION:

City  staff is  requesting  Council  approve resolution 1101 to create  a new bank  account with

Columbia  Bank  for EMS billing purposes.
