What We Do

Skagit County Planning and Development Services is made up of five teams (Permit Technicians, Planning, Fire, Building, and Code Compliance) which helps to protect the safety, character, and long-term health and viability of Skagit County’s communities, working lands and natural resources.

The Department’s five teams work with teams from the Public Health Department and Public Works Department to ensure development in unincorporated Skagit County, follows local rules, is done in harmony with local community values, while also protecting our working and natural resources lands.

The Department guides long-range community planning efforts through the County’s Comprehensive Plan, Capital Facilities Plan, zoning map changes and updates to construction and development regulations.

The Department also helps the public address concerns by responding to requests for investigations regarding potential land use or building violations.

Commercial - Property Assessed Clean Energy & Resiliency (C-PACER) Program

C-PACER is a financing tool for business owners to take out a private loan to pay for projects that improve efficiency and resiliency. The loan is secured by a recorded County assessment lien on the property that is immediately assigned to a private lender who then administers the loan and lien. This lien arrangement means the loan lien will stay with the property if the property is sold to another owner. The County does not have any further involvement in the loan. C-PACER financing is now available to businesses in county-wide.

C-PACER financing allows business owners to secure loans to make critical building improvements which reduce operating expenses, improve the health of occupants, and strengthen long-term sustainability. C-PACER can support improvements in new and existing commercial, industrial, non-profit, and multi-family buildings.

Questions on C-PACER and how to apply may be directed to the Skagit County Planning & Development Services Department at pds@co.skagit.wa.us or 360-416-1320.

Permits on the Swinomish Reservation

In 1998, Skagit County and the Swinomish Indian Tribal Community executed a memorandum of understanding (contract 3913) that established a cooperative approach to permitting on the Swinomish Reservation. Per the MOU:

Both Skagit County and the Swinomish Tribe have established zoning designations for all property within the reservation.
An application for a land use permit on the reservation may be filed with either Skagit County or the Swinomish Tribe. Each agency will forward applications to the other agency. Both agencies will issue the permits required by their governments.
Exception: for property that is (1) within the reservation, (2) owned by a tribal member, and (3) is owned in fee simple, the Swinomish Tribe is the exclusive land use authority and Skagit County does not issue permits.

Administrative Official Interpretations

The Planning and Development Services Director may issue an Administrative Official Interpretation (“AOI”) of the meaning, application, or intent of any provision of Title 14, or for questions regarding a map boundary or an alleged scrivener’s mapping error that does not involve reconsideration or rebalancing of designation criteria.
