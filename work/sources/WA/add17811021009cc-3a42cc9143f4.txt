2. Public Works

A. Presentation – Wastewater Rate Study

Mr. Phillips provided an overview of the wastewater rate study. A A

rate study was last completed in 2019 and is typically performed

every 5-10 years. Planning factors include needed CIP projects

(conveyance system, pump stations, Wastewater Treatment Plant

and equipment); upcoming retirements and funding 3 new FTE

positions; rebuilding operating reserves to a 90-day target;

restoring the $1 million annual transfer to the capital fund; and

planning for impacts of new development.

Consultant Kevin Lorentzen from HDR, Inc. provided additional

background and presented four rate scenarios, their impact to

reserve funds and residential monthly impact.

The rate study included a Connection Fee Analysis, determining the

maximum allowable fee the City could charge. The current fee is

$9,275 and the study determined the maximum allowable fee would

be $16,094.50. Council will be making policy decisions on the level

of connection fees and whether and how to phase in over time.

Council will discuss the wastewater rate and fee study at the August

19th Development Services Committee meeting.

3. Legal

Development Services Committee

A. Virtual Currency Kiosk Ban: Mr. Rogerson presented a draft

ordinance that would add Chapter 5.70 to the municipal code

prohibiting virtual currency kiosks in the City. These ATMs are being

utilized to scam and defraud vulnerable classes of citizens. The ATMs

convert deposited cash into crypto currency. Related timelines,

enforcement, and penalties were discussed. Council asked to modify

the ordinance requiring removal of equipment within thirty days of the

effective date of the ordinance. The updated ordinance will be

presented for approval at the August 26th regular City Council meeting.

B. Kratom Ban: Mr. Rogerson presented a draft ordinance that would add

Chapter 8.70 to the municipal code relating to the sale of kratom

products. Kratom is a psychoactive substance made from the leaves of

the Mitragyma Speciosa tree; there are synthetic products as well.

Kratom is not currently regulated by federal or state government.

Natural versus synthetic products, concentration levels, and

enforcement were discussed. Staff will present the ordinance for

approval at the August 26th regular City Council meeting.

4. Public Works

A. Wastewater Rate and Fee Study: Mr. Phillips reviewed driving

factors for the wastewater rate and fee study: preserve core

operations, stabilize fund balances, reinvigorate infrastructure funding,

and secure workforce succession. Deferred collection system

maintenance, aging infrastructure, digester redundancy concerns, and

the combined sewer project were considered as part of the current

study. Council discussed rate increase options and project priorities.

Staff is in the process of implementing Waterworth software. The

software will be used to connect infrastructure timelines to financial

forecasts, essentially providing an ongoing rate study/planning tool for

wastewater, solid waste, and surface water utilities, eliminating the

need to hire a consultant to execute the study.

Mr. Phillips will ask the consultant to update the study based on

Council’s suggested rate increases of 7% and 6.5% over the next two

years. Staff will finalize the Waterworth purchase to provide the

ongoing planning information; Council will base future rates on the

information.

Mr. Phillips reviewed the proposed sewer connection fee increase. Mr.

Rogerson explained how connection fees and impact fees are

calculated, regulated, and how funds for each may be used. Mr. Phillips

noted that State mandated regulatory projects, including the nutrient

removal projects, were not included as capacity related capital costs.

PROJECT NAME:N 15th Street Complete Streets Improvements (Fir St to Highlands Ave)

PROJECT #:T-26-06

DEPARTMENT Public Works PHASES PROGRAMMEDDesign, ROW

PROJECT CATEGORYNew PHASES NOT PROGRAMMED Construction

NEW CAPITAL ASSETS (YES/NO)Yes PEDESTRIAN ADA IMPROVEMENTS Yes

PROJECT DESCRIPTION

Project proposes to complete sidewalks and bike lanes on both sides of the street, add curbs and gutters, replace driveway ramps and aprons to meet the new street and sidewalk grades, pave the street where currently unsurfaced, provide street

side parking on the west side of the street, install landscape strip with street trees along both sides of the street where space allows, and replace the existing street asphalt that has grown too worn for effective rehabilitation. 15th Street is identified as

a minor arterial in the City, but has never been fully paved, lacks continuity for pedestrian and cyclist users north of the Highlands Ave intersection, and has very limited street drainage and underground stormwater conveyance. Existing street

pavement for much of the project footprint was rated generally "Poor to Very Poor" in the 2025 Pavement Preservation Plan Update, substantially limiting options for effective rehabilitation of existing pavement (the preservation plan update

recommends full-depth reclamation instead). The project will require stormwater utility improvements to convert existing surface drainage channels to underground pipes and catch basins, in order to provide street drainage and meet Ecology

stormwater management design requirements. This project will also replace existing pedestrian crosswalk ramps with ramps compliant with the currently-adopted version of the Public Right-Of-Way Accessibility Guidelines (PROWAG), as needed.

This project is consistent with the collector/arterial street pedestrian and bicycle policies identified in the 2016 Comprehensive Plan - Transportation Element, as well as the walkability and bikability goals for key city collector/arterial streets and

between destinations, identified by Objective 3.1 of the 2016 Comprehensive Plan – Transportation Element. This project is also consistent with ongoing City efforts to upgrade pedestrian routes to current Americans with Disabilities (ADA)

requirements for public rights-of-way

ANTICIPATED OPERATIONS AND MAINTENANCE IMPACT OF PROJECT

New sidewalk, curb/gutter, bike lane striping, and stormwater conveyance will require increased, though periodic, street maintence activities.

RESOLUTION NO: 1110

A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY OF MOUNT VERNON, WASHINGTON, ADOPTING AN

AMENDMENT TO THE 2027-2032 SIX-YEAR TRANSPORTATION IMPROVEMENT

PROGRAM FOR CONTINUING IMPROVEMENT OF THE CITY’S TRANSPORTATION

SYSTEM

WHEREAS, the City of Mount Vernon has the responsibility to maintain and operate the

street network located within the City rights-of-way; and

WHEREAS  the City of Mount Vernon Public Works Department is responsible for

developing and annually updating a Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) that

identifies transportation improvement projects for the next six years following adoption; and

WHEREAS, the planned improvement projects identified in the TIP are consistent with the

City of Mount Vernon’s Vision Statement, its Comprehensive Plan, and as authorized by

Chapter 35.77.010 RCW: and

WHEREAS, the 2027-2032 TIP was adopted by resolution by the City Council of the City of

Mount Vernon on June 24, 2026: and

WHEREAS, the adopted TIP may be amended to address changes arising from changes to

projects or emergent opportunities, as authorized by Chapter 35.77.010 RCW: and

WHEREAS, a public hearing was held on August 12, 2026, to receive public comments on

the proposed amendment to the adopted 2027-2032 Six-Year TIP;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF

MOUNT VERNON, WASHINGTON, AS FOLLOWS:

Section 1. Acceptance. The attached amendment to the 2027-2032 Six-Year

Transportation Improvement Program is hereby accepted and shall be the guide for

the planning and construction of necessary transportation improvements in the City

of Mount Vernon.

PASSED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF MOUNT VERNON, WASHINGTON,

AT A REGULAR MEETING THEREOF THIS 26th  DAY OF AUGUST 2026.

ITEM: 7.C

MEETING DATE: August 26, 2026

SUBJECT: Approval of Ordinance - Prohibit Virtual Currency Kiosks

PRESENTER: Kevin Rogerson

RECOMMENDED ACTION:

Approve Ordinance 3944

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:

Virtual currency kiosks, also known as crypto ATMs, are frequently used by

criminals to manipulate, intimidate, and threaten victims into using those

kiosks to send cash. Once sent these virtual currency transactions are quick

and largely irrevocable, so that once a user transfers or deposits funds, that

money is generally unrecoverable. A 2024 FBI report found that adults ages

60 and older accounted for over 85 percent of reported fraud losses

involving crypto kiosks.

The proposed ordinance would render it unlawful for any person or entity to

host, allow, operate, permit, locate or place a Virtual Currency Kiosk within

the City of Mount Vernon. Any Virtual Currency Kiosk existing in the City as

of the Effective Date of this Ordinance must be removed within 30 days

after the Effective Date.

A violation is a civil infraction subject to civil penalties in the amount of

$500 per day not including statutory assessments. In addition, the City may

cancel or revoke the business license or registration of any Virtual Currency

Kiosk Operator in violation of the proposed ordinance.

ITEM: 7.D

MEETING DATE: August 26, 2026

SUBJECT: Approval of Ordinance - Prohibit Sale of Kratom

PRESENTER: Kevin Rogerson

RECOMMENDED ACTION:

Approve Ordinance 3945

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:

Kratom currently lacks any federal or state regulations and is not subject to

government-mandated safety checks. The USFDA has determined that 7-

hydroxymitragynine (“7-OH”), a naturally occurring alkaloid found in the

kratom plant is a dangerous substance. Kratom Products which contain 7-

OH and related compounds are sold at various retail locations in the City

posing a risk to residents, including youth and other vulnerable populations.

The proposed ordinance prohibits the selling or permitting to be sold,

distribution, or advertising for sale or distribution any Kratom Product as

defined in the proposed ordinance in the City of Mount Vernon. Possession is

not included in the prohibition.

A violation is subject to a $250 fine not including statutory assessments and

potential business license revocation.

WHEREAS, the FDA has not approved any prescription or over-the-counter drug

products containing kratom or its two main alkaloids, mitragynine and 7-0H; and

WHEREAS, the safety profile of kratom products is not well established and its

effect on minors is largely unknown; and

WHEREAS, kratom is not federally regulated in the United States and is not

subject to government-mandated safety checks, resulting in a lack of oversight and

accepted safety standards for use, regulation of ingredients, purity levels, and dosage; and

WHEREAS, the State of Washington has not enacted any laws or regulations

governing the sale or use of kratom; and

WHEREAS, kratom products are sold at various retail locations in Mount

Vernon posing a risk to the City's residents, particularly youth and vulnerable

populations; and

8.70.010 Purpose and Intent.

The purpose and intent of chapter 8.70 MVMC  is to protect the public health and safety

of Mount Vernon residents by prohibiting access to kratom products to all individuals, to

include any products containing 7-hydroxymitragynine, mitragynine, or any extract,

synthetic alkaloid, or synthetically derived compound.

7.55.020 Definitions.

For the purposes of this chapter, the following words shall be defined as:

"Advertise" means any communication to one or more persons identifying that kratom

products are being offered or sold by any person, cooperative, organization, or legal

entity, including but not limited to physical displays of kratom products, signs located at

a business; signs located in places other than at a business, including billboards;

advertisements on vehicles; advertisements in paper media such as newspapers,

magazines, flyers, cards, or business cards; or advertisements in electronic media such as

internet websites, social media, electronic classified advertisements, cell phone

applications, and television or radio advertisements.

"Distribute" means to furnish, give away, exchange, transfer, deliver or supply, whether

or not for monetary gain.

"Kratom" or "Kratom Product" means any kratom analogue, food product, food

ingredient, dietary ingredient, dietary supplement, or beverage that contains any part of

the leaf of the plant mitragyna speciosa, including extracts containing natural or synthetic

alkaloids mitragynine or 7-hydroxymitragynine or any synthetically derived compound of

such plant and is manufactured as a powder, capsule, pill, beverage, or any other

consumable form.

"Kratom retailer" means any person, cooperative, organization, or legal entity that sells

kratom products or that advertises, represents, or holds itself out as selling or maintaining

kratom products within the City of Mount Vernon.

"Sell or "sale" means to offer, carry, stock, furnish, exchange, transfer, deliver, or supply

for monetary gam.

8.70.030 Prohibition on Sale or Distribution of Kratom Products

No person, cooperative, organization, or legal entity may sell, distribute, advertise for

sale or distribution, or permit to be sold any Kratom Product in the City of Mount

Vernon.

8.70.040 Violation - Penalty

A. Any person, cooperative, organization, or legal entity who violates MVMC

8.70.030 shall be issued a class 1 civil infraction p per Chapter 7.80 RCW with a fine of

$250 not including statutory assessments. Each separate sale, advertisement, or

distribution of Kratom is considered an independent violation subject to the penalties

listed herein. In the case of a single violation occurring over a period of multiple days,

each 24-hour period the violation is committed, continued, or permitted shall be a

separate and distinct violation subject to the penalties herein. Any Mount Vernon police

officer or limited commission code enforcement officer appointed by the Mayor pursuant

to MVMC 2.08.020 may enforce this chapter.

B. Any Kratom retailer found guilty of violating MVMC 8.70.030 may have its

business license or registration revoked.
