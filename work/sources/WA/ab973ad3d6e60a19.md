# North Bend City Council approves adoption of BESS moratorium, authorizes letter to Washington UTC

source: http://northbendwa.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/2666
fetched: 2026-09-23T12:37:31+00:00

On May 19, 2026, the North Bend City Council voted unanimously to approve an ordinance enacting a 12-month moratorium related to Battery Energy Systems (BESS) storage within city limits. Additionally, the Council authorized Mayor Miller to draft a letter to the Washington State Utilities and Transportation Commission (UTC), objecting to the siting of the BESS by Jupiter Energy at the currently proposed location in unincorporated King County, located within the Urban Growth Area (UGA) of the City of Snoqualmie.

BESS are large-scale power storage solutions that capture electrical energy during low-demand periods and discharge based on residential and commercial demand. These systems typically use lithium-ion technology housed in modular containers. According to the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, BESS can help stabilize electrical grids but also pose potential public health and safety risks due to battery fires, fire emissions, and long-term environmental impacts.

The moratorium prohibits the acceptance, processing, and/or approval of applications for land development proposals relating to or incorporating BESS. Learn more about the moratorium by reading the [May 19 City Council packet](https://northbendwa.gov/ArchiveCenter/ViewFile/Item/7326) (beginning on page 31).

“Our role is to look to the future. Land use planning and keeping ourselves up to date with technology and environmental risks need to be incorporated in that,” commented Mayor Pro Tem Tremolada.

“As we go through this process, if at the end, we make a land use decision, we need to have the data and the due diligence behind it. Secondly, if it helps our neighbors, we are a valley. What happens next door affects us both positively and negatively. Let’s do the work. Let’s figure this out and really look to the future for not only our town, but the upper valley and all of the valley as a whole,” he continued.

Following discussion and noting that battery storage does not necessarily store energy produced by renewable energy sources, the Council approved one amendment to the ordinance, striking the words, “produced by renewable energy sources.” It now reads as approved: “Whereas, battery energy storage systems are commonly used to store energy for use during periods of higher demand, to stabilize fluctuations in the electrical grid, or to serve as backup energy sources.”

Following the Council’s authorization, Mayor Miller is drafting a letter to the UTC, objecting to the siting of the BESS by Jupiter Energy at the currently proposed location in unincorporated King County, located within the UGA of the City of Snoqualmie. The letter will be filed with the commission before the May 26, 2026, deadline.

The city will continue to provide community updates on the BESS moratorium and related topics.
