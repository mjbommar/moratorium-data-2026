Sept. 14, 2026: City Council Declares 120-day Data Center Moratorium

At its September 14 meeting, the Woodburn City Council held a public hearing and voted to declare a 120-day moratorium on new data center development.

What does the moratorium cover?

The moratorium temporarily pauses the acceptance, review, and approval of City permits and development proposals for new data storage, processing, and information technology centers, commonly known as data centers. It gives the City time to take a closer look at how data centers could affect our community before considering future projects.

• Data Center Moratorium Fact Sheet

It does not apply to other types of industrial development.

The City currently has no pending or active data center applications.

During the 120-day period, the City will:

• Review zoning and development regulations.

• Evaluate public utility and infrastructure needs.

• Reassess data center siting criteria.

• Study potential impacts to water, energy, noise, the environment, and surrounding properties.

• Develop clear standards for evaluating future proposals.

Why now?

Woodburn has been planning for future industrial growth. In 2024, the City identified data centers as one industry that could bring jobs and investment. In 2025, the City expanded its urban growth boundary by 237 acres for future industrial development and employment growth.

Since then, the City has seen increased interest in industrial development in the Southwest Industrial Reserve.

At the same time, data center technology and the scale of these facilities have changed rapidly. Communities across Oregon and the country are looking more closely at the impacts of large-scale data centers, including energy and water use, utility costs, noise, environmental impacts, and the use of employment land.

The City believes it is important to take a step back and make sure our regulations are prepared for these changes.

The City has also hired Thomas Consulting Group, a specialty consultant that works with local governments on data center planning and regulations. The consultant will conduct an independent review and help develop recommendations for future projects.

The goal is simple: take time now to understand the potential impacts and ensure Woodburn is prepared before new data center projects are considered.
