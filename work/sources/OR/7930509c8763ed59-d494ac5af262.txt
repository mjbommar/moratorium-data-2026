Public Hearing - Data Center Moratorium

The Board reconvened as the Board of County Commissioners and held a public hearing on a proposed data center moratorium.

Staff presented the proposed moratorium as a temporary suspension of county approval of land use and building permits for new data centers or expansion of existing data centers as primary uses in unincorporated Clackamas County. Staff stated that the moratorium would last up to 120 days, with a possible six-month extension only after another public hearing and findings that the need still exists and reasonable progress is being made.

Staff described the proposed moratorium as based on a “compelling need” rationale and identified potential public harms related to noise, heat, air impacts, water demand, electricity demand, proximity to residential areas, low employment density, and limited vacant industrial land. Staff also stated that the moratorium would affect only one type of industrial use and would allow staff time to develop possible ZDO amendments, if the Board amended the long-range planning work program.

Stephanie Hill - Supported a temporary moratorium, citing concerns about electricity, water, infrastructure, noise, environmental impacts, tax breaks, enforceable protections, and long-term effects on families and communities.

Jan Lee - Speaking as a Clackamas Soil and Water Conservation District board member, said the board unanimously supported the moratorium and raised concerns about water allocation, unknown withdrawals and discharges, drought planning, cost shifting to residents, and diesel generator emissions.

John Charles - Opposed the moratorium, arguing that there are no data centers or pending applications in unincorporated Clackamas County, little suitable industrial land, no compelling need, and that staff and public time would be wasted.

Tom Archer - Suggested the main issue is electricity generation and supply rather than data centers themselves, and proposed requiring data centers to build on-site fusion generators and share power with the grid.

Karen Odell - Supported the pause, expressing concern that data centers could use farmland, water, and public resources while benefiting few people; the clerk identified the speaker as Karen Odell, though the speaker did not state a name in the captured audio.

Jim Johnson - Speaking as a Clackamas Soil and Water Conservation District board member and land use planner, supported the moratorium and urged comprehensive code amendments, including clear definitions and limits on siting, to protect resources and residents.

Stephen Hunter - Suggested the issue was politically motivated and should be tabled until the next year, argued some existing facilities may meet the definition of data centers, and said the county should distinguish among types and impacts of data centers.

Jennifer Brooks - Asked why data centers would be considered before impacts are definitively understood, including effects on health, land, water, trees, air, and the environment.

Les Poole - Supported the moratorium but urged simple standards focused on electricity use, proximity to neighboring properties, and power costs; asked whether applications would be accepted during the moratorium and cautioned against turning the work into an overly complex process.

John Vinje - Speaking as a Clackamas County resident and Sierra Club volunteer, supported the moratorium and raised concerns about utility infrastructure, higher customer rates, substations, transmission lines, and major water use by hyperscale data centers.

Lloyd Marbet - Speaking for the Oregon Conservancy Foundation, supported the moratorium for as long as needed to review AI data center impacts, including nuclear power connections and financial risks associated with AI infrastructure investment.

Larky Hodges - Supported the moratorium and raised concerns about evaporative cooling, chemicals concentrating in groundwater, diesel generator emissions during outages, and the risk that AI investments could become a financial bubble.

RJ Cook - Presented a poem supporting a long or permanent moratorium, emphasizing protection of farms, families, rural homes, and livability, and urged inclusion of battery energy storage systems in the moratorium.

During Board deliberation, commissioners discussed the moratorium as a temporary pause to allow additional research, policy development, and public engagement on land use, water, electricity, infrastructure costs, utility rates, economic opportunity, rural impacts, and potential future zoning changes.

Commissioner Helm moved to adopt the resolution imposing a data center moratorium. Commissioner Savas seconded the motion. Motion passed 5-0.

Pulled Item - Sheriff’s Office Vehicle Purchase

The Board discussed authorization to purchase six fully uplifted Chevrolet Tahoe 4x4 police pursuit vehicles from Dick Hanna Chevrolet using a State of Oregon master blanket purchase order, with a total contract value of $524,725.08, funded by contract cities and asset forfeiture with no county general funds involved.

Commissioner Savas raised concerns about potential engine reliability issues, vehicle downtime, warranty limits, and the county’s financial exposure if failures occur outside warranty coverage. Sheriff’s Office fleet staff explained that the vehicles carry a five-year or 100,000-mile drivetrain warranty, that the vehicles were needed for specialized uses requiring a larger platform, and that the Sheriff’s Office was evaluating other pursuit-rated vehicle options for future purchases.

Pulled Item - Robert Half Temporary Staffing

The Board discussed a board order authorizing a purchase order with Robert Half Incorporated for temporary staffing to fill case manager positions for health-related social needs and short-term rental assistance programs, with an agreement value of $600,000 for one year, funded through the Oregon Health Authority and Metro Supportive Housing Services funds with no county general funds involved.

Health, Housing, and Human Services staff explained that the funding is short-term, that demand exceeds existing staff capacity, and that using temporary staffing is intended to help distribute program funds before funding deadlines while avoiding creation of county positions that would later need to end when funding expires. Staff estimated the additional staffing would help serve approximately 305 households through health-related social needs funding and up to 570 households through short-term rental assistance.

Motion: Commissioner Helm moved to approve item D3. Commissioner West seconded the motion. Motion passed 5-0.
