# Data centers

Learn about changes to zoning rules related to data centers in Minneapolis.

## Overview

The City is updating its zoning rules for data centers. Data centers hold computers that store and process information. These computers help the internet work.

Partly because of new AI technology, data centers are growing fast across the country. Companies want to build them in cities, suburbs, and rural areas. Many other communities are also updating their rules to manage the impact of data centers.

Earlier this year, the City Council approved a temporary ordinance. It pauses new data center proposals in most of the city. This pause gives the City time to create new standards.

[Share your comments](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/business-services/planning-zoning/amendments/proposed-adopted/data-centers/comments/)

## Goals of the code change

We want to make sure Minneapolis has strong rules for data centers so we can limit any negative effects on the community. We also want to look at ways to reuse empty office and commercial buildings.

People have told us they’re worried about things like energy use, water use, and noise. Cities in Minnesota can’t make their own rules for building components or systems because the state building code controls those. This means the City cannot address some issues in the way people might expect.

With new zoning rules, the City plans to:

* Define what a data center is
* Limit how big data centers can be
* Decide where data centers are allowed
* Require data centers to report their planned energy and water use
* Reduce noise impacts

If a business or institution has servers that only serve that same property, those systems will usually not be counted as a data center.

## Relevant sections of the zoning code

These chapters of the zoning code may change as part of the amendment:

* Chapter 535, Overlay Districts
* Chapter 540, Built Form Overlay Districts

### New definition

#### Data center

A principal use where the primary function is to house computers and/or related equipment for the purpose of collection, processing, storage, or retrieval of data.

### Review process

* A conditional use permit (CUP) is required for any new or expanded data center
* CUP includes a public hearing at the City Planning Commission (CPC)
* CPC decides to approve or deny the CUP
* Decision is final unless appealed
* Appeals go to the City Council

### Where the use is allowed with a conditional use permit

* Downtown zoning districts DT1 and DT2

### Size limits

* Size limits are based on the amount of “white space” (server and equipment area)
* Downtown districts: white space capped at 175,000 sq. ft.

### Building requirements

* No new buildings may be built for a data center
* New data centers must be placed in existing buildings at the time the ordinance becomes effective.
* Minor additions allowed (for example, enclosing equipment)

### Application requirements

* Must disclose expected use of:
* Water
* Electricity
* Generators
* Sound attenuation plan
* Must submit written evidence showing coordination with the electric utility provider

### Server room placement rules

Rooms with servers (where most noise occurs) cannot:

* Be within 20 feet of an exterior building wall
* Be within 100 feet of the exterior wall of a residential dwelling unit or rooming unit

### Equipment placement

Equipment must be inside the building or on the roof

### Additional downtown rules

* No server rooms or equipment allowed on the first or second floor
* Data centers established after Jan. 1, 2026 cannot be the only principal use on the lot

### Overlay districts no longer under consideration

Initial ideas included a potential overlay district. It would have allowed for review of smaller-scale data centers outside downtown. Based on feedback the overlay district will not move forward. It will not be included in the recommendations presented to the City Planning Commission and City Council in October and November.

### Clarifications

Spaces that do not count as active ground‑floor functions under sections 550.70 and 550.600 include:

* Servers
* Distribution equipment
* Cooling
* Generators and similar equipment

Note: The City’s Health Department is currently developing regulations for stationary generators
