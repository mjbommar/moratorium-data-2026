## Proposed Data Center

### September 14, 2026 Meeting Recap

The first reading of a proposed ordinance that would remove the current proposed data center from a moratorium was approved by the City Council at its meeting on August 10. A second reading of the proposed ordinance was approved by the City Council at its meeting on August 24. The third and final reading was approved by the City Council at its meeting on Monday, Sep. 14. The amended ordinance will now have to be published in the City’s paper of record, *The Pioneer Press*.

Next steps:

* The City Council will consider the developer’s major site plan application at its September 28 regular meeting.
* If the major site plan application is approved at that meeting, the developer would need to have an approved development contract with the City. That is a standard requirement for all developments in Inver Grove Heights.
* Once that is approved, the speed at which the project can move forward depends on how quickly the developer applies for and is issued a building permit from the City.

The construction timeline would then be in the hands of the developer.

### June 26, 2026 Meeting Recap

If you missed the City Council meeting on Friday, June 26, regarding the proposed moratorium on data center development in IGH, here is recap.

**The vote:**

The City Council voted 3-2 to approve a one-year moratorium on data center development in IGH that includes the current proposed data center at 5842 Carmen Avenue East.

**What this outcome means:**

The developer has threatened legal action against the City of IGH if the moratorium includes the current project. Any resulting legal action is yet to be determined.

**New factors to consider:**

At approximately 4 p.m. on Thursday, June 25, the City received notice of an Environmental Assessment Worksheet (EAW) petition that IGH residents had filed with the State of Minnesota regarding this project. An EAW requires 100 signatures and pauses the proposed development process so that potential environmental impacts of the proposed development can be further assessed, above and beyond the information required by City Code. The City will work with staff and consultants to study potential impacts during the 30-day period allowed by the state.

**What’s next:**

The Council still needs to vote on whether or not they will approve the site plan for the proposed data center development. This vote has been tabled to a future meeting, date to be determined. Consideration on this application cannot be acted upon until after a determination on the EAW is made. More information will be shared as it's available.

### Proposed Data Center Development Update, June 23, 2026

On Monday, June 22, the Inver Grove Heights City Council met to consider an application for the proposed data center development at 5842 Carmen Avenue East. You can stream a recording of the meeting by clicking [here](https://townsquare.tv/IGH-VOD?site=6&showid=5758).

Just before the 6 p.m. meeting, Councilmembers received new information on the project and on the related, threatened $150 million litigation against the City. To allow the City Council more time to review the new information, the Council voted to recess the meeting and reconvene it this Friday, June 26 at 8 a.m.

**Where the Proposed Development Currently Stands as of June 23, 2026**

Moratorium Consideration

Responding to public concerns, on May 11, 2026, the City Council initiated consideration of a moratorium on data center development in IGH. If approved, the moratorium would pause data center development in the community, allowing the City time to study the topic in depth. That moratorium currently includes the proposed data center development application. A second reading of the moratorium was approved on June 8, 2026. The third and final vote on the moratorium had been scheduled for the June 22, 2026, Council meeting.

Threatened Litigation

Because the pending moratorium includes the proposed data center development and was drafted retroactively after the City received the data center application, the developer has threatened the City with litigation if the project is denied. The developer cited damages of up to $150 million in its communications with the City regarding a potential lawsuit. The Council wants to make a sound decision about the data center that considers concerns expressed by residents, while also protecting taxpayers from absorbing the costs of litigation.

Councilmembers received an update regarding the threatened litigation in a closed session prior to their 6 p.m. regular meeting on Monday. The meeting was not public because it was subject to attorney/client privilege and was therefore confidential. Councilmembers had questions and concerns related to the new information provided at the closed session, resulting in the Council pausing the regular meeting to allow the City’s legal counsel time to seek clarification and answer those questions.

Council to Reconvene the Meeting on Friday, June 26

Options for dates to reconvene the meeting are limited because the deadline to approve or deny the proposed data center project is July 1, 2026. If, on June 26, the City Council were to vote to approve the moratorium as written, the adopted moratorium must be published in the official City newspaper (*Pioneer Press*) before a final decision on the proposed data center application can occur.

**Why the Deadline?**

All cities are required to follow the “60 Day Rule” as established by State statute. This means a City must act on any proposed development application within 60 days from the date of application. Statute also allows cities to extend a project review period for an additional 60 days, up to a 120-day total review period. But, after 120 days, extensions may only be granted by the applicant. Failure by the City to act on an application, without an extension from an applicant, results in an automatic approval of the project.

The data center developer has not been willing to grant an extension of the current review period. Therefore, the final 120-day deadline for approving or denying the data center application remains July 1, 2026.

If the pending moratorium is approved as drafted, which *includes* the current proposed data center development, the City Council would need to take separate action to *deny* the proposed data center development. This can only happen during a short window of time – after the moratorium is published in City’s official newspaper, but before July 1, 2026.

Conversely, if the City Council amends the draft moratorium to *exclude* the current proposed data center development, the City Council would also need to take separate action to *approve* the data center application.

**What to Expect Next**

The IGH City Council will meet at 8 a.m. on Friday, June 26 in Council Chambers at City Hall (8150 Barbara Avenue) to consider the moratorium and proposed data center site application. This meeting is open to the public. If you cannot attend the meeting in person, it can be streamed at townsquare.tv or watched live on Channel 14/799 HD.

Comments can be sent to [CityClerk@ighmn.gov](mailto:cityclerk@ighmn.gov). If comments are received by 3 p.m. on Thursday, June 25, they will be provided to the City Council prior to or at Friday’s meeting.

### Proposed Data Center Project Background

The City is **reviewing a site application** for a proposed data center located at 5842 Carmen Ave East in Inver Grove Heights. The data center building would be on the site of the former Travel Tags, Inc., and would be one-third the size of the previous Travel Tags building at **50,000 square feet**.

According to the developer, the power usage for this facility is anticipated to be **five (5) megawatts per day**. As with any development, this business must abide by noise and light pollution standards stipulated in City Code.
