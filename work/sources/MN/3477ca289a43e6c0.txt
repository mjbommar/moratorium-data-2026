Request
A request for review and approval of an interim ordinance establishing up to a one-year
moratorium on the permitting of data centers within the unincorporated areas of Blue Earth
County under the zoning authority of Blue Earth County. This moratorium will allow County
staff to conduct an evaluation and develop the appropriate performance standards and zoning
ordinance amendments.

Background and Statement of General Need
Within the last two years, over a dozen data centers have been proposed in the State of
Minnesota with locations varying from Twin Cities metropolitan areas to small rural towns in
both southern and northern Minnesota. While the location and proposals vary, what is consistent
is market demand for data centers (and artificial intelligence) appears to be increasing.

In response to this growing demand, Blue Earth County Property and Environmental Resources
staff conducted a review and evaluated the following items related to Data Centers:
    • Ordinance Requirements for Data Centers for multiple jurisdictions within the state
        (completed as a comparative analysis)
           o Evaluating definitions, allowable zoning districts, approval types and processes,
                necessary setbacks, and general restrictions
    • Current Blue Earth County Zoning Ordinance (as it relates to Data Centers), with special
        consideration to permitting pathways
    • Existing Zoning Districts (and potential use implications to land area)

The information located below is intended to provide background and review information,
detailing what was found during the staff review, and potential approaches and next steps.

General Overview
A data center is a facility that contains servers, storage systems, networking equipment and
electrical and cooling infrastructure needed for continuous running. The facilities support cloud
computing and AI by providing the physical infrastructure behind virtualized services, as well as
business applications and online services.
While definitions of data centers vary by jurisdiction, in general, data centers are defined as a
facility that houses information technology (IT) infrastructure for building, running, and
delivering applications and services and for storing and managing the data associated with those
applications and services.

Identified Concerns Related to Data Centers
Many public interest groups and private landowners have also expressed concerns related to the
surge of data centers. Commonly voiced concerns tend to relate to environmental, health, and
social and community challenges with development. A detailed description of general concerns
can be found below:
Environmental Impacts
    • Depending on construction, data centers have the potential to consume large amounts of
        water resources, as water is needed for cooling, which can strain local resources.
    • Due to the need for generators, cooling fans and lighting, it is potentially possible to have
        noise, vibration, and heat emission impacts.
    • Large, paved data center campuses increase impervious surface area, and impact the
        hydrologic cycle causing potential impacts of stormwater contamination, erosion and
        sedimentation, and reduced groundwater recharge (due to decrease in infiltration).

Operational, Technical, and Infrastructure Impacts
   • Data centers cause a great deal of impact to the electrical grid by increasing the energy
       demand and presenting challenges for grid management and reliability.
   • Hazard mitigation planning is often necessary in data centers as potential hazards include
       heat stress from equipment, gas leaks, and fire hazards.
   • During construction, the increase in traffic may cause a burden to the existing road
       network due to project scale and road classification.
   • Data centers require a significant amount of land for development often concerning
       communities as land is a finite resource.

Social and Community
   • While tax revenue is generated from the developed data center structure, there are
        minimal long-term jobs created (i.e. most jobs are rooted in construction and not ongoing
        operation).

Data Centers in Minnesota
As part of Blue Earth County’s review of data centers, staff completed a comparative analysis of
local jurisdiction ordinance requirements for data centers. A total of six local government
ordinances were reviewed (Farmington, Pine Island, Rochester, Duluth, Monticello, and St.
Louis County). The review included an evaluation of the definition, zoning district in which data
centers were allowed, approval type or process, setbacks, impervious area, and general standards
or special conditions tied to noise restrictions, building design and aesthetics, backup power
regulations and water use requirements, traffic restrictions, and security/fencing.
The results of the evaluation determined that zoning district allowances and approval
type/process varied greatly. The appropriate zoning district ranged from Mixed Use, Industrial,
Business, Commercial, and Planned Unit Development (PUD). The process for permitting
included all of the following:

Status of Jurisdictions within Comparative Analysis
Of the six local governments reviewed within the Ordinance Analysis, lawsuit challenges have
occurred for two. The Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy (MCEA) has challenged
the environmental review of the data center in Pine Island, and citizens of Farmington have filed
a lawsuit against the city to pause negotiations between the city and a data development
company.
Local Governments with Moratoriums in Place
A number of Minnesota communities have enacted temporary moratoriums on data centers.
While this is not an exhaustive list, examples include Minneapolis, Eagan, Inver Grove Heights,
Rosemount, Carver, Waite Park, Mankato, Le Sueur County, and Wright County. These
moratoriums are intended to pause new proposals long enough for local governments to study the
cumulative impacts of data center development and establish appropriate standards to address
those impacts.

Blue Earth County Zoning Ordinance
The existing Blue Earth County Zoning Ordinance does not specifically identify data centers as a
use, nor is it included within the definition section. Because of this, staff evaluated which
existing use categories most closely correspond to data center operations and ranked the
following (from most to least applicable):
   1.   General light manufacturing, assembly plants and facilities
   2.   Warehousing
   3.   Electrical products, manufacture and assembly
   4.   Planned industrial parks
   5.   Electrical substations

Each of these use categories carries different permitting requirements—some may be permitted
uses while others may require a conditional use permit—and they align with different zoning
districts. Establishing a clearly defined use category for data centers is important to ensure
proper permitting, consistent application of standards, and clear expectations for both the
applicant and the County.

Land Use Plan
The Blue Earth County Land Use Plan includes the following relevant items:

Land Use Objective #1: “Maintain ordinances that appropriately respond to the residential,
agricultural, industrial and commercial trends of Blue Earth County.”

Development Implementation Action #3: “Regularly review and amend the zoning ordinance
and land division regulations to ensure standards correlate with current development trends.”

Required Notices
The required public hearing notice was published in the Free Press on July 25, 2026. It was also
mailed to all cities and townships in the County on July 24, 2026.

STAFF AND AGENCY REVIEW

County Attorney Review
The proposed interim ordinance has been reviewed by the County Attorney’s office. The review
noted that the moratorium and interim ordinance are established to protect public health, safety,
and general welfare; clarified that the ordinance remains in effect for up to one year from its
adoption; specified that it applies to all pending and future zoning applications; added a section
addressing ordinance enforcement; and included general grammatical updates.


Findings
Staff have developed the following findings regarding the proposed interim ordinance.

   1. An interim ordinance in alignment with the County Land Use Plan, Land Use Objective #1,
      prohibiting the permitting and establishment of data centers within the jurisdiction is
      necessary for the purpose of protecting the planning process and the health, safety, and
      welfare of the residents of Blue Earth County, for up to one year from the date of adoption.

   2. It is in the interests of public health, safety, and the general welfare of impose a moratorium
      and adopt an interim ordinance to prohibit the permitting and establishment of data centers in
      Blue Earth County.

   3. Minnesota Statutes Section 394.21 grants counties the authority to regulate land development
      by adopting and amending official controls.

   4. Minnesota Statutes Section 394.34 allows counties to pass a resolution to halt development of
      a particular use, if the county conducts an evaluation in pursuit of amending their zoning
      ordinance.

RECOMMENDATION
Due to the potential impact of Data Centers, Blue Earth County staff recommend an interim
ordinance to protect the public health, safety and general welfare of residents of Blue Earth

County. This will temporarily prohibit the establishment or permitting of data centers while staff
conduct a thorough, purposeful, and comprehensive evaluation of the following items:
   • Land use compatibility and siting
   • Permitting pathways
   • Environmental Impacts (electrical consumption, water use, noise, vibration, heat
      emissions, and air quality)
   • Infrastructure cost impacts (economic, taxation, use of emergency services and roadways)
   • Technical Review (grid impacts and cooling technologies)

Within the evaluation period, staff will conduct a series of engagement activities to gather public

allow staff an opportunity to reflect on community priorities, establish clear, predictable and
appropriate standards, protect public health, safety, and welfare, ensure long-term compatibility
with data centers and surrounding land use, and balance economic development with
environmental stewardship.

After completing the public participation (engagement) and evaluation of the items above, staff
will then develop appropriate performance standards, zoning controls, and the regulatory
framework necessary to thoughtfully manage data centers within the county.

SECTION 1. DEFINITION
For purposes of this Interim Ordinance, “Data Center” means a facility used primarily for the storage,
management, processing, and transmission of digital data, a facility that houses computer or network
equipment, systems, services, appliances, and other associated components related to digital data
storage and operations, together with its accessory and appurtenant facilities, which may also include
offices, air handlers, back-up power generators, water cooling systems and water storage facilities,
utility substations, and other associated infrastructure necessary to support sustained operations.

SECTION 2. MORATORIUM
A. No new Data Center may be established, constructed, or permitted within Blue Earth County for
   the duration of this Interim Ordinance.
B. No land use or zoning permit applications related to a Data Center shall be accepted, processed,
   or approved during the moratorium.

C. This temporary ordinance shall apply to any pending or future zoning applications.

SECTION 3. EVALUATION
During the moratorium period, County staff, in coordination with the Blue Earth County Board of
Commissioners, shall conduct an evaluation addressing:
•   Energy consumption and electrical infrastructure impacts
•   Water use and cooling technologies
•   Noise, vibration, and heat emissions
•   Air quality and environmental impacts
•   Stormwater and wastewater management
•   Emergency services and hazard mitigation
•   Land use compatibility and siting criteria
•   Infrastructure impacts
•   Best practices from other Minnesota counties and jurisdictions
The evaluation shall result in recommended performance standards and zoning amendments for
consideration by the County Board.

SECTION 4. DURATION
This Interim Ordinance shall remain in effect for twelve (12) months from the date of adoption,
unless earlier repealed or extended as permitted under Minnesota Statutes Section 394.34.

SECTION 5. ENFORCEMENT
Blue Earth County may enforce any provision of this Ordinance by any and all means authorized by
Blue Earth County Ordinances and State Statute.

SECTION 6. SEVERABILITY
If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase of this Interim Ordinance is determined to be
invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not affect the
validity of the remaining portions of the Ordinance, which shall remain in full force and effect, and
shall not invalidate any other section, provision, or part thereof.

SECTION 7. EFFECTIVE DATE
This Ordinance shall take effect immediately upon its passage and shall remain in effect until June
27, 2027, or until the adoption of the official controls being studied, whichever occurs first.
