P&Z Data Center Legislation Discussions: Community Voices and Public

Engagement

October 26, 2026 | Session Three

Discussion recap

The October 26 P&Z Committee discussion provided an opportunity for community members to

ask questions, share concerns, and offer ideas about data center development in Memphis.

Key discussion points

 The development pause provides time to prepare permanent standards.

Councilman Warren explained that the recently passed 135-day pause within Substitute

Ordinance No. 5986 allows the Division of Planning and Development to draft

recommendations to regulate future data center development. He discussed potential

protections near homes and schools, cooling methods, and cleaner power options.

These details remain part of the work to develop permanent standards.

 Residents requested clearer information and easier access to documents.

Councilman Spinosa announced a new City Council webpage intended to consolidate

meeting recordings and documents for public access. The P&Z Data Center Committee

Hearings Webpage can be accessed at https://memphistn.gov/planning-zoning

committee-hearings-on-data-center-legislation/

 Future locations and neighborhood protections remain central questions.

Residents requested maps showing potential locations for data centers and asked how

future facilities could be separated from homes, schools, and sensitive areas.

 Community members proposed broader ways to share economic benefits.

Speakers emphasized including residents in developing community benefits

arrangements. Ideas included job training, residential solar investment, a community

wealth fund, and opportunities for local residents to share in ownership or financial

returns. Councilman Warren discussed a proposed community benefit agency and said

he would bring suggestions for larger contributions to business leaders.

 Speakers offered different perspectives on investment and timing.  Some comments

emphasized the economic opportunity associated with data centers and the importance

of establishing clear rules promptly. Others emphasized community trust, meaningful

participation, and protections for residents.

Follow-ups raised during the discussion

 Public information: Consolidate recordings and documents on the City Council’s P&Z

Committee Hearings webpage.

 Siting and neighborhood protections: Continue DPD’s review of existing zoning and

potential locations. Residents requested maps and clarification about available land.

 Community benefits:  Continue discussing resident participation, investment and

ownership ideas, contribution levels, and questions about state authority. |

 Continuing oversight: Councilman Warren proposed planning staff updates

approximately every two weeks as recommendations develop.

What comes next?

The Thursday, October 29  session will focus on community benefits and continued discussion of

outstanding questions. Community input from October 26 will help inform that conversation as

Council continues reviewing proposed protections and benefit arrangements.
