7. Approval of Electronic Fund Transfer Policy – As part of management’s continued

efforts to minimize vulnerabilities, Finance Director Janet Jennings and Town

Administrator Glenn Rosenoff worked together to go through eligibility requirements

through our insurance provider Public Entity Partners (PEP) for the Cyber Extension

Coverage. Ms. Jennings drafted a formal Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) Policy and

Procedures, which is a critical control for mitigating cybersecurity-driven financial risk.

As cybercrime increasingly targets payment systems, strong governance over

electronic fund transfers is essential to protecting organizational assets, ensuring

accountability, and meeting fiduciary obligations.

Cyber incidents involving unauthorized electronic payments, particularly business

email compromise, phishing, and credential theft, remain one of the most common and

costly categories of cyber loss. These attacks frequently bypass technical controls by

exploiting process weaknesses and human decision-making. Without a clearly defined

and enforced EFT policy, the town is exposed to undue financial, operational, and

reputational risk. This policy matters to protect financial assets, reduce exposure to

cyber-enabled fraud, improve governance and accountability, strengthen detection and

response capabilities, and support regulatory, audit, and insurance expectations. The

policy represents a low-cost, high-impact measure that significantly reduces exposure

to some of the most prevalent cyber risks facing local governments today.

Upon policy approval from the BMA, staff will forward all other required documents we

feel will make us eligible for the Cyber Extension Coverage. The documents required

for eligibility are as follows:

• A copy of our written policy for electronic funds transfers

• A copy of written procedures for identifying and responding to data breaches

• The results report from our latest network security audit

• Details regarding data breaches, malware or virus infections or social

engineering events within the last 5 years that have compromised our data or

caused financial loss

• Data is backed up daily to a device on-site and to East and West Coast data

Policy Statement:


The Town of Jonesborough recognizes the use of various electronic payment methods as a

safe and efficient method to process certain disbursements. The Town is committed to

establishing controls and procedures for the utilization of electronic funds transfers (EFTs).

This policy provides a framework of procedures to ensure that proper protocols are followed,

and that adequate oversight is in place for the use of EFTs.

Purpose:


The primary goal of this policy is to ensure EFTs are initiated, executed and approved in a

secure manner. This policy establishes general guidelines for using EFTs for payables and

receivables.

Definitions:


• Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT): The electronic exchange (transfer of money from one

bank account to another), either within a single financial institution or across multiple

institutions, through computer-based systems. Wire transfers and ACH payments are

examples of EFTs.  This form of disbursement is authorized under state law.

• Automated Clearing House (ACH):  An electronic payment delivery system that

processes electronic credit and debit transactions, including direct deposits, within the

United States using the American Bankers Association (ABA) number. These should

be set up in the vendor master that denotes this payment method.

• Banking Information: Information from the payee or their bank regarding their account.

This information includes bank name, account name, account number, routing number,

bank contact information and any other information necessary to transmit funds.

• Wire T Transfer: An electronic transfer of funds from one bank account to another initiated

directly with the payer's bank.  This type of transfer utilizes a system operated by the

Federal Reserve Banks.

General Guidelines:


The Town utilizes EFTs for receipt of intergovernmental payments, grant payments, auto

drafted utility billing payments and other revenues where practical. EFTs are also used for the

transmittal of payroll, certain withholdings, tax deposits, bond payments, credit card processing

fees, banking fees and other disbursements where practical or required.

All EFT payments will be coordinated and submitted through the Finance Department. The

Finance Director or their designee will approve all new requests and any changes t to electronic

funds transfer requests. They will further ensure that the payment via EFT is necessary, all

required documentation is provided and appropriately approved, that the request and banking

account information is accurate and valid and that the transaction is accurately recorded in the

general ledger system.

All EFTs are subject to applicable Purchasing Policies and all other policies and procedures in

relation to the purchase of goods and/or services.

The Town’s preferred payment method is via check for vendor invoices. Payroll payments are

made via ACH (direct deposit). Wire transfers should only be used under specific

circumstances that require immediate funds to settle a transaction. Bond payments are made

by wire transfer. Exceptions to these must be pre-approved by the Finance Director.

If bank fraud is discovered in the EFT process, the fraud must be reported to the Finance

Director immediately. In the absence of the Finance Director, the Town Administrator must be

Page 11 of 59


notified. The Finance Director must notify the Town Administrator of the potential fraud as soon

as possible after it is detected. After determination that a fraud has occurred, the Finance

Director must notify the State of Tennessee Comptroller’s Office within five (5) working days.

Steps will be taken with the Town’s banking institution to mitigate the fraud, and the appropriate

entities will be notified as necessary.

This policy will be reviewed periodically for accuracy and process verification. The Finance

Director and Town Administrator are authorized to make minor administrative changes to this

policy, provided such changes are consistent with state and federal laws.

Procedures:


The Finance Department is the only department authorized to initiate EFTs. Finance shall be

responsible for the review of EFT requests to ensure compliance, completeness and proper

general ledger recording. This method of payment will be used only when authorized by the

Finance Director or their designee.

To promote the safety of Town funds in the EFT environment, the following procedures will be

used by all Town employees involved in processing payments via EFT.

• The procedure to initiate an EFT is subject to the same financial policies, procedures

and controls that govern disbursement by any other payment method.

• Authentication of new EFT requests and changes to existing EFTs are required prior to

the transactions being input in the computer-based banking system and include the

following steps:

o Validate: All new electronic payment instruction requests received, even if the

request is internal.

o Contact: The supplier or requester must be contacted directly by phone to

confirm any requests for payment method or payment instruction changes. Do

not use the contact information provided on the request to change payment

method or payment instructions. Contact information known to be genuine must

be used, such as t the contact information in t the master file or information collected

from the original contract.

o Verify: The new information provided on the payment instructions must be

verified with the known contact (i.e., contact bank to confirm correct account

name, number and routing information).

o Document: The verification process that was followed must be documented to

validate payment instructions.  The person responsible for entering/updating

instructions and the person approving t the n new/updated instructions must approve

the record of verification.

• When ACH payments are approved, they will be set up in the master file database in

the financial accounting system by individuals authorized to perform vendor

maintenance.

• All invoices will be approved in accordance with the Town’s Purchasing Policies and

entered into the financial accounting software by Accounts Payable.

• Transmission of the ACH file from financial system to bank will occur through secure

single user account login by authorized Finance staff, verifying the number of EFTs

submitted to the bank and that the total matches the report in the financial accounting

system.

• Bank balances will be monitored daily for unusual or unexpected transactions.

• Reconciliation of banking activity to the general ledger will be accomplished in a timely

manner with investigation and resolution of reconciling items.

Wire Transfers:


The Finance Department is the only department authorized to initiate wire transfers. Finance

shall be responsible for the review of wire requests to ensure compliance, completeness and

proper general ledger recording. This method of payment will be used only when authorized by

the Finance Director or their designee.

The Town will utilize security measures offered by the Town’s banking institution to prevent

unauthorized individuals from initiating or modifying a wire transfer. Online banking systems

should only be used by employees with proper system credentials and separate banking user

IDs.

All wire transfer requests, including back-up wire information, invoice or other supporting

documents will be forwarded to those authorized in the Finance Department to initiate wires.

The wire transfer request must include the name and address of the payee, and full payment

instructions including banking information. The bank and invoice information must be verified.

If there is an inconsistency with the information provided, the wire initiator will contact the

initiating party to obtain additional information. If all information agrees with the documentation,

the wire will be requested with the Town’s financial institution by the authorized Finance

employee.

The wire transfer request and all documentation will be forwarded to the Finance Director or

their designee for payment approval. The pending wire information is reviewed online against

the back-up documentation. If there is an error, the wire will be rejected online, and the wire

initiator will make any necessary corrections to the data. If all information is correct, the wire

will be approved. The payment approval confirmation should be attached to the documentation

for future reference. Upon completion of the wire transfer, the entry will be recorded in the

financial accounting system by the proper Finance employee.

The Town may establish a wire template for wires that will be completed on a regular basis for

items such as bond payments or regularly scheduled payments.

ACH Payments to Vendors:


The procedure to initiate an ACH payment is subject to the same procedures and controls that

govern disbursement by any other payment mechanism including a check payment. ACH

transactions will not be made without proper authorization of affected parties. Currently,

vendors are paid primarily through the check processes.

All vendor payment requests for ACH processing must be justified and reviewed by the Finance

Director. If approved, all security measures in place for wire transfers will apply.

Payroll Direct Deposit:


For the processing of Direct Deposit disbursements for payroll, each new employee is required

to complete a Direct Deposit Authorization Form. This form must contain name, signature and

bank information documentation to ensure proper setup. Account documentation must include

a voided check or a bank notification stating the bank’s routing number in addition to the

employee’s account number. The completed form is provided to Human Resources who will

forward it to Payroll. Information is entered from the form into the employee record within the

payroll system. If an employee wishes to change direct deposit information, a new form must

be completed in person and signed. This information is limited to Human Resources personnel

and Finance staff responsible for payroll. Al new employee setup or existing employee

changes will be reviewed and approved by the Human Resources Director and Payroll.

Page 13 of 59


The financial accounting system will generate an electronic direct deposit transmittal file and a

Direct Deposit report that will be used to complete the direct deposit transmission. The

electronic generated banking file from the financial accounting system is electronically

transmitted to the Town’s banking institution, authorizing the debit and credit of funds between

banks.

Once the direct deposit file is uploaded to the bank, the Finance Director will verify the amount

matches the Direct Deposit payroll reports.

Payroll Withholding:


The Town currently pays certain invoices relating to payroll withholdings and expenses through

an EFT process. Through a secure single user account, payroll staff initiate an ACH payment

on the vendor site for the specific amount identified for each payroll cycle. The information

including vendor, disbursement amount and payroll cycle is maintained along with confirmation

that the ACH disbursement was accepted by the vendor.

Additionally, EFT payments are processed through a secure IRS portal for federal taxes

withheld and employer and employee paid Social Security and Medicare taxes. The total of

payments transmitted are verified to the payroll system and general ledger entries.

Internal Controls:


The following internal controls are in place to validate that all available safety precautions are

utilized:

• Utilization of bank offered security measures to prevent unauthorized individuals from

initiating or modifying a transfer.

• Each user initiating or approving wire transfers must have a separate banking user

identification.

• Wire transfers are only initiated with approval of the Finance Director.

• Utilization of computer standards, policies and procedures to protect the computers and

computing processes used for EFTs from computer malware.

• Ensuring a secure process for creating, securing, sending and authenticating direct

deposit transmittal files to prevent unauthorized modification or submission.

• Bank balances are monitored daily for unusual or unexpected transactions.

• Bank balances are reconciled to the general ledger at least monthly.

• All confidential information will be stored in locked cabinets and in offices with locked

doors.

The proposed ordinance amends the Jonesborough Municipal Code Title 11 (Planning and

Zoning), Chapter 5 (General Provisions Relating to Zoning):

• Defines Data Center and Cryptocurrency Mining

• Adds parking standards for those uses

• Provides that the applicant must submit an application outlining regulations

• Important criteria include Yard, Location, and Separation Distances; Noise

Performance Standards; Water Usage and Wastewater Sustainability; Electrical

Demand and Grid Impact; and Annual Reporting

• The application must be approved by the Board of Mayor and Aldermen

The purpose of regulating data centers is because they are high-impact, infrastructure

intensive facilities that can significantly affect land use, utilities, and neighborhoods. Data

centers operate 24/7 and often include large cooling systems and rooftop mechanical

equipment; and backup generators (with periodic testing). Additionally, they can create noise

impacts, can strain water supply and wastewater operations, and may require new

substations or trigger transmission upgrades (or both).

The proposal safeguards potential impacts from data centers regarding noise, water and

wastewater, power, and creates predictable standards for developers.

Infrastructure improvements and costs associated with this type of development, industrial

land use, would be the responsibility of the owner and/or developer.

After some citizen comments about data centers at the Planning Commission, Town

Administrator Glenn Rosenoff added the following under the Noise Performance Standards:

• Such sound study shall be conducted using generally accepted methodology and

measured at the property line of any sensory receptor. This addition provides

clearer measurements standards as part of the required study.

• Fan Enclosure Requirements: All rooftop fans are required to be enclosed in

sound-attenuating housings that reduce output by 15-20 dBA.

• Equipment Enclosure: All cooling, ventilating, and power equipment are required to

be enclosed or screened to reduce both visual and noise impacts.

The final item under Old Business was discussion and possible action concerning

approval of an Ordinance amending the Town of Jonesborough, Tennessee, Municipal code,

Title 13, Chapter 2, Sewer Use and Wastewater Treatment in its entirety. At a minimum, EPA

and TDEC require a review of our pretreatment local limits every 5 years. This process is also

triggered whenever a significant change is made to any new or existing industrial discharge

to ensure it remains effective and up to date. The review process helps to identify any

changes in the treatment plant’s performance, influent flow, or pollutant characteristics that

may require adjustments to the local limits. This ensures that the pretreatment program

continues to meet the necessary standards and effectively reduces the discharge of

potentially toxic pollutants into the environment.

Page 22 of 59


While this process is necessary, amendments to other aspects of the Sewer Use Ordinance

(SUO) are often unnecessary. TDEC has acknowledged that recent changes, such as tap

fees, are minor and do not require prior approval. The National Pretreatment Regulations,

developed as part of the Streamlining Rule, required significant changes to Jonesborough’s

SUO. Jonesborough met these requirements with the revisions made in 2018. Since then, the

only significant changes have been the development of local limits.

Removing the publication of the local limits from the SUO in 13-208(2) and referencing the

TDEC approved local limits as an external source document, with the addition of 13-208(3),

allows Jonesborough to develop local limits without requiring future modification to the SUO.

This change will reduce the number of public notices published and shorten the overall

implementation timeframe during future review processes.

Town of Jonesborough Concession Stand Operating Rules and Regulation


The Town of Jonesborough Parks and Recreation Reserves the Right to operate the concession

stand for any and all events. In the event a concession stand is rented or leased, the below policies

and procedures are to be followed. The Parks and Recreation department operates concession

stands at Persimmon Ridge Park, Old Jonesborough Middle School, and Tiger Park.

Town of Jonesborough, Tennessee, acting through its Parks and Recreation Department


(hereinafter “Town” or “Licensor”), and

Approved user (hereinafter “Concessionaire”).

1. PURPOSE AND PREMISES


1.1 The Town grants to Concessionaire the non‐exclusive right (unless otherwise agreed by

amendment of this agreement) to operate the concession stand located at the requested facility, for

the purpose of selling food, drinks, and related concession‐type items (“Concession Services”) during

events as approved by the Town.

1.2 The concession stand facility, equipment, fixtures, and the area immediately surrounding it

(grounds, parking, walkways / bleachers, etc.) are included in the Premises for which this Agreement

applies.

2. OPERATIONS, HOURS AND EVENTS


2.1 Concessionaire shall operate the concession stand during all events at the requested Park as

scheduled by the Town or as otherwise agreed.

2.2 Concessionaire shall open and staff the concession stand during the hours required for such

events, and in such condition as may be required by the Town and in compliance with all health,

safety, building, and fire code requirements.

3. EXCLUSIVE SUPPLY OBLIGATIONS


3.1 Drink Sponsorship – Coke: The Town has an exclusive drink sponsorship agreement with Coca‐

Cola. Concessionaire must use Coca-Cola products (including water) as the sole provider of bottled /

canned / fountain drinks and must order exclusively through the designated Coke representative as

specified by the Town. All orders shall be approved and/or placed by Town employees designated

and approved by the Town Administrator or Town Recorder.

3.2 Food / Other Supplies – US Foods: The Town has an agreement with US Foods for supply of

certain basic items for resale. Concessionaire shall source those basic items through US Foods in

accordance with the supply agreement of the Town, except in cases where the Town approves

otherwise in writing. All orders shall be approved and/or placed by Town employees designated and

approved by the Town Administrator or Town Recorder.

3.3 Prices / Menu: Concessionaire shall submit a proposed menu and price list for Town approval

before commencement of operations or any modification. Prices should be reasonable and consistent

with comparable concessions.

4. BUILDING, EQUIPMENT & MAINTENANCE


4.1 Town Responsibilities – Ordinary and Reasonable Maintenance: The Town shall cover the cost of

ordinary, customary, and reasonable repairs necessary to preserve the building structure (roof, walls,

plumbing, electrical, permanent fixtures), and equipment provided by Town, except where damage

arises due to negligence, misuse, or willful misconduct by Concessionaire or its agents.

4.2 Concessionaire Responsibilities – Damage & Cleaning:

• Concessionaire is responsible for any damage beyond ordinary wear and tear or beyond what

would be expected in preserving the building. If Concessionaire or its employees, agents,

volunteers cause damage, Concessionaire shall repair or pay for repair (as determined by

Town).

• Concessionaire shall keep all Town‐provided equipment, fixtures, counters, sinks, serving

surfaces, etc., clean after each use. Failure to properly clean may result in delays in the Town

making payouts of any amounts owed to Concessionaire, and/or the imposition of cleaning

fees or deductions from those payouts.

4.3 Equipment Inventory: An inventory of all equipment provided by Town shall be attached as Exhibit

A. Concessionaire shall inspect and acknowledge condition at the start of term.

5. TRASH, SANITATION & AREA CLEANUP


5.1 Concessionaire shall be responsible for disposing of all trash generated in and around the

concession stand during and after each event. The location of disposal will be communicated to the

lessee as each site has its own disposal area.

5.2 Concessionaire shall walk surrounding areas, including fields, bleachers, walkways, dugouts, and

adjoining areas, after each event to clean up all litter or debris caused by attendees or Concession

operations.

5.3 Bathroom Cleaning: When bathrooms are in use in connection with events where concession

stand is operating, Concessionaire (or those using the concession stand for the event) shall clean the

bathrooms to a reasonable standard. The Town staff is typically not available during these events;


thus, the primary responsibility lies with Concessionaire or event‐organizers. Cleaning supplies will be

provided by the Town.

regulations applicable to operations, food service, health inspection, fire safety, labor/employment,

and environmental matters.

1. Purpose and How to Use This Booklet


This booklet is provided to organizations (“User Groups”) approved to operate a Parks and Recreation

concession stand during games, tournaments, leagues, and special events. It consolidates operational rules,

financial accountability standards, cleaning expectations, and vendor requirements so every group runs the stand

in a consistent, transparent, and safe manner.

The concession stand is a Town facility. Operating it is a privilege that requires careful stewardship of Town

property, accurate handling of public funds, and respectful treatment of patrons.

Key Principles

• All sales must be processed through Clover.

• Cleaning is required after each event day inside the stand, around the stand, on bleachers, and in restrooms.

• Coca‑Cola is the exclusive beverage supplier (including water).

• In most cases, the concession stand is stocked prior to your event.

• Failure to follow the procedures in this booklet can delay payouts and may impact future concession

eligibility.

2. Roles and Responsibilities


2.1 Town of Jonesborough Parks & Recreation

The Town provides the concession facility, certain equipment, and operational oversight, including training on

Clover and reconciliation procedures. The Town also retains the right to set or update policies necessary for

financial accountability and facility preservation.

2.2 User Group

The User Group is responsible for staffing, daily operations, safe and courteous customer service, and meeting

all cleanup and reconciliation requirements described in this booklet.

2.3 Concession Lead

Each User Group should designate one individual as the Concession Lead for each event day. The Concession

Lead is responsible for ensuring procedures are followed, forms are complete, deposits are secured, and the

facility is locked and left in good condition.

• Ensure all staff know the opening/closing procedures.

• Keep the reconciliation form with the drawer throughout the shift.

• Verify refunds are documented and included in end-of-f-day paperwork.

• Verify trash/grounds/restrooms are addressed before leaving.

• Be the primary contact for Town staff questions during the event.

3. Revenue Split and Payout Process


Revenue split arrangements will be determined as part of the approved rental or use agreement for each event or

organization.

3.1 Net Revenue Definition

Net revenue equals total gross sales minus allowable deductions, including:

• Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): food, beverage, and supply costs purchased for resale through approved

channels.

• Refunds: any customer refunds processed via Check Request.

• Fees: applicable payment processing fees and any system-related fees reflected in Clover reporting.

• Cleaning fees or damage charges (only if assessed due to non-compliance or damage outside ordinary wear

and tear).

3.2 Documentation Required for Payout

• Nightly reconciliation form completed and signed by the Concession Lead (or designee).

• Clover end-of-f-day report printed and included with the deposit paperwork.

• All refunds documented and included in reconciliation totals.

• Deposit bag contents match the reconciliation form.

3.3 Payout Timing

Checks for the User Group’s net revenue share are typically issued within ten (10) business days after the Town

receives complete, accurate paperwork. Incomplete forms, missing reports, or unresolved discrepancies may

delay payout.

4. Required Systems and Approved Vendors


4.1 Mandatory Point-of-f-Sale System: Clover

Clover is the required system for all concession transactions. No off-f-system sales are permitted. All cash and

card payments must be rung through Clover to ensure accurate reconciliation, reporting, and auditability.

4.2 Exclusive Beverage Sponsor: Coca‑Cola

Coca‑Cola is the exclusive beverage supplier for Parks and Recreation concessions. All beverage products,

including bottled water, must be ordered through the Town’s designated Coca‑Cola representative. No outside

beverage brands may be sold. In most cases, drinks are stocked prior to the event.

4.3 Approved Food Supplier: US Foods

The Town maintains an agreement with US Foods to supply basic concession items. User Groups must source

applicable items through US Foods unless written approval is provided by the Town for an exception. In most

cases food is stocked prior to the event.

5. Clover POS: Daily Use Standards

5.1 Logins and Accountability

• Use only your pre-assigned Clover code to access the register.

• Do not share codes.

• The Concession Lead should know who is assigned to each code during the event.

5.2 Transaction Standards

• Ring every sale through Clover—r—cash and card.

• Provide receipts when requested.

• Do not ‘round’ totals outside Clover; use Clover discount functions only when approved.

• Process refunds through Refund Request Form (see Section 6.4).

5.3 Reporting

At the end of each event day, print the end-of-f-day report (exact report name may vary by Clover configuration).

Include this printed report with your reconciliation paperwork in the deposit bag.

6. Cash Handling Policy – – Attached Below


This cash handling policy applies to every User Group operating a Parks and Recreation concession stand.

Compliance is required to protect the User Group, Town staff, and the integrity of payouts.

6.1 Starting Cash

Starting cash for the register is provided by the User Group unless otherwise agreed in writing. The Town

strongly recommends starting with the same amount each day to reduce errors and confusion.

6.2 Opening Shift

Follow these steps before selling your first item:

• Confirm you have your starting cash (bills and coins) organized for making change.

• Log into Clover using your pre-assigned code.

• Open the register and insert your starting cash.

• Complete the reconciliation form: record the starting cash amount and the date.

• Sign the reconciliation form (Concession Lead or designee).

• Store the reconciliation form under the cash drawer (recommended) until the shift ends.

6.3 During-Shift Standards

• Keep the drawer closed when not actively making change.

• Never mix personal money with register money.

• Do not ‘borrow’ from the drawer for any purpose.

• If the drawer must be left unattended, secure it per Town instruction.

• If a discrepancy is noticed during the shift, notify the Concession Lead immediately.

6.4 Refunds and Voids

Refunds can only be processed by completing the refund request form. The Town will issue a check for the

refund. If anyone requests a refund, the refund amount must be recorded on the reconciliation form and reflected

in reporting.

• Keep any printed receipts associated with refunds/voids with your daily paperwork.

6.5 Closing Shift

At the end of the day, complete the following steps in order:

• Close sales in Clover and prepare to print the end-of-f-day report.

• Count all cash and coins in the drawer (do not rush).

• Record the counted cash and coins on the reconciliation form.

• Print the Clover end-of-f-day report and attach it to the reconciliation form.

• Fill in the cash sales and card sales totals from Clover.

• Note any refunds processed that day.

• Calculate overage/shortage. If there is a reason, explain it briefly; if accurate, leave explanation blank.

• Place the reconciliation form and printed Clover report in the deposit bag with the cash and any required

receipts.

6.6 Deposit Drop Procedure

After closing and verifying totals, secure the deposit bag and place it in the designated drop safe. Town staff will

retrieve deposits the following business morning.

Concession Stand Cash Handling Policy


All concession operations in the Parks and Recreation system shall utilize the Town-approved Clover Point-off

Sale (POS) system for processing transactions. The Clover system serves as the official record of all sales

activity, and daily cash reports must reconcile with Clover transaction reports.

At the beginning of each operating shift, authorized personnel will be issued a designated amount of start-up

cash (“cash drawer”) by the Town or an approved user organization. This start-up cash is intended solely for

making change and shall not be used fofor purchasing inventory or other expenses. Staff are responsible for

safeguarding all cash while it is in their possession.

All cash collected during operations must be secured in the drop safe located within the concession stand. The

drop safe code shall not be shared with any user group, volunteer, or concession operator. The safe may only be

opened by the Parks and Recreation Director or an authorized Town designee. Under no circumstances should

cash be stored outside of the designated safe or left unattended.

At the close of each operating period, staff must count all cash and complete the official Town deposit sheet and

daily reconciliation report. The total cash on hand must match the Clover sales report, accounting for the start

up cash amount provided.

Daily cash reconciliation reports must be counted and signed by two adult individuals who participated in the

counting process. Their signatures confirm the accuracy of the cash count and reconciliation.

If the cash count is less than the amount reflected in the Clover report, the shortage must be clearly documented

on a separate line of the reconciliation report and shall not be deducted from reported revenue totals. This

procedure ensures accurate tracking of sales and prevents reconciliation imbalances at the conclusion of the

event. Any verified shortages will be deducted from funds owed to the user organization during the concession

settlement process. Discrepancies may also result in delayed payment processing while the matter is reviewed.

Refunds are not permitted through the Clover POS system. All refunds must be requested through the Town of

Jonesborough and will be issued by official Town check only, following standard municipal financial

procedures.

All individuals operating the concession stand or handling funds are required to complete Town-provided cash

handling and POS training prior to assuming duties. Compliance with this policy is mandatory and subject to

review by the Town.
