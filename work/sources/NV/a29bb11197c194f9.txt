RESOLUTION NO. 2026- 18

A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF
WHITE PINE COUNTY, NEVADA, IMPOSING A TEMPORARY
MORATORIUM ON THE ACCEPTANCE, PROCESSING, AND APPROVAL OF
LAND USE APPLICATIONS FOR DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT IN WHITE
PINE COUNTY, PENDING COMPLETION AND ADOPTION OF DATA
CENTER-SPECIFIC ZONING AND LAND USE REGULATIONS; DIRECTING
STAFF TO PREPARE SUCH REGULATIONS; AND PROVIDING FOR
RELATED MATTERS.

WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of White Pine County, Nevada (the
"Board") is the duly elected governing body of White Pine County (the
County") and possesses all powersmecessary and proper to protect the public
health, safety,.and welfare of County residents pursuant to NRS 244.137 through
244.146 and Article 4, Section 25 of the Nevada Constitution:

WHEREAS, the Board is authorized to establish zoning districts and regulate the use of
land in the“uni corporated areas of the County pursuant to Nevada Revised
Statutes (NRS) Chapier 278, and specifiéally’NRS 278.250 and 278.260, and'to
adopt and amend the*County's Master Plan pursuant to NRS 2787150 through
278.220:

WHEREAS, data centers are large-scalecommereial and industrial facilities housing
server infrastructure that stores and processes data for cloud computing, artificial
intelligence applications, e-commerce, financial transactions, and national
security systems; such facilities are among the most intensive consumers of
electricity and water of any commercial:land use type;

WHEREAS, as of mid-2026, approximately 70 data center locations are planned, under
construction, or operational in Nevada; and the rapid expansion of artificial
intelligence tools has dramatically accelerated demand for new data center
development throughout the state, including in rural Nevada counties;


WHEREAS, at the April 24, 2026 meeting of the Nevada Legislature's Joint Interim
Standing Committee on Public Lands, White Pine County officials and Bureau of
Land Management representatives discussed proposed data center projects in
White Pine County, signaling that the County has been identified by the data
center industry as a potential development target;

WHEREAS, the County's current Title 17 Zoning Code does not define or classify data
centers as a distinct land use category, and does not contain specific development
standards addressing the substantial impacts that data center facilities impose on
water resources, electric infrastructure, public roads, emergency services, noise,
and community character;

WHEREAS, the County's existing approval process for industrial and commercial Jand
uses — including zoning compliance review, special use and conditional use
permits, site plan approval, building permits, fire code review, utility agreements,
and road and infrastructure assessments — was not designed with the scale,
intensity, and unique impacts of data center development in mind;

WHEREAS, Nevada is the driest state in the nation, with annual rainfall averaging 7.1
inches in southern Nevada and 12.85 inches in the northeast, and more than half
of the state's groundwater basins are over-appropriated, meaning that more water
rights have been allocated than water physically exists underground;

WHEREAS, White Pine County's Steptoe Valley aquifer is already experiencing
decline, and the County's groundwater resources are substantially committed to
existing agricultural, municipal, and mining uses; modem data centers can
require between 300,000 and 5 million gallons of water per day depending on
size and cooling technology, and without the adoption of water-use standards the
County has no mechanism to assess or limit the impact of data center
development on its scarce water supplies;

WHEREAS, White Pine County is served by a rural electric infrastructure more limited
than that of Nevada's urban corridor; center cooling systems alone can account
for over 40 percent of a facility's electricity consumption; and the County has not
assessed the capacity of the local transmission grid to accommodate large-scale
data center demand without adverse impacts to existing residential and
commercial ratepayers;

WHEREAS, once a data center receives a land use approval, vested rights may attach,
limiting the County's ability to impose more protective standards in the future;
water rights once allocated to a data center cannot easily be reclaimed; and road
and utility infrastructure commitments create long-term obligations, such that the
harm from approval under inadequate standards is, in material respects,
irreversible;



WHEREAS, multiple Nevada local governments have recently imposed or are actively
considering temporary moratoriums on data center development, including: the
City of Reno, which imposed a moratorium through August 2027; Nye County,
which imposed a moratorium pending adoption of an ordinance estimated to take.
approximately 90 days to draft; Humboldt County, which adopted a temporary
moratorium on July 6, 2026, running through July 13, 2027, while updating its
Regional Master Plan and drafting a data center ordinance; and the City of
Henderson, which is considering a 180-day moratorium;

WHEREAS, these actions by peer Nevada jurisdictions confirm that a temporary
moratorium is a recognized, reasonable, and legally defensible exercise of local
government planning authority to protect the public while comprehensive
regulations are developed;

WHEREAS, the Board finds that the imposition of a temporary moratorium on the
acceptance, processing, and approval of data center land use applications is
necessary and appropriate to protect the public health, safety, and welfare of
White Pine County residents; to preserve the County's scarce water resources and
rural electric infrastructure; and to allow staff sufficient time to study the impacts
of data center development and prepare regulations adequate to address those
impacts; and

WHEREAS, the Board further finds that this moratorium is an interim emergency
measure adopted pursuant to.the County's police powers and planning authority,
and that the moratorium period is the minimum time reasonably necessary to
accomplish its protective purpose.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners
of White Pine County, Nevada, as follows:

Section 1. Findings Incorporated. The Board hereby finds and declares that each of
the recitals set forth above is true and correct and is hereby incorporated into this
Resolution as a finding of the Board.

Section 2. Moratorium Imposed. Effective upon adoption of this Resolution, a
temporary moratorium (the "Moratorium") is hereby imposed on the acceptance,
processing, and approval of the following land use applications within White Pine
County where the proposed use is a data center:

Applications for rezoning or zoning map amendments;

Applications for special use permits or conditional use permits;
Applications for site plan approval;

Applications for building permits for new data center construction; and

Any other discretionary land use application where a data center is the primary
proposed use.



For purposes of this Resolution, “data center" means a facility or building, or portion
thereof, used primarily to house computer servers, networking equipment, and related
infrastructure for the purpose of storing, managing, processing, or disseminating data,
including but not limited to hyperscale data centers, colocation data centers,
enterprise data centers, and modular or prefabricated data center facilities. The term
includes facilities proposed to be developed in phases where the ultimate use is data
center operations.

Section 3. Exceptions. The Moratorium shall not apply to:

e Data center projects that have received all required final land use approvals prior
to the effective date of this Resolution, and for which building permits were
issued prior to the effective date of this Resolution, provided that such permits
remain valid and in force;

e Applications for building permits solely for the maintenance, repair, or tenant
improvement of an existing, lawfully operating data center that do not increase
the facility's footprint or intensity of use;

¢ Modifications or amendments to existing approved pemnits.for data centers,
where such modifications do not require a new or amended discretionary land use
approval; and

e Emergency repairs necessary to protect public safety at an existing lawfully
operating data center.

Section 4. Duration. The Moratorium shall be in effect for a period of one hundred
eighty (180) calendar days from the effective date of this Resolution, unless sooner
terminated by action of the Board upon adoption of data center-specific zoning and land
use regulations applicable to White Pine County, or unless extended pursuant to Section 5
of this. Resolution.

Section 5. Extension. Prior to the expiration of the Moratorium, the Board may, by
Resolution adopted after a duly noticed public hearing, extend the Moratorium for one
additional period not to exceed one hundred eighty (180) days, upon findings that: (a) the
planning study and ordinance development process directed in Section 6 has not been
completed; (b) continuation of the Moratorium is reasonably necessary to protect the
public health, safety, or welfare; and (c) the Moratorium as extended does not constitute
an undue or unreasonable restriction on the use of private property. The Board shall not
extend the Moratorium beyond such time as is reasonably necessary to adopt permanent
data center regulations.

Section 6. Direction to Planning Staff. is hereby directed to undertake the following
tasks during the Moratorium period, and to report on progress to the Board at intervals of
not less than thirty (30) days:



Conduct a water supply and demand analysis in coordination with the Nevada
Division of Water Resources to assess adjudicated water rights, existing
commitments, available supplies, and the capacity of County water resources to
accommodate data center development;

Assess electric grid capacity and transmission infrastructure constraints in
coordination with Mt. Wheeler Power and the Nevada Rural Electric Association,
including analysis of potential cost impacts on existing County ratepayers;
Evaluate road, traffic, and public infrastructure capacity to accommodate
construction-period and operational traffic associated with data center facilities;
Review and compile data center regulations adopted or under development by
other Nevada jurisdictions, including the City of Reno, Humboldt County, Nye
County, the City of Fernley, the City of Henderson, and others, as well as any
best practices published by the Truckee Meadows Regional Planning Agency or
other regional or state planning bodies;

Draft proposed amendments to White Pine County Code Title 17 (Zoning) to
define data center as a distinct use category and establish development standards
addressing, at a minimum: permitted zoning districts; conditional use permit
requirements; setbacks from residential uses and property boundaries; noise
standards and monitoring; water use efficiency and disclosure requirements;
cooling technology standards; backup power and generator requirements; air
quality compliance; utility service agreements and cost allocation; buffering and
visual screening; fire and emergency access; road improvement obligations;
decommissioning and site restoration; and community benefit agreement
requirements;

Prepare a model Development Agreement template for data center projects for
use in conjunction with any conditional use permit approval;

Conduct at least one duly noticed public community meeting to receive input
from County residents, property owners, and other stakeholders prior to
presenting the draft ordinance to the Board of Commissioners; and

Present the draft data center ordinance to the White Pine County Board of
Commissioners for a duly noticed public hearing and recommendation to the
Board, and thereafter to the Board for consideration and adoption prior to
expiration of the Moratorium.

Section 7. Pending Applications. Any data center land use application that was
submitted to the County prior to the effective date of this Resolution and on which final
action has not yet been taken shall be suspended as of the effective date of this Resolution
and shall remain suspended for the duration of the Moratorium. The County shall notify
all applicants with pending applications of the suspension within ten (10) calendar days
of the effective date of this Resolution. Application fees paid for suspended applications

. Shall be retained in full and applied to the applicable application upon reinstatement
following expiration or termination of the Moratorium.

Section 8. No Vested Rights. Nothing in this Resolution shall be construed to create
vested rights in favor of any applicant or property owner with respect to any data center
development that has not received all required final land use approvals as of the effective
date of this Resolution.


Section 9. Severability. If any provision of this Resolution, or its application to any
person or circumstance, is held invalid by a court of competent jurisdiction, the
remaining provisions of this Resolution and their application to other persons or
circumstances shall remain in full force and effect.

Section 10. Effective Date. This Resolution shall take effect immediately upon its
adoption by the Board of County Commissioners of White Pine County, Nevada.

Section 11. Recording and Notice. The County Clerk is directed to cause this
Resolution to be recorded in the Official Records of White Pine County and to publish a
summary notice of the Moratorium in a newspaper of general circulation in White Pine
County within ten (10) calendar days of adoption. The Board of Commissioners shall
post notice of the Moratorium on the County's website and at the Building/Planning
Department's public counter.
