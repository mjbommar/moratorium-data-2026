CITY OF FAFAIRFAFAX, IOWAWA


ORDINANCE NO. [____]


AN ORDINANCE IMPOSING A MORATATORIUM ON HIGH-IMPAPACT DATATA TA CENTERS AND AMENDING THE CITY ZONING CODE TO ESTATABLISH PROHIBITED USE CLASSIFICATATION, UTILITY AND INFRASTRUCTURE CERTIFICATATION REQUIREMENTS, AND EXCLUSIVE ELIGIBILITY IN DESIGNATATED HEAVAVY INFRASTRUCTURE DISTRICTS


BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF FAFAIRFAFAX, IOWAWA:


SECTION 1. PURPOSE AND INTENT


The City Council of the City of Fairfax, Iowa, finds and determines that High-Impact

Data Centers represent a distinct category of industrial infrastructure with operational

characteristics, utility demands, and potential community impacts that are substantially

difffferent from traditional commercial, residential, and light industrial uses.

High-Impact Data Centers may require extraordinary electrical capacity, dedicated utility

infrastructure, significant water resources, specialized cooling systems, expanded

transmission or distribution infrastructure, increased construction activity, and other

municipal or regional infrastructure considerations beyond those typically associated

with existing permitted uses within the City.

The City Council further finds that the orderly development of land within the City

requires that uses with utility-scale infrastructure demands be evaluated through a

separate and deliberate legislative process to ensure compatibility with existing and

future land-use plans, protection of public infrastructure, preservation of municipal

services, and protection of the public health, safety, and welfare.

The purpose and intent of this Ordinance is to:

1. Establish an immediate moratorium on the acceptance, processing, review, or

approval of High-Impact Data Center applications within the City of Fairfax;

2. Establish High-Impact Data Centers as a prohibited use in all existing zoning

districts unless expressly authorized by a future ordinance adopted by the City

Council;

3. Require that any future consideration of High-Impact Data Centers be subject to

specific legislative authorization, infrastructure capacity review, utility certification

requirements, economic and workforce impact evaluation, and all other

requirements established by the City;

4. Ensure that any party seeking approval of a High-Impact Data Center bears the

full burden of demonstrating compliance with all applicable requirements

established by the City; and

5. Protect the City’s ability to evaluate future development proposals based upon

long-term community impacts, infrastructure capacity, responsible growth, and

the public interest.

Nothing in this Ordinance shall be interpreted as creating any entitlement, expectation,

or right for any person or entity to obtain approval of a High-Impact Data Center within

the City of Fairfax.

SECTION 2. MORATATORIUM AND IMPLEMENTATATATION


A moratorium is hereby imposed on the acceptance, filing, processing, review,

consideration, approval, or issuance of any permit, zoning application, site plan

approval, subdivision approval, utility extension approval, building permit, or any other

municipal approval related to the development, construction, expansion, or operation of

any High-Impact Data Center within the City of Fairfax.

The moratorium shall apply to all new High-Impact Data Centers, expansions of existing

facilities that would result in classification as a High-Impact Data Center, and any

phased or related development determined to constitute a High-Impact Data Center

pursuant to this Ordinance.

The moratorium shall remain in effffect unless modified or repealed by a subsequent

ordinance adopted by the City Council.

This moratorium is enacted as a legislative land-use measure to allow the City to

establish appropriate standards for evaluating utility-scale digital infrastructure and to

ensure that such uses do not proceed without appropriate consideration of land-use

compatibility, infrastructure capacity, utility impacts, economic considerations, and

protection of the public health, safety, and welfare.

During the effffective period of this moratorium, the City shall not accept, process, or

consider any application that seeks approval of a High-Impact Data Center as a

permitted, conditional, special, accessory, or otherwise allowable use under the City's

existing zoning regulations.

Nothing in this Section shall be interpreted as creating a requirement or obligation for

the City Council to establish a zoning district, approve a zoning amendment, or

otherwise authorize High-Impact Data Centers in the future.

SECTION 3. HIGH-IMPAPACT DATATA TA CENTER DEFINITION


For purposes of this Ordinance, a “High-Impact Data Center” means any facility,


campus, or development engaged in large-scale computational processing, artificial

intelligence computing, cloud computing, cryptocurrency processing, data storage, or

similar digital infrastructure operations that, by reason of its energy consumption, water

demand, cooling requirements, infrastructure needs, or operational characteristics,

creates impacts substantially difffferent from traditional commercial or light industrial

uses.

A facility shall be considered a High-Impact Data Center if it meets any one or more of

the following criteria:

A. Electrical Demand.

The facility has a planned, requested, contracted, or anticipated electrical

demand of ten (10) megawatts or greater, whether measured by initial operation,

peak demand, phased development, or ultimate planned capacity.

B. Dedicated Utility Infrastructure.

The facility requires or anticipates the need for dedicated electrical substations,

transmission upgrades, significant distribution system improvements, grid-scale

interconnection, or other utility infrastructure beyond typical commercial or light

industrial service requirements.

C. Industrial-Scale Cooling Requirements.

The facility utilizes or requires industrial-scale cooling systems, including but not

limited to water-based cooling, liquid cooling, cooling towers, chilled water

systems, or other specialized thermal management infrastructure.

D. Backup Generation Systems.

The facility utilizes or requires large-scale backup electrical generation systems,

including generators or other onsite power systems, necessary to maintain

continuous operations during electrical interruptions or grid disruptions.

E. Utility-Scale Operational Characteristics.

The facility demonstrates continuous energy, water, wastewater, or other

infrastructure demands that materially exceed those normally associated with

existing commercial or light industrial uses within the City.

F. Functional Infrastructure Determination.

A facility may be classified as a High-Impact Data Center if, based upon its

proposed design, operational characteristics, infrastructure requirements, or

anticipated impacts, the facility functions as utility-scale digital infrastructure

rather than a traditional commercial or light industrial use.

The determination of whether a proposed use constitutes a High-Impact Data Center

shall be based upon the actual or reasonably anticipated function, infrastructure

demand, and operational characteristics of the facility, regardless of the name,

ownership structure, business classification, industry designation, or other description

applied by the applicant.

This Ordinance shall be interpreted based upon the intensity, infrastructure

requirements, and community impacts of a proposed use and not solely upon the

technology, industry, or business activity conducted within the facility.

SECTION 4. AGGREGATATION, FUNCTIONAL INTEGRATATION, AND ANTI-PHASING REQUIREMENTS


For purposes of determining whether a proposed development constitutes a

High-Impact Data Center, the City shall evaluate the totality of the proposed

development and shall not allow the division, separation, or phasing of a development

to avoid classification under this Ordinance.

A. Aggregation of Related Development.

All buildings, structures, phases, expansions, parcels, equipment, infrastructure,

or related components that are part of a common development plan or that are

functionally integrated shall be considered together as a single facility for

purposes of determining whether the development qualifies as a High-Impact

Data Center.

B. Common Ownership, Control, or Operation.

Development components shall be aggregated when they are under common

ownership, common control, common operation, common lease arrangement,

shared infrastructure agreement, or otherwise function as part of a coordinated

development, regardless of whether such components are held by separate legal

entities.

C. Phased Development.

The City shall evaluate proposed development based upon the full reasonably

foreseeable buildout of the project, including all planned, anticipated, or

reasonably related future phases. An applicant shall not avoid classification as a

High-Impact Data Center by limiting the initial phase of development below

applicable thresholds.

D. Shared Infrastructure Evaluation.

Facilities that share or rely upon common electrical infrastructure, substations,

transmission connections, water systems, wastewater systems, cooling systems,

road improvements, or other significant infrastructure shall be evaluated as a

single development for purposes of this Ordinance.

E. Expansion Classification.

Any expansion, modification, or addition to an existing facility that causes the

facility to meet the definition of a High-Impact Data Center shall result in

classification of the entire integrated facility as a High-Impact Data Center.

F. Burden of Demonstrating Separate Development.

The applicant shall bear the burden of demonstrating, by competent and credible

evidence, that separate parcels, facilities, phases, or components are

independent developments and should not be aggregated under this Ordinance.

SECTION 5. PROHIBITED USE CLASSIFICATATION


High-Impact Data Centers are hereby classified as a PROHIBITED USE within all


existing zoning districts of the City of Fairfax, including but not limited to residential,

commercial, mixed-use, and light industrial zoning districts.

No existing zoning district within the City shall be interpreted to permit, conditionally

permit, or otherwise authorize the development, construction, expansion, or operation of

a High-Impact Data Center.

A High-Impact Data Center shall not be approved through a conditional use permit,

special exception, variance, administrative interpretation, site plan approval,

development agreement, or any other discretionary process unless and until the City

Council expressly authorizes such use through a future ordinance establishing

appropriate zoning classification and requirements.

The classification of High-Impact Data Centers as a prohibited use is based upon the

City Council’s legislative determination that such facilities represent a distinct land use

category with infrastructure, utility, environmental, transportation, and operational

characteristics that require separate consideration from traditional commercial and

industrial uses.

Nothing in this Section shall be interpreted as creating an obligation, commitment, or

expectation that the City Council will establish a zoning district or otherwise authorize

High-Impact Data Centers in the future.

SECTION 6. NO RIGHT TO CONSIDERATATION


A High-Impact Data Center shall have no right to zoning review, conditional use permit

consideration, variance consideration, site plan review, development agreement

consideration, or any other discretionary review process within any zoning district where

such use is not expressly authorized by ordinance.

The submission of an application, request for information, concept plan, pre-application

materials, utility discussions, or any other communication regarding a High-Impact Data

Center shall not create any right, vested interest, expectation of approval, or entitlement

to continued processing under any existing or prior zoning regulation.

Applications submitted for a High-Impact Data Center in a zoning district where such

use is prohibited shall be rejected and shall not be processed.

The City shall have no obligation to modify its zoning regulations, create a new zoning

classification, provide alternative locations, or otherwise take legislative action to

accommodate a proposed High-Impact Data Center.

Nothing in this Section shall limit the authority of the City Council to consider future

legislative amendments, zoning changes, or other policy decisions regarding

High-Impact Data Centers in accordance with applicable law.

SECTION 7. MITIGATATION DOES NOT CREATATE ZONING ELIGIBILITY


No payment, fee, infrastructure contribution, utility agreement, tax arrangement,

economic development incentive, community benefit agreement, mitigation proposal, or

other offffered compensation shall:

A. Convert a prohibited High-Impact Data Center use into a permitted use; B. Create zoning eligibility where such eligibility does not otherwise exist;


C. Establish any right, entitlement, or expectation of approval; or


D. Substitute for compliance with the zoning requirements, infrastructure


standards, utility certifications, and other requirements established by this

Ordinance.

The City Council finds that mitigation measures, financial contributions, or development

agreements may address certain impacts of development but do not eliminate the need

for proper land-use classification and zoning compatibility review.

No agreement, payment, contribution, or proposed benefit shall limit, waive, or

supersede the City’s authority to determine whether a proposed use is appropriate for a

particular location.

Zoning classification shall control over any private agreement, economic incentive

arrangement, or negotiated mitigation proposal.

SECTION 8. UTILITY AND INFRASTRUCTURE CERTIFICATATION REQUIREMENT


No High-Impact Data Center shall be eligible for consideration unless the applicant

demonstrates compliance with all applicable utility and infrastructure requirements

established by this Ordinance.

The applicant shall bear the burden of obtaining and submitting verified certifications

from applicable utility providers, governmental entities, and infrastructure authorities

demonstrating that the proposed facility will not create unacceptable impacts to existing

customers, municipal services, or public infrastructure.

Certifications shall be prepared or confirmed by the applicable utility provider or

responsible authority and shall not rely solely upon statements, projections, or

representations provided by the applicant or its consultants.

The required certifications shall include, but not be limited to, the following:

A. Electrical Capacity Certification.

Certification from the applicable electric utility provider confirming that adequate

electrical capacity exists to serve the proposed facility without degrading

reliability or service quality for existing customers, or documentation confirming

that all required upgrades are fully funded, approved, scheduled, and completed

prior to operation.

B. Water Supply Certification.

Certification from the applicable municipal or regional water provider confirming

that adequate water supply capacity exists to serve the proposed facility without

negatively affffecting existing customers, community needs, or future planned

growth, or documentation confirming that all required expansions are fully

funded, approved, scheduled, and completed prior to operation.

C. Wastewater Capacity Certification.

Certification from the applicable wastewater authority confirming adequate

treatment and collection capacity exists for the proposed facility without reducing

service reliability, exceeding permitted capacity, or creating additional burdens on

existing users, or documentation confirming all required improvements are fully

funded, approved, scheduled, and completed prior to operation.

D. Grid Interconnection Certification.

Certification from the applicable electric utility provider and relevant regulatory

authorities confirming approval of all required substations, transmission

improvements, distribution upgrades, and grid interconnection requirements

necessary to serve the proposed facility.

E. No Ratepayer Impact Certification.

Certification from the applicable utility provider confirming that costs associated

with serving the proposed facility, including generation, transmission, distribution,

capacity, infrastructure, and operational requirements, will not be shifted to

existing residential, commercial, or industrial utility customers.

F. Public Infrastructure Impact Review.

The applicant shall provide documentation identifying anticipated impacts to

public infrastructure, including but not limited to roads, emergency services,

stormwater systems, and other municipal resources, together with proposed

measures necessary to address such impacts.

Failure to provide complete and verified certifications required by this Section shall

constitute grounds for rejection of the application.

SECTION 9. ECONOMIC AND WORKFORCE IMPAPACT ANALYLYSIS


No High-Impact Data Center shall be eligible for consideration unless the applicant

submits a comprehensive third-party impact analysis, funded by the applicant pursuant

to the City's cost recovery requirements, evaluating the anticipated economic,

workforce, environmental, and community impacts of the proposed development.

The applicant shall bear the burden of demonstrating that the proposed facility provides

sufffficient public benefit and does not create unacceptable burdens on the City, its

residents, existing businesses, or public resources.

The analysis shall include, but not be limited to, the following:

A. Economic Impact Analysis.

The applicant shall provide an independent economic analysis evaluating:

1. The anticipated economic benefits of the proposed facility;

2. The estimated permanent economic activity generated within the City;

3. The anticipated impact on existing businesses, employment sectors, and

economic development opportunities;

4. The ratio of land consumption, infrastructure demand, and public resource

utilization compared to anticipated economic benefits; and

5. Any requested public incentives, subsidies, tax arrangements, or

economic development assistance.

B. Workforce and Local Employment Analysis.

The applicant shall provide a workforce impact analysis evaluating:

1. The number of permanent full-time jobs expected to be created;

2. The number of temporary construction jobs expected to be created;

3. The wage levels, benefits, and skill requirements associated with

permanent positions;

4. The anticipated availability of qualified local workers;

5. The applicant's plans to prioritize local hiring and workforce development

opportunities; and

6. Whether the proposed facility is expected to displace, reduce, or

negatively impact existing employment opportunities within the City or

surrounding region.

The City may consider the permanent employment created by the proposed

facility in relation to the facility's demands on land, utilities, infrastructure, and

public services.

C. Environmental Impact Analysis.

The applicant shall provide an environmental impact analysis evaluating:

1. Expected water consumption and water sourcing;

2. Wastewater generation and disposal;

3. Cooling system operations and associated chemical use;

4. Potential air emissions, including emissions associated with backup

generation systems;

5. Noise impacts;

6. Hazardous materials, chemicals, or substances used or stored on-site;

7. Waste generation and disposal methods; and

8. Any other environmental impacts identified by the City.

D. Community Impact Analysis.

The applicant shall evaluate impacts to:

1. Public infrastructure;

2. Emergency services;

3. Transportation systems and construction traffffic;

4. Municipal services;

5. Existing residents and businesses; and

6. Long-term community planning goals.

E. Failure to Demonstrate Compliance.

Failure to provide complete, accurate, and verifiable information required by this

Section, or failure to demonstrate that the proposed facility provides sufffficient

community benefit relative to its impacts, shall constitute grounds for rejection of

the application.

SECTION 10. EXCLUSIVE ZONING ELIGIBILITY REQUIREMENT


High-Impact Data Centers shall not be permitted within any existing zoning district of the

City of Fairfax unless expressly authorized by a future ordinance adopted by the City

Council.

No existing zoning district, including residential, commercial, mixed-use, or light

industrial districts, shall be interpreted to allow a High-Impact Data Center as a

permitted use, conditional use, special exception, accessory use, or otherwise allowable

development.

Any future consideration of a High-Impact Data Center shall require the City Council to

first adopt an ordinance specifically establishing the applicable zoning classification,

standards, and requirements for such use.

Any future zoning classification allowing consideration of High-Impact Data Centers

shall include, at a minimum:

A. Specific identification of High-Impact Data Centers as an eligible use;


B. Appropriate location and land-use compatibility standards; C. Utility and infrastructure certification requirements;


D. Economic, workforce, environmental, and community impact analysis


requirements;

E. Any additional development standards determined necessary by the City


Council to protect the public health, safety, welfare, and long-term interests of the

City.

Nothing in this Section shall be interpreted as requiring the City Council to create a

zoning district, amend the zoning code, designate a location, or authorize High-Impact

Data Centers in the future.

SECTION 11. ADMINISTRATATIVE DETERMINATATION


The City offfficial responsible for zoning administration, as designated by the City

Council, shall review proposed uses and determine whether such use constitutes a

High-Impact Data Center pursuant to the definitions and standards established in this

Ordinance.

In making such determination, the City offfficial shall consider the totality of the proposed

use, including but not limited to:

A. Electrical demand and anticipated utility requirements; B. Water consumption and cooling system requirements; C. Wastewater generation and treatment requirements;


D. Required infrastructure improvements or expansions; E. Operational characteristics and intensity of use; F. Whether the proposed facility functions as utility-scale digital infrastructure


rather than a traditional commercial or light industrial use; and

G. Whether related facilities, phases, parcels, or infrastructure should be


aggregated pursuant to Section 4 of this Ordinance.

The determination shall be based upon the actual or reasonably anticipated function

and impacts of the proposed development and shall not be controlled solely by the

applicant’s description, business classification, ownership structure, or proposed project

name.

If the City offfficial determines that a proposed use constitutes a High-Impact Data

Center, the use shall be subject to all applicable requirements, restrictions, and

prohibitions established by this Ordinance.

Any applicant affffected by a classification determination may appeal such determination

to the City Council in accordance with procedures established by the City.

Nothing in this Section shall be interpreted as granting the City offfficial authority to

approve a prohibited use or waive any requirement of this Ordinance.

SECTION 12. NO IMPLIED PERMISSIBILITY OR ENTITLEMENT


The absence of a specific prohibition, reference, or classification within any zoning

district or other City regulation shall not be interpreted as allowing, authorizing, or

creating eligibility for a High-Impact Data Center.

High-Impact Data Centers shall be considered prohibited unless expressly authorized

by ordinance adopted by the City Council.

The City’s failure to identify a particular development scenario, technology, ownership

structure, business model, or operational approach shall not create an entitlement,

vested right, or expectation that such use is permitted.

Any determination regarding the applicability of this Ordinance shall be based upon the

purpose, intent, definitions, standards, and requirements established herein.

The City Council intends that this Ordinance be interpreted to protect the City’s authority

to regulate land use, infrastructure demands, and development impacts through

appropriate legislative action.

SECTION 13. APPLICATATION COMPLETENESS AND REJECTION


An application, request, or submission related to a High-Impact Data Center shall be

considered complete only upon the applicant’s demonstration of compliance with all

applicable requirements of this Ordinance.

The applicant shall bear the burden of submitting complete, accurate, and verifiable

information necessary for the City to evaluate compliance.

Applications shall be rejected or returned without further processing if they:

A. Propose a use prohibited under the City’s zoning regulations;


B. Fail to satisfy the definition, aggregation, or classification requirements


established by this Ordinance;

C. Fail to provide required utility, infrastructure, economic, workforce,


environmental, or community impact documentation;

D. Contain incomplete, inaccurate, misleading, or insufffficient information


necessary for review; or

E. Fail to demonstrate compliance with any applicable requirement established


by this Ordinance.

The City shall have no obligation to supplement, correct, or complete an applicant’s

submission.

The submission of additional information, revisions, modifications, or amended

materials shall not create any right to approval or continued consideration unless the

applicant has first demonstrated compliance with all applicable requirements.

Failure to satisfy any requirement of this Ordinance shall constitute sufffficient grounds

for rejection.

SECTION 14. COST RECOVERY RY AND APPLICANT RESPONSIBILITY


The City may require an applicant seeking review of a High-Impact Data Center

proposal to reimburse the City for all reasonable costs incurred in reviewing, evaluating,

and processing the application.

Eligible costs may include, but are not limited to:

A. Legal review and consultation; B. Engineering analysis; C. Planning and zoning review;


D. Utility and infrastructure evaluation;


E. Environmental review;


F. Economic and workforce impact analysis review; G. Outside professional consultants retained by the City; and


H. Any other reasonable costs directly associated with review of the proposed


development.

The City may require payment, escrow funding, reimbursement agreements, or other

financial security mechanisms before review proceeds.

The applicant shall be responsible for all costs associated with demonstrating

compliance with this Ordinance.

Failure to provide required cost reimbursement or financial security shall constitute

grounds for suspension or rejection of the application.

Nothing in this Section shall be interpreted as creating any obligation for the City to

incur expenses, conduct studies, or perform analysis on behalf of an applicant seeking

approval of a prohibited or restricted use.

SECTION 18. LEGISLATATIVE FINDINGS AND OFFICIAL RECORD


The City Council hereby adopts the following legislative findings in support of this

Ordinance:

A. Distinct Land Use Classification.


The City Council finds that High-Impact Data Centers constitute a distinct land

use category due to their extraordinary electrical demand, infrastructure

requirements, water consumption, cooling systems, operational intensity, and

potential impacts on municipal services and community resources.

B. Infrastructure Considerations.


The City Council finds that High-Impact Data Centers may require significant

investments in electrical generation, transmission, substations, water supply,

wastewater treatment, transportation infrastructure, and other public or private

infrastructure systems.

C. Utility and Ratepayer Protection.


The City Council finds that the potential impacts of utility-scale infrastructure

demands require careful review to ensure that existing residents, businesses,

and utility customers are not adversely affffected by increased costs, reduced

reliability, or diminished future capacity.

D. Environmental and Resource Considerations.


The City Council finds that High-Impact Data Centers may create impacts related

to water consumption, wastewater generation, cooling operations, chemical use,

emissions from backup generation systems, noise, and other environmental

considerations that require evaluation before approval.

E. Economic and Workforce Considerations.


The City Council finds that the economic impacts of High-Impact Data Centers

must be evaluated in relation to land consumption, infrastructure demands, public

resource utilization, permanent employment opportunities, workforce impacts,

and overall community benefit.

F. Transportation and Municipal Service Impacts.


The City Council finds that large-scale development activities associated with

High-Impact Data Centers may affffect transportation systems, construction traffffic,

emergency services, municipal operations, and long-term infrastructure planning.

G. Need for Legislative Review.


The City Council finds that High-Impact Data Centers require separate legislative

consideration because they possess characteristics substantially difffferent from

traditional commercial, industrial, and technology-related uses currently

contemplated by the City’s zoning regulations.

H. Protection of Public Health, Safety, and Welfare.


The City Council finds that establishing appropriate restrictions, review

standards, and approval requirements for High-Impact Data Centers is necessary

to protect the public health, safety, welfare, orderly development, and long-term

interests of the City of Fairfax.

I. No Displacement of Existing Uses.


The City Council finds that existing commercial, industrial, and

technology-related businesses and other uses that are compatible with current

zoning classifications are not the subject of this Ordinance. This Ordinance is

narrowly intended to address utility-scale digital infrastructure facilities whose

characteristics diffffer materially from traditional economic development uses.

The City Council further adopts the findings, staff ff reports, engineering evaluations, utility

correspondence, expert analyses, public testimony, meeting records, and other

materials presented to or considered by the City Council in connection with this

Ordinance as part of the offfficial legislative record supporting adoption.

The legislative record is adopted to demonstrate the factual and policy basis for the City

Council’s determination that High-Impact Data Centers require separate classification

and regulatory treatment.

The legislative record is not intended to serve as adjudicative evidence in a

quasi-judicial proceeding but instead represents the legislative basis for the City

Council’s zoning policy decision.

The City Council expressly incorporates these findings into this Ordinance as support

for its enactment.
