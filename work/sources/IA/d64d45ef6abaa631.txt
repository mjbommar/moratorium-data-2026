RESOLUTION

WHEREAS, the Federal Disaster Mitigation Act of 2000 was signed into law on October 30,
2000, placing new emphasis on state and local mitigation planning for natural hazards and
requiring communities to adopt a hazard mitigation action plan to be eligible for pre-disaster and
post-disaster federal funding for mitigation purposes; and

WHEREAS, a Hazard Mitigation Plan was prepared by the Cedar County Emergency
Management Agency, with assistance from JEO Consulting Group, Inc.

WHEREAS, the purpose of the mitigation plan was to lessen the effects of disasters by
increasing the disaster resistance of the region and participating jurisdictions located within the
planning boundary by identifying the hazards that affect Cedar County Board of Supervisors and
prioritize mitigation strategies to reduce potential loss of life and property damage from those
hazards, and

WHEREAS, FEMA regulations require documentation that the plan has been formally adopted
by the governing body of Cedar County Board of Supervisors in the form of a resolution and
further requesting approval of the plan at the Federal Level; and

NOW, THEREFORE, the governing body of Cedar County Board of Supervisors does
herewith adopt the Cedar County Hazard Mitigation Plan Update in its entirety as approved by
FEMA; and

PASSED AND APPROVED this 28th day of July, 2026.
Ayes: All

SPEED LIMIT RESOLUTION SPEED LIMIT ON COUNTY PAVED SECONDARY
ROADS RESOLUTION

WHEREAS, the Board of Supervisors of Cedar County, Iowa is empowered under the authority

of Sections 321.255 and 321.285, subsection 4, of the Code of Iowa to determine upon the basis

of an engineering and traffic investigation conducted by the County Engineer that the speed limit
of any secondary road is greater than is reasonable and proper under the conditions existing, and
may determine and declare a reasonable and proper speed limit; and

WHEREAS, Senate File 378 amended Section 321.285 of the Code of lowa effective July 1,
2026, changing the statutory speed limit on primary roads from fifty-five (55) miles per hour to
sixty (60) miles per hour unless otherwise provided; and

WHEREAS, the County Engineer has conducted additional engineering and traffic investigations
to evaluate the reasonable and proper speed limit for the County's paved secondary roads under
the revised statutory provisions as directed by the Board of Supervisors; and

WHEREAS, the County Engineer recommends maintaining the existing posted speed limits
equal to or lower than fifty-five (55) miles per hour where designated on paved secondary roads
by previous speed studies and established by separate resolutions of the Board of Supervisors;


NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Cedar County Board of Supervisors that:

The speed limit of fifty-five (55) miles per hour or less as determined by the completed
engineering study and traffic investigation due to the statutory speed limit change, the speed
limit shall be established and appropriate signs erected at the locations described as follows on
the following individual paved roadway segments:

1) Logan Avenue from State Highway 38 to Stone Mill Road, speed limit set to fifty-five
(55) miles per hour.

2) Stone Mill Road from Logan Avenue west to the gravel segment, speed limit set to forty-
five (45) miles per hour.

3) 120" Street from the Linn County Line east thirty-two hundred (3200) feet to the end of
the road, speed limit set to fifty-five (55) miles per hour.

4) Lisbon Boulevard from the Linn County Line east to Adams Avenue, speed limit set to
forty-five (45) miles per hour.

5) 150" Street from Quincy Avenue (X64) east to State Highway 30, speed limit set to fifty-
five (55) miles per hour.

6) Old Lincoln Highway from Vermont Avenue east to the Lowden City Limit, speed limit
set to fifty-five (55) miles per hour.

7) Old Lincoln Highway from the Clinton County Line west to the Lowden City Limit,
speed limit set to fifty-five (55) miles per hour.

8) Golfview Drive and Willow Drive within Golfview Subdivision located north of
Highway 927 (F58), speed limit set to twenty-five (25) miles per hour.

9) 290" Street (F44) from Wilson Avenue west twenty-four hundred (2400) feet in the
unincorporated Village of Sunbury, speed limit set to forty-five (45) miles per hour.

Passed and approved this 28"" day of July, 2026, by the Cedar County Board of Supervisors.
