# Board Approves Plan to Temporarily Pause Legislative Data Center and Substation Applications

source: https://www.loudoun.gov/CivicAlerts.aspx?aid=10874
fetched: 2026-09-23T14:13:20+00:00

County News Posted on September 17, 2026

During its September 15, 2026, Board of Supervisors meeting, the majority of the Board voted in favor of a plan to pause the Board’s action on data center and electrical substation applications that require legislative approval for up to 12 months, the maximum allowed by Virginia law. The Board’s vote follows a legal opinion from the Loudoun County Attorney indicating that a moratorium on data center approvals is not legal. Next, the Board will consider a resolution to enact the Board’s new policy during its October 20, 2026, meeting. The Board indicated the resolution should be retroactive, making the pause effective September 15.

The Board’s reason for pausing action on legislative applications is to allow time for the county to complete the ongoing review of current data center policies and regulations. The project proposes amendments to the county’s Comprehensive Plan (CPAM) and Zoning Ordinance (ZOAM) pertaining to [data center standards and locations](https://www.loudoun.gov/datacenterstandards). The outcome of the CPAM and ZOAM project has the potential to impact existing and new legislative applications for data centers. The project is expected to wrap up in the summer of 2027.

Under the terms of the pause approved by the Board, final votes on data center and substation applications will be paused for one year. Virginia law requires action on such legislative applications within one year. The Loudoun County Zoning Ordinance states the one-year period begins at the time applications are sent to a Planning Commission public hearing. Developers may continue to submit applications to the county, which may progress through the Planning Commission process during the Board’s pause; however, the Board’s intent is to pause any final votes on applications until after the CPAM and ZOAM project is complete, or up to one year. The pause would not cause any applications to become inactive. The Board may consider exceptions to the pause for applications that involve grid reliability or if the statutory period for a decision is about to expire. The Board’s action does not impact applications that are subject to administrative processes.

The Board previously approved an end to by-right data center development, requiring all data center projects to come before the Board for a vote through the county’s special exception process. The Board is also scheduled to consider amendments to its grandfathering rules for data centers during its October 6, 2026, meeting.

More information about [data centers](https://www.loudoun.gov/DataCenters) and proposed [transmission lines](https://www.loudoun.gov/transmissionlines) is available on the Loudoun County’s website.
