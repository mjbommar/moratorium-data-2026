ORDINANCE #100-25
A TEMPORARY ZONING ORDINANCE REGULATING UTILITY-SCALE ALTERNATIVE ENERGY IN ALL TURNER COUNTY ZONING DISTRICTS

Chairman Hybertson recused himself and Vice Chairman Ciampa opened the floor for the reading of Ordinance #100-25 a temporary zoning ordinance regulating utility-scale alternative energy in all Turner County Zoning Districts. 

Vice Chairman Ciampa asked if anyone wanted to speak in favor of the ordinance and the following people spoke: Todd Christensen, Senator Kevin Jensen, Steve Brockmuller, Representative Karla Lems and Jessie Ebbesen. Todd Christensen stated that it is what he brought to the Board meetings previously and what they are asking for is not unreasonable in the County. It gives us the time to evaluate what is going on and to make sure it is fair for everyone in the County. Whether it is for or against, hopefully we will be able to work together to get it right. Senator Kevin Jensen District 16 stated that we don’t want to see uncontrolled growth in this district of these types of things. We need to have some guidelines, and this ordinance is a good starting point. Steve Brockmuller stated that this is a very good first step that they are taking. Representative Karla Lems District 16 stated that with all the people that have weighed in on this issue in the County it would be good to press the pause button and revisit this ordinance to give them the protections they are asking for. With the directives coming down from President Trump it is a good time to reevaluate some of these projects. Jessie Ebbesen stated all he is looking for is time and that is what this ordinance would do as there are people’s homes that will be surrounded by towers and that needs to be looked at. 

Vice Chairman Ciampa asked if anyone here was opposed to the ordinance and wanted to speak. The following people spoke, Tom James with NextEra Energy Resources, Allen Anderson and Miles Schumacher. Tom James stated that it is very important to have a strong economic development. With the Wind Project, they have over 200 landowners that are currently participating with them and supporting them that are being directly affected by the turbines. The landowners will make over 1.2 million dollars annually. Tax revenues that would be coming into Turner County alone is estimated at approximately 500 thousand dollars a year. During the construction phase, they would be bringing in about 400 jobs, and after the construction phase, there would be 5 full-time high salary jobs. They are trying to build this project with help of the community, transparency, and making it beneficial to all parties involved. Allen Anderson stated that in 2019 we went through this process and the hard work was done, based on the state level. The ordinance matches what the state of South Dakota has for extensive resources. From a science and engineering perspective this is a good ordinance which is meant to protect people that are in the community but let them allow to use their property rights. People that do not want the project want to muck up the system and create the process that we have a task force for to go through that. It is fine that people don’t want the project. It is not to get a better ordinance, as we already have that, it is to make the project to go away. The impact to this is hundreds of people in our county want to use their property, as they see economically fit, and in a healthy and safe way, and not lose the right to use their property. Miles Schumacher stated that we have an ordinance in place that is protective of the welfare and safety of the people in our county. There is another level and in order to build this project we have to permit through the SD Public Utilities Commission. They will require to build this project at a higher standard than what our existing ordinance. In his experience it does not make sense to jeopardize the economic impact of this project by delaying it by doing something that has already been done, which is putting an adequate ordinance in place. 

Vice Chairman Ciampa closed the floor to public comment. 

Motion by Hagemann, seconded by Benson, to approve the reading of Ordinance #100-25 A temporary zoning ordinance regulating utility-scale alternative energy in all Turner County zoning districts. Roll call taken: Benson aye, Hagemann aye, Kaufman aye, and Vice Chairman Ciampa aye. Motion carried. 


PURPOSE: The Turner County Board of Commissioners has determined there is a present need to study the impact of utility-scale alternative energy systems in all Turner County Zoning Districts in order to evaluate whether current zoning regulations reasonably address concern for public health, safety and welfare.
WHEREAS, SDCL § 11-2-10 authorizes the Board of County Commissioners, in the interest of public health, safety and general welfare, to adopt temporary zoning controls for a reasonable period of time in order to study, identify and consider necessary land use regulations; and
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED that the Turner County Board of Commissioners hereby authorize a one (1) year moratorium on the issuance of any new permits and/or approval of land uses for proposed utility-scale alternative energy systems, including both solar and wind energy systems, regulated under Ordinance 67-19 (Amending the 2008 Revised Zoning Ordinance for Turner County by amending Section 3.00, A-1 Agricultural District; Section 9.00, I-2 General Industrial District; Section 13.13, Additional Use Regulations, Wind Energy Conversion Systems (WECS); Section 20.08, Conditional Use Permits, Expiration; Section 22.09, Zoning Board of Adjustment, Limitations; and Section 27.02, Definitions) of the Turner County Zoning Ordinance, in all applicable (A-1 Agricultural, RR Rural Residential, C Commercial, I-1 Light Industrial, and I-2 General Industrial) Turner County Zoning Districts. This moratorium shall expire on August 25, 2026, unless otherwise extended by law. 
BE IT ORDAINED by the Board of County Commissioners of Turner County, the provisions and adoption of this ordinance shall take effect upon completed publication per SDCL §§ 11-2-10 & 7-18A-8.
Approved this 26th day of August, 2025.
