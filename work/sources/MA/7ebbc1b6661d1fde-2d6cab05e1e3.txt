§ 180-5. Intent and purpose.


The Demolition Delay Bylaw is enacted for the purpose of preserving and protecting

significant buildings and other structures (barns or out-buildings which are generally

associated with the significantly historic building) within the Town of Plymouth which

constitute or reflect distinctive features of the architectural, cultural, economic, political or

social history of the Town and to limit the detrimental effect of demolition on the character

of the Town. Through this bylaw, owners of preferably preserved buildings are encouraged

to seek out alternative options that will preserve, rehabilitate or restore such buildings and

residents of the Town are alerted to impending demolitions of significant buildings. By

preserving and protecting significant buildings, streetscapes and neighborhoods, this bylaw

promotes the public welfare by making the Town a more attractive and desirable place in

which to live and work. To achieve these purposes, the Plymouth Historic District

Commission Historical Commission is authorized to advise the Building Commissioner

with respect to demolition permit applications. The issuance of demolition permits is

regulated as provided by this bylaw.

§ 180-6. Definitions.


As used in this article, the following terms shall have the meanings indicated:

COMMISSION — The Plymouth Historic District Commission Historical Commission.

ARTICLE 26: Citizen’s Petition – TOWN STEERING COMMITTEE ARTICLE


To see if the Town will vote to rescind and annul the vote taken under Article 27 of April 22, 1991;

Article 30 of April 4, 1992; Article 7 of December 1992; Article 38 of April 12, 1994; and Article

38 of April 13, 2004 at the Annual Town Meeting, which established the five Town Steering

Meeting committees: Manomet Village Steering Committee, Cedarville Steering Committee,

North Plymouth Steering Committee, West Plymouth Steering Committee and Plymouth Center

Steering Committee.

And to take action thereafter to establish seven (7) permanent, independent Town Steering

Committees comprising of the following areas: North Plymouth Steering Committee, Plymouth

Center Steering Committee, West Plymouth Steering Committee, Manomet Steering Committee,

Pinehills Steering Committee, Cedarville Steering Committee and Redbrook & South Steering

Committee (hereinafter the “committees”).

The committees will function as an independent advisory body of the Town to promote civic

engagement, steer information back to town-boards, provide independent oversight, advocate the

needs of the area, and ensure community interests are represented in the planning and development

of the Town initiatives and relevant Town Meeting articles. At the request of a Town Board, Town

Meeting Member, commission, committee, department and citizen, the committees may provide

guidance or endorse a vote of support on a specific proposal.

The committees shall advise the Planning Board on special permits, variances and subdivision

plans from the Planning Department with respect to issues affecting their geographical area. The

committees will assist in the implementation of the recommendations outlined in each committee’s

master plan. When considering any significant action that primarily and directly affects the

geographic area served by the committees, the Select Board or its designee shall provide

reasonable notice of such pending action to the affected committees.

The committees, as an independent advisory body of the Town – separate from the authority,

supervision, and control of any single board, Town Manager, committee, commission or

administration department, except as required by Massachusetts General Laws, Town Meeting

vote and applicable bylaws, shall strengthen public trust, foster transparency and serve as a liaison

between the Town government and the residents of Plymouth. No appointed body (COPC, Select

Board, or Planning Board) and town-employed officials shall influence the deliberations or

recommendations and what item is placed on the committee’s agenda. Members of the COPC shall

not serve as appointed steering committee members.

The committees shall function in an advisory capacity only. They shall have no authority to bind

the Town, expend public funds, or exercise any executive or legislative power. Recommendations

of the committees shall be considered but shall not be binding upon any elected boards.

Members of committees shall be residents of the precinct or precincts that fall principally within

the geographic boundaries of the steering committee area. Residents of an adjoining or overlapping

precinct may serve on committees if they can demonstrate reasonable geography identity with the

steering committee area. The appointing authority shall determine whether a prospective member

has demonstrated sufficient geographic identity with the steering committee area. No member may

serve simultaneously on two committees.

Membership of each committee shall be seven (7) members appointed for three-years staggered

terms. Initial appointment shall be made as follows:

Three (3) members appointed by the Committee of Precinct Chairs (COPC), serving a one-year

term;

Two (2) members appointed by the Board of Selectmen serving a two-year term;

Two (2) members appointed by the Planning Board serving a full three-year term.

Committees shall notify the appointed body of members whose cumulative attendance falls below

fifty percent (50%) in a twelve – month period.

Any Town Meeting Member who lives in an area defined as “committees” and who is not

appointed to the committee shall be non-voting “ex-officio” member with rights to make or second

motions.

Meetings are to be conducted in accordance with generally accepted rules of parliamentary

procedures and Open Meeting Laws. Robert’s Rule of Order may be used as a guide in matters

requiring clarification. Committees may be re-organized at any time and are encouraged to hold

meetings within their geographic area to promote community participation.

The committees shall write an annual report voted by committee members, and file it with the

Town Clerk’s office for inclusion in the Annual Town Report for the Town of Plymouth. The Town

Clerk will notify all Committees of the annual deadline. Failure to submit an annual report does

not dissolve the Town Steering Committee. Town Steering Committees may only be dissolved by

Town Meeting Members at the Annual Town Meeting.

By Petition: A Anthony Mayo, et al.
