CITY OF LOWELL

In City Council

ORDINANCE

An Ordinance Amending "Code of the City of Lowell, Massachusetts", with respect to

Chapter 290, Thereof Entitled, "Zoning” by adding and amending various sections.

---------------

WHEREAS, with the proliferation of Data Center expansion in Massachusetts, cities

and towns have taken moratoriums under advisement, in order to review their zoning and land

use, and to formulate regulations governing the impacts of these facilities, which require

adoption by ordinance of the governing body of a city or town; and

WHEREAS, the City of Lowell’s zoning and land use and development regulations are

found in the Lowell Zoning Code, w which currently makes no specific provision for Data Centers

within the City, either by right or special use permit.

BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF LOWELL, as follows:

"Code of the City of Lowell, Massachusetts", hereinafter called the "Code", adopted by

the City Council on December 23, 2008, as amended, is hereby further amended with respect to

Chapter 290, entitled "Zoning,” adopted December 7, 2004, as amended, as follows:

1. Amend Article II Definitions by inserting a new paragraph as follows:

DATA CENTERS: a  a building or series of buildings that houses and supports the high-performance

servers, storage systems, networking equipment, and related computing infrastructure and

equipment necessary for storing, processing, and distributing data and applications.

2. Amend Article VII Special Regulations b by inserting a new Section 7.11 as follows:

7.11 360-DAY MORATORIUM ON “DATA CENTERS”

1. The purpose of this temporary moratorium is to provide the City an opportunity to better

understand the impacts, if any, that the construction, expansion, and operation of Data

Centers will have on City infrastructure and, more generally, the overall quality of life for

the residents of Lowell. As a result, the City shall conduct an analysis and/or

comprehensive study to determine the impact of said construction on police, fire, and

emergency public safety, the school district, water, sewer, roadway infrastructures and the

safety of the general public. This list of potential impacts is not exhaustive and may include

additional impacts, as identified.

2. There is hereby imposed, effective immediately, a 360-day moratorium on any new

construction and development within the City of Lowell, MASSACHUSETTS, of “Data

Centers”, as defined above. Said moratorium prohibits acceptance by the City of Lowell of

any new applications and permits for zoning permissions and decisions for the intended

purpose of the development and operation of a Data Center. Should the Lowell Zoning

Code be amended prior to the expiration of the 360-day period to regulate Data Centers,

the moratorium shall terminate on the effective date of amendment. The moratorium p period

may be extended, one (1) time, by a 2/3 vote of the City Council, not to exceed 180-days.

3. During the term of this moratorium, City staff shall review current Zoning Codes and

Regulations, seek recommendations and input from industry experts and professionals with

specific knowledge of Data Centers and any impacts these facilities have on the

community, and to propose revisions to the Lowell Zoning Code, when deemed

appropriate, to be placed before the Lowell City Council for consideration.

4. The Department of Planning and Development shall consult with other City departments

to ensure any recommendations address the impact on power, water, sewer availability and

capacity limitations, noise, as well as any potential legal implications. Said

recommendations shall also consider the impact on traffic and any other adverse impact on

adjacent infrastructure.

5. Emphasis should also be given to enforcement of regulations following construction and

development, including operational limitations reasonably intended to ameliorate public

concerns of legitimate land uses.

6. The Lowell Department of Planning and Development shall develop a timeline, within the

360-day period, to allow for recommendation and adoption of amendments to the Lowell

Zoning Code in accordance with the procedures set forth in M.G.L. c. 40A. If during this

study, staff find and conclude any provisions of the Lowell Zoning Code require

revision(s), recommendation(s) shall be made accordingly to the Lowell City Council.

3. Amend Article XII: Table of Industrial Uses, by inserting a new section “w” as follows:

9. INDUSTRIAL USES Zone Min. Parking Req. Notes Shared

PermittedParking

Chart

w. Data Centers N/A N/A N/A

4. This Ordinance shall take effect upon its passage in accordance with Section 1.7 of the

"Lowell Zoning Ordinance" and the provisions of Chapter 43 and 40A of the General Laws of

Massachusetts.

APPROVED AS TO FORM:

The City of Lowell has been providing property tax relief in the form of a Tax Increment Finance (TIF) or Tax

Increment Exemption (TIE) to Developers and Companies for over thirty (30) years. These tax exemption

agreements are only executed for eligible projects under the following state programs:

1. The Massachusetts Economic Development Incentive Program (EDIP): j job creation tax incentive

program offered by state. Tax credit from state requires tax exemption match from municipality.

2. The Housing Development Incentive Program (HDIP): market-rate housing tax incentive program.

Tax credit from state requires tax exemption match from municipality.

3. The Urban-Center Housing Program Tax Increment Finance (UCH-TIF): affordable housing tax

incentive program from state. Tax credit from state requires tax exemption match from municipality

These programs provide a p partial property tax exemption on the incremental value of the real estate taxes

generated by the investment, which are known as TIFs for the EDIP program and TIEs for the housing

programs. TIF/TIE exemptions only affect the growth in property taxes for a predetermined period of time.

The baseline taxes due on the building will continue to be paid in full throughout the TIF/TIE term, as well as

a percentage of the new tax growth each year (see Fig.1). TIF/TIE duration typically ranges from 5-20 years

per state’s guidelines.

Fig. 1: How TIEs/ TIFs work:

When considering whether to offer a TIF to a company moving to Lowell or a TIE to a developer looking to

build housing in the city, we consider a number of factors including:

• location

• condition of the property they plan to move to or expand

• amount of investment the developer plans to make

• number and types of jobs they plan to bring to Lowell

• number of residential units that will be added to Lowell’s housing stock.

DPD staff evaluates each project in detail t to ensure it benefits L Lowell’s residents and it aligns with the goals and

priorities of the Lowell Forward Master Plan.

For companies under the EDIP program, they are required to submit annual reports to both the state and the

municipality providing data on the total investment as well as job creation. All TIF agreements executed to date

include explicit language requiring companies to give priority to Lowell residents when seeking job candidates

as well as working in partnership with MassHire, UMass Lowell, Middlesex Community College, and Greater

Lowell Technical High School. EDIP program participants have a strong track record of providing thousands of

The surface soils at the Site largely consist of a layer of topsoil. Beneath the topsoil at the Site is

a layer of imported soil between one-foot to over six-feet thick. Ash present below the imported

soil layer has intermingled with the imported soil and has contaminated the imported soil in some

bones, shoes, and other anthropogenic material. At some locations, native soil below the

contaminated fill layer is a mixture of sand and silt. Borings on the southern and eastern side of

Site History

The earliest identified map of the area of the Site is provided by an 1836 Plan and Profile of the

Boston & Lowell Railroad. The map identifies the approximate location of the Site in a swampy

area south of an unnamed tributary of the Concord River.

Maps dated 1875, 1876, and 1891 show northern portions of the Site occupied by the Lowell Fair

Grounds. The Fair Grounds occupied the northern portions of the Site from 1860 until about

1910 when the fairgrounds land was divided up by the North Land Company. The 1876 map

identifies several structures and a 0.5-mile oval track on the Fair Grounds. The former

fairgrounds area has been identified by the current investigations as the location of extensive

historic filling. This indicates that the historic filling of this area had taken place sometime

between 1836 and 1874. Maps dated 1887 and 1896 identify the Lowell Bleachery between the

Fair Grounds and the properties along Fay Street.

Site Environmental Investigations and Remedial Actions

Following is a summary of response actions performed by TRC and others and a summary of the

compliance history associated with RTN 3-25722.

Site Assessment/Investigation – Geotechnical Consultants, 1998

A  site investigation was performed at the 86 Fay Street property by Geotechnical Consultants (GC)

in 1998 for the Merrimac Valley Housing Partnership. The extent of this investigation included

the 86 Fay Street property as well as the adjacent parcels. The investigation identified fill

consisting of ash and cinders with traces of glass and metal mixed in on the 86 Fay Street property.

Four test pits and three monitoring wells were installed on the Site. MassDEP Headspace

Procedure screening readings in the samples collected in these borings and test pits were below

detection limit at low levels (i.e., 2 parts per million maximum) indicating very low or no volatile

organic chemicals were present in the contaminated fill.

The results of the site investigations indicated that ash was not present in the southern and eastern

portions of the property but was present at thicknesses of up to 15.5 feet along the northern property

line. Identified exceedances of reportable concentrations in soil included lead, beryllium, and total

petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH).

Site Assessment/Investigation – Engineering & Consulting Resources, Inc., 2006

Much of the historical information about the subject properties was identified by Engineering &

Consulting Resources, Inc (ECR) during a site assessment in early 2006 in response to an identified

reportable condition at the 86 Fay Street property.

In January 2006, tests pits were excavated at 86 Fay Street and samples of soil and groundwater

were collected on behalf of a potential buyer. The soil and groundwater grab samples were

submitted for laboratory analysis of volatile organic compounds (VOCs), TPH, and priority

pollutant metals. The laboratory data indicated detections of lead, beryllium, mercury, and zinc at

concentrations above MCP Reportable Concentrations for Soil Category 1 (RCS-1) and/or

Groundwater Category 2 (RCGW-2). MassDEP assigned Release Tracking Number (RTN) 3-

25722 to the Site in March 2006.

Soil samples collected during drilling activities by ECR indicated that Site soils consisted of about

three feet of topsoil mixed with ash. The topsoil was underlain by ash fill ranging from 9 to 14

feet deep. The ash fill was underlain by silt and sand to at least 20 feet deep.

ECR collected eight soil samples for analysis of MCP metals and four soil samples for analysis of

extractable petroleum hydrocarbons (EPH) and volatile petroleum hydrocarbons (VPH). The

laboratory data indicated concentrations of arsenic, barium, lead, chromium, nickel, thallium, and

zinc above S-1/GW-2 and S-1/GW-3 standards in all soil samples. EPH and PAHs were detected

in two samples with concentrations of benzo(a)anthracene, chrysene, benzo(b)fluoranthene, and

benzo(a)pyrene above the MCP S-1/GW-2 and S-1/GW-3 standards in effect at the time.

ECR collected groundwater from the four Site monitoring wells in June. The groundwater samples

were analyzed for VPH, EPH, and MCP metals. The lababoratory data did not indicate

concentrations of analytes above the applicable MCP  groundwater standards.

Geophysical Survey and Site Investigation – TRC, 2010

A geophysical survey was performed by Hager Geoscience for TRC at the 86 Fay Street parcel in

2010. The survey indicated that ash thickness was decreasing from north to south across the

property. The top of the ash was estimated to be approximately 3.5 feet deep along the boundary

and the bottom of the ash was estimated to be between 8.5 to 9 feet resulting in an ash thickness

of approximately 5-5.5-feet along the property boundary.

TRC oversaw the excavation of 10 test pits at 86 Fay Street. Evidence of fill was observed in all

10 test pits and was the predominant material at each test pit except for a test pit located on the

southwest corner of the property. The depth to the top of a distinctive fill layer ranged from 0.3

feet to 2 feet and it was typically encountered within the top foot of soil and was usually present

the entire depth of test pitting activities, which stopped at a maximum of 7 feet deep.

Headspace readings in the ash were below detection l limit indicating very low or no volatile organic

chemicals were present in the ash fill. The ash fill contained materials such as ash, cinders, coal,

bottles and glass, porcelain, metal, and leather.

TRC collected soil samples from each of the 10 test  pits at the 86 Fay Street property in 2010 at a

depth of 0 to 3 feet and submitted them for laboratory analysis of MCP 14 Metals and PAHs. Two

samples were collected from debris piles onsite and d submitted for laboratory analysis of volatile

organic compounds, PAHs, extractable petroleum hydrocarbons, and MCP 14 metals. The

laboratory data indicated concentrations of benzo(a)pyrene, dibenz(a,h)anthracene, arsenic,

barium, cadmium, chromium, lead, and nickel above MCP Method 1 S-1 soil cleanup standards in

at least one test pit or debris pile sample.

Release Abatement Measure Activities – TRC, 2012

In 2012, a Release Abatement Measure (RAM) was completed at 86 Fay Street. Excavation of the

Conceptual Site Model

OHM impacts at the Site are attributable both to contaminated fill and past uses at the Site.

• The area of the Site is located within or very near r an unnamed tributary of the Concord

River which was filled between 1836 and 1874. The historical record indicates that the Site

was historically filled and graded prior to 1938 and likely much earlier in the mid- to late

1800s.

• The contaminated fill has impacted soil at 86 Fay Street, 74 Fay Street, 78 Fay Street, 1170

Gorham Street (O’Donnell Park), and 106 Lundberg Street.

• Fill material is thickest in the central portion of f the Site and reduces in thickness to the

north (i.e., north of the O’Donnell Park/Baseball Field) and south (i.e., within Swede

Village). The contaminated fill appears to have been intermingled with the over- and

underlying native soil during the filling and subsequent Site development activities which

has resulted in a mixture of contaminated fill and soil at the Site.

• Contaminants of concern at the Site are primarily lead and carcinogenic PAHs related to

the presence of ash mixing in with soil. These impacts appear consistent with contaminants

present in the contaminated fill prior to emplacement, as there is no indication of non

contaminated fill releases of VOCs, petroleum hydrocarbons, or asbestos-containing

material (ACM), or other oil or hazardous materials  originating from activities at the

location of emplacement.

• There is no evidence that the contaminated fill contains a generated hazardous waste.

• The contaminated fill consists primarily of soil mixed with white ash with minor amounts

glass, porcelain, coal, clinkers, burnt wood, animal bones, shoes, and other anthropogenic

materials generally associated with contaminated fill with no other evidence of chemical

production waste, manufacturing waste, or other production waste. There are portions of

the Site in which the ash is not mixed with soil and is the sole constituent of the

contaminated fill.

• The vicinity of the subject properties has been identified by the MassDEP as the Gorham

Street Landfill which is classified as an inactive municipal solid waste landfill; however,

historical records reviews conducted by the City of f Lowell and Site investigation results

indicate no evidence that a landfill was operated at the Site by the City.
