Emergency Ordinance/Proclamation 2026-003 Restricting the Sale and use of

fireworks in the Unincorporated Areas of Sierra County.

III: PUBLIC COMMENT

Bob Whelan: Spoke in opposition of the ordinance stating that he is Associate

Pastor of New Hope Revival Church and they do ONE big fundraiser a year

and that is Firework sales…. He also stated that the terrain here is not

heavily wooded and not a huge fire danger. This is the 250th anniversary of

America and we should be able to celebrate as Proud americans. It’s

unamerican not to be able to keep the tradition of celebrating with fireworks.

Denny Womack- New Revival Church, asking to please not ban any fireworks.

The Governor is only URGING Counties to ban them not demanding it. It’s

more rains to come.

People go to other areas and buy the fireworks and still bring them back to

Commissioner Paxon- Is there a motion to Convene as the County Canvassing

Board?

Commissioner Hopkins moved to Convene as the County Canvassing Board.

Commissioner Jones seconded the motion. Clerk Whitehead did a Roll call

vote and motion carried.

Clerk Amy Whitehead presented the Canvass packet and results to

Commissioners and stood for questions.

Commissioner Hopkins expressed his concerns regarding the low voter

Whitehead stated no except for some write in votes that were written

incorrectly.

Attorney Pato asked if there were any omissions or errors on the election

returns? Clerk Whitehead stated that there were none. And do the returns

contain the executed certificates? Clerk Whitehead stated yes they do.

Commission Vice Chair Hopkins moved that they Certify the election and

approve the canvass as presented. Commissioner Jones seconded the motion.

Motion carried with Commissioner’s Districts 1-2-3 voting yes.

V: RESOLUTIONS-ORDINANCES-PROCLAMATIONS

A: Emergency Ordinance /Proclamation 2026-003 Restricting the sale and use

of fireworks in the unincorporated areas of Sierra County.

Attorney Pato: This ordinance declares extreme or severe drought conditions

exist within the unincorporated areas of Sierra County and that pursuant to

the fireworks licensing and safety ac-imposes temporary restrictions on the

sale and use of certain types of fireworks to protect public health and safety.

Much discussion took place regarding the ban with Commissioners asking

questions to our Emergency Manager Ryan Williams. He provided answers

and statistics. Commission Chair Paxon called Sheriff Baker to the podium

and Sheriff Baker stated that Ban or No ban- the SO gets tons of calls on the

day and night of July 4th

.

Commissioner Hopkins asked Attorney Pato if they pass the ordinance, can it

be lifted if we get moisture in Sierra County? Attorney Pato stated that the

Ordinance would have to be modified and he would be happy to do that.

Commissioner Hopkins made a motion to adjust the ordinance to state that the

ban can be lifted IF Sierra County receives some moisture between now and

the holiday. The lifting of the ban would be determined by the County

Manager and Emergency Management. Commission Chair Paxon seconded

the motion. Commissioner Jones asked for some discussion on the motion

stating that he is not comfortable putting the pressure of the decision to lift

the ban on ANY County Employee should Sierra County receive moisture.

He stated that he cannot support the motion because it is strictly

unenforceable and the County has never enforced it.

Commission Chair Paxon stated that there is a motion and a second and he

asked for a roll call vote re: passing the ordinance with the ability to lift the

ban with some moisture at the discretion of the County Manger and

Emergency Manager.

Clerk Whitehead did a roll call vote with Commission Chair Paxon voting

FOR the ban with opportunity to lift with moisture. Commissioner Hopkins

voted FOR. Commissioner Jones voting NO. Motion passes with

Commissioner District’s 1-2 voting yes and 3 voting no.

Ban will last for 30 days from the date of adoption.

III. DEVELOPMENT REFERRAL & NOTIFICATION PROCESS

The County agrees to refer development applications subject to this MOU to the City at the

earliest practical stage of review. The City shall be provided a standard review and

comment period of fifteen (15) business days whenever practical.

The City may review and provide comments regarding utility capacity, drainage and

floodplain impacts, roadway access, emergency access, infrastructure compatibility, and

long-range planning consistency.

The Parties may conduct joint staff meetings for developments involving significant

infrastructure impacts or coordination needs.

IV. DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS & LAND USE COMPATIBILITY

The Parties acknowledge that properties located adjacent to City limits often function as

transitional areas between County and municipal development patterns.

For developments subject to this MOU, the Parties recommend consideration of

development standards generally consistent with the intent and character of C-1

Commercial District standards and/or T-1 Transitional District standards as identified in

Chapter 16 of the City of Truth or Consequences Municipal Code.

Nothing in this MOU transfers zoning authority from the applicable governing jurisdiction.

V. WATER, WASTEWATER & UTILITY SERVICE CONNECTIONS

Prior to approval of development requiring connection to City utility systems, the developer

shall coordinate with the City regarding utility service availability, system capacity,

infrastructure requirements, and utility extension feasibility.

Any development connecting to City utility systems shall be subject to all applicable water

tap fees, wastewater tap fees, utility connection charges, capacity fees, meter fees,

inspection fees, impact fees, and infrastructure cost recovery requirements in accordance

with City ordinances and fee schedules in effect at the time of application.

Developers requesting utility service outside City limits may be required to fund or

construct utility line extensions, oversized infrastructure, easement dedication, off-f-site

improvements, and fire protection improvements.

Unless otherwise approved by the City Commission through separate agreement, all utility

infrastructure connected to City systems shall remain subject to City standards regarding

ownership, maintenance responsibility, inspection authority, and acceptance procedures.

VI. DRAINAGE, FLOODPLAIN & EMERGENCY ACCESS COORDINATION

The Parties agree to coordinate review of developments that may impact regional drainage

systems, floodplain management, stormwater runoff, emergency vehicle access, fire

protection service, and public safety infrastructure.

VII. LONG-RANGE PLANNING & INFRASTRUCTURE COORDINATION

The Parties agree to maintain ongoing communication regarding growth patterns, utility

expansion planning, transportation planning, infrastructure investments, economic

development opportunities, future service demands, and shared GIS mapping and planning

information.

VIII. NON-BINDING COOPERATION

This MOU is intended to establish procedures for coordination and cooperation between the

Parties and does not create vested development rights, require approval of any application,

require the City to provide utility service, or limit the authority of either governing body.

IX. TERM, AMENDMENT & TERMINATION

This MOU shall become effective upon approval by both governing bodies and execution by

authorized representatives of the Parties.

This MOU shall remain in effect indefinitely unless terminated by either Party in accordance

with the provisions of this section.

This MOU may be amended at any time by mutual written agreement of the Parties and

approval by the respective governing bodies when required.

Either Party may terminate this MOU upon thirty (30) days written notice to the other

Party.

Termination of this MOU shall not affect obligations, approvals, utility agreements, or

infrastructure commitments previously approved or executed prior to the effective date of

termination.

Pursuant to Cooperative Service Agreement No. 24-73-35-2390-RA between the county of Sierra

and APHIS-WS, this Work Plan defines the objectives, plan of action, resources and budget for the

maintenance of an Integrated Wildlife Damage Management (IWDM) program to protect residents,

property, livestock, crops, and natural resources from damage caused by predators and other

nuisance wildlife to be conducted from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027.

USDA-WS is a federal agency with a broad mission that includes carrying out wildlife damage

management activities. USDA-WS has continuously maintained an effective IWDM program to

resolve conflicts with wildlife throughout New Mexico, including Sierra County. USDA-WS is

available and qualified to conduct wildlife damage management services as necessary to accomplish

the County’s goals.

I. OBJECTIVES/GOALS

Wildlife Services’ overall goal is to maintain a biologically-sound IWDM program to assist

property owners, businesses, private citizens, and governmental agencies in resolving wildlife

damage problems and conduct control activities in accordance with applicable Federal, State and

local laws and regulations. Assistance may be in the form of providing technical assistance or

direct control activities. Recommendations and control activities will ensure long term solutions

and incorporate the Integrated Wildlife Damage Management approach.

The scope of this program is limited only by the financial resources allocated by the cooperator and

APHIS-WS.  Although successful elimination of any specific threat is not guaranteed, all reasonable

efforts will be made to resolve or mitigate human-wildlife conflicts within financial and regulatory

constraints.

II. PLAN OF ACTION

To accomplish this goal, the following general field services will be provided:

Non-lethal application – this will be the primary consideration.

Technical Assistance – WS will provide technical support to Sierra County on other issues that

may impact predatory and/or nuisance species control including habitat modifications, response

Outreach – include meeting with the constituents of the county, school group demonstrations, and

the New Mexico Law Enforcement Protection Fund ("LEPF") for the benefit of Sheriff for

the restricted and express purposes of providing training and other law enforcement

activities as are authorized by the provisions of the New Mexico Law Enforcement

Protection Fund Act, NMSA 1978, §§ 29-13-1 et seq. ("Act"); and

WHEREAS, the parties wish to amend the existing Contract Agreement for Services to

identify it as a Joint Powers Agreement and to add a provision for the contribution of an

additional Thirty Thousand Dollars ($30,000) by City for providing trained and certified

personnel; and

WHEREAS, in consideration of the funds received, Sheriff desires to provide trained and

certified personnel, equipment, and law enforcement protection for the citizens of the City

Elephant Butte, its’ residents, and visitors; and

Page 1 of 6

B. To annually provide funds available to City from the New Mexico Law

Enforcement Protection Fund to County, together with annually providing

County an additional Thirty Thousand Dollars ($30,000) frfrom City for

providing trained and certified personnel.

to this agreement is the power to enforce the law which is granted the City and the

Sheriff by state constitution and statute.

power within the City pursuant to this agreement. Sheriff shall provide services in

and Sheriff's deputies have all the powers of municipal officers and shall have the

authority to enforce the municipal ordinances of the City of Elephant Butte. In

addition, the Sheriff, f, any of his deputies, or animal control officer shall be

authorized to investigate animal control complaints within the City, issue citations

for violations of City animal control ordinances, impound animals, and otherwise

enforce the City of Elephant Butte animal control ordinances to the extent permitted

by law. The City hereby authorizes such personnel to act on behalf of the City for

purposes of enforcing its animal control ordinances during the term of this

Agreement.

5. Administering Agency. There shall be no separate entity created by this

Agreement. Sheriff shall be responsible for administering the law enforcement

provisions of this Agreement, and all employees engaged in the Sheriff's services

hereunder shall be employees of County.

RESOLUTION NO. 2026-51

RESOLUTION OPPOSING DEVELOPMENT OF LARGE-SCALE DATA CENTERS

WITHIN SIERRA COUNTY

WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Sierra County met in a duly noticed regular

Commission meeting on ____________, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. in the Sierra County Administration

Building, 1712 North Date Street, Truth or Consequences, New Mexico 87901; and

WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners of Sierra County exercises the powers of the

County as a body politic and corporate pursuant to NMSA 1978, Section 4-38-1; and

WHEREAS, pursuant to NMSA 1978, Section 4-37-1, counties possess authority to provide for

the safety, preserve the health, promote the prosperity, and improve the order, comfort, and

convenience of the county and its inhabitants; and

WHEREAS, Sierra County has long supported the establishment, operation, and continued

success of Spaceport America and recognizes the significant economic, educational, scientific,

technological, and tourism benefits that the Spaceport has provided and continues to provide to

Sierra County, the surrounding region, and the State of New Mexico; and

WHEREAS, the Board of County Commissioners remains committed to supporting the continued

growth and success of Spaceport America through aerospace, commercial spaceflight, advanced

manufacturing, research, testing, education, and other innovative industries that complement the

Spaceport's mission and provide meaningful economic opportunities for Sierra County residents;

and

WHEREAS, Sierra County is a predominantly rural county whose economy has historically relied

upon agriculture, ranching, tourism, outdoor recreation, retirement communities, and the

responsible development of natural resources; and

WHEREAS, agriculture and ranching remain integral to Sierra County's economy, culture, and

heritage and depend upon the continued availability of reliable water resources; and

WHEREAS, Sierra County's water resources are finite, increasingly scarce, and among the

County's most valuable public resources, supporting municipalities, agriculture, ranching,

businesses, wildlife, recreation, and future economic development; and

WHEREAS, the County continues to experience prolonged drought conditions, increasing

demands upon groundwater resources, and uncertainty regarding long-term water availability,

requiring careful stewardship and conservation of available water supplies; and

WHEREAS, the Board believes that responsible economic development should occur in a manner

that protects Sierra County's limited water resources, agricultural economy, rural character,

environmental quality, and the long-term interests of its citizens; and

WHEREAS, the Board recognizes that while many forms of economic development are

compatible with these objectives, certain industrial uses requiring extraordinary quantities of water

and electrical power may not be consistent with the long-term sustainability of Sierra County; and

WHEREAS, the Board has become aware of a proposal to construct and operate one or more

large-scale data centers at or associated with Spaceport America; and

WHEREAS, modern hyperscale data centers frequently require substantial quantities of water for

cooling operations, consume extraordinary amounts of electrical power, require significant

supporting infrastructure, and may place considerable demands upon regional natural resources;

and

WHEREAS, the Board is concerned that the proposed development of large-scale data centers

could adversely affect Sierra County's groundwater resources, agricultural operations, ranching

community, future water availability, electrical infrastructure, environmental resources, and rural

quality of life; and

WHEREAS, the Board further believes that preserving Sierra County's limited water resources

for residents, agriculture, ranching, existing businesses, and future generations constitutes a

compelling public interest; and

WHEREAS, the Board recognizes that Spaceport America is owned and operated pursuant to

state law by the New Mexico Spaceport Authority and that Sierra County possesses no regulatory

authority over development occurring upon Spaceport America property; and

WHEREAS, notwithstanding the County's limited jurisdiction, the Board believes it has both the

right and the responsibility to express the concerns of Sierra County residents regarding projects

that may substantially affect the County's natural resources, economy, and quality of life; and

WHEREAS, the Board respectfully encourages the New Mexico Spaceport Authority and all state

agencies involved in evaluating future development proposals to carefully consider the long-term

impacts of water-intensive industrial development upon Sierra County and to prioritize

development that is compatible with the County's natural resources and long-term sustainability.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of County Commissioners of Sierra

County that the Board hereby reaffirms its longstanding support for Spaceport America and

recognizes its importance as a driver of innovation, scientific advancement, economic

development, job creation, tourism, and educational opportunity for Sierra County and the State

of New Mexico, and that the Board supports continued investment and development at Spaceport

America, including aerospace activities, commercial spaceflight, advanced manufacturing,

research, testing, education, and other compatible industries that responsibly utilize the Spaceport's

unique assets while protecting Sierra County's natural resources and quality of life;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED b by the Board of County Commissioners

of Sierra County that the Board hereby expresses its firm opposition to the development of large

scale data centers within Sierra County, including a proposed development of a data center at

Spaceport America, based upon concerns regarding the consumption of substantial quantities of

limited water resources, potential adverse impacts to groundwater supplies and long-term water

availability, the potential effects upon agriculture, ranching, and other traditional water-dependent

industries, extraordinary electrical demand and resulting infrastructure impacts, potential

environmental impacts associated with industrial-scale operations, the preservation of Sierra

County's rural character and quality of life, and the long-term sustainability of Sierra County's

natural resources for current and future generations.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED b by the Board of County Commissioners

of Sierra County that the Board directs County Administration to transmit executed copies of this

Resolution to the Governor of New Mexico, the New Mexico Spaceport Authority, the State Land

Office, the Office of the State Engineer, the New Mexico Environment Department, the New

Mexico Economic Development Department, members of the Sierra County legislative delegation,

and such other governmental officials as the County Manager determines appropriate, and calls

upon these parties h having jurisdiction over the proposed project carefully evaluate long-term water

consumption, groundwater sustainability, agricultural impacts, environmental impacts, electrical

infrastructure requirements, alternative cooling technologies that substantially reduce or eliminate

water consumption, and the cumulative impacts of additional industrial development upon Sierra

County's limited natural resources.

APPROVED, ADOPTED, AND PASSED this 21st day of July, 2026.
