# TOWNSHIP TO TAKE ACTION ON DATA CENTERS

source: https://www.bricknj.gov/news_detail_T19_R80.php
fetched: 2026-09-23T12:44:36+00:00

Citing concerns about the potential impact data centers could have on the quality of life of residents, the Brick Township Council is taking action to restrict their development within the community.

The Council will consider an ordinance at their September 8 meeting that establishes a formal definition of a data center and clarifies that data centers are not included within existing land use categories unless specifically permitted.

“I have heard from many residents who are concerned about the possibility of having a data center in Brick Township, and I share many of those same concerns,” said Mayor Lisa Crate. “When we reviewed our existing land use ordinances, we recognized that there was a need to clearly address this type of development. This ordinance will eliminate any ambiguity and ensure that data centers cannot simply be classified under another existing use.”

The proposed ordinance seeks to eliminate uncertainty over whether data centers could fit within existing permitted uses by formally defining what constitutes a data center.

Under the ordinance, a data center is defined as “a building, structure, facility, campus, or portion thereof primarily utilized for the housing, storage, operation, processing, management, routing, transmission, switching, distribution, or support of digital data, electronic information, computer servers, telecommunications equipment, cloud-computing infrastructure, artificial intelligence computing systems, high-performance computing systems, cryptocurrency mining equipment, network equipment, or similar electronic or technological equipment.”

The ordinance further specifies that a data center shall not be “deemed a warehouse, distribution facility, storage facility, office use, light industrial use, utility use, telecommunications use, research facility, manufacturing use, self-storage facility, or any other use category unless expressly identified.”

“Data centers have had profound impacts on some of the communities where they have been built,” said Council President Steve Feinman. “There are concerns about their enormous energy and water usage, potential environmental impacts and constant noise. This Council wants to protect Brick’s residents and families from those potential impacts, and the best way to do that is to act now before it becomes a larger issue.”

There have been no applications to build a data center submitted to the township.

Mayor Crate said she fully supports the ordinance and will sign it immediately upon its passage.

The proposed ordinance is scheduled for first reading at the September 8, 2026 Township Council meeting. If adopted, the ordinance would take effect 20 days after publication.
