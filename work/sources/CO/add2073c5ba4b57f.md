## Resolutions

Resolutions are Council actions on administrative matters or general policy.

### Resolution to Conduct Ethics Hearing R26-063 by a Third Party Instead of City Council

**Vote Passes 4-1 with Mayor Simpson excused and Councilmember Davis recused**

* **What is this?** City Council can delegate decision-making to an independent third-party officer to conduct the ethics hearing related to an alleged breach of confidentiality by Councilmember Sharon Davis following a City Council executive session. This decision follows a preliminary investigation into the allegations. The allegations relate to sections 2-275, 2-378, and 2-379 of the [City’s Code of Ethics](https://library.municode.com/co/arvada/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH2AD_ARTVIIICOET).
* **Why does it matter?** One or more members of Council may be witnesses, impeding their ability to serve as decision-maker. Approving the resolution avoids conflict, preserves fairness and credibility of the proceeding, and honors the parties’ joint request.
* **What happens next?** Following the hearing officer’s decision, City Council would still be responsible for determining whether to dismiss the complaint or impose any penalty as available in City Code and required by two-thirds vote.

## Ordinances (First Reading)

Ordinances relate to local city laws and generally refer to local rules and regulations. Ordinances require a First Reading announcing the date for a Public Hearing. The first reading of ordinances are voted on together.

**Vote passes 6-0 with Mayor Simpson excused. All Public Hearings for the ordinances below are set for October 6, 2026 at 6:15 p.m.**

### Annexation and Zoning of the land for the Replacement Water Treatment Plant (Item 1: A, B, C)

This project includes three ordinances under one project. Two ordinances annexing the two parcels of land, and one for the rezoning.

* **What is this?** The City is seeking to annex and rezone land currently located in unincorporated Jefferson County for the [replacement of the Arvada Water Treatment Plant (AWTP)](http://arvadaco.gov/watertreatment). Annexation places the land parcel within city limits. Zoning designates the type of land use per the Land Development Code as Parks and Open Space. All government uses are placed within this designation in Arvada. Zoning will also provide an exception to 35-foot height restriction in the Land Development Code. A maximum height of 55 feet is proposed for the facility.
* **Why does it matter?** The AWTP is at the end of its useful life and needs to be replaced to continue providing high-quality, reliable drinking water to Arvada. The replacement of the AWTP was identified as a key priority in utilities long-range planning efforts. Following an extensive siting study, the parcel in question has been identified as the preferred site for the replacement plant and has been purchased by the City.
* **What happens next?** The ordinance will return for a public hearing and Council vote on October 6th. Placing the proposed facility within City limits streamlines the permitting and approval process. This should result in a more efficient design and construction process, saving time, money, and additional resources for initial construction and future modification. Annexation also places the property within the jurisdiction of City of Arvada emergency services and response.

### Repealing and Reenacting Table 3-1-2-2 Residential Land Use by Zoning District

* **What is this?** To comply with Colorado Proposition 123, which provides affordable housing funding to cities, the City must enact an expedited review process for developments that meet affordable housing criteria. In the City's current Land Development Code, residential projects in Mixed Use zoning districts are subject to a Conditional Use permit, which can cause the review process to take longer than the state-mandated 90 days. The City team has recommended that Council remove the Conditional Use requirement from affordable housing developments in Mixed Use zoning districts.
* **Why does it matter?** If the Conditional Use requirement remains in place after the end of this year, the City would be out of compliance with the state. This means Arvada would lose eligibility for funding affordable housing projects under Proposition 123.
* **What happens next?** The ordinance will return for a public hearing and Council vote on October 6th. If approved, the City would no longer require the Conditional Use permit for affordable housing developments in Mixed Use zoning districts.

### Authorizing a Moratorium on Primary Use Data Center Applications

* **What is this?** A temporary 18-month moratorium on the submission and acceptance of applications for primary use data center facilities within Arvada city limits.
* **Why does it matter?** Data centers are a rapidly growing industry that can have significant impacts on the electric grid, utility rates, community resilience, and the environment. The City’s current zoning, land use, and building codes do not address data centers as a distinct land use, leaving the City without adequate standards to evaluate impacts related to setbacks, noise, water consumption, and energy use associated with these facilities.
* **What happens next?** The ordinance will return for a public hearing and Council vote on October 6th. The moratorium would allow the City time to develop appropriate definitions, standards, and conditions for data centers before additional applications are received.
