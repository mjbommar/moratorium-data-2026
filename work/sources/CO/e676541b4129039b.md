# Jefferson County Issues Temporary Moratorium on Data Centers

source: https://www.jeffco.us/m/newsflash/home/detail/2596
fetched: 2026-09-23T12:06:19+00:00

![](https://www.jeffco.us/ImageRepository/Document?documentID=45338)

jeffco.us Home - News Posted on May 19, 2026

During a public hearing on Tuesday May 19, the Jefferson County Board of Commissioners voted to issue a 10-month moratorium on data centers within the county. This moratorium applies to any new applications for development of, or rezoning to allow data centers, but excludes land that is already specifically zoned to allow data centers through a Planned Development, provided such data center is at least 1,500 feet from any dwelling.

“It’s critical for us to evaluate how data centers will interact with the Wildland Urban Interface (WUI), our water supply, the environment and our community’s overall health,” Jefferson County Commissioner Rachel Zenzinger said. “This moratorium is a necessary pause for us to gather that information.”

WUI refers to areas where development meets or intermingles with undeveloped wildland vegetation, increasing risks related to fire. Jefferson County Planning Manager Russell Clark said county staff are taking this time to evaluate how other communities are addressing the rising demand for data centers. Commissioners noted that they are weighing environmental concerns with potential economic benefits for the community.

“While we recognize the potential job growth of a new data center, commissioners must weigh the impact on our community,” Jefferson County Commissioner Lesley Dahlkemper said. “The next 10 months will allow us to work closely with our community and staff to gather input and develop thoughtful county policies.”

This decision comes as communities across the country grapple with the impacts data centers have on local infrastructure, natural resources, utility costs and public health. Data centers often require substantial amounts of electricity and water for cooling, which can strain local resources and increase utility costs for residents.

“There’s an increase in demand for data centers, but modern-day data centers come with a variety of considerations. In Jeffco, our land, water and energy are our most cherished resources, and we want to ensure that we are being responsible caretakers of those resources,” Jefferson County Commissioner Andy Kerr said.

Many cities and counties nationwide, including the City and County of Denver, have issued moratoriums ranging from 6 months to a year.
