3) GENERAL BUSINESS

a. RESOLUTION NO. 26-38: A RESOLUTION OF THE TOWN COUNCIL OF
THE TOWN OF HUDSON APPROVING A POLICY ON PUBLIC COMMENT
DURING TOWN COUNCIL MEETINGS

Town Manager Lange presented a revised public comment policy consisting of eleven
provisions detailing the rules for public participation, including requirements for speaker
recognition, designated comment periods, speaker residency disclosures, time limits, and the
handling of written, phone, and in-person statements. The proposal also outlined guidelines
regarding the scope of topics, behavioral expectations, and the presiding officer's authority to
manage the comment periods.

During the council's discussion on the proposed revisions, differing viewpoints were raised
regarding the restrictions. Mayor Hammock suggested further limiting the duration of the
second comment period at the end of meetings to one minute. Conversely, Councilmember
Reyes expressed opposition to eliminating remote comment options, emphasizing the council's
duty to listen and stating an intention to vote against the resolution, while noting that the main
issue is helping public commenters better understand when to speak on specific agenda items.

Councilmember Nolf made a motion, seconded by Mayor Pro Tem Post to approve Resolution 26-38.

The vote was as follows:

Aye: Mayor Hammock, Mayor Pro Tem Post, and Councilmember Buesgens
Nay: Councilmembers Reyes, Cole, and Nolf

Mayor Hammock declared the motion failed with a tie, 3-3- vote.

d. July 2026 HFPD Town Council Update

Assistant Chief Brian Jackson presented the monthly update for the Hudson Fire Protection
District (HFPD), introducing himself and noting his 24 years of fire service experience,
including specialty rescue teams in Boulder County. Assistant Chief Jackson committed to
providing these routine updates moving forward to maintain transparency and share a forward
vision for the district.

The update highlighted a current call volume of 588 service calls for 2026, which is tracking at
a typical yearly average despite a general climb in call loads and code requirements. Ongoing
district activities include community events, station tours, collaborative safety planning for
fireworks, and the completion of recent Apparatus Rodeo training. Additionally, the district's

7) COUNCIL COMMENTS

Councilmember Cole provided an update regarding the State Highway 52 Coalition Meeting,
reporting that funding for the County Road 52 and County Road 59 roundabout is not yet
secured despite prior indications. It was noted that federal legislative tracking shows support
for the project is being picked up; however, the county remains uncertain regarding alternative
funding mechanisms if federal support does not materialize. Additionally, project delivery has
been delayed due to extended ditch and property negotiations with an involved landowner,
causing the Colorado Department of Transportation (CDOT) to push the timeline out to
September 2027, prompting the county to evaluate options at County Road 4 and Hayesmount.

Councilmember Reyes requested an update regarding the property and water rights for a parcel
to the south. Town Manager Lange responded that the augmentation pond project is scheduled
for review during the September budget meetings, with construction likely projected for 2027.

Mayor Pro Tem Post requested an opportunity to address the public regarding a proposed
immigration detention facility and concluded by wishing the community a happy Fourth of
July.
