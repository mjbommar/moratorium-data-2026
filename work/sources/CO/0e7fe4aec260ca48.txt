Authorization under Article 16-100.A of the Boulder County Land Use Code for Text
Amendments to the Land Use Code related to Data Centers and Detention Centers
STAFF: Ethan Abner, Long Range Planner II
Public Testimony will not be taken—action requested.
SUMMARY
Staff request authorization from the Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) to amend the
Boulder County Land Use Code (“the Code”) as it relates to data center and detention
center uses, pursuant to Article 16 of the Code. Staff will update Article 4 as well as other
articles or provisions in the Code that may be necessary to integrate these changes.
Staff presented preliminary information about data centers and detention centers at a work
session with the BOCC on May 26, 2026. The Commissioners expressed concerns about
recent land use trends and impacts related to data centers and detention centers. Many
communities are facing challenges related to the development of resource-intensive data
centers or the detention of individuals in structures or places that were never intended to
support these land uses. The primary concern is that the Code does not currently deﬁne
data centers or detention centers as speciﬁc uses, identify the scale at which these uses
may or may not be appropriate, or identify zoning districts in which these uses may or may
not occur. The Commissioners expressed interest in prohibiting these uses in the

unincorporated county. Through a Code change, staff will deﬁne these uses and identify
the places and scale at which they may or may not be permitted in the unincorporated
county.
Pending this authorization, staff will also request that the BOCC consider enacting a
moratorium on Boulder County’s processing of applications for new projects related to
data center or detention center uses while we draft regulations (Attachment A). The
purpose of this moratorium is to allow staff time to formulate and publicly review
amendments to the Land Use Code, while avoiding development which may run counter to
the purpose and intent of any proposed regulations. The timeline for this Code update will
align with the duration of the moratorium, with completion of the regulations within six
months or less. Due to the nature of this update and direction received from the
Commissioners, staff anticipate that public engagement will consist primarily of informing
the public of the update, collecting feedback on the draft text amendments, and the
opportunity to provide public testimony at public hearings before the Planning Commission
and Board of County Commissioners.
ACTION REQUESTED
Staff request authorization from the Board of County Commissioners to pursue text
amendments related to data centers and detention centers, including changes to Article 4
of the Boulder County Land Use Code, and any other relevant articles or provisions that
may be necessary to incorporate these changes pursuant to Article 16 of the Land Use
Code.

                                             Recitals

      A. For purposes of this Resolution, a Data Center is defined as a facility designed or used
primarily for the centralized storage, management, processing, or transmission of digital data,
characterized by high-density computing equipment, specialized electrical and cooling systems,
and backup power over 500 KW capacity. Facilities with an IT equipment load of 500 KW or
greater are considered Data Centers. A Detention Center is defined as a facility designed or used
primarily to confine individuals under civil, criminal, or immigration authority for any duration,
regardless of whether the stay is intended to be temporary, short-term, or for transfer purposes.

      B. Data and Detention Centers have the potential to create an unmanageable burden on
critical energy and water resources and affect the character of the surrounding area.

     C. Data Centers use significant amounts of electricity and water to power servers and cool
equipment. They also generate noise and emissions from air compressors, backup generators, or
HVAC systems supporting the cooling of servers and other equipment and tend to have security
and safety features such as security gates, fences, or bright lighting.

      D. Detention Centers have significant land use impacts including adding density to areas
without supporting infrastructure. Detention centers are generally incompatible with residential
areas and with the rural economy and landscape and may include tall security fences, barbed
wire, outdoor lighting, gated accesses, and guard towers or other prominent support structures.

     E. Boulder County has a long history through the Boulder County Comprehensive Plan (the
“Comprehensive Plan”) and the Boulder County Land Use Code (the “Code”), of environmental
protection and directing development to urban areas where services and infrastructure exist.

      F. The Code was enacted to promote the health, safety, and general welfare of the present
and future inhabitants of Boulder County, and to guide future growth, development and the
distribution of land uses within the county. The Code plays a large role in determining how
unincorporated Boulder County will look in the future by the way it guides new construction and
development today.

      G. The Code does not specifically define Data or Detention Centers, nor does it identify
these uses as permitted in a particular zoning district. Code amendments may therefore be
needed to regulate and establish standards for distance, noise and other ancillary impacts of Data

      H. The Local Government Land Use Control Enabling Act, C.R.S. §§ 29-20-101 et
seq., provides the county with the broad authority to plan for and regulate the use of land to
best protect and promote the health, safety, and general welfare of the present and future
inhabitants of Boulder County and to guide future growth, development, and distribution of
land uses within Boulder County.

      I. Boulder County Community Planning & Permitting staff have begun to analyze
whether the existing Code provisions are sufficient to protect and promote the public health,
safety, and welfare, in light of the possible impacts of Data and Detention Centers on the
surrounding community.

     J. Staff anticipate the time needed to analyze necessary amendments to the Code, as well as
develop a plan to propose and adopt Code amendments, is approximately six months in total.

      K. In light of the foregoing, the Board determines that circumstances warrant the
immediate enactment of a temporary moratorium on processing applications for Data and
Detention centers in the unincorporated county. This moratorium will remain in place during
the county’s planning and land use regulation amendment process to protect and promote
the public health, safety, and welfare, and to avoid development which may contravene the
results and purpose of this study and process.

      L. The Board further determines that it will schedule and hold a public hearing on this
temporary moratorium and related matters as soon as practicable after this Resolution’s adoption,
for the purposes of receiving public comment on the moratorium and considering whether to
terminate, extend, or otherwise amend the moratorium.

                                  Therefore, the Board resolves

    1. A temporary moratorium on processing applications for Data and Detention Centers, as
defined above, in the unincorporated county is reasonable and necessary to protect the public
health, safety, and welfare of the county. This moratorium begins immediately on the date of
this Resolution and ends at the close of business on December 2, 2026. The purpose of the
moratorium is to allow time to formulate and publicly review necessary amendments to current
county land use regulations. The Community Planning & Permitting Department is directed not
to accept, process, or approve any Data or Detention Center applications after the effective date
of this Resolution.

    2. Staff shall set and notice a public hearing to occur no later than 45 days from the date of
this Resolution for the purpose of deciding whether the moratorium imposed by this Resolution
should be terminated or otherwise amended.
