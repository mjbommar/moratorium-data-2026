Ref. Fremont County BOCC Meeting 6/11/2024 17:58

1. Recognition of Fremont County Department of Transportation staff for their

efforts on Oak Creek Grade on May 29, 2024, putting out a fire on a USPS

mail truck.

Commissioner McFall asked Gerry Buser and Ron Blanchard to approach the podium t to recognize them


for their courageous behavior.

Mr. Blanchard said that on that day another coworker was working further down the road and radioed


that there was smoke rising in the distance and didn’t know if it was an accident or what was going on.

Mr. Blanchard offered to run up around the corner to check it out. When he made it around the corner, he

saw the mail truck engulfed in flames and beginning to roll down the hill. Thankfully the driver had

already exited the vehicle. Mr. Blanchard decided to roll up to the mail truck with the grater and set the

brake to let the mail truck glide into the grater, making it come to a halt. He choked the wheels, put the

grader in reverse in case of the need to flee quickly, when the flames settled, he backed away to a safe

distance and put a rock behind the tire to prevent it from rolling down the hill more. He then radioed Mr.

Buser asking for their location to see if they assist with the situation.

Mr. Buser explained how it was perfect timing considering the trucks were already loaded and on the


way. He put the vehicle in high gear and pushed the gas pedal to the floor, blowing smoke and going

approximately 20 miles an hour up the hill c carrying 3,500 gallons of water. He backed up to the mail truck

and used the rear sprayer to douse the vehicle and put out the remainder of the flames, saving the area

from what could have been a catastrophic fire.

Commissioner Bell, Grantham, and McFall all expressed deep appreciation for Mr. Buser and Mr.


Blanchard.

Ref. Fremont County BOCC Meeting 6/11/2024 23:08

2. Presentation by Marty Peek, Fremont County Veteran’s Service Officer,

regarding the Veterans’ Honor Flight to Washington D.C

Commissioner McFall introduced Evan Royal Schmidt, he is a World War II Veteran that was honored


this year and participated in the Honor Flight to Washington D.C and Marty Peek that assisted with the

presentation.

Mr. Peek began the presentation by explaining that Mr. Schmidt has been a Florence resident his entire


life, he is a hundred years old, and served in the South Pacific at the cease of hostilities. The purpose of

the Honor Flight is to bring Veterans back to Washington D.C to see their memorial at no charge to the

Veteran. Mr. Peek explained that the trip was very well organized, and action packed. There were

crowds at every location including fire department and the police departments. The first day included a

visit to the World War II Memorial where Mr. Peek spent several hours with Mr. Schmidt viewing and

discussing the Memorial.

Mr. Schmidt spoke about how that trip was one of the most memorable times of his life.


Mr. Peek wanted to add that at the Memorial there were 10 school busses present with hundreds of


middle school children that had obviously had a history class on World War II because they were

fascinated that Mr. Schmidt was a real-World War II Veteran. The other places that they visited that day

included the Navy Memorial, Lincoln Memorial, Vietnam Memorial Wall, and the Korean Memorial.

The following day they visited the Arlington National Cemetery which contains the Tomb of the

Unknown Solider where they were able to watch the changing on the guard, the Air Force Memorial,

and the Marine Corps Memorial. When the group reached Denver Airport there were swarms of people

welcoming them home by cheering, hollering, and clapping.

Ref. Fremont County BOCC Meeting 6/11/2024 52:12

5. Resolution No. 24, Series of 2024 Permitting the Lawful Possession of

Firearms in Fremont County Government Buildings

Commissioner Grantham said that this resolution is the result of legislation that was passed this year


under Senate Bill 131 that basically prohibits the carrying of firearms in what is deemed sensitive

spaces. Some of these spaces include county buildings and many other government buildings, but there

was an opt out option allowing local governments to essentially ignore that new law.

Commissioner Grantham moved to approve Resolution No. 24 series 2024 permitting the lawful


possession of firearms in Fremont County government buildings. Commissioner Bell seconded the

motion. Upon Vote: Commissioner Grantham, aye; Commissioner Bell, aye; Commissioner McFall, aye.

The motion carried by unanimous consent.

Ref. Fremont County BOCC Meeting 6/11/2024 1:01:51

6. Resolution No. 25, Series of 2024 Imposing an Emergency Temporary

Moratorium on the Construction of Major Utility Projects in All Zone

Districts Within Fremont County, Colorado

Commissioner Bell r recited Resolution No. 25. The County has received some inquiries on t things such as


electric battery storage units, wind power, and solar power. The purpose of this is to prohibit accepting or

working through any of those applications until the BOCC sets rules in place.

Commissioner McFall said that it’s important to set these regulations to be able to control things such as


how big the project can be, the location, or only be allowed in certain zones.

Commissioner Bell moved to approve Resolution No. 25 series of 2024 imposing an emergency


temporary moratorium on the construction of major utility projects in all zone districts within Fremont

County, Colorado. Commissioner Grantham seconded the motion. Upon Vote: Commissioner Bell, aye;

Commissioner Grantham, aye; Commissioner McFall, aye. The motion carried by unanimous consent.
