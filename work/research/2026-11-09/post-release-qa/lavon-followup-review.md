# Lavon recovered-source review

The recovered WFAA-syndicated Yahoo article explicitly reports that Lavon's council unanimously approved a data-center moratorium and describes a 90-day term with the possibility of another 90 days. This is stronger evidence of a reported vote than the excerpt previously available. **It does not reliably establish an August 6 or August 7 official vote or effective date:** the captured article has an August timestamp but also refers to official approval on a second reading in mid-November. That chronology makes the date and version of the captured text uncertain. The report is secondary evidence of a vote; the instrument, vote minutes, final term and operative dates remain to be verified.

The capture's JSON-LD `datePublished` and `dateModified` both say **2026-08-07 02:05:42 UTC**. This is **August 6, 9:05:42 p.m. Central Daylight Time** in Lavon. The article references a Thursday meeting, and August 6, 2026 was Thursday. If the publication timestamp and the captured article text describe a contemporaneous account of that Thursday, August 6 is the natural calendar inference for the meeting. The source does not print that vote date, establish a precise official vote time, or establish that the present text was unchanged since that timestamp. Its mid-November second-reading reference is an apparent temporal conflict, not proof of a specific update, timestamp error, or second instrument. August 7 is the UTC publication date, not an independently established Lavon vote date. The earlier tracker attributed August 7 adoption, but that attribution does not resolve the discrepancy.

The article's concluding sentence is awkwardly worded: it associates the 90-day period with official approval at a second reading in mid-November and says a further 90-day extension would require a vote. It does not establish that the 90 days had already begun, a definite end date, or an enacted extension. The similar subject, term, project context and mid-November reading are indications of a possible relationship to the October proposal, but are insufficient to identify the same instrument or prove two distinct actions. The November reference may reflect changed text or a metadata/content discrepancy; its cause is unknown.

The retained October 20 official agenda, item 6A, pages 1–2, scheduled a public hearing, discussion and first reading for a proposed temporary moratorium. It is an agenda, not minutes. The separately retained October 21 local report says council advanced the first reading, but staff said it did not take effect that night; a resident described a proposed 90 days and possible 90-day extension. The November 3 official agenda, page 3 item 10, lists a November 17 second reading. This is a schedule, not evidence of the outcome. Neither that first reading nor the syndicated report establishes current force. The official vote record, signed ordinance(s), effective date and later action remain open.
