The official chamber summaries identify [Sherman](https://legislature.maine.gov/LawMakerWeb/summary.asp?LD=1259&SessionID=9), [Stanley](https://legislature.maine.gov/LawMakerWeb/summary.asp?LD=1722&SessionID=12), and [Dill](https://legislature.maine.gov/LawMakerWeb/summary.asp?LD=192&SessionID=9) as the original sponsors. Dill’s bill was a House bill introduced in 2011; the database combines her House and Senate service in one session row, and the bill summary identifies her as a representative.

The actual rendered Sessions grids, including their party cells, were inspected in the preserved HTML and text. Maine’s [Law and Legislative Reference Library](https://legislature.maine.gov/lawlibrary/legislators/9212) links to the biographical database. Its [Sessions search description](https://history.mainelegislature.org/Presto/content/AdvancedSearch.aspx?ctID=NjJkY2RjYTMtM2Q5Zi00NmUyLWJjOTctMDA0NWVkZWNmNTRk) says the library maintains it, identifies sources for modern records, and describes party data as reliably available from the early 1870s. The library describes the database as informational rather than an official record of service.

The legislative sessions and bill events occurred in 2011–2012 and 2017–2018; the party grids were captured on November 9, 2026. The retained bill history and text were captured earlier. Their body and text hashes, the sponsor summaries, and all 15 newly archived navigation and source versions were independently recomputed successfully. The captured redirect and unrendered responses are not treated as party evidence. Closure is limited to these named historical records and their identified holds.
