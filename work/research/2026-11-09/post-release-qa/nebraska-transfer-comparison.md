# Nebraska LB1111 introduced text versus enacted LB1010

Two guarded summary cells now distinguish the introduced proposal, the enacted act and a section-specific effective date. This is a direct textual comparison, not proof of the complete AM2422 amendment history or an assessment of act-wide current force. All bill identities and recorded stages remain distinct and unchanged.

Sources: **I** = retained seven-page LB1111 introduced print; **E** = retained twelve-page LB1010 enacted slip; **S** = retained compiled section 70-1506; **H** = retained LB1111 history. Original URLs, acquisition clocks, body/text hashes and nine visually reviewed page rasters are pinned in [evidence](nebraska-transfer-evidence.json). No new source acquisition occurs.

| Topic | LB1111 introduced | LB1010 enacted | Source locator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data-center classes | General data-center definition has no MW floor; a separate “large data center” has peak demand at least 20 MW. | Section 70-1506 defines a data center with peak demand at least 10 MW. The separate Large Load Customer Regulation Act covers new/expanded retail interconnections exceeding 20 MW at one site. These are different classes, not one interchangeable threshold. | I p.4; E pp.1,7 |
| Statewide PRB report | Representative organization would file a statewide data-center load report with PRB, including forecasts, generation/transmission needs/costs and stranded/decommissioning information. | The proposed section 70-1025 amendment is absent from this retained act; its data-center report is instead an owner/operator report to the Department of Water, Energy, and Environment and the Natural Resources Committee. | I pp.2-3; E pp.1,7-8 |
| Annual reporting details | Supplier website would disclose specified information about proposed/existing data centers; PRB report would compile it. | Owner/operator report is due by September 30 each year and includes water use, tax exemptions and incentive payments along with listed facility/energy information. The final supplier-website provision concerns cryptocurrency-operation counts/energy use, rather than reproducing the proposed general data-center website scheme. | I pp.3,6-7; E p.8 |
| Utility cost/risk terms | Public supplier would have mandatory rate/contract duties for “large data centers.” | Section 70-1506 authorizes supplier cost conditions for its data-center class; the separate greater-than-20-MW act supplies additional interconnection/cost-risk standards. Do not erase the difference between those provisions or between “shall” and “may.” | I pp.4-5; E pp.1,7 |
| Decommissioning | Large-data-center terms would include local requirements, a plan and security bond/instrument by the first year after commercial operation. | Section 70-1506 directly requires the owner/operator to bear all decommissioning costs; it does not reproduce that plan/first-year-bond formulation. This comparison does not waive other law or contract requirements. | I p.5; E p.8 |
| Community benefit agreement | Utility-imposed terms would require the large data center to enter an agreement mitigating adverse impacts. | Direct owner/operator duty for the act’s data-center class to enter an agreement with affected communities. | I p.5; E p.8 |
| Private supply/self-generation | Proposed service-limitation and interconnection commands appear in section 70-1506. | Those commands are not reproduced in the compared final section; separate large-load flexibility standards remain distinct. | I p.6; E pp.1,7-8 |
| Crypto and curtailment | Would narrow the crypto definition to a data center at least 1 MW and no more than 20 MW; would extend the stated interruption duty to large data centers. | Retains the at-least-1-MW facility-based crypto definition and crypto-specific interruption clause in section 70-1506. Separate large-load curtailment procedures must not be conflated with that clause. | I pp.3,7; E pp.1,7-8 |
| Effective date | The named bill was postponed; no effective-date inference is made for it. | Compiled section 70-1506 cites LB1010 section 12 and explicitly lists July 18, 2026. That date is accepted only for the identified section, leaving the flat act-wide date blank. | H terminal history; S source/effective-date note |

The enacted slip states Governor approval April 14. H records postponement and a provisions/portions cross-reference on April 17. That later docket-entry date does not prove amendment adoption then; no full or exact transfer through AM2422 is inferred. The underlying PRB-minutes account remains unverified even though the final text can now be compared directly.
