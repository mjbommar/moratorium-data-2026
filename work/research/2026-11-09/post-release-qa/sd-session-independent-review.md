It was compiled under the Legislative Research Council’s direction from the Legislative Information System and says the session concluded March 30, 2026. Each entry stops at the stage described below. This is a conclusion about these named bills and the final index’s record; it is not a survey of successor legislation or current law.

The [2026 calendar](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/284593.pdf) lists 38 legislative days. The [joint rules](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/295722.pdf), rules 7-17, 7-19, 7-19.1 and 7-20, distinguish table and deferral and permit recall or taking a bill from the table: ordinarily by a majority of committee members-elect subject to the stated deadline, or for immediate consideration by two-thirds under the exception. Thus the committee votes alone do not prove a bill could never return. The final index supplies the additional post-session record for the four named entries.

Joint rules 5-11, 5-11.1, 5-12 and 5-13 address notice and reconsideration, including what happens after a notice-giver misses the stated deadline and the rule for the final seven legislative days. SB 193’s index entry preserves the second notice. It does not itself show what happened to that notice; no subsequent motion or missed deadline is inferred. The existing phrase “never brought back to a vote” is more categorical than the retained evidence supports. The private adjudication provides a separate exact replacement with its row guard.

Prior material-field review inspected the [introduced HB 1005](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/26317/291571), [introduced HB 1301](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/27287/301188), and [SB 193 amendment 193C](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/304644.html). For SB 232, the prior adjudication inspected the [introduced bill](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/27269/301228) and [adopted 232A](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/301393.html); the [bill page](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/27269) identifies Howard as prime and links to her [2026 profile](https://sdlegislature.gov/Legislators/Profile/4763/Detail), which identifies Taffy Howard as Republican. The session index also supplies the member identities and parties for the other three bills. Its bill entries name House prime Roe for HB 1005 (page 93), Healy, Larson and Muckey for HB 1301 (page 175), and Senate prime Crabtree for SB 193 (page 66). Its member headings identify Kent Roe, district 04, R (PDF page 228); Erin Healy, district 10, D (page 216); Liz Larson, district 10, D (page 199); Erik Muckey, district 15, D (page 222); and Casey Crabtree, district 08, R (page 192). These match the recorded cells. HB 1005’s cell names the House prime sponsor; the index also names Crabtree as Senate prime, so the cell is a selective sponsor listing. The prior proposal judgments and these affiliation sources clear the identified material-field questions within the named-bill scope.
