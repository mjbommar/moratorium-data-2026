The recorded failed-stage questions for **SB 234 and SB 235** can be closed within the retained-source observation scope. **SB 232 remains unresolved**: its recorded tabling can be reopened, and the retained history does not establish a later revival or terminal disposition. Proposed cell changes are separate from these recommendations and require coordinator review.

- **SB 232:** The [bill history](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/27269) records adoption of 232A, failed amended do-pass 3–5, and tabling 5–3 on February 18; no subsequent action appears in the history captured November 6 UTC. Remove the unqualified “bill died” assertion from the status. The introduced 50 MW threshold and adopted two-of-four definition and exemption are supported by the retained versions and prior field review. The bill identifies Senator Howard as prime, but the full name and party were not independently established by a retained profile in this review. The existing failed categories remain unconfirmed; do not change them automatically solely because current law was not researched.
- **SB 234:** The [history](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/27289) records adoption of 234A and deferral to the 41st legislative day 5–4, with no subsequent action in the captured history. The guide explains the procedural meaning of that deferral. The retained introduced and adopted versions and prior judgments support the mixed proposal and the strictly greater-than-100-MW utility/water chapter definition. The actual sponsor link matches the [2026 Casey Crabtree profile](https://sdlegislature.gov/Legislators/Profile/4741/Detail), which identifies him as Republican.
- **SB 235:** The [history](https://sdlegislature.gov/Session/Bill/27290) records adoption of 235A, failed amended do-pass 4–5, and deferral to the 41st legislative day 5–4, with no subsequent action in the captured history. The guide supports the same bounded failed-stage conclusion. The retained introduced text and amendment strike markup support deletion of the proposed exemptions and the strictly greater-than-100-MW chapter definition, consistent with prior field judgments. Its sponsor link matches the same 2026 Republican profile.

The legislature’s [committee guide, page 5](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/Student%20Reference%20Series/72124.pdf), explains that a committee majority can bring a tabled bill back and that postponement to a nonexistent 41st day kills a bill because the legislature cannot meet for more than 40 legislative days. Its [glossary, page 3](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/Student%20Reference%20Series/72118.pdf), similarly describes tabling as postponement with possible later consideration. These are official student reference documents, not the formal 2026 Standing Rules: both response headers identify a 2021 canonical version and the guide includes a 2021 chart. The guide supports an interpretation of the recorded outcomes; it does not itself provide a separate final-disposition entry or establish every possible procedural exception.
