# Loveland: resolution and term adjudication

The city-published, signed [Resolution R-48-2026](https://cilovelandco.civicweb.net/document/521898/) establishes that the suspension was adopted on September 15, 2026 and became effective on adoption. Section 3 provides a twelve-month term, ending earlier if City Council approves, adopts and finalizes comprehensive data-center regulations in the Unified Development Code and/or Municipal Code, or if Council sooner terminates the suspension. The resulting twelve-month endpoint is **September 15, 2027, conditional on no earlier qualifying action**. Whether an earlier action occurred, and the suspension's current legal force, remain unverified.

The resolution's third page visibly bears mayoral and clerk signatures and a city seal; the fourth bears an attorney approval-as-to-form signature. The city's [unofficial voting results](https://cilovelandco.civicweb.net/document/519022/) record a 5–4 amendment from nine to twelve months, followed by 7–2 approval of the amended resolution. The voting document does not identify the amendment's mover. Its label is unofficial voting results, not approved minutes.

The earlier six-month draft and unsigned nine-month copy do not establish the adopted term. An October 13 report described staff as saying on October 12 that the moratorium began in October; the signed resolution resolves the original effective-date question in favor of September 15. The October account remains dated context and does not establish a later legal change. The signed copy was published as an October 27 study-session attachment and retrieved November 9; neither date is its adoption or effective date, and retrieval does not establish continuing force.

Recommend the guarded changes in the private adjudication: identify the resolution, remove the unsupported mover attribution from the proposed vote description, state the full earlier-ending conditions, and enter the conditional endpoint. The existing outcome and sector require no change. Retain the recorded active category without treating it as a fresh finding of current force. Whole-record closure is **not** recommended on this narrow review alone.
