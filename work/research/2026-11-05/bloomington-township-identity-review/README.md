# Bloomington: primary township identity terms

This follow-up adds primary government naming and electorate evidence to the
[earlier ballot/returns recovery](../illinois-remaining-ballot-review/README.md).
It does not allocate a canonical identity or prove passage of the placement motion.
Canonical totals remain sixteen published measures / 40 pending frozen groups.

Exa recovered the [June 1 special-town-meeting agenda](https://bloomingtonil.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=12760,plainText=false)).
Page 1 identifies the meeting body as **Town of the City of Bloomington**, with a
township logo. Its Public Policy Vote item proposes advisory questions for voters
in **City of Bloomington Township**, McLean County, Illinois, at the November 3
election. The data-center wording matches the actual specimen question after
removing the agenda label and layout whitespace.

These primary terms support a township government record distinct from the city
municipality addressed by the question. They improve on a city-only provisional
seed and a secondary report of township sponsorship. They do not justify making
the municipality a township alias, assuming boundary equivalence, or linking the
advisory question automatically to the separate city-council moratorium.

The [official city homepage](https://www.bloomingtonil.gov/) links the
[records portal](https://bloomingtonil.portal.civicclerk.com/). Its current event
list separately names **City of Bloomington Township Board of Trustees** and
links documents served by the same CivicClerk API tenant. The retained HTML
hyperlinks were checked directly, providing publisher context for the agenda.
The portal's future meeting listing is not evidence that a meeting occurred.

The source is an **agenda**, not minutes. It establishes a scheduled June 1
meeting and proposed placement context; it does not show a vote, passage,
qualification certification or final statutory placement route. Those fields
remain unresolved. As the preceding packet records, the specimen's signed
true-copy statement also does not certify the election result.

The original client text's ordering was poor. A separately registered
`pdftotext -layout` extraction preserves the ordered text from both PDF pages,
while leaving the original body/text untouched. Only page 1 received substantive
visual review. Three source versions and six claims pass hash/text/visual/archive
checks. One value-specific receipt reproduces the reviewed page.

The bounded follow-up used two Exa searches ($0.014) and five of six fetch
reservations. Two fetched frontend assets were reference-chase support artifacts;
they are retained privately and are not accepted as legal evidence. The three
evidentiary sources are the agenda, government homepage and records portal.
The original placement disposition, boundary reference, election certification,
later implementation and legal history remain open.
