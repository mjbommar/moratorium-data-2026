# Downe Township primary ballot review

Status: official sample ballot reviewed; candidate remains unpublished.

Recovered the county-hosted two-page Downe sample ballot through bc-web. Both rendered PDF pages were inspected. PDF page 1 contains the ballot faces and identifies the Township of Downe and November 3, 2026 election; page 2 contains voting instructions and the same election date. The public question asks whether voters support data centers. Its interpretive statement expressly describes gauging public sentiment and explains that no expresses opposition. This supports advisory sentiment classification; it does not enact a ban or authorize a project.

The exact English question ends with a period. The interpretive statement contains a printed “what” typo, retained in the review instead of silently corrected. This is a county-published sample version, not proof of a cast ballot, result, later canvass or operative law. Referral resolution and result-source review remain open. The original frozen candidate and canonical snapshots are unchanged by this review.

The JSON review pins source bytes, extracted text and both visually inspected renders. Source retrieval uses the persistent capped client in `/data0/moratorium-refresh-2026/new-jersey-ballot-primary-review`; raw source and render files remain private.

## Referral history

Both two-page township resolutions were read completely in separately registered full-document Tesseract OCR and visually checked against all four 150-DPI renders. Their original empty retrieval extractions were preserved.

R-99-2026, adopted July 13, reports a petition with 240 signatures and recites a binding initiative procedure and concurrent first-reading ordinance proposal to make data centers non-permitted in all zones. Those recitals are attributed to the resolution; petition sufficiency, applicable statutory rules and ordinance adoption are not independently established.

R-111-2026, adopted August 17, refers a public-input question. Its attached November 3 question and interpretive statement agree substantively with the later county sample ballot, including the printed “what” typo. Both resolutions show five affirmative committee votes. Printed copy-certification blocks are not result certifications; their date/signature lines are blank.

The governing-body referral is directly evidenced, while the earlier petition remains part of the origin history. The July binding-initiative recital and later sentiment wording must both survive review; neither establishes that the vote enacted a prohibition. The concurrent proposed ordinance and later disposition remain open.

The retained NJ.com return reports 62 yes and 425 no with 100 percent of precincts reporting. The freshly retrieved county index did not supply a linked 2026 general-election report. Do not label the secondary return certified or infer a legal ban. See `resolution-review.json` for source and render hashes.

## Ordinance version limits

The township-hosted Ordinance 2026-04 PDF was recovered and all five scanned pages were OCRed and visually read. Although the title describes making data centers non-permitted throughout the township, this version’s body contains recitals, purpose/findings, severability, conditional effectiveness and notice/planning-board referral directions. No distinct zoning-code amendment or explicit operative prohibition clause was identified in the reviewed five pages. That is a version-specific observation, not a conclusion about the township’s final law.

The packet also mixes July 13 adoption language with an unsigned August 17 post-hearing notice. Its record sheet identifies July 13 introduction and August 17 hearing but leaves final adoption, final vote and final-passage publication/posting fields blank. The five affirmative marks are introduction votes. Effectiveness is conditioned on final passage and required publication. These features prevent using the title or packet alone as proof of a currently operative ban.

The township notice index separately links a revised introduction and final-passage notice. Those links are retained in `ordinance-version-review.json` for the next review. No operative restriction or ballot-to-instrument causal link is accepted yet.
