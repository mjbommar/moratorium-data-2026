## Harwood proposed ArticleX

The full16-page legal body was read as proposed text. It requires enhanced procedures for qualified industrial development that needs **discretionary municipal approval**; a ministerial permit alone does not trigger it. It includes digital infrastructure alongside other industrial activities and expressly retains municipal legislative authority. It is not a blanket data-center ban, resource cap, or future project-voter-approval requirement.

- **Any one major threshold:** peak electrical demand at least100MW; development area at least100acres; specified water-demand test; generation at least100MW; or major-source/TitleV/equivalent air authorization.
- **At least two moderate thresholds:** peak demand at least50MW; area at least50acres; water at least25000gallons/day; generation at least50MW; qualifying air permit; or a consistent uniform additional city-ordinance threshold.

The major water rule preserves its exact logical distinction:50000gallons/day-or-greater or exceeding5%of available city water capacity, whichever threshold is less, evaluated under foreseeable peak/seasonal/emergency conditions. No city capacity is invented and the expression is not flattened into one fixed limit. Common development, phases, existing-plus-proposed modifications and supporting infrastructure are considered collectively.

Independent professional review, reasonable necessary cost reimbursement where required, public materials/participation, at least one hearing and written evidence-supported findings are procedural requirements. Missing information defers final discretionary action; it is not automatic permanent denial. Review costs are not an automatic financial bond. The proposal retains governing-body authority, state/federal preemption and vested rights, and creates no otherwise-unauthorized cause of action. Its effect depends on charter-amendment procedures and nonfinal discretionary approvals, with no accepted calendar date or implementation record.

The state reporting view also contains a different Harwood sales/use-tax question and Horace fireworks question. Neither is combined with the charter result.
