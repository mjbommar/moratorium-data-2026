# Reviewed ballot records: scope and interpretation

The [generated results view](ballot-results.md) follows the pinned canonical
publication. It is a reviewed subset, not the complete November election census.
Each displayed row is a current result observation, and multiple observations of
one measure remain separate. Blank, other-vote and turnout categories are not
filled with zero when the source omits them.

The November 5 initial cohorts cover Eliot Question 5 and Sabattus's Data Center
Moratorium question at the November 3, 2026 elections. Both questions ask enactment
of a temporary ordinance. Their bindingness and restrictive yes direction describe
the proposed mechanism; neither is a finding of present enforceability.
[Official Eliot sample ballot](https://www.eliotme.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3950/Sample-Ballot---Town-PDF),
[official Sabattus sample ballot](https://storage.googleapis.com/juniper-media-library/571/2026/10/Nov%202026%20Sabattus%20Sample%20Ballot.pdf).

The signed returns certify the respective counts. Sabattus's clerk certifies on
November 4; the return omits total ballots and blank votes. Eliot's moderator return
is dated November 3 and reports blanks and total ballots separately. Certification
of election results remains distinct from certification or implementation of an
ordinance.
[Eliot return, PDF page 3](https://www.eliotme.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3997/Election-Results-November-3-2026---Eliot-Maine-PDF),
[Sabattus clerk return](https://storage.googleapis.com/juniper-media-library/571/2026/11/Sabattus%20-%20Clerk%20Return%20of%20Votes%20-%20Municipal%20Election.pdf).

A separate secondary report supplies reported approval for both questions. The
canonical records distinguish that reported outcome from a calculated passage rule.
No denominator, threshold or legal passage rule is invented from vote arithmetic.
The earlier Eliot official-source-only observation remains unchanged; an explicit
successor adds the reported approval. Supersession removes the earlier observation
from the current view, not from history.
[Secondary approval report](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2026/11/05/politics/elections/maine-election-towns-temporary-data-center-bans/).

Eliot's proposed ordinance specifies 180 days from effectiveness and an accessory
server-equipment exception. Paragraph 5 refers both to voter enactment and seven
days after adoption. Sabattus's question summarizes a nominal May 2, 2027 endpoint
(180 days), subject to extension or repeal by municipal officers; its operative
mechanism/definition, applicability and effective-date clauses require ordinance
review. The question summary is not substituted for executed operative text.
[Eliot official proposed text](https://www.eliotme.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3909/Ballot-Question-5----Proposed-Ordinance---Data-Center-Moratorium-PDF).

Canterbury Article 4 is an earlier measure at the March 10, 2026 annual town
election. Its question proposes a permanent all-zone zoning prohibition, not a
temporary moratorium. The town-posted results show 322 yes and 63 no; the derived
385 is only yes plus no, not ballots cast. The clerk's true-copy attestation is
not treated as a dated certification. Bindingness, passage rule, outcome and
operative legal effect remain unresolved. The proposed definition includes
commercial/third-party computing and cryptocurrency facilities, with on-site
business/institutional and residential accessory-equipment exclusions.
[Official Canterbury results](https://www.canterburynh.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Town-Meeting-Election-Results-3_10_26.pdf),
[official proposed Article 4 text](https://www.canterburynh.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-Warrant-Article-4-Data-Centers.pdf).

Nottingham's September 8, 2026 special-election question proposes a twelve-month
permitting pause. The town-posted marked document retains a SAMPLE BALLOT
heading and shows 1,335 yes / 143 no. The derived 1,478 is only yes plus no;
certification and outcome remain unknown. Proposed text anchors twelve calendar
months to adoption, with earlier legislative-body repeal. Its embedded/accessory
computing exclusion requires all three conditions: the specified 25% floor-area
cap, on-site enterprise service without primarily providing third-party services,
and no leasing of data services to third parties. Proposal signatures record
Planning Board support on July 1, not voter enactment. Bindingness, formal referral,
operative adoption and later legal force remain unresolved.
[Official Nottingham marked ballot](https://www.nottingham-nh.gov/sites/g/files/vyhlif3611/f/news/2026_nottingham_special_town_election_results.pdf),
[official proposed ordinance](https://www.nottingham-nh.gov/sites/g/files/vyhlif3611/f/news/nottingham_temporary_moratorium_on_data_centers_ordinance_final_signed.pdf).

Wiscasset's November 3 Question 2 asks enactment of Article X Miscellaneous,
X-19 Data Center Moratorium; the signed warrant numbers it Article 4. The town's
unofficial results show 1,363 yes / 712 no. Blanks and question-specific ballots
cast remain unknown; the broader election page's total is not assigned to this
question. Proposed text specifies six calendar months following enactment,
retroactive coverage for applications filed on or after November 3, and conditional
extensions of at most 180 days each. Those terms do not establish an operative
adoption date, certified outcome or current legal force.
[Official Wiscasset sample ballot](https://www.wiscasset.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1725/Sample-Ballot---Town-Referendum---November-3-2026),
[unofficial results](https://www.wiscasset.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/211),
[proposed ordinance text](https://www.wiscasset.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1743/Data-Center-Moratorium-Ordinance---Final).

North Berwick's November 3 Question 2 asks enactment of a zoning amendment to
define and prohibit data centers. Its signed clerk return certifies 2,114 yes /
582 no on November 3; blanks and ballots cast remain unknown. Proposed text
requires specified primary computing uses and at least 25,000 square feet of
aggregate defined Data Center Area, including associated impervious surfaces.
Accessory server/IT infrastructure is excluded below the specified aggregate
threshold. The proposed land-use table marks NO in all eleven listed district
columns. Result certification does not establish its operative legal interval.
[Official North Berwick ballot](https://www.townofnorthberwick.org/DocumentCenter/View/1601/Town-Referdum-November-4-2026),
[signed results](https://www.townofnorthberwick.org/DocumentCenter/View/1639/2026-Election--Town-Results),
[proposed definition and table, pages 20–21](https://www.townofnorthberwick.org/DocumentCenter/View/1615/November-2026-Referendum).

Wells's November 3 Article 5 asks enactment of a retroactive data-center
moratorium and incorporates warrant Appendix 1. The clerk certificate records
5,818 yes / 1,282 no / 459 blanks, signed November 4. Total ballots cast remains
unknown. The submitted proposal uses at least 100,000 square feet aggregate
defined Data Center Area, including associated impervious surfaces. Its 180-day
term runs from June 16 applicability, while effectiveness is conditioned on Town
Meeting enactment. These clauses do not establish a new 180-day November term
or prove the current legal interval. A separate agenda draft leaves its threshold
blank; it is not substituted for the warrant version.
[Official Wells warrant](https://wellstown.org/DocumentCenter/View/8514/November-3-2026-Special-Town-Meeting-Warrant?bidId=),
[signed results](https://www.wellstown.org/DocumentCenter/View/8559/November-3-2026-Official-Election-Results?bidId=).

Peru's town-hosted November 3 results image labels Article 2 Data Center
Moratorium and reports 578 yes / 182 no. Exact ballot wording and yes-vote
direction remain unresolved. The separate 774 Total Voters is not assigned as a
question-specific denominator; blanks and certification remain unknown. Proposed
text specifies a 180-day pause from effectiveness, an exception for routine
upgrades to existing telecommunications facilities, and possible later legislative
repeal/extension/replacement. September proposal signatures do not establish
voter enactment or an operative interval.
[Official Peru result image](https://townofperumaine.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/v.png),
[pre-election proposal notice](https://townofperumaine.org/temporary-moratorium-of-the-development-and-permitting-of-data-centers-to-be-on-the-november-2026-ballot/),
[proposed ordinance](https://townofperumaine.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Temporary-Moratorium-of-the-Development-and-Permitting-of-Data-Centers.pdf).

Brownfield's November 3 warrant Article 1 asks approval of a temporary moratorium;
the town tally labels it Question 1 and shows 671 yes / 191 no. Its separate
862 ballots-counted declaration is preserved with its source label and scope,
without assigning a ballots-cast denominator. Certification and blanks remain
unknown. Proposed text uses at least 5,000 square feet aggregate defined area
and literal less-than 250 kW computing / less-than 1 MW service wording, whose
legal application remains unresolved. The proposed 180 days run from September
16 applicability, distinct from Town Meeting adoption/effectiveness.
[Official Brownfield warrant](https://brownfieldme.civicpluswebopen.com/media/6711),
[tally](https://www.brownfieldmaine.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1000/Referendum-Results),
[proposed ordinance](https://brownfieldme.civicpluswebopen.com/media/6701).

Freedom's source-qualified tally photograph identifies November 3 Article 3,
asking adoption of the Data Center Moratorium Ordinance. It shows 331 yes / 115
no, plus VOIDS 24 and a circled 470 total. The latter observations remain separate
from standard vote categories and denominators. The public account is named
Freedom Town Office, but affiliation and certification have not been independently
authenticated. The town-linked draft has blank adoption/applicability dates and
no operative expiration clause; its at-least-180-day preamble study need is not
an accepted term. Passage outcome and current legal force remain unresolved.
[Source tally post](https://www.facebook.com/FreedomMaineTownOffice/posts/secret-ballot-election-results/1459298219642313/),
[official town-linked draft](https://www.freedomme.org/_files/ugd/8d8f5c_be3f789c00ae4e1eb75f14bae34aac9e.pdf).

Alhambra, California, Measure DB retains the November 5 county snapshot of 15,237
yes / 3,132 no, explicitly superseding the older 13,314 / 2,889 observation.
Certification, reporting completeness and final passage outcome remain unknown.
The actual question adopts an ordinance affirming the existing council ban; a
failed measure would leave that council ban in place, according to the city
attorney. The June 22 predecessor adoption and July 27 election referral remain
separate acts. Unsigned Word attachments supply proposed clauses, not certified
voter enactment. The accessory exclusion requires both incidental use and strict
support of on-site operations; no acreage threshold is asserted for Alhambra.
[County returns](https://results.lavote.gov/text-results/4348),
[scoped review](../work/research/2026-11-05/california-ballot-review/README.md).

Monterey Park, California, Measure NDC is the earlier June 2 election, with
certified 10,388 yes / 1,372 no and source-reported approval. Its contest-labelled
canvass supplies 12,381 ballots cast and 32,781 registrations; the difference from
yes/no is not assigned to blanks or under/overvotes. The result observation uses
the June 30 city-clerk certification phase, while source claims retain June 26
county certification, July 1 council declaration and signed Ordinance 2280 dated
July 2 separately. The land-based one-acre definition and conjunctive under-one-acre
accessory/incidental exclusion remain explicit. The ten-day Council-certification
rule, implementation and current force remain unresolved. Ordinance 2280 is not
silently merged with council Ordinance 2278 or prior moratoria 2272/2274.
[Certification and ordinance packet](https://montereypark.ca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/19275/2026-R56-Certifying-Election-June-2-2026),
[publication scope](../work/research/2026-11-05/california-promotion/README.md).

No current legal-force restriction, instrument event or measure/instrument link has
been accepted in these initial cohorts, and their publication does not add active
legacy moratorium rows. Other jurisdictions, earlier and future elections, legal
implementation, passage rules and the full freshness accounting remain work in progress.
