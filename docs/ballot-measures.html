Reviewed ballot records: scope and interpretation

The generated results view follows the pinned canonical publication. It is a reviewed subset, not the complete November election census. Each displayed row is a current result observation, and multiple observations of one measure remain separate. Blank, other-vote and turnout categories are not filled with zero when the source omits them.

The November 5 initial cohorts cover Eliot Question 5 and Sabattus's Data Center Moratorium question at the November 3, 2026 elections. Both questions ask enactment of a temporary ordinance. Their bindingness and restrictive yes direction describe the proposed mechanism; neither is a finding of present enforceability. Official Eliot sample ballot, official Sabattus sample ballot.

The signed returns certify the respective counts. Sabattus's clerk certifies on November 4; the return omits total ballots and blank votes. Eliot's moderator return is dated November 3 and reports blanks and total ballots separately. Certification of election results remains distinct from certification or implementation of an ordinance. Eliot return, PDF page 3, Sabattus clerk return.

A separate secondary report supplies reported approval for both questions. The canonical records distinguish that reported outcome from a calculated passage rule. No denominator, threshold or legal passage rule is invented from vote arithmetic. The earlier Eliot official-source-only observation remains unchanged; an explicit successor adds the reported approval. Supersession removes the earlier observation from the current view, not from history. Secondary approval report.

Eliot's proposed ordinance specifies 180 days from effectiveness and an accessory server-equipment exception. Paragraph 5 refers both to voter enactment and seven days after adoption. Sabattus's question summarizes a nominal May 2, 2027 endpoint (180 days), subject to extension or repeal by municipal officers; its operative mechanism/definition, applicability and effective-date clauses require ordinance review. The question summary is not substituted for executed operative text. Eliot official proposed text.

Canterbury Article 4 is an earlier measure at the March 10, 2026 annual town election. Its question proposes a permanent all-zone zoning prohibition, not a temporary moratorium. The town-posted results show 322 yes and 63 no; the derived 385 is only yes plus no, not ballots cast. The clerk's true-copy attestation is not treated as a dated certification. Bindingness, passage rule, outcome and operative legal effect remain unresolved. The proposed definition includes commercial/third-party computing and cryptocurrency facilities, with on-site business/institutional and residential accessory-equipment exclusions. Official Canterbury results, official proposed Article 4 text.

Nottingham's September 8, 2026 special-election question proposes a twelve-month permitting pause. The town-posted marked document retains a SAMPLE BALLOT heading and shows 1,335 yes / 143 no. The derived 1,478 is only yes plus no; certification and outcome remain unknown. Proposed text anchors twelve calendar months to adoption, with earlier legislative-body repeal. Its embedded/accessory computing exclusion requires all three conditions: the specified 25% floor-area cap, on-site enterprise service without primarily providing third-party services, and no leasing of data services to third parties. Proposal signatures record Planning Board support on July 1, not voter enactment. Bindingness, formal referral, operative adoption and later legal force remain unresolved. Official Nottingham marked ballot, official proposed ordinance.

No current legal-force restriction, instrument event or measure/instrument link has been accepted in these initial cohorts, and their publication does not add active legacy moratorium rows. Other jurisdictions, earlier and future elections, legal implementation, passage rules and the full freshness accounting remain work in progress.

Canonical tables are available at data/expanded/<table>.jsonl in a checkout. Resolve the directory once when reading several tables. The immutable first Eliot snapshot and Sabattus/result-update snapshot preserve the exact tables, source versions and publication plans. Visual source review is not a claim of full-document extraction coverage.

The Sabattus review packet and publication receipt record local publication. The replay receipt and offline consistency check validate retained publication behavior; they do not reacquire sources or establish current legal force. GitHub release, full public exports, remaining measures and consumer updates retain the separate release gates.
