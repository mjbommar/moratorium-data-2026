Expanded candidate data contracts, version 1.0.0

These additive contracts describe research candidates and reviewed canonical expanded records. They do not modify the legacy inventory or state-policy CSVs. The existing codebook's moratorium grain and exclusion of permanent bans remain unchanged. A successful validation is structural consistency, not proof of legal effect, source accuracy, identity, completeness, or permission to publish. An initial reviewed snapshot writer now supplies separate identity, evidence, field-review and atomic-publication gates; reviewed canonical records include Eliot, Sabattus, Canterbury, Nottingham, Wiscasset, North Berwick, Wells and Peru, with explicit result supersession. See the generated results view. Identity decisions have a separate append-only registry.

Files and validation

JSON Schema 2020-12 contains a named $defs schema for each JSONL table. Every record has schema_version: "1.0.0". Use UTF-8, one object per line, in files named <table>.jsonl. Missing tables are allowed and mean no records supplied, not that the universe was searched. Unknown JSONL filenames and duplicate IDs within a table fail. Examples in synthetic jurisdiction fixture are entirely invented; ZZ and example.invalid are deliberately fictitious.

~/projects/bc/bc-modules/.venv/bin/python scripts/expanded_data.py \
  work/schemas/expanded/fixtures/synthetic
~/projects/bc/bc-modules/.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_expanded_data.py -v

Use --moratoria data/moratorium_inventory.csv and/or --state-actions data/state_legislation.csv when tagged references use those legacy types. The validator reads these CSVs only to resolve their ID columns; missing files, columns, or IDs fail. Restrictions resolve to the supplied JSONL records. The CLI is read-only and returns 0 for valid input and 1 for invalid input. Its certified count is current explicitly certified result observations, not measures or legal instruments; independent observations may describe the same measure. Only explicit supersession removes an observation from that count. It is a diagnostic, not a public tally. Missing rows and unresolved references cannot establish absence.

Grains and semantics

The table definitions are jurisdictions, elections, ballot_measures, measure_provisions, measure_results, local_restrictions, instrument_events, measure_instrument_links, source_documents, claims, review_decisions, search_runs, search_attempts, search_sightings, and coverage_observations. Each table's singular ID field is its key. IDs are opaque and must be allocated from a reviewed, collision-checked registry; the identity registry now supplies explicit UUID allocation, observation aliases and merge/split lineage. It performs no identity inference and does not promote domain records. Names and titles are not keys. Distinct same-name governments must have distinct IDs. This first validator requires a measure and its election to use the same jurisdiction ID; overlapping administrative and electoral jurisdictions need a future explicit relationship contract rather than a guessed equivalence.

A measure has independent lifecycle, bindingness, yes-direction and referendum polarity. Provisions capture mechanisms, covered uses, exemptions, and thresholds with original unit, measurement basis, aggregation and scope. An election result is an observation with separate reported and calculated outcomes and explicit certification. A complete precinct count does not certify a result. For a mechanically calculated outcome this version uses the stated denominator, threshold and strict or inclusive comparison. It reverses restricts/facilitates for a defeated question; mixed, neutral and unresolved remain as stated. A direction is not a legal-effect assertion. Unspecified totals stay null, never zero. The validator allows optional outcome_direction_basis to distinguish calculated (the default for legacy candidates), reported, and unresolved. A reported basis requires a source_reported value method for reported_outcome and a matching claim scoped to that exact result observation. It does not invent a passage rule, certify the result, establish legal effect, or override contradictory calculable arithmetic. The validator checks arithmetic only when inputs are available, and compares reported percentages to the chosen denominator with 0.11 percentage point rounding tolerance. Optional yes_no_votes records the arithmetic sum without inventing total valid votes or turnout; blank_votes remains distinct from overvotes and undervotes. Optional source_vote_categories preserve original labels/counts such as VOIDS, without mapping them to blanks, other votes, undervotes or overvotes. Optional source_vote_totals preserve printed totals with their original label and an explicit scope. Neither field supplies a denominator or changes standard counts. Each item requires nonnegative count and supporting claim IDs. Every claim must match the exact result, source document, predicate (source_vote_category or source_vote_total) and complete label/count/scope value. Duplicate label/scope entries and forced mapping keys are rejected. Absent fields remain compatible with earlier snapshots. Public summaries display these observations separately.

value_methods identifies text, OCR, visual review, source reporting or derivation per result field with supporting claim IDs. Optional provision/restriction duration distinguishes enactment, adoption, effectiveness and applicability anchors; no dates are equated without source/legal review. It retains calendar months/years versus days, the legal anchor and its uncertain date; it does not automatically calculate an operative expiration. Complicated passage rules should remain unknown and be described in evidence; they cannot be represented or calculated completely by this first increment.

instrument is a tagged reference (moratorium, restriction, or state_action) so legacy identifiers do not collide. Accepted measure-to-instrument relations require a current accepted decision scoped to that exact relation, and each supporting claim needs its own current accepted decision scoped to its predicate. Claims identify the document version and their subject. Accepted decisions require supporting claim IDs, and those claims must address the target. A claim decision uses that claim itself as its supporting ID; this records review of the source assertion, not independent corroboration. Superseded decisions do not count as current; competing successors fail. An advisory measure cannot itself enact or uphold an instrument through an accepted link. Independent subsequent adoption requires its own event and evidence. An advisory result never creates an active moratorium: this validator generates no instrument or status at all.

Source records distinguish payload and extracted-text hashes and retain retrieval, access and publication classification. Create a new document ID when bytes change; old claims keep their original document reference. Search ledgers retain attempts, errors, retries, sightings, run scope and spend; null spend means unknown, not free. Coverage observations describe a bounded search, not proof of absence. These contracts do not check URL bodies, quote spans, rights, reviewer competence or whether the cited source actually supports a claim.

scripts/audit_refresh_returns.py adds a separate pre-review check against retained bounded-client artifacts: declared source hashes, independently located excerpts, and candidate seed coverage against the frozen discovery list. A payload-only document with null text hash can pass payload accounting but cannot prove a text claim. Visual claims require their own reviewed source/page/render provenance; an unlocated text quote is not automatically a visual claim. The audit does not promote candidates or establish legal effect, and its success cannot substitute for substantive review. Frozen seeds describe that source list, not a full census. Earlier pilot evidence is reusable only through its pinned input manifest and fresh body/text hash checks. The audit explicitly records universal newline decoding where required to reproduce the original pilot text hash; it does not silently replace source bytes. Additional omission leads have their own frozen registry and bounded recovery jobs, distinct from the initial 50-seed list.

scripts/build_ballot_census.py compiles terminal ballot packets into a separate review snapshot. Every packet gets its own ID namespace, including claim subjects, result revisions and value-method claim references; external legacy instrument IDs and free-form source values remain unchanged. Explicit identity decisions pin both measure records before grouping aliases. Grouping does not delete observations or accept legal effect. A reviewed input overlay must match its review receipt's output hashes. The builder refuses to overwrite snapshots and records input and expanded-output hashes. Combined unreviewed claims remain private until excerpt and evidence review; the public census manifest is not canonical ballot promotion.

Visual transcription reviews require a pinned claim, values, source PDF hash, page and rendering parameters. visual_claim_review.py rehashes the retained PDF and reproduces the approved page-image hash. This verifies the reviewed rendering chain, not the reviewer judgement, election certification or legal effect. The return audit reports visual verification separately from located text evidence. Reviewed overlays are audited separately from unmodified worker returns. Each overlay must match its receipt's output hashes; accepted claim decisions are checked against verified evidence. An accepted result transcription does not accept the measure's bindingness or legal implementation.

Dates and limitations

date_bounds has inclusive earliest/latest possible dates, precision and original text. day requires equal non-null bounds; unknown precision requires null bounds. Jurisdiction validity and coverage periods use [start, end); convert source-inclusive end dates only with evidence and retain original wording. Full timestamps require a UTC offset and are kept distinct from date-only facts. Adoption, effective date and applicability start are separate. When known bounds establish retroactivity, require an explanation and an applicability-start claim; validation does not adjudicate that claim. Null or overlapping bounds do not imply chronology. Duration remains source text; calendar months and years are not converted into 30/365-day terms, and expiry is not calculated. The legacy duration_days field has historical conversion assumptions described in the codebook and is not reinterpreted here.

This is a first contract increment, not the P1 pilot exit: it does not validate all legal chronology or certification evidence, infer external instrument status, verify accepted claims, reconcile independent result revisions, model all legal passage rules, or implement authoritative IDs, migrations, CSV exports, review application, atomic promotion, source capture, or release. A source-backed pilot, identity registry, broader review gates and transaction design remain required before canonical use. Certification and active-status counts must be derived from reviewed, scoped evidence and a declared cutoff, never from these diagnostic counts.
