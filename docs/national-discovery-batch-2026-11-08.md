## Exact scope

The [frozen proposed plan](../work/research/2026-11-08/national-discovery-proposal/plan.json)
and [cost/count preview](../work/research/2026-11-08/national-discovery-proposal/preview.json)
contain **600 explicit cells**: all 50 states, five existing sectors plus a neutral
recovery query, in both new-action discovery and independent election discovery.
Each cell has one Exa and one Google/SerpAPI variant: **600 calls per provider,
1,200 total search reservations**. The neutral coordinate is a search aid, not a
new canonical sector or an assertion that all general land-use measures apply to
data centers. Cryptocurrency search terms retain the documented translation from
`cryptocurrency_mining`.

Queries include proposed/adopted actions, extensions, repeal/replacement and
injunctions; election queries include approval/prohibition, petitions, failed
qualifications and withdrawals. Broad permanent restrictions are leads for legal
history or the appropriate expanded restriction object, not automatic additions
to the temporary-moratorium inventory. Unknown official hosts are not converted
into domain filters. Original query variants, sightings, errors and empty results
remain visible; repetition is not independent corroboration or evidence of absence.

The existing November 7 research target remains. Actual collection times and
post-cutoff events are recorded separately. New retrieval does not make every
older claim current, and the phase does not imply a national municipal census.
The first twelve cells span the five sectors and neutral recovery in both lanes;
they are additional search diagnostics, not a fresh extraction holdout or proof of
full pilot acceptance. Review findings may stop subsequent work; they never make
unretrieved or unreviewed facts eligible for promotion.

[Exa's current pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing) lists Auto search at $7 per 1,000
requests with up to ten results. The prepared plan explicitly disables content
extras. Its 600 searches therefore have an estimated $4.20 base allocation.
A private, credential-free record of the actual SerpAPI account metadata establishes
$0.01 per search as a subscription allocation; 600 searches allocate $6.00.
[SerpAPI's pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing) and
[free Account API](https://serpapi.com/account-api) document the plan/quota model.
The combined estimate is **$10.20**, with the larger $25 admission ceiling and
nonrefundable holds covering uncertain or failed attempts. This is not an invoice
or a promise that provider pricing cannot change. Existing unpriced runs remain
unpriced; this prospective allocation does not rewrite their accounting.

The new priced path binds the exact plan, runtime, non-secret effective settings
and price assumptions to a separately hash-pinned contract. It uses the existing
locked attempt ledger and native `bc_core.BudgetMeter` before dispatch. Every
search retains its full hold after success, failure, cancellation or unknown cost;
no automatic retry, cap reset, refill or namespace switch recovers spent capacity.
Known returned charges and unknown charges remain distinct. A returned charge
above its reserved amount blocks later admissions for review, without claiming
to reverse an unexpected charge already incurred.

One native searcher and its limiter instances are shared across cells. Actual
SerpAPI pacing will be pinned at no more than 2,700 searches/hour, below the
verified account limit; the coordinator must also account for other processes.
The $25 concerns this phase's search allocation. Direct HTTP/browser resources,
existing subscriptions, CLI computation/review, taxes and final invoices are not
silently represented as free or covered by that amount. No plan upgrade, early
renewal, credit purchase or paid model-extraction trial is authorized here.

Source follow-up uses the same phase ledger with HTTP first. Pydoll requires an
actual prior HTTP attempt on that URL and a substantive rendered-content need.
Two-browser concurrency is a coordinator scheduling limit, not a claimed persisted
semaphore. Full source bodies, provider payloads and browser/network captures stay
private. Capture, publication, event and legal-effect clocks remain separate.
